## Final Explanation reviewer findings (unverified; some are false alarms)
- **Section 3 exemplar (b), classification of A table salt**: The exemplar says table salt is a compound 'like B water and H sugar' and gives the reason as 'made of more than one kind of element joined together', which is fine. However, the Section 3 prompt asks for D, A and M only, and the exemplar adds extra substances. This is not an error. The real issue is that the exemplar introduces the formula 'sodium chloride', a term never taught in the lessons.
- **Section 2 exemplar (c) and Section 5 exemplar vs sand/iron data**: The exemplar gives the loss reason as 'spillage and some material sticking to the equipment', yet each recovered mass is below 3 g, so the iron lost only 0.1 g, sand 0.2 g and salt 0.3 g. The exemplar does not note that the loss is spread unevenly, nor that the mass of sand could have retained water if not fully dried. This is a minor omission and not a numerical error.
- **Section 1 prompt table, E iron filings heat test and Section 1 exemplar**: The exemplar's heat-test comparison omits the table's A salt 'crackles, no melting' and E iron 'glow orange-red' results, and states that C sand 'does not change' while claiming burning as a chemical property only for sulphur. Iron changing to a dull blackish solid on heating is also a chemical change (oxidation) that the exemplar does not use, so the evidence for a chemical property is incomplete.
