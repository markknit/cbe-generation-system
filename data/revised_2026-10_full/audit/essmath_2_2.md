## Final Explanation reviewer findings (unverified; some are false alarms)
- **Part 4(b) exemplar**: The argument that a non-perpendicular line through the midpoint gives 'smaller' perpendicular distances is muddled. It also uses property 2 as an assumption, so it is circular. The correct reasoning is that the reflection of P in a line that is not perpendicular to PP′ lies off the line PP′, so it cannot be P′. The mirror line is exactly the set of points equidistant from P and P′.
- **Part 4(c) exemplar**: The equation step reuses the labels (x₁, y₁) for the midpoint, although they were defined as the coordinates of P. This is confusing, and the exemplar says to use the midpoint as the point (x₁, y₁) with gradient −1/m. The midpoint should be given its own symbols, such as (xₘ, yₘ). The special cases are also inconsistent: when PP′ is vertical, m is undefined, so the method cannot be applied directly.
- **Lesson 3 vs Lesson 8, and Part 5 exemplar**: Lesson 3 says the 'perpendicular mapping flips the left–right orientation' and Lesson 8 says it 'swaps left and right'. In fact reflection reverses only the direction perpendicular to the mirror. Part 5 also says the 'perpendicular' direction is reversed, but then explains the letter 'A' flip as left–right. For a mirror on the floor or ceiling the flip would be up–down. The explanation is loosely stated.
