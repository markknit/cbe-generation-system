# The 14 guidelines for lesson plan creation, edits and changes (from the project owner, 2026-10-14)

1. Storyline and phenomenon
- Can one anchor phenomenon carry every lesson, and does each lesson visibly come back to it?
- Does each lesson end on a question that motivates the next one?
- Is the Driving Question Board revisited and updated, not built once and dropped? (Always call it "Driving Question Board".)
- Do supporting phenomena connect to the anchor and stay scientifically sound?
- Can students give a final explanation of the phenomenon using everything they have learned?

2. KICD alignment
- Are strand and sub-strand numbers consistent throughout?
- Are the specific learning outcomes, key inquiry questions, core competencies, values, PCIs and cross-subject links present and tied to real activities, not just listed?
- Is CBE language used throughout (Grade 10, not "Form 1")?

3. Time and pacing
- Does the overview total match the sum of the lessons?
- Do each lesson's activities fit its period: 40 minutes single, 80 minutes double?
- Are timings realistic for large classes with shared equipment?

4. Sequence logic
- Do prerequisites come before they are needed?
- Do students experience something before they are given the vocabulary for it?
- Does any resource or slide spoil a discovery before the lesson it belongs to?

5. Student thinking
- Are common misconceptions named for the teacher, with a plan to surface and address them?
- Are expected student answers given, so teachers know what "good" sounds like?

6. Models and analogies
- Is each analogy mapped the same way everywhere it appears (text, tables, sketches)?
- Are each analogy's limits stated?

7. Sensemaking scaffolds
- Is Claim-Evidence-Reasoning defined correctly? (Claim = answer to the question; Evidence = data or observations; Reasoning = the science idea linking them.)
- Are Summary Tables completed and used to build the final explanation?

8. Practical feasibility and safety
- Are materials available in Kenyan partner schools, with low-cost substitutes listed?
- Are expected observations realistic with school equipment?
- Does every practical include safety notes?

9. Local and cultural accuracy
- Are names, institutions and examples current and correct (e.g., KALRO, not KARI)?
- Do contexts feel authentic to Kenyan students, with no deficit or rescuer framing?

10. Summative assessment
- Does an end-of-unit task assess the explanation of the phenomenon, not just recall?
- Are CBE rubric levels (Exceeds, Meets, Approaches, Below Expectations) included?

11. Resources and links
- Does every link open and land on the exact item named (not a homepage or search page)?
- Does each resource match its stated purpose and level?
- Is any resource flagged "not an exact match" still in the SoW? If so, suggest a replacement or removal.

12. Inclusion
- Are there language supports for English learners, including Kiswahili where it helps?
- Are there accommodations for learners with special needs, and more than one way to show understanding?

13. Teacher usability
- Could a teacher who did not write this teach it tomorrow? Check for answer keys, look-fors, and "if students say X, do Y" guidance.

14. Consistency
- Does the SoW use the same template, headings, terms and phase names as SeaVuria's other SoWs (Predict, Observe, Explain, Driving Question Board, Model Building)?
