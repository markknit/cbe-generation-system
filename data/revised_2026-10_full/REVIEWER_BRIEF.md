You are revising ONE existing Kenyan CBE Grade 10 STEM sub-strand (Lesson Sequence + Final Explanation) so that it
meets the 14 guidelines in `GUIDELINES_14.md` and fixes the known audit items in `audit/<module>.md`. The existing
documents are the BASE. You make targeted corrections, you do not regenerate or start over. Teachers will review
every change you make, in Word tracked changes and in a journal built from your change records. Quality matters
more than speed or cost: read everything carefully, think about the science and the teaching, and verify.

## Hard constraints
1. Keep the existing anchoring phenomenon and driving question (teachers chose them). Do not add or remove lessons, do not reorder lessons, do not change lesson titles unless a title is factually wrong.
2. The partner's JSON contract is strict: change only the TEXT/values of the existing fields (kind=edit on strings; kind=set for whole values such as a rubric array). Do not add new keys. New content (e.g. a misconception note, a Kiswahili support, an expected student answer, a safety note) goes into an existing text field (teacherMoves, sensemakingStrategy, formativeAssessment, safetyNotes, overview...). The generic Differentiation & Inclusion table at the end of every document is generated by code and is not part of the data; lesson-specific supports belong in teacherMoves/learnerExperience.
3. Edits are exact find-and-replace: `old` must occur exactly once in the named field. To insert text, include a short existing anchor in `old` and repeat it in `new`. Keep `old` as short as is still unique. Larger rewrites inside one lesson are allowed when the content is wrong (mark severity "substantial"), but keep the lesson's structure, phases and the teachers' storyline.
4. After a change, hunt down every other place the same fact appears (other phases, other lessons, `summaryTablePrompt`, the Final Explanation prompts and exemplars, rubric, UNIT fields) and make a consistent record there too. A fix that creates a new contradiction is worse than none. Check numbers by recomputing them yourself.
5. Resources and links (guideline 11) come from a matcher and are not in the data: do NOT try to edit links. If a lesson's text leans on a specific video/slide that may spoil a discovery or does not fit, add a `flag` record.
6. Do not run generate/render/API commands. Do not touch anything outside `$CBE_REVISED_DIR/` and your scratch folder.

## What to do
1. Read the whole dump slowly, lesson by lesson, plus the audit file. 2. For each of the 14 guidelines, check the whole sub-strand and the Final Explanation; fix what fails. Typical real problems: science errors (verify with your own knowledge: units, formulae, chemistry, biology facts, maths answers), dataset/number mismatches, sequence problems (vocabulary or results used before they are taught), wrong "in Lesson N we did X" cross-references, time that does not add up for 40/80 minute periods or against UNIT.totalDuration, missing/incorrect safety notes for practicals, "Form 1" language, outdated institution names (KALRO not KARI, etc.), deficit/rescuer framing, missing expected student answers or "if students say X, do Y" moves, missing misconceptions, CER defined wrongly (Claim = answer; Evidence = data/observations; Reasoning = science idea linking them), phase names/Driving Question Board naming (always "Driving Question Board"), missing CBE rubric levels (Exceeds, Meets, Approaches, Below Expectations) in the Final Explanation rubric, missing language supports (English learners, Kiswahili terms where helpful). Do not invent problems to look busy: if something already meets a guideline, leave it.
3. Be proportionate: add inclusion/misconception/look-for content only where it is missing and it matters for that lesson; keep additions short and specific. Prefer fixing over flagging.
4. Teacher-decision items (two acceptable options, local knowledge needed, or a judgement call): make your best concrete suggestion as a normal change with `"teacher_decision": true`. Use `kind: "flag"` only when you really cannot make a concrete suggestion.
5. Every record needs `why` (plain English a teacher understands) and, for category `science`, a `reference` naming a real source (e.g. KICD Grade 10 curriculum design, a named textbook/OpenStax chapter, IUPAC, a recognised standards body, NIST, WHO). Name source and topic; do not invent page numbers or URLs. If you cannot name a real source, make the change `teacher_decision: true` and say so.
6. When finished, run `python3 scripts/revise_apply.py apply ...` for real, then re-dump and re-read the changed lessons to confirm they read well and nothing contradicts. Report in at most 12 lines: records applied/rejected, how many science/substantial/teacher-decision/flag, the 3 most important fixes, and anything you could not resolve.


## Changes in this round (these override anything above that conflicts)
**A. Group knock-on edits under one root fix.** Each real problem is ONE fix, even when it needs edits in many places. Give every record that stems from the same root fix the same `"group"` key (e.g. `"G3"`), and put a one-line plain-English `"group_headline"` on the root record only (e.g. "Balance setup could not work: 100 g needed 60 cm on a metre rule; now 20 cm"). Teachers will see ONE journal item per group with the knock-on edits listed beneath it, so the headline must make sense on its own. The individual edits stay separate records (exact find/replace) and remain visible one by one in the tracked-change documents. Records that stand alone need no group.
**B. Fewer, meaningful records.** No micro-edits: if two changes sit in the same field, do one edit covering both. Do not make a record for a purely cosmetic word swap unless it serves a guideline; fold small wording fixes into the nearest related record. Do not split a timing trim across ten records: one group, one headline. Every record must serve a named guideline.
**C. `teacher_decision: true` only for genuine judgement calls.** Use it ONLY when (1) two defensible options exist and you chose one, (2) the fix needs local or classroom knowledge you cannot have (local plants, markets, equipment teachers actually have, Kiswahili/local terms, ATP/practical standards), (3) you changed a storyline or lesson design substantially, or (4) you could not name a real source for a science fix. NOT for: clear factual or arithmetic corrections, knock-on edits (only the root carries the flag), timing trims, or added supports/misconception notes/expected answers. Aim for no more than about 1 in 10 records, and set it on the group root, not on every member. Say in `why` what exactly the teacher must decide.
**D. KICD wording check (guideline 2).** Compare `UNIT.learningOutcomes`, the key inquiry questions, and the sub-strand content/competencies with the KICD curriculum text. The PDFs are in `data/raw/curriculum_pdfs/` (`pdftotext -layout FILE out.txt`, then search for the sub-strand name). KICD March 2025 wording is verbatim for specific learning outcomes, content bullets, key inquiry questions, core competencies, values and PCIs. Where the data paraphrases or drops an outcome, restore the KICD wording (category `consistency`, criterion 2, reference "KICD Grade 10 curriculum design, <subject>, <strand/sub-strand>") and check each lesson's slo still serves it. If no KICD PDF covers this sub-strand (e.g. General Science, Core/Essential Mathematics may be missing), say so in your report and flag it; do not invent KICD wording. Also check that every KICD outcome is actually taught and assessed.
**E. UNIT.totalDuration.** Recompute it from the lesson durations (sum of lesson minutes, using the real `duration` of each lesson) and make it correct and consistent with the phase minutes inside lessons. Put the calculation in `why`.
**F. Final Explanation rubric.** The contract allows only three level fields; the printed headings are fixed by the template. Do not try to add a 4th level. Write the rubric descriptors so the level names Exceeds / Meets / Approaches Expectations appear in the descriptor text where it helps, and add one flag record (no group, `teacher_decision: true`) saying the Below Expectations level cannot be represented in the current contract.
**G. Verify science twice.** For every science correction, recompute independently (arithmetic, units, formulae, chemical equations) and say in `why` how you checked. A wrong correction is worse than leaving the original.
**H. Report** (at most 14 lines): records applied/rejected, number of groups, science/substantial/teacher-decision/flag counts, the 3 most important fixes, KICD wording findings, and anything unresolved.

## Lessons from the pilot (full run additions)
- Do not read, edit or `apply` any module other than yours. Use ONLY your own scratch folder.
- Report as in H. Also state in one line: sub-strand number/name mismatches against KICD (flag, do not rename), and any KICD content that is not taught.
- The sub-strand's student-facing text may be in English with Kiswahili supports; never put unverified Kiswahili in as fact: mark such records `teacher_decision: true` (one grouped record, not one per term).
- Keep the teachers' anchoring phenomenon unless it is scientifically false as stated; if it is, keep the phenomenon but correct the explanation, and mark the group `teacher_decision: true`.
- NEVER edit the `phase` label of a framework phase (the contract allows only these exact strings: "Predict Phase", "Observe Phase", "Explain Phase", "Driving Question Board (DQB) Creation", "Model Building Phase"). The renderer and the partner's validator key on them. If you think a label is wrong, add one flag record instead.
