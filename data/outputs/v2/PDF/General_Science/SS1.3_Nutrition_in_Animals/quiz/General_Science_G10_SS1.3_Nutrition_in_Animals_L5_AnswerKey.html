Q1. Before doing any tests, a learner predicts which nutrients are found in a cup of milk from the Kenyan meal samples. Which prediction is correct?
  1. Milk contains protein only, with no fat
  2. Milk contains fat only, with no protein
  3. Milk contains both protein and fat
  4. Milk contains neither protein nor fat because it is a liquid
Correct answer: C
Milk is a familiar source of both protein and fat, so it should give a positive Biuret test and a positive ethanol emulsion test. Many learners wrongly think milk has no fat.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Biuret test on the six labelled samples (NaOH first, then CuSO₄)
Q2. In the Biuret test for protein, a learner puts 2 cm³ of bean broth in a test tube. Which sequence of steps is correct?
  1. Add 4–5 drops of copper sulphate solution first, then add dilute NaOH
  2. Add absolute ethanol first, then pour into cold water
  3. Add iodine solution first, then heat the tube in a water bath
  4. Add dilute NaOH first, mix gently, then add 4–5 drops of 1% copper sulphate solution
Correct answer: D
NaOH is added first and then copper sulphate. Adding the reagents in the wrong order gives a false blue result instead of violet.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: recording Biuret results and the whole-class collated results table
Q3. During the Biuret test, a group records a violet colour in the tube containing nyama choma extract. What should they conclude?
  1. Reducing sugar is present in the nyama choma
  2. Fat is present in the nyama choma
  3. Starch is present in the nyama choma
  4. Protein is present in the nyama choma
Correct answer: D
A violet or purple colour with Biuret reagent is the positive result for protein. Starch, fat and reducing sugars are detected by other tests.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: ethanol emulsion test on butter, cooking oil and the other samples
Q4. In the ethanol emulsion test, a learner dissolves 2 cm³ of cooking oil in 5 cm³ of absolute ethanol. The learner then pours the solution into a test tube containing 5 cm³ of cold water. What result shows that fat is present?
  1. The mixture turns brick-red
  2. The mixture turns milky white as an emulsion forms
  3. The mixture turns blue-black
  4. The mixture turns violet
Correct answer: B
Fat dissolves in ethanol but is insoluble in water. When the solution is poured into water, tiny fat droplets form a milky-white emulsion, which is a positive result.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher safety demonstration and bench safety monitor check for flames
Q5. A learner is about to add absolute ethanol to a test tube during the ethanol emulsion test. What safety step must be done first?
  1. Put on a wet cloth so it can be used to blow out a flame if needed
  2. Warm the ethanol gently over a flame so it dissolves the fat faster
  3. Smell the ethanol closely to check that it is the correct liquid
  4. Make sure no flame or spirit lamp is burning on the bench
Correct answer: D
Ethanol is flammable, so all flames on the bench must be out before it is used. Heating it or sniffing it closely is unsafe.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Question 1 – why nyama choma turns violet with Biuret reagent
Q6. Nyama choma gives a strong violet colour with Biuret reagent. Which explanation of this colour change is correct?
  1. Copper ions react with starch molecules to form a violet complex
  2. NaOH turns violet when it reacts with fat droplets
  3. Copper ions coordinate with the peptide bonds in protein chains, producing the violet colour
  4. Ethanol dissolves the protein and turns violet in water
Correct answer: C
In the Biuret test, copper ions coordinate with the peptide bonds in protein chains. More peptide bonds give a stronger violet colour.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Question 2 – link between protein in nyama choma and pepsin
Q7. Learners have shown that nyama choma is rich in protein. Which statement correctly links this to digestion in the stomach?
  1. Lipase in the stomach digests the protein into amino acids
  2. Amylase in the stomach digests the protein at neutral pH
  3. Pepsin, a protease, digests the protein, and the low pH of gastric acid activates it
  4. Bile from the gall bladder digests the protein in the stomach
Correct answer: C
The stomach secretes pepsinogen, which is activated into pepsin by the acid (low pH). Pepsin is a protease that breaks protein into peptides.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: annotating the cumulative alimentary canal diagram at the small intestine (bile salts + lipase)
Q8. Cooking oil gave a positive ethanol emulsion test. Which statement about its digestion in the small intestine is correct?
  1. Bile salts emulsify the fat, and then lipase digests it into fatty acids and glycerol
  2. Fat is digested in the stomach by pepsin before it reaches the small intestine
  3. Lipase emulsifies the fat, and then bile salts digest it into fatty acids and glycerol
  4. Bile is an enzyme that chemically digests the fat into fatty acids and glycerol
Correct answer: A
Fat is insoluble in water, so bile salts must first emulsify it into small droplets. Lipase can then digest it into fatty acids and glycerol. Bile is not an enzyme.
