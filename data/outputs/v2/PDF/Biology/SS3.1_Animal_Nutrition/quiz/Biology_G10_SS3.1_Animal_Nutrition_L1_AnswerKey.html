Use after: Predict phase — Phase 1 (Predict): reading the Kakamega farm scenario and Lake Nakuru scene
Q1. On a maize farm in Kakamega, a learner sees insects damaging maize plants. One insect leaves ragged holes in the leaves. Which insect is it?
  1. Grasshopper
  2. Mosquito
  3. Aphid
  4. Fish eagle
Correct answer: A
The farm scene describes grasshoppers chewing ragged holes through maize leaves. Aphids cluster on stems and leave the leaves intact. Mosquitoes feed on blood, and the fish eagle is a bird at Lake Nakuru.
Use after: Predict phase — Phase 1 (Predict): recording predictions on the board under 'Our Starting Ideas'
Q2. During the prediction activity, a classmate writes: 'Mosquitoes can drink blood because they are stronger than grasshoppers.' You have only just started the lesson. What is the best way to treat this prediction?
  1. Record it under 'Our Starting Ideas' and check it later against evidence
  2. Mark it wrong straight away and cross it out
  3. Ignore it because it is not a real idea
  4. Accept it as the final answer so the class can stop investigating
Correct answer: A
In this lesson, predictions are not corrected at the start. They are written on the board as starting ideas and revisited once evidence has been gathered.
Use after: Observe phase — Phase 2 (Observe): two-column observation chart during the video clips
Q3. A learner watches a mosquito feeding on skin and writes an observation in a chart. Which observation is the most specific and best based on evidence?
  1. The mosquito bit the skin
  2. The mosquito pushed a thin tube into the skin
  3. The mosquito was hungry
  4. The mosquito is stronger than other insects
Correct answer: B
A good observation describes exactly what can be seen, including the shape of the part touching the food. 'A thin tube pushed into the skin' does this. 'Bit' is vague, and 'stronger' and 'hungry' are ideas, not observations.
Use after: Observe phase — Phase 2 (Observe): Clip 2, flamingo sweeping motion, and the board words PIERCE / CHEW / SUCK SAP / FILTER / TEAR
Q4. At Lake Nakuru, thousands of flamingos sweep their bent beaks sideways through the water. Which action word from the class observation list best describes how a flamingo feeds?
  1. Chew
  2. Filter
  3. Pierce
  4. Tear
Correct answer: B
The observation words listed were PIERCE, CHEW, SUCK SAP, FILTER and TEAR. A flamingo sweeping its beak through water is FILTER. Tearing belongs to the fish eagle, chewing to the grasshopper and piercing to the mosquito.
Use after: Observe phase — Phase 2 (Observe): observation chart for all five animals (mosquito, grasshopper, aphid, flamingo, fish eagle)
Q5. Which animal is matched correctly to how it was seen feeding?
  1. Grasshopper: pierces skin to take blood
  2. Mosquito: filters tiny food from lake water
  3. Aphid: sucks sap from a plant stem
  4. Fish eagle: chews maize leaves
Correct answer: C
Aphids cluster on stems and take plant sap. The mosquito pierces skin for blood, the grasshopper chews leaves, and the fish eagle tears fish. The other pairings mix these up.
Use after: Explain phase — Phase 3 (Explain): exit check question 'What do we call the process by which animals obtain and use food?'
Q6. In this lesson, animal nutrition is introduced as a new topic. Which statement best describes animal nutrition?
  1. The process by which animals take in oxygen from the air
  2. The process by which animals get rid of waste from their bodies
  3. The process by which animals obtain and use food for energy, growth and repair
  4. The process by which green plants make food using sunlight
Correct answer: C
Animal nutrition is how animals obtain and use food for energy, growth and repair. Making food from sunlight is plant photosynthesis, taking in oxygen is breathing, and removing waste is excretion.
Use after: DQB phase — Phase 4 (DQB): Driving Question Board, questions about 'INSIDES' and the Bridge to the Sub-Strand
Q7. At the end of the lesson, learners have studied the outside feeding structures of five animals. One learner asks: 'What happens inside the animal's body after the food gets in?' What is the status of this question?
  1. It has been fully answered by the feeding videos
  2. It has been fully answered by the Structure → Function → Food table
  3. It has been answered because the mosquito is stronger than the grasshopper
  4. It is still an open question that the rest of the sub-strand will investigate
Correct answer: D
The lesson only looked at the outside feeding structures. What happens inside the animal is the 'Bridge to the Sub-Strand' and stays on the Driving Question Board to be investigated in later lessons.
Use after: Model Building phase — Phase 5 (Model): first annotated sketch model of one animal's feeding structure
Q8. Learners are asked to draw a first annotated sketch model of one animal's feeding structure. Which statement best describes this 'starting model'?
  1. It is a final answer that will not be changed again
  2. It should be copied from a textbook so that it has no mistakes
  3. It shows the learner's current best thinking and will be revised in later lessons
  4. It must be completely correct before it can be handed in
Correct answer: C
Learners are told the model is a STARTING model that does not need to be correct or complete. It is dated and revised in later lessons as understanding grows.
