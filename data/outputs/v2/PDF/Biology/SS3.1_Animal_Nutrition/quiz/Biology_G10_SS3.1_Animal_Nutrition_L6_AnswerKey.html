Q1. A lion feeds mainly on the meat of animals such as wildebeest. Which type of tooth does a lion mainly use to stab and grip its prey?
  1. Canines
  2. Molars
  3. Dental pad
  4. Incisors
Correct answer: A
Canines are long, pointed teeth that stab and grip prey. Incisors cut or nip, and molars crush or grind. A dental pad is not a tooth.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: counting teeth on the labelled skull diagrams and evidence-gathering table
Q2. A skull diagram shows the teeth on ONE side of a lion's mouth only. Upper jaw, one side: incisors 3, canines 1, premolars 3, molars 1. Lower jaw, one side: incisors 3, canines 1, premolars 2, molars 1. How many teeth does the lion have in its whole mouth?
  1. 15
  2. 30
  3. 28
  4. 60
Correct answer: B
One side has 8 upper teeth and 7 lower teeth, which is 15. The diagram shows one side only, so multiply by 2 to get 30 teeth.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: calculating the dental formula for the cow (Cow I 0/3, C 0/1, PM 3/3, M 3/3 × 2)
Q3. On one side of a cow's mouth, the upper jaw has incisors 0, canines 0, premolars 3 and molars 3. The lower jaw has incisors 3, canines 1, premolars 3 and molars 3. How many teeth does the cow have in its whole mouth?
  1. 16
  2. 30
  3. 64
  4. 32
Correct answer: D
Upper jaw, one side: 0+0+3+3 = 6. Lower jaw, one side: 3+1+3+3 = 10. One side has 16 teeth, so the whole mouth has 16 × 2 = 32.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher modelling of the lion calculation and the carnassial teeth
Q4. A lion has special teeth called carnassials. The last upper premolar and the first lower molar slide past each other like scissor blades. What is the main job of the carnassial teeth?
  1. Filtering small food particles from water
  2. Holding grass against a dental pad
  3. Shearing (slicing) meat
  4. Grinding grass into a paste
Correct answer: C
Carnassial teeth work like scissor blades to shear flesh into pieces small enough to swallow. This links tooth shape to a meat diet.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Comparison Table and 3-sentence structure-function claim (cow: no upper incisors, dental pad)
Q5. A cow has no upper incisors or upper canines. Which statement best explains how this is an adaptation to its diet of grass?
  1. It uses a dental pad with its lower incisors to grip and pull grass, then grinds it with broad, ridged molars.
  2. It swallows grass whole because its teeth are not needed for feeding.
  3. It uses sharp canines to tear grass and does not need incisors.
  4. It uses carnassial teeth to shear grass into small pieces.
Correct answer: A
The cow grips and pulls grass between the lower incisors and the upper dental pad. The broad, ridged molars then grind the tough plant material. Cows have no carnassials and no upper canines.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: connecting mammal dentition to the mosquito proboscis and fish eagle beak evidence
Q6. A lion's canines pierce flesh, a mosquito's needle-like proboscis pierces skin, and a fish eagle's hooked beak tears fish. What pattern do these feeding structures show?
  1. Feeding structures are only for chewing, whatever the animal eats.
  2. The shape of an animal's feeding structure fits the type of food it eats and how it obtains that food.
  3. All animals of a similar size have the same feeding structures.
  4. Animals that need food to survive all use the same tools to get it.
Correct answer: B
Different animals have very different feeding structures, and each is shaped for its own food and feeding action. This is the structure-function link the driving question explores.

Q7. A human has incisors, canines, premolars and molars (dental formula I 2/2, C 1/1, PM 2/2, M 3/3). The human diet includes both ugali and nyama choma. Which statement best links the human teeth to this diet?
  1. Humans have mostly flat, grinding teeth like a cow because meat is not eaten.
  2. Humans have only incisors because food is cut before it is swallowed.
  3. Humans have large, stabbing canines like a lion because meat is the main food.
  4. Humans have a mixture of cutting, tearing and grinding teeth because they eat both plant and animal foods.
Correct answer: D
A human is an omnivore. The mixed set of teeth (incisors to cut, canines to tear, premolars and molars to grind) suits a diet of both plant and animal foods.
