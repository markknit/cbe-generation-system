Q1. A group is laying out a life-sized model of the human digestive system. Which order shows the correct path of a swallowed meal of ugali and sukuma wiki?
  1. Mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum
  2. Mouth → oesophagus → stomach → large intestine → small intestine → rectum
  3. Mouth → oesophagus → small intestine → stomach → large intestine → rectum
  4. Mouth → stomach → oesophagus → small intestine → large intestine → rectum
Correct answer: A
Food moves from the mouth down the oesophagus into the stomach, then through the small intestine, the large intestine and finally the rectum. Putting the large intestine before the small intestine is a common sequencing error.
Use after: Observe phase — Transport observer moves the clay ball through the model and narrates each stage
Q2. The transport observer places the clay ball (a bolus of ugali) at the mouth station. What happens to the ugali here?
  1. Glucose from the ugali is absorbed into the blood
  2. Pepsin and acid begin digesting the protein in the ugali
  3. It is chewed into smaller pieces, and amylase in saliva starts changing starch into sugar
  4. The ugali passes through unchanged until it reaches the stomach
Correct answer: C
Chewing is mechanical digestion, and salivary amylase begins chemical digestion of starch. Nothing is absorbed in the mouth, and pepsin and acid work in the stomach.
Use after: Observe phase — Accessory organs: paper labels SALIVARY GLANDS, LIVER with GALL BLADDER and PANCREAS with strings to the tube
Q3. In the model, a string is run from the label LIVER with GALL BLADDER to the point where its juice enters the digestive tube. Where does the string end?
  1. At the large intestine
  2. At the stomach
  3. At the start of the small intestine
  4. At the mouth
Correct answer: C
Bile from the liver and gall bladder, together with pancreatic juice, enters at the start of the small intestine. Saliva is the juice that enters at the mouth.
Use after: Explain phase — Meal Journey Table: the Stomach row
Q4. A learner says, "The stomach digests everything and absorbs the food into the blood." Which statement about the stomach is correct?
  1. It churns food, and acid and pepsin start digesting protein
  2. It absorbs most glucose and amino acids into the blood
  3. It absorbs water and passes out the fibre
  4. It is a long tube lined with villi
Correct answer: A
The stomach is a muscular bag that churns food, and its acid and pepsin start protein digestion. Absorption of most nutrients happens in the small intestine, and water is absorbed in the large intestine.
Use after: Explain phase — Meal Journey Table: what happens to sukuma wiki (fibre, vitamins, iron) at each organ
Q5. Sukuma wiki contains a lot of fibre. What happens to this fibre as it moves through the human digestive system?
  1. It is digested to glucose in the small intestine
  2. It is not digested and leaves the body as faeces
  3. It is absorbed into the blood in the stomach
  4. It is broken down by pepsin and used for protein
Correct answer: B
Humans cannot digest fibre. It passes through the large intestine, where water is absorbed from it, and leaves as faeces.
Use after: Explain phase — Class discussion linking to the anchoring phenomenon: which stages can a mosquito skip?
Q6. A mosquito drinks liquid blood, which is rich in protein. Compared with a human eating ugali, which statement is correct?
  1. The mosquito must chew the blood first, like a grasshopper, before it can be digested
  2. The mosquito can skip chemical digestion because blood is already a liquid
  3. The mosquito can skip chewing, but the blood proteins still have to be chemically digested and absorbed
  4. The mosquito needs no digestion or absorption because blood is already part of a body
Correct answer: C
Liquid food does not need chewing or grinding (mechanical digestion). Blood proteins are still large molecules, so enzymes must break them down and the nutrients must be absorbed.
Use after: Model Building phase — Revised Animal Nutrition Model panel: nutrients at the correct absorption site and the villi link to the flamingo's lamellae
Q7. The small intestine is about 6 to 7 metres long and its lining is covered in villi. Why does the small intestine have this structure?
  1. To absorb water and pass out the fibre
  2. To churn the food and mix it with acid
  3. To give a large surface area for absorbing glucose, amino acids, vitamins and iron into the blood
  4. To store faeces until they leave the body
Correct answer: C
The length and villi greatly increase the surface area for absorbing digested nutrients. This is the same structure-function idea as the flamingo's lamellae, which increase surface area for filtering food.
