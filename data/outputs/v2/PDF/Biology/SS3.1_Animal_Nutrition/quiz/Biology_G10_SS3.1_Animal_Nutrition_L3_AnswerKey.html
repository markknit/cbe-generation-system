Q1. A farmer in Kakamega sees maize plants wilting with aphids clustered on the stems, but the leaves have no holes or torn edges. What best explains why there is no visible chewing damage?
  1. Aphids eat the leaves only at night, and the plant repairs the holes by morning
  2. Aphids chew the leaves so slowly that the holes are too small to see
  3. Aphids push a needle-like stylet into the stem and suck phloem sap, so nothing is chewed or torn
  4. Aphids inject a poison that dissolves the leaf surface and closes the holes
Correct answer: C
An aphid has piercing-sucking mouthparts. A fine stylet bundle slides into the stem and draws up liquid phloem sap, so the plant weakens and wilts without any chewing holes.
Use after: Observe phase — Clip 1 and Clip 2: aphid and mosquito rows of the comparison observation chart (column 2: food source)
Q2. Which pair correctly matches each insect with the food it takes using its piercing-sucking mouthparts?
  1. Aphid: blood; female mosquito: phloem sap from a plant
  2. Aphid: nectar from flowers only; female mosquito: solid leaf tissue
  3. Aphid: phloem sap from a plant; female mosquito: blood
  4. Aphid: solid leaf tissue; female mosquito: blood
Correct answer: C
Aphids pierce plant stems to reach liquid phloem sap. Female mosquitoes pierce skin to draw blood. Neither feeds on solid tissue.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: physical modelling of the stylet (food canal vs salivary canal prompt)
Q3. In a straw model of a mosquito's mouthparts, two thin straws are taped side by side inside a wider straw. One thin straw is the food canal and the other is the salivary canal. Why does the real mouthpart need two separate channels?
  1. One channel carries blood up into the mosquito and the other carries saliva down into the skin
  2. One channel is for the mosquito to breathe through and the other is for hearing
  3. Both channels carry blood so that the mosquito can feed twice as fast
  4. One channel is for the left side of the mouth and the other is for the right side
Correct answer: A
Food has to go in and saliva has to go out at the same time. The food canal carries blood upward and the salivary canal carries saliva into the tissue, so two channels are needed.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: physical modelling of the stylet (labelling labium, food canal, salivary canal, stylet bundle)
Q4. A group builds a straw model of a piercing-sucking mouthpart. The wider outer straw (or foil tube) surrounds the inner stylet bundle. Which real part does it represent, and what is its job?
  1. The stylet blade, which cuts the skin open and tears it
  2. The salivary canal, which carries blood to the gut
  3. The food canal, which chews solid food into small pieces
  4. The labium sheath, which protects and guides the stylet bundle as it enters the tissue
Correct answer: D
The labium forms the outer sheath. It protects and guides the inner stylet bundle, which slides forward into the skin or stem. It does not carry food or saliva.

Q5. After building the model, the teacher explains how the needle-like stylet bundle of a mosquito relates to the mouthparts of a grasshopper. Which statement is correct?
  1. The mosquito stylet is a single tube made from the grasshopper's labium alone
  2. The mosquito stylet is made of the grasshopper's mandibles fused together into one thick tooth
  3. The mosquito stylet is built from the same basic parts as a grasshopper's mouthparts, but each part is stretched into a thin blade
  4. The mosquito stylet is a completely new structure that has nothing in common with a grasshopper's mouthparts
Correct answer: C
The stylet bundle uses the same parts as a chewing mouth (labrum, mandibles, maxillae, hypopharynx), but each is modified into a thin blade that pierces rather than chews.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: physical modelling of the stylet (teacher note on female mosquitoes)
Q6. In the Kakamega evening, a learner is bitten by a mosquito. Which statement about mosquito feeding is correct?
  1. Only male mosquitoes take blood, and females feed on plant sap like aphids
  2. Mosquitoes chew small pieces of skin with their mandibles and swallow them
  3. Only female mosquitoes take blood, which they need for their eggs; males feed on nectar and plant juices
  4. Both male and female mosquitoes take blood, because they need it to survive
Correct answer: C
Only female mosquitoes pierce skin and take blood, because they need it for their eggs. Males feed on nectar and plant juices, and no mosquito chews skin.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative mouthpart diversity model: tsetse fly question (Kenyan context label: Western Kenya/Rift Valley)
Q7. A tsetse fly has the same kind of piercing-sucking mouthparts as a mosquito. What does this tell you about its food, and why does it matter in Kenya?
  1. It feeds on nectar only, so it helps pollinate crops
  2. It feeds on solid leaves, so it damages maize crops
  3. It feeds on blood from cattle, wild animals and people, and can pass on trypanosomes that cause nagana and sleeping sickness
  4. It feeds on phloem sap, so it spreads maize diseases
Correct answer: C
Piercing-sucking mouthparts take liquid food. The tsetse fly takes blood, so it can pass trypanosomes to cattle (nagana) and to people (sleeping sickness).
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative mouthpart diversity model: connecting statement and 'SAME GOAL, DIFFERENT TOOLS' arrow
Q8. A learner adds piercing-sucking mouthparts next to chewing mouthparts in the class mouthpart model. Which connecting statement is scientifically correct?
  1. Both types of mouthparts use the same blades to tear food, but only the chewing type swallows it
  2. Chewing mouthparts use mandibles to break solid food, while piercing-sucking mouthparts use a needle-like stylet to reach liquid food without damaging the surface tissue
  3. Chewing mouthparts reach liquid food, while piercing-sucking mouthparts use mandibles to break solid food
  4. Piercing-sucking mouthparts are used for a different goal, because those insects do not need nutrients
Correct answer: B
All insects need nutrients, but the tools differ. Chewing mouthparts break up solid food, while piercing-sucking mouthparts use a stylet to take liquid sap or blood and leave the surface unchewed.
