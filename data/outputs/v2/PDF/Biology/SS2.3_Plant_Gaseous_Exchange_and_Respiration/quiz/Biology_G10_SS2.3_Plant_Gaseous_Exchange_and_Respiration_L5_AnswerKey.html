Q1. Mama Wanjiku mixes yeast, flour, sugar and warm water to make mandazi dough. After some time the dough rises and is full of air pockets. Which gas mainly fills these pockets, and where does it come from?
  1. Oxygen, released by the flour as the dough warms
  2. Carbon dioxide, made by the yeast when it respires without oxygen
  3. Methane, made when the sugar is mixed into the dough
  4. Water vapour, made when the flour dissolves in water
Correct answer: B
Yeast breaks down sugar by anaerobic respiration inside the dough, where oxygen is limited. This releases carbon dioxide, which gets trapped and makes the dough rise.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Gallery Walk, Station 1 (Uji Fermentation)
Q2. Uji made from millet in western Kenya is left to ferment and becomes sour. Which product of anaerobic respiration makes the uji taste sour?
  1. Oxygen, made by the millet grains
  2. Ethanol, made by yeast
  3. Lactic acid, made by bacteria
  4. Methane, made in a biogas digester
Correct answer: C
Bacteria in fermenting uji break down sugars without oxygen and produce lactic acid, which gives the sour taste. Ethanol is the product when yeast ferments, as in busaa.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Explain the Phenomenon, Sentence 2 (word equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast)
Q3. Which word equation correctly shows anaerobic respiration in yeast, as in busaa brewing and mandazi dough?
  1. Ethanol + carbon dioxide → glucose + energy
  2. Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy
  3. Glucose → lactic acid + energy
  4. Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy
Correct answer: D
In yeast, glucose is broken down without oxygen into ethanol and carbon dioxide, releasing a small amount of energy. The equation with oxygen is aerobic respiration, and lactic acid is made by bacteria and animal muscle.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Live Demo (yeast, sugar and balloon bottles A, B and C)
Q4. Three identical bottles each hold 200 mL of warm water (37 °C) and 1 teaspoon of sugar, with a balloon over the mouth. Bottle A has no yeast, Bottle B has 1 g of yeast and Bottle C has 2 g of yeast. After 10 minutes, which bottle's balloon is inflated the most, and why?
  1. All three equally, because they have the same amount of sugar
  2. Bottle B, because a medium amount of yeast works best
  3. Bottle A, because sugar alone makes the most gas
  4. Bottle C, because more yeast ferments the sugar faster and makes more carbon dioxide
Correct answer: D
More yeast means faster fermentation of the sugar, so more carbon dioxide is released to inflate the balloon. Bottle A has no yeast, so almost no gas is made.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Gallery Walk, Station 6 (Alcoholic Fermentation in Waterlogged Plant Roots)
Q5. Sukuma wiki roots in flooded (waterlogged) soil can produce ethanol and carbon dioxide. Why do the roots respire anaerobically in this situation?
  1. Water fills the soil spaces, so very little oxygen reaches the roots
  2. The roots have run out of glucose to use
  3. Waterlogged soil has too much oxygen for the roots to use
  4. Plant roots always respire anaerobically, whatever the soil conditions
Correct answer: A
In waterlogged soil the air spaces are filled with water, so oxygen is limited and the root cells switch to anaerobic respiration. This produces ethanol and carbon dioxide, as in yeast.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the ANAEROBIC BRANCH with the '2 ATP' label to the cumulative respiration model
Q6. A student adds an anaerobic branch to their respiration model and labels the energy released from one glucose molecule. Which label is correct when comparing it with aerobic respiration?
  1. Anaerobic: 0 ATP, because no energy is released without oxygen
  2. Anaerobic: 36–38 ATP, the same as aerobic respiration
  3. Anaerobic: 2 ATP, much more than the 36–38 ATP from aerobic respiration
  4. Anaerobic: 2 ATP, much less than the 36–38 ATP from aerobic respiration
Correct answer: D
Anaerobic respiration releases only about 2 ATP per glucose, while aerobic respiration releases 36–38 ATP. This is the trade-off of respiring without oxygen.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Gallery Walk, Station 4 (Biogas Digester, Kisumu dairy farm)
Q7. On a dairy farm in Kisumu, cow dung and plant waste are placed in a closed digester. Microbes break the waste down and produce methane gas for cooking. Which statement about this process is correct?
  1. It is aerobic respiration, because the microbes need plenty of oxygen
  2. It is anaerobic respiration, because the microbes break down the waste without oxygen
  3. It is neither, because methane cannot be made by living things
  4. It is photosynthesis, because plant waste is used
Correct answer: B
The sealed digester has little or no oxygen. The microbes break down the dung and plant waste by anaerobic respiration, and the methane they produce is used as fuel.
