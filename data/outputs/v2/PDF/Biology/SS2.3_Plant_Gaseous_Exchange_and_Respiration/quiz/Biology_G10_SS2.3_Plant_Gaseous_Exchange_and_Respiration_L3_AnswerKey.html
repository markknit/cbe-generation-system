Q1. Bubbles came from the stomata on sukuma wiki leaves. Which statement is the best scientific idea about what makes guard cells open a stomatal pore?
  1. Water moves into the guard cells, so they swell and become turgid and the pore opens
  2. Gases pushing against the guard cells force the pore open
  3. The plant decides to open the pore when it wants gas
  4. The pore opens when guard cells lose water and become flaccid
Correct answer: A
Guard cells open the pore when water enters them by osmosis and they become turgid. Saying the plant 'decides' is a vague idea, not a mechanism.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Animation 1 (potassium ion theory), pause-and-sketch of K+ and water movement
Q2. In the potassium ion theory, K+ ions are actively pumped into guard cells in the light. What happens next?
  1. The water potential inside the guard cells falls, so water enters by osmosis and the guard cells become turgid
  2. The water potential inside the guard cells stays the same, so the pore stays closed
  3. The water potential inside the guard cells rises, so water leaves by osmosis and the guard cells become flaccid
  4. The K+ ions move water in by active transport, with no osmosis involved
Correct answer: A
More solute (K+) inside lowers the water potential in the guard cells. Water then moves in by osmosis, the cells become turgid and the pore opens. Many students wrongly think a high solute concentration means high water potential.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Animation 2 (starch-sugar hypothesis) and the two-column comparison table
Q3. In the starch-sugar hypothesis, what happens to the guard cells in the light, and why does the pore open?
  1. Sugars are pumped out of the guard cells, so the pore opens because the cells are lighter
  2. Starch stays unchanged, but K+ ions move out, so the guard cells become flaccid and the pore opens
  3. Starch is changed into soluble sugars, which lowers the water potential, so water enters by osmosis and the guard cells become turgid
  4. Soluble sugars are changed into starch, which raises the water potential, so water leaves and the pore opens
Correct answer: C
In the light, starch is converted to soluble sugars. This lowers the water potential in the guard cells, so water enters by osmosis and the guard cells become turgid. In darkness the sugars change back to starch.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher adds the photosynthetic theory after Animation 2 and asks which step is the same in all three ideas
Q4. Three ideas explain how guard cells open: the photosynthetic theory, the starch-sugar hypothesis and the potassium ion theory. Which step is the same in all three?
  1. Guard cells lose water in the light so that the pore opens
  2. More solute builds up in the guard cells and water follows it in by osmosis
  3. Water is pumped into guard cells directly using ATP
  4. Guard cells use K+ ions as the only solute
Correct answer: B
In all three ideas the guard cells gain solutes (sugars from photosynthesis, sugars from starch, or K+ ions). This lowers the water potential, and water follows by osmosis. The theories differ in the source of the solutes.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER scenario card on the maize seedling near Kisumu that wilts at noon and recovers at dusk
Q5. A maize seedling in a shamba near Kisumu wilts at noon on a hot sunny day, and its stomata close. Which explanation fits the potassium ion theory?
  1. K+ ions move into the guard cells, so water enters and the guard cells become turgid and close the pore
  2. K+ ions stay in the guard cells, but water is pumped out using sunlight energy
  3. K+ ions move out of the guard cells, so water enters by osmosis and the pore closes
  4. K+ ions move out of the guard cells, so water leaves by osmosis, the guard cells become flaccid and the pore closes
Correct answer: D
When K+ leaves the guard cells, their water potential rises and water moves out by osmosis. The guard cells lose turgor, become flaccid and the pore closes, which reduces water loss.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: writing connection notes that link today's mechanism to the Lesson 1 bubble observation
Q6. Students in Nairobi saw more bubbles from sukuma wiki leaves in warm water. Which chain of events correctly links guard cell action to the gas escaping as bubbles?
  1. Water leaves the guard cells, they become flaccid, the pore opens, and more gas escapes
  2. K+ enters the guard cells, water enters by osmosis, the guard cells become turgid, the pore opens, and more gas escapes
  3. The pore opens first, then K+ enters the guard cells, then water enters and more gas escapes
  4. Gas escapes first, which makes the guard cells turgid, and then the pore opens
Correct answer: B
The mechanism runs in order: K+ moves in, water follows by osmosis, the guard cells become turgid, the pore opens, and more gas can leave the leaf. This is the kind of causal chain that links the mechanism to the bubbles.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: error-detection task on the projected inset with K+ arrows pointing outward on an open pore
Q7. A student draws a zoomed-in model of a stomatal pore that is OPEN. The guard cells are labelled turgid, but the K+ arrows point out of the guard cells. What is the error?
  1. The K+ arrows are correct, because K+ leaving draws water in
  2. The arrows should show water moving out of the guard cells, because turgid cells lose water
  3. The K+ arrows should point into the guard cells, because K+ entering lowers the water potential and water follows in
  4. The guard cells should be labelled flaccid, because an open pore means the guard cells have lost water
Correct answer: C
An open pore needs turgid guard cells. This needs K+ to move in, which lowers the water potential so water moves in by osmosis. K+ arrows pointing out show the closed state.
