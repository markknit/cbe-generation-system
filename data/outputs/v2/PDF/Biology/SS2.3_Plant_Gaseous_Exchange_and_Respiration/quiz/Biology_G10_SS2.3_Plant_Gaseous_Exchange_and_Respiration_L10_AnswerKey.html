Use after: Predict phase — Groups identify their independent variable, dependent variable and at least two controlled variables on a sticky note
Q1. A group is testing how water temperature (10°C, 25°C and 40°C) affects gas production by yeast in sugar solution. They count the bubbles from the delivery tube every minute. Which one is the dependent variable?
  1. Mass of yeast used
  2. Amount of sugar used
  3. Water temperature (10°C, 25°C, 40°C)
  4. Number of bubbles per minute
Correct answer: D
The dependent variable is what is measured as the result, here bubbles per minute. Temperature is the independent variable, while sugar and yeast mass are controlled variables.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Yeast fermentation investigation with a 1 cm cooking-oil layer to exclude air
Q2. In a yeast investigation, a group adds a 1 cm layer of cooking oil on top of the yeast and sugar mixture. What is the purpose of the oil layer?
  1. To make the bubbles larger
  2. To keep the mixture warm
  3. To exclude air so that any gas produced is made without oxygen
  4. To give the yeast extra food
Correct answer: C
The oil floats on the surface and stops air from reaching the yeast. Any gas produced is therefore made without oxygen, which tests for anaerobic respiration (fermentation).
Use after: Observe phase — Teacher anomaly probe during circulation: 'Before you cross it out, ask yourselves, could that be real?'
Q3. During a yeast investigation, one bubble count is very different from the other readings in the table. What should the group do?
  1. Record it honestly and ask what might have caused it
  2. Stop the investigation and start again
  3. Change it so that it matches the other readings
  4. Cross it out at once because it must be a mistake
Correct answer: A
Honest scientists record all readings, including unexpected ones. An unusual reading may be real evidence, so the group should look for a possible cause before deciding anything.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: groups conduct at least two trials and begin calculating means at the 30-minute mark
Q4. A group puts yeast in sugar solution at 40°C. In Trial 1 they count 22 bubbles per minute. In Trial 2 they count 26 bubbles per minute. What is the mean number of bubbles per minute?
  1. 48
  2. 24
  3. 4
  4. 26
Correct answer: B
Mean = (22 + 26) ÷ 2 = 48 ÷ 2 = 24 bubbles per minute.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim–Evidence–Reasoning (CER) written explanation for each group's findings
Q5. A group found that yeast in sugar solution at 40°C made more gas than at 25°C. Which sentence is the best reasoning to explain this result?
  1. Warm water makes the yeast breathe in more oxygen from the air.
  2. Warmth increases the activity of enzymes in yeast cells, so fermentation of sugar is faster and more carbon dioxide is released.
  3. Yeast needs stomata to open wider in warm water.
  4. The temperature made more bubbles because higher temperature increased the number of bubbles.
Correct answer: B
Good reasoning links the data to a biological process: enzymes in yeast work faster in warmth, so fermentation releases CO₂ faster. The first option only repeats the claim and is circular.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: teacher narrates connections between Group 3's oil-layer claim and Group 1's temperature claim
Q6. One group found that yeast in sugar solution under a layer of oil still produced gas. Another group found that a warmer sugar solution produced more gas. Together, what do these two findings suggest?
  1. Yeast needs oxygen to make gas, and cold speeds up the process
  2. Yeast can make carbon dioxide without oxygen, and warmth speeds up the process
  3. The oil itself produces the gas, and warmth has no effect
  4. Yeast only makes gas in cold water without sugar
Correct answer: B
Gas under the oil layer shows CO₂ is made without oxygen (fermentation). More gas in warmth shows that temperature increases the rate of the process.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: teacher prompt 'Where would you add the cytoplasm and the fermentation equation to your model?'
Q7. Fermentation (anaerobic respiration) releases energy from glucose and produces carbon dioxide. Where in a plant or yeast cell does fermentation take place?
  1. In the cytoplasm of the cell
  2. In the guard cells only
  3. In the cuticle covering the leaf
  4. In the stomata on the leaf surface
Correct answer: A
Fermentation happens inside the cell, in the cytoplasm. Stomata, guard cells and the cuticle are structures involved in gas exchange at the leaf surface, not the site of fermentation.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Biogas simulation, comparing with a teacher-prepared bottle started 2 to 3 days earlier
Q8. In a biogas simulation, a group puts chopped kitchen scraps in water in a bottle with a delivery tube to an inverted water-filled bottle. Gas collects very slowly. Why do they compare their bottle with one the teacher started 2 to 3 days earlier?
  1. Because the older bottle will have no gas, which gives a fair control
  2. Because the teacher's bottle uses a different gas
  3. Because older bottles always give less gas than new ones
  4. Because gas from waste builds up slowly, so the older bottle shows what the gas production looks like after several days
Correct answer: D
Microorganisms break down waste slowly, so gas collects over days. The earlier-started bottle lets the group see the later stage of the process within one lesson.
