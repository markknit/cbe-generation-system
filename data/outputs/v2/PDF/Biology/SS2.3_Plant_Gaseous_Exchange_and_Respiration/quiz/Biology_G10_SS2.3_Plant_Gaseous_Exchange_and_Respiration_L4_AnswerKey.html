Q1. Two bags hold fresh sukuma wiki leaves. One bag is in light. The other is wrapped in black polythene, so no light reaches the leaves, but it still contains air. Photosynthesis cannot happen in the dark bag. Where does the carbon dioxide (CO₂) in the dark bag come from?
  1. There is no CO₂ in the dark bag because plants only make CO₂ in light
  2. From respiration in the leaf cells, which goes on in light and in darkness
  3. From photosynthesis, which carries on slowly in the dark
  4. From the black polythene, which gives off CO₂
Correct answer: B
Respiration happens in living cells all the time and releases CO₂, so CO₂ is made in the dark even though photosynthesis stops. Photosynthesis uses CO₂ and needs light.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: question 3 on the mandazi dough in Gikomba Market
Q2. A mandazi vendor in Gikomba Market notices that her dough rises faster on a warm day than on a cold day. Which explanation is best?
  1. Warm air only dries the dough, so it rises by taking in water
  2. Warmth makes the yeast enzymes work faster, so the yeast respires faster and makes CO₂ gas faster
  3. Warmth makes the yeast produce oxygen, which pushes the dough up
  4. Warmth kills the yeast, and the dead yeast makes the dough swell
Correct answer: B
Warmth increases the activity of the enzymes of respiration in yeast. More CO₂ is produced and the gas makes the dough rise faster.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: structured reading 'Two Roads to Energy: Aerobic and Anaerobic Respiration'
Q3. Which word equation correctly shows aerobic respiration?
  1. Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy
  2. Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy
  3. Glucose + carbon dioxide → oxygen + water + energy
  4. Carbon dioxide + water + energy → glucose + oxygen
Correct answer: A
In aerobic respiration glucose reacts with oxygen to give carbon dioxide, water and energy. The first choice is anaerobic respiration in yeast and the third is photosynthesis.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Yeast CO₂ Investigation, setting up Tube A (aerobic) and Tube B (anaerobic)
Q4. In the yeast investigation, Tube B was set up with water that had been boiled and cooled, and a 1 cm layer of cooking oil was poured on top. Why were these two steps done?
  1. To make the yeast grow faster by keeping it warm
  2. To add more glucose to the mixture
  3. To remove or keep out oxygen, so the yeast can only respire anaerobically
  4. To stop CO₂ from being made at all
Correct answer: C
Boiling drives out dissolved oxygen and the oil layer stops air from entering. This makes Tube B anaerobic so it can be compared with Tube A, which has air.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Yeast CO₂ Investigation, calculating the total turbidity score for each tube at the 20-minute mark
Q5. A group tests how much CO₂ yeast makes. They score the limewater from 0 (clear) to 3 (milky-white). Tube A scores at 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 minutes were 0, 1, 1, 2 and 3. What is the total turbidity score for Tube A?
  1. 6
  2. 8
  3. 7
  4. 3
Correct answer: C
Add the scores at all five time points: 0 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 7.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Data Analysis & Equation Construction, question 1 (what Tube B tells you about CO₂ without oxygen)
Q6. In a yeast investigation, the limewater connected to the anaerobic tube (no oxygen, oil layer on top) turned milky after 15 minutes. What does this show?
  1. The oil layer produced the CO₂
  2. Yeast needs oxygen to make CO₂
  3. Anaerobic respiration releases oxygen
  4. Yeast can make CO₂ without oxygen, so anaerobic respiration also releases CO₂
Correct answer: D
Limewater turns milky when CO₂ is present. CO₂ was made in the tube with no oxygen, so anaerobic respiration in yeast also produces CO₂.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Data Analysis & Equation Construction and Part B: Whole-Class Equation Gallery (aerobic and anaerobic equations)
Q7. Which statement correctly compares aerobic and anaerobic respiration in yeast?
  1. Both need oxygen, but only anaerobic respiration makes CO₂
  2. Both release the same amount of energy, but anaerobic respiration makes water
  3. Aerobic respiration needs oxygen and releases more energy; anaerobic respiration needs no oxygen, releases less energy and makes ethanol and CO₂
  4. Aerobic respiration needs no oxygen and gives less energy; anaerobic respiration needs oxygen and gives more energy
Correct answer: C
Aerobic respiration (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy) needs oxygen and releases more energy. Anaerobic respiration in yeast (C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ + energy) needs no oxygen and releases less energy.
Use after: Explain phase — Part C: Connecting to Phenomenon (warm and cold water sukuma wiki bubbles, including the word ENZYME)
Q8. Sukuma wiki leaves in warm water gave off more bubbles than leaves in cold water. Which statement is the best scientific response, based on what yeast respiration showed?
  1. Enzymes are not affected by temperature, so warmth cannot change the bubbles
  2. Warm water may speed up the enzymes of respiration so cells make CO₂ faster; the bubbles should be tested with limewater to be sure
  3. Warm water makes the leaf stop respiring, so the bubbles must come from photosynthesis
  4. Warm water proves that the bubbles are CO₂ from respiration, so no further test is needed
Correct answer: B
Enzymes are proteins that speed up reactions, and warmth increases their activity, so respiration and CO₂ production may be faster. We tested yeast, not the leaf bubbles, so this is a hypothesis that needs a limewater test.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the zoomed-in plant cell layer (mitochondrion, aerobic BLUE and anaerobic RED pathways) to the cumulative model
Q9. A student draws a plant cell to show respiration. Where should the student label the site of aerobic respiration, and where does anaerobic respiration (making ethanol and CO₂) start?
  1. Aerobic in the stoma; anaerobic in the guard cell
  2. Both in the cell wall
  3. Aerobic in the mitochondrion; anaerobic in the cytoplasm
  4. Aerobic in the cytoplasm; anaerobic in the mitochondrion
Correct answer: C
The mitochondrion is the site of aerobic respiration and needs oxygen. Anaerobic respiration happens in the cytoplasm without oxygen.
