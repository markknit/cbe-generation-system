Q1. Mwalimu Njoroge's class put one freshly cut sukuma wiki leaf in warm water (about 42°C) and a similar leaf in cold water (about 15°C). What did the class observe?
  1. More bubbles came from the leaf in the cold water than from the leaf in the warm water
  2. More bubbles came from the leaf in the warm water than from the leaf in the cold water
  3. Only the leaf in the cold water gave bubbles
  4. Both leaves gave exactly the same number of bubbles
Correct answer: B
The phenomenon showed streams of bubbles in warm water and far fewer in cold water. This question only checks the observation, not the reason for it.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: students mark exactly where bubbles appear on the leaf outline diagram
Q2. Students watched submerged sukuma wiki leaves and recorded where the bubbles appeared. Which statement matches what they observed?
  1. Bubbles streamed from particular points on the leaf, not evenly from the whole surface
  2. Bubbles came only from the water around the leaf, never from the leaf
  3. Bubbles came off evenly from the whole leaf surface at once
  4. Bubbles appeared only after the leaf had been in the water for one hour
Correct answer: A
The anchoring phenomenon is that bubbles stream from specific points on the leaf surface. Students marked these spots on their leaf outline diagrams.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: structured observation with annotated diagram (separating where from why)
Q3. During the observation, the teacher said, "Record exactly where bubbles appear, not why." Which note written by a student is an observation and not an explanation?
  1. "The warm water is boiling the leaf."
  2. "Bubbles streamed from small spots on the leaf, and there were more streams in the warm beaker."
  3. "The bubbles are oxygen."
  4. "The leaf is breathing out like our lungs."
Correct answer: B
An observation records what can be seen or counted, such as where the bubbles appear and how many there are. The other notes are ideas about why, or what the gas is, and have not been tested yet.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: two beakers (warm ~42°C and cold ~15°C) with two leaves of similar size, observed for 5 minutes
Q4. A group wants a fair comparison of bubbles in warm and cold water. Which set-up is best?
  1. Two fresh sukuma wiki leaves of similar size, one in warm water and one in cold water, watched for the same 5 minutes
  2. Two sukuma wiki leaves of similar size, both in warm water, watched for different lengths of time
  3. One large sukuma wiki leaf in warm water and one small leaf in cold water
  4. A sukuma wiki leaf in warm water and a different type of leaf in cold water
Correct answer: A
A fair comparison changes only the water temperature. The leaf type, leaf size and observation time stay the same.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: introducing the unit driving question and saying today's observation is only the first clue
Q5. After the observation, a student asks, "What gas is inside the bubbles?" At this point in the unit, which response is best?
  1. It is definitely carbon dioxide, so write that down.
  2. It is definitely oxygen, so write that down.
  3. It is only air stuck on the outside of the leaf, so the question does not matter.
  4. We have not explained that yet. We will look for evidence in later lessons.
Correct answer: D
This is the anchoring lesson, so the gas is deliberately left unexplained. Students record the question and keep it for the unit investigation instead of being given an answer.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: students write 'What I noticed', 'What I wonder' and 'What I think I already know' sticky notes
Q6. On the Driving Question Board, which blue "What I wonder" note is the most useful, investigable question about the sukuma wiki bubbles?
  1. Do all kinds of leaves produce the same number of bubbles in warm water?
  2. Why is biology hard?
  3. Who made the first beaker?
  4. Is sukuma wiki tasty?
Correct answer: A
A productive wonder question can be investigated by testing leaves in warm and cold water. The other questions are not about the phenomenon or cannot be tested with the lesson's materials.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: students draw their first Initial Model of a sukuma wiki leaf cross-section
Q7. Students draw an initial model of a sukuma wiki leaf and write at the bottom: "I am not sure about ___." Why do they write this?
  1. It shows what they still need to find out, so they can revise the model in later lessons
  2. The teacher will give the full answer at the end of this lesson
  3. The model will be marked wrong unless every part is correct
  4. Parts they are unsure about must be rubbed out before the model is shown to a partner
Correct answer: A
The initial model is not graded. It is a baseline of honest thinking, and the 'not sure' statement points to what students will investigate and revise during the unit.
