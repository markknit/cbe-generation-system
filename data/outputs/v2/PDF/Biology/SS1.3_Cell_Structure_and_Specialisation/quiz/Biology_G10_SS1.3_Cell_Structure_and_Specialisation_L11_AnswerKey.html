Use after: Explain phase — Zone D: Cell Organisation Hierarchy (cell to organism using the skin as the example)
Q1. A student draws the organisation of the skin from the smallest level to the largest. Which order is correct?
  1. Skin organ, epidermal cell, epithelial tissue, whole organism, integumentary system
  2. Epithelial tissue, epidermal cell, integumentary system, skin organ, whole organism
  3. Epidermal cell, skin organ, epithelial tissue, whole organism, integumentary system
  4. Epidermal cell, epithelial tissue, skin organ, integumentary system, whole organism
Correct answer: D
Cells join to form tissues, tissues form organs, organs work together in organ systems, and systems make up the whole organism. For skin this is epidermal cell, epithelial tissue, skin organ, integumentary system, organism.
Use after: Explain phase — Zone A and Zone B: labelled plant cell and animal cell, noting two structures absent from the animal cell
Q2. Which structure is found in a plant cell but is absent from an animal cell?
  1. Cell wall
  2. Cell membrane
  3. Nucleus
  4. Mitochondria
Correct answer: A
Plant cells have a rigid cell wall outside the cell membrane that supports the cell, and animal cells do not. Both plant and animal cells have a cell membrane, a nucleus and mitochondria.
Use after: Explain phase — Zone C: three specialised wound-healing cells with structure-function arrows (fibroblasts)
Q3. Fibroblasts help to rebuild a wound by making and secreting collagen. Which structural feature of a fibroblast best matches this job?
  1. It is full of lysosomes, which digest the collagen
  2. It has no nucleus, so it can be packed with collagen
  3. It is flat and tightly packed, so it covers the wound surface
  4. It is rich in rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, which make and send out proteins
Correct answer: D
Collagen is a protein. Rough endoplasmic reticulum makes proteins and the Golgi apparatus processes and packages them for secretion, so fibroblasts are rich in both.
Use after: Explain phase — Zone C: three specialised wound-healing cells with structure-function arrows (neutrophils or macrophages)
Q4. After a cut, neutrophils and macrophages move to the wound and engulf bacteria. Which feature helps them digest the bacteria once engulfed?
  1. They have many lysosomes containing digestive enzymes
  2. They have no nucleus, so the bacteria cannot escape
  3. They contain chloroplasts that make food from the bacteria
  4. They have a cell wall that crushes the bacteria
Correct answer: A
Neutrophils and macrophages have an irregular shape and many lysosomes. Lysosomes contain enzymes that digest engulfed bacteria.
Use after: Explain phase — Zone C: three specialised wound-healing cells (platelets)
Q5. Which statement about platelets, the cell fragments that start clotting when you cut your finger, is correct?
  1. They are cells that make collagen to rebuild the skin
  2. They are small cell fragments with no nucleus that become sticky when activated and start clotting
  3. They are flat cells that divide and move across the wound to close the surface
  4. They are large cells with a nucleus that engulf bacteria
Correct answer: B
Platelets are small cell fragments with no nucleus. When activated they become sticky and release clotting proteins to start clotting. The other descriptions fit neutrophils, fibroblasts and epidermal cells.
Use after: Explain phase — Zone C: three specialised wound-healing cells with structure-function arrows (epidermal cells)
Q6. Epidermal cells close the surface of a cut finger. Which description of these cells fits this job?
  1. Long cells rich in rough endoplasmic reticulum that make collagen
  2. Flat, tightly packed cells that divide and migrate across the wound
  3. Cells with many lysosomes that digest bacteria
  4. Round cells with no nucleus that float in the blood
Correct answer: B
Epidermal cells are flat and tightly packed, and they divide and migrate to close the wound surface. This links their structure to their function of covering the wound.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB review: cumulative verbal answer to the driving question (why cells look so different and how shape determines the job a cell can do)
Q7. Platelets, neutrophils, fibroblasts and epidermal cells all help to heal a cut, yet they look very different under a microscope. What is the best explanation?
  1. Each type of cell has a different shape and different organelles that suit its own job, so shape helps determine function
  2. They are completely different organisms that happen to live in the skin
  3. Only the cells with a nucleus have a specific job, so they all look different
  4. Cell shape is random and has nothing to do with what the cell does
Correct answer: A
This answers the driving question. Cells are specialised, and each cell type has a shape and organelles suited to its job, such as clotting, engulfing bacteria, making collagen or covering the wound.
