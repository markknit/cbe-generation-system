Use after: Predict phase — Prediction Card: 'I predict I will see ___ and I predict I will NOT see ___'
Q1. You have drawn and labelled an onion epidermal cell. You are about to look at a cell from the inside of your own cheek. Which structure should you predict you will NOT see in the cheek cell?
  1. Cell membrane
  2. Nucleus
  3. Cell wall
  4. Cytoplasm
Correct answer: C
Cheek cells are animal cells, and animal cells have no rigid cell wall. They do have a nucleus, cell membrane and cytoplasm, as plant cells do.
Use after: Observe phase — Slide preparation: adding ONE drop of methylene blue stain to the cheek smear
Q2. In the cheek cell practical, a drop of methylene blue stain is added to the smear on the slide. Why is the stain used?
  1. It colours parts of the cells, especially the nucleus, so they show up more clearly
  2. It makes the cheek cells grow larger so they are easier to see
  3. It kills bacteria so the cheek cells can be seen more safely
  4. It makes a cell wall form around each cell
Correct answer: A
Cheek cells are almost transparent. Methylene blue stains them, mainly the nucleus, so their structure can be seen and drawn.
Use after: Observe phase — Focusing the microscope under high power and recording TOTAL magnification on the drawing
Q3. A learner views cheek cells using the ×40 objective lens. The eyepiece lens is ×10. What is the total magnification to record on the drawing?
  1. ×30
  2. ×400
  3. ×4000
  4. ×50
Correct answer: B
Total magnification = objective × eyepiece = 40 × 10 = ×400.
Use after: Observe phase — Teacher demonstration of the cheek smear: 'thin enough to read newsprint through it'
Q4. The cheek cell smear should be spread thinly on the slide. Why?
  1. A thick smear makes the stain wash off
  2. A thick smear makes cells overlap, so individual cells cannot be seen clearly
  3. A thin smear makes the cell wall easier to see
  4. A thin smear makes the cells divide so there are more of them
Correct answer: B
If the smear is too thick, cells pile on top of each other and light cannot pass through. A thin layer lets single cells be seen and drawn.
Use after: Observe phase — Drawing Checklist Clipboard: checking that no cell wall is labelled on the cheek cell drawing
Q5. A learner has drawn a cheek cell and labelled the outer boundary as 'cell wall'. What is the correct response?
  1. The label is correct, because all cells have a cell wall
  2. The outer boundary should be labelled 'nucleus'
  3. The label is correct, because the stain makes the cell wall visible
  4. The label is wrong: cheek cells have no cell wall, and the outer boundary is the cell membrane
Correct answer: D
Animal cells such as cheek cells lack a cell wall. Their outer boundary is a thin cell membrane, so a cell wall label is a misconception.
Use after: Explain phase — Evidence Harvest T-chart and the 3-sentence explanation about the absence of a cell wall
Q6. Cheek cells have no cell wall. What does this suggest about animal cells?
  1. They cannot have a nucleus
  2. They are flexible and can have irregular shapes
  3. They are always perfectly rectangular
  4. They have no cell membrane
Correct answer: B
Without a rigid cell wall, animal cells can change or vary in shape. This helps explain how platelets can change form to help a clot form in a wound.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model update: writing one sentence for each structure ABSENT in the animal cell
Q7. When you update the plant half of your model, you write what a missing structure does in the plant cell. Which match between a structure and its function in a plant cell is correct?
  1. Cell wall: gives rigid support and a fixed shape
  2. Large central vacuole: controls the cell's nucleus
  3. Cell wall: lets the cell change shape easily
  4. Chloroplast: makes the cell rigid
Correct answer: A
The cell wall is rigid and supports the plant cell, giving it a fixed shape. Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis, not support.
