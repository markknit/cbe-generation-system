Use after: Observe phase — Cold-call round, Stage 3 (clot forms): Student B on platelet structure
Q1. When a finger is cut, platelets help to stop the bleeding. Which statement about platelets is correct?
  1. They are long cells that secrete collagen to rebuild the skin
  2. They are tiny cell fragments with no nucleus that become sticky and release chemicals that start clotting
  3. They are biconcave discs packed with haemoglobin that carry oxygen to the wound
  4. They are large cells with a big nucleus that engulf bacteria
Correct answer: B
Platelets are small cell fragments without a nucleus. When activated they become sticky and release clotting chemicals that plug the cut. Engulfing bacteria, carrying oxygen and making collagen are jobs of other cells.
Use after: Observe phase — Cold-call round, Stage 3 (clot continued): Student C on the organelle that makes proteins
Q2. A clot is held together by a net of a protein called fibrin. Which cell structure makes proteins?
  1. Ribosomes
  2. Cell membrane
  3. Mitochondria
  4. Lysosomes
Correct answer: A
Ribosomes, many of them on the rough endoplasmic reticulum, join amino acids to make proteins. Lysosomes digest materials and mitochondria release energy.
Use after: Observe phase — Cold-call round, Stage 4 (immune response): Student D on neutrophils
Q3. Neutrophils arrive at a wound and engulf and digest bacteria. Which organelle, full of digestive enzymes, makes this digestion possible?
  1. Nucleus
  2. Chloroplast
  3. Cell wall
  4. Lysosome
Correct answer: D
Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that break down the bacteria the neutrophil has engulfed. Chloroplasts and cell walls are not found in animal cells, and the nucleus holds DNA rather than digesting material.
Use after: Observe phase — Cold-call round, Stage 5 (tissue rebuilding): Student F on fibroblasts
Q4. Fibroblasts secrete collagen to rebuild tissue in a wound. Which organelle is highly developed in fibroblasts, and why?
  1. Lysosomes, because fibroblasts must digest bacteria before they can make collagen
  2. Cell wall, because fibroblasts must stay stiff to close the wound
  3. Rough endoplasmic reticulum, because collagen is a protein that the cell makes and sends out
  4. Chloroplasts, because fibroblasts need to trap light energy to repair tissue
Correct answer: C
Collagen is a protein. Cells that make and secrete a lot of protein have much rough endoplasmic reticulum (with the Golgi apparatus). Fibroblasts are animal cells, so they have no chloroplasts or cell wall.
Use after: Observe phase — Cold-call round, Stage 6 (skin closes): Student G on epidermal cells
Q5. Epidermal cells divide to close the surface of a wound. What must happen inside an epidermal cell before it can divide?
  1. The cell must lose its nucleus, as red blood cells do
  2. The cell wall must grow thicker to separate the new cells
  3. The nucleus must copy the cell's DNA, and mitochondria must supply the energy
  4. The lysosomes must release collagen into the cell
Correct answer: C
Before division the nucleus copies its DNA so each new cell gets a full set, and mitochondria supply the energy. Epidermal cells keep their nucleus and, as animal cells, have no cell wall.
Use after: Observe phase — Cold-call round, Synthesis: Student H on why cells with the same DNA look so different
Q6. Most cells in one person's body that have a nucleus carry the same DNA. Why do skin cells and white blood cells look so different?
  1. White blood cells have no DNA, so they cannot look like skin cells
  2. Skin cells use all their genes, while white blood cells use none
  3. They have different DNA, because each cell type inherited its DNA from a different parent cell
  4. Each cell type has different genes switched on, so it makes the structures and proteins its job needs
Correct answer: D
Specialisation happens because different genes are switched on in different cells, so each cell makes the structures and proteins needed for its own job. The DNA itself is the same in all cells that have a nucleus.
Use after: Explain phase — 'Then vs. Now' written comparison, using the word wall (organelle, cell wall, chloroplast, vacuole, etc.)
Q7. Which of these structures is found in plant cells but NOT in animal cells?
  1. Mitochondria
  2. Cell membrane
  3. Nucleus
  4. Cell wall
Correct answer: D
Plant cells have a cell wall (and chloroplasts and a large vacuole). Animal cells do not have a cell wall. The cell membrane, nucleus and mitochondria are found in both.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board closure: classifying cards as ANSWERED, PARTIALLY ANSWERED or BEYOND THIS UNIT
Q8. During Driving Question Board closure, a question card asks something that needs more biology than the class has learned in this sub-strand. How should the class classify it?
  1. ANSWERED
  2. It should be removed from the board
  3. PARTIALLY ANSWERED
  4. BEYOND THIS UNIT
Correct answer: D
A question that needs more biology than has been learned so far is classed as BEYOND THIS UNIT and moved to the Future Questions Wall. ANSWERED means there is strong evidence, and PARTIALLY ANSWERED means some evidence but gaps remain.
