Q1. E. coli is a prokaryote that can cause food poisoning in poorly stored nyama choma. Which statement correctly describes where its genetic material (DNA) is found?
  1. E. coli has no DNA at all because it has no nucleus
  2. The circular DNA lies in a nucleoid region in the cytoplasm, with no nuclear membrane around it
  3. The DNA is enclosed in a nucleus with a nuclear membrane, just like in a human cheek cell
  4. The DNA is kept inside mitochondria
Correct answer: B
A prokaryote has its circular DNA in the nucleoid region, which is not surrounded by a nuclear membrane. It still has DNA, but not a membrane-bound nucleus.
Use after: Observe phase — Whole-class review of the comparison table: the ribosome row and membrane-bound organelles
Q2. A learner says: "E. coli and a human cheek cell both have ribosomes, so they must be the same type of cell." Which response is correct?
  1. Ribosomes are present only in E. coli, because they are needed by prokaryotes
  2. Ribosomes are present in both cells and are not membrane-bound, so having ribosomes does not make the cells the same type
  3. Ribosomes are present only in the cheek cell, because they are membrane-bound organelles
  4. Ribosomes are absent from both cells, because both are too small to have them
Correct answer: B
Ribosomes are found in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes and are not surrounded by a membrane. The difference lies in membrane-bound organelles, which only eukaryotes have.
Use after: Observe phase — Comparison table: approximate size (E. coli 1–5 micrometres, cheek cell 10–100 micrometres)
Q3. A human cheek cell is about 25 times bigger than an E. coli cell. If E. coli is about 2 micrometres long, about how big is the cheek cell?
  1. 50 micrometres
  2. 500 micrometres
  3. 12.5 micrometres
  4. 27 micrometres
Correct answer: A
Multiply the size of E. coli by 25: 2 × 25 = 50 micrometres. This fits the 10–100 micrometre range for eukaryotic cells such as the cheek cell.
Use after: Observe phase — Comparison table: cell wall (present or absent; composition)
Q4. Which statement about cell walls is correct for E. coli and a human cheek cell?
  1. Neither cell has any structure outside the plasma membrane
  2. E. coli has a cell wall made of peptidoglycan, while the human cheek cell has no cell wall
  3. Both have a cell wall made of peptidoglycan
  4. E. coli has no cell wall, while the human cheek cell has a peptidoglycan cell wall
Correct answer: B
E. coli has a peptidoglycan cell wall outside its plasma membrane. A human cheek cell is an animal cell and has only a cell membrane, with no cell wall.
Use after: Explain phase — Cold-call consolidation round: Brian's question applying prokaryote vs eukaryote to Amina's cut
Q5. Bacteria that could infect a cut on a finger are prokaryotes. Which observation best shows that they are built differently from the body's skin cells?
  1. They have no membrane-bound nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles
  2. They contain DNA, which skin cells do not contain
  3. They are bigger than skin cells
  4. They have ribosomes, which skin cells do not have
Correct answer: A
Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, unlike the eukaryotic cells of the body. Ribosomes and DNA are present in both, and bacteria are much smaller, not bigger.
Use after: Explain phase — Formal introduction of the terms prokaryote and eukaryote with their Greek etymologies
Q6. The word "eukaryote" comes from the Greek words eu = true and karyon = nucleus. What does the word tell us about a eukaryotic cell?
  1. It has a true nucleus, enclosed by a nuclear membrane
  2. It lives before other cells appeared
  3. It has no nucleus
  4. It is a very small cell with a cell wall
Correct answer: A
Eu means true and karyon means nucleus, so a eukaryote has a true, membrane-bound nucleus. A prokaryote (pro = before) is a cell with no true nucleus.
Use after: Model Building phase — Version 1 add-on: Prokaryote vs. Eukaryote model with the orange/blue/green colour code
Q7. A learner draws two cells side by side and uses a colour code: orange = feature found in prokaryotes only, blue = feature found in eukaryotes only, green = feature found in both. What colour should the learner use to annotate ribosomes?
  1. Ribosomes should not be annotated, because they are not found in either cell
  2. Orange, because ribosomes are found in prokaryotes only
  3. Green, because ribosomes are found in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes
  4. Blue, because ribosomes are found in eukaryotes only
Correct answer: C
Ribosomes are present in both E. coli and the human cheek cell, so they belong in the green (both) category.
