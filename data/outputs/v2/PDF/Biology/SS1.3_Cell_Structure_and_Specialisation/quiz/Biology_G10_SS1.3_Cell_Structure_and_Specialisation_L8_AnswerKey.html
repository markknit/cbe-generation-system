Q1. In Biology, what does 'cell specialisation' mean, and why does it matter for a many-celled body such as a human?
  1. A cell changes its DNA each time it needs a new job
  2. All cells in the body have the same shape so that each one can do every job
  3. Only blood cells are specialised, so they do all the work of the body
  4. A cell has a particular structure that suits it to one main job, so different cells can each do one job well and the body can do many things at once
Correct answer: D
Specialisation means a cell has a structure suited to one main job. Because different cells each do one job well, a many-celled body can clot blood, fight bacteria and rebuild skin at the same time.

Q2. A mature red blood cell has a biconcave shape and no nucleus. How does having no nucleus help the cell do its job?
  1. It lets the cell divide faster to make more red blood cells
  2. It makes the cell long enough to carry signals
  3. It lets the cell fight bacteria by engulfing them
  4. It leaves more room for haemoglobin, so the cell can carry more oxygen
Correct answer: D
With no nucleus, there is more space inside the cell for haemoglobin, which carries oxygen to tissues such as healing skin.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: data card (red blood cell lives ~120 days, ~170,000 circuits)
Q3. A red blood cell has no nucleus and lives for only about 120 days. Which statement best explains the link between these two facts?
  1. Without a nucleus the cell cannot repair itself or make new proteins, so it wears out after about 120 days
  2. Without a nucleus the cell is too heavy to travel round the body for longer
  3. Without a nucleus the cell is always attacked by white blood cells
  4. Without a nucleus the cell uses up all its haemoglobin in 120 days
Correct answer: A
The missing nucleus gives more room for haemoglobin but has a cost: the cell cannot repair itself or make new proteins, so it has a short life.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Structure-Function Graphic Organiser, nerve cell row (long axon prompt)
Q4. A nerve cell (neuron) has a very long axon. How does this feature help the neuron do its job?
  1. It lets the cell contract and pull on bones
  2. It allows a signal, such as pain from a fingertip, to travel a long distance to the brain
  3. It gives the cell more space to store haemoglobin
  4. It lets the cell swim towards other cells
Correct answer: B
A long axon lets one cell carry electrical signals over long distances, for example from a finger to the brain.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Structure-Function Graphic Organiser, muscle cell row (mitochondria prompt)
Q5. A muscle cell (myocyte) contains many mitochondria. Why does this suit the cell's function?
  1. Mitochondria release the energy the cell needs for repeated contraction
  2. Mitochondria carry oxygen around the body
  3. Mitochondria make the cell long so it can carry signals
  4. Mitochondria help the cell to engulf bacteria
Correct answer: A
Mitochondria release energy from respiration. A muscle cell contracts a lot, so it needs a large supply of energy.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: evidence pack diagram of the sperm cell
Q6. A sperm cell has a streamlined head, a long flagellum and a midpiece packed with mitochondria. What is the job of the midpiece?
  1. It supplies the energy the flagellum needs for swimming
  2. It stores food for the early embryo
  3. It carries oxygen to the head of the sperm
  4. It protects the cell with a thick outer layer
Correct answer: A
The midpiece is packed with mitochondria, which release the energy needed to move the flagellum so the sperm can swim.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: sickle-cell anaemia argument question (CER with micrograph comparison)
Q7. Sickle-cell red blood cells still contain haemoglobin, yet a person with sickle-cell disease suffers from anaemia and blocked blood vessels. What is the best explanation?
  1. The curved, stiff cells cannot squeeze through the smallest vessels, so they block them, and they break down early so fewer red cells remain
  2. The sickle cells have a very large nucleus that blocks the vessels
  3. The sickle cells are too flexible and burst open in the blood vessels
  4. The sickle cells have no haemoglobin at all, so they cannot carry oxygen
Correct answer: A
A changed gene makes the cells curved and stiff. They block narrow vessels, so tissues get less oxygen, and they break down early, so there are fewer red cells (anaemia). This shows that shape determines function.
Use after: End of lesson — Explain: 'Connection to the Healing Cut' column, sperm cell row
Q8. In the healing-cut activity, learners wrote 'not involved in healing' for the sperm cell. What does this show about specialisation?
  1. Sperm cells are not real cells because they play no part in healing
  2. All specialised cells must help with wound healing
  3. A cell is specialised for one main job, so not every cell takes part in every process
  4. Specialised cells can change their structure to do any job needed
Correct answer: C
Each specialised cell has a structure suited to one main job. A sperm cell is built for reproduction, so it is not involved in healing a cut.
