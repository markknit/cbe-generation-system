Q1. A root hair cell has a long, thin, tube-like extension that reaches between soil particles. How does this feature help the cell do its job?
  1. It carries sugars from the leaves down to the root
  2. It holds many chloroplasts so the root can make food
  3. It gives a large surface area so more water and mineral salts can be absorbed from the soil
  4. It lets the cell open and close a pore to control water loss
Correct answer: C
The long root hair extension greatly increases the surface area in contact with soil water, so the cell absorbs water and mineral salts faster. Root hair cells have no chloroplasts and do not carry sugars.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Match-the-Card group activity, guard-cell CER (Claim-Evidence-Reasoning) presentation
Q2. A pair of guard cells is kidney-shaped, with a thick inner wall and a thin outer wall. When water enters the guard cells, what happens to the stoma and why?
  1. The stoma opens because the thin outer walls stretch more than the thick inner walls, so the cells bend apart
  2. The stoma stays the same because guard cells have no control over water
  3. The stoma opens because the guard cells lose their cytoplasm and become hollow
  4. The stoma closes because the thick inner walls stretch more than the thin outer walls
Correct answer: A
When water enters, the thin outer walls stretch more than the thick inner walls, so the pair bends apart and opens the pore. When water leaves they straighten and the pore closes.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Turkana vs Kericho guard cell comparison (fingers 1 or 2)
Q3. Two photographs show stomata on leaves of the same kind of plant taken at the same time of day. In dry Turkana the stomata are mostly closed. In humid Kericho they are mostly open. Which explanation best fits the guard cells in Turkana?
  1. The guard cells lose water and become less swollen, so the pore closes and saves water
  2. The guard cells have no chloroplasts, so they cannot open
  3. The guard cells die and are replaced by xylem vessels
  4. The guard cells gain extra water, swell, and close the pore
Correct answer: A
In dry conditions the plant is short of water. The guard cells lose water and become less swollen, so the pore closes and less water is lost.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: consolidation mini-lecture and exit-check sentence 'Is the xylem vessel alive?'
Q4. A learner says: "A xylem vessel is still alive because it carries water up the plant." Which statement is correct?
  1. The xylem vessel is dead because it has end walls that block the flow of water
  2. The xylem vessel is dead at maturity: it has no living contents, and the hollow lumen with strong lignified walls forms the pipe
  3. The xylem vessel is alive but has no cell wall
  4. The xylem vessel is alive and keeps a nucleus near its wall
Correct answer: B
Xylem vessels form from living cells that lose their contents. The hollow, lignified walls left behind form a pipe, so specialisation ends in the cell's death. They have no end walls.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: consolidation mini-lecture point (c), sieve plates of phloem
Q5. Phloem sieve tubes have perforated sieve plates at their ends. What is the function of these sieve plates?
  1. They let sugars flow from one sieve tube cell to the next
  2. They make the walls strong with lignin so the tube does not collapse
  3. They absorb water from the soil
  4. They carry out photosynthesis in the stem
Correct answer: A
The holes in the sieve plates let dissolved sugars pass between neighbouring sieve tube cells. Lignin strengthening belongs to xylem, and photosynthesis and water absorption are done by other cells.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: consolidation mini-lecture point (d), palisade cell chloroplast density and photosynthesis rate
Q6. Palisade mesophyll cells are tall and cylindrical and packed with many chloroplasts. How does this suit their function in the leaf?
  1. The many chloroplasts help the cells absorb water and mineral salts from the soil
  2. The many chloroplasts make the cells hollow so water can pass through
  3. The many chloroplasts let the cells open and close the stomata
  4. The many chloroplasts trap a lot of light for photosynthesis
Correct answer: D
Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll that traps light energy. Dense chloroplasts in palisade cells give a high rate of photosynthesis in the leaf.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the 'Plant Side' to the Cell Specialisation Model, arrows for cells working together in the root
Q7. A learner draws arrows to show how specialised cells work together in the root of a bean seedling. Which arrow correctly shows this?
  1. Guard cells absorb water from the soil, then phloem sieve tubes carry it to the root hairs
  2. Palisade cells absorb mineral salts from the soil, then root hair cells carry them to the leaf
  3. Xylem vessels absorb water from the soil, then root hair cells carry it up the stem
  4. Root hair cells absorb water and mineral salts from the soil, then xylem vessels transport them upwards
Correct answer: D
Root hair cells absorb water and mineral salts, and xylem vessels carry them up the plant. The other options give the jobs to the wrong cells.
