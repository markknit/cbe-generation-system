Use after: Observe phase — Structured reading handout, paragraph A: Carbon dioxide entering through the stomata
Q1. A student reads that scientists measure carbon dioxide inside and outside a leaf chamber. Through which part of the leaf does carbon dioxide from the air enter the sukuma wiki leaf for photosynthesis?
  1. The roots and xylem
  2. The chloroplasts
  3. The veins that carry water
  4. The stomata
Correct answer: D
Carbon dioxide from the air enters the leaf through the stomata. Roots and xylem carry water, not carbon dioxide.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 2 of guided discussion: building the word equation together
Q2. Which word equation correctly shows photosynthesis?
  1. Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
  2. Carbon dioxide + chlorophyll → glucose + water
  3. Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (with light energy and chlorophyll)
  4. Water + oxygen → glucose + carbon dioxide
Correct answer: C
Carbon dioxide and water are the reactants. Glucose and oxygen are the products. Light energy and chlorophyll are shown above the arrow. The first option is the reverse of the process.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 1 and 2 of guided discussion: difference between reactants and light energy and chlorophyll
Q3. Chlorophyll appears above the arrow in the photosynthesis equation, not among the reactants. Which statement about chlorophyll is correct?
  1. It is a raw material that the plant takes in with carbon dioxide
  2. It is absorbed from the soil and used up to make glucose
  3. It is released with oxygen as a product
  4. It is the green pigment that captures light energy and is not used up
Correct answer: D
Chlorophyll is the pigment in the chloroplasts that absorbs light, mainly red and blue. It is not on either side of the equation because it is not used up. Carbon dioxide and water are the substances that are used up.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 3 of guided discussion: building the chemical equation by counting atoms
Q4. The balanced equation for photosynthesis is 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2. How many oxygen atoms are there on the left-hand side (reactants) of this equation?
  1. 24
  2. 6
  3. 12
  4. 18
Correct answer: D
6CO2 has 6 × 2 = 12 oxygen atoms and 6H2O has 6 × 1 = 6 oxygen atoms, so there are 18 in total. The right side also has 18: 6 in C6H12O6 and 12 in 6O2.
Use after: Explain phase — Application question: which raw materials explain wilting at midday?
Q5. A kale plant in a kitchen garden wilts at midday even though the soil is moist. Which explanation fits what the lesson teaches?
  1. The plant has run out of chlorophyll because of too much sunlight
  2. Strong midday sunlight makes the plant lose water by transpiration faster than the roots can supply it
  3. The soil has no water left, so the plant cannot take any up
  4. Too much carbon dioxide has entered through the stomata and made the leaves limp
Correct answer: B
Peak sunlight increases transpiration, so water is lost faster than it is taken up even when the soil is moist. The plant recovers overnight when the rate of loss falls.
Use after: Explain phase — Application question: which raw materials explain pallor in shade?
Q6. A kale plant moved under a mango tree stops wilting. After several weeks its leaves become pale yellowish-green and it grows slowly. Which explanation fits best?
  1. Shade makes the stomata open wider, so the leaves lose more water
  2. Shade removes carbon dioxide from the air around the leaves
  3. Shade gives the plant too much water, which washes the green colour out of the leaves
  4. Less light reaches the leaves, so less chlorophyll is made and less glucose is produced
Correct answer: D
Reduced light means less chlorophyll is made over time, which causes pallor. Less light also means less photosynthesis, so less glucose is made and growth is slow.
Use after: Model Building phase — My Photosynthesis Model, Version 1: four arrows for raw materials coming in
Q7. A learner draws a model of a kale plant with arrows showing where each photosynthesis input comes from. Which set of labels is correct?
  1. Carbon dioxide from the air through the stomata; water from the soil through roots and xylem; light from the sun onto the leaf; chlorophyll inside the chloroplasts
  2. Carbon dioxide from the soil; water through the stomata; light from the roots; chlorophyll in the xylem
  3. Carbon dioxide from the air through the roots; water through the stomata; light from the soil; chlorophyll in the air spaces
  4. Carbon dioxide from the xylem; water from the air through the leaf surface; light through the roots; chlorophyll outside the plant
Correct answer: A
Carbon dioxide enters through the stomata. Water enters through the roots and moves up the xylem. Light falls on the leaf surface. Chlorophyll is inside the chloroplasts of the palisade mesophyll cells.
