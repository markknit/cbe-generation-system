Q1. In a cross-section of a dicot leaf such as mango, which layer is made of tightly packed, column-shaped cells that contain many chloroplasts and is found just below the upper epidermis?
  1. Spongy mesophyll
  2. Xylem
  3. Lower epidermis
  4. Palisade mesophyll
Correct answer: D
Palisade mesophyll cells are long, closely packed and sit just under the upper epidermis, where they hold most of the chloroplasts and capture the most light.
Use after: Explain phase — Jigsaw expert group: epidermis/cuticle function statement
Q2. Why are the cuticle and the upper epidermis of a leaf transparent?
  1. So that sugars can leave the leaf quickly
  2. So that light can pass through to the palisade cells below
  3. So that water can enter the leaf from the air
  4. So that the leaf can lose more water vapour
Correct answer: B
The cuticle and the upper epidermis are transparent so light reaches the chloroplasts in the palisade mesophyll, where photosynthesis happens.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 4: locate stomata on the lower epidermis and sketch a stoma with its two guard cells
Q3. A student looks at the lower epidermis of a leaf under the microscope and sees tiny pores, each surrounded by two bean-shaped cells. What are these pores and cells called?
  1. Palisade cells and spongy cells
  2. Air spaces and vascular bundles
  3. Stomata and guard cells
  4. Xylem vessels and phloem tubes
Correct answer: C
Stomata are the pores in the leaf surface, and each is controlled by a pair of guard cells that make it open or close.
Use after: Explain phase — Jigsaw expert group: spongy mesophyll/air spaces function statement
Q4. The spongy mesophyll has many air spaces between its loosely arranged cells. What is the main advantage of these air spaces?
  1. They carry water up from the roots
  2. They let carbon dioxide diffuse through the leaf to the photosynthesising cells
  3. They store sugars made during photosynthesis
  4. They stop light from entering the leaf
Correct answer: B
Air spaces provide a continuous route for gases, so carbon dioxide that enters through the stomata can diffuse to the mesophyll cells.
Use after: Explain phase — Jigsaw expert group: vascular bundles function statement
Q5. In a leaf's vascular bundle, which tissue carries water and mineral salts up from the roots, and which carries sugars made in the leaf to other parts of the plant?
  1. Phloem carries water and minerals; xylem carries sugars
  2. Xylem carries both water and sugars; phloem carries only minerals
  3. Phloem carries both water and sugars; xylem carries only minerals
  4. Xylem carries water and minerals; phloem carries sugars
Correct answer: D
Xylem supplies water and mineral salts to the leaf, while phloem exports sugars from the leaf to places such as roots and young leaves.
Use after: Explain phase — Synthesis question: 'What else moves through an open stoma besides gases?'
Q6. Besides carbon dioxide and oxygen, what else moves out through open stomata on a hot, sunny day?
  1. Mineral salts
  2. Liquid sugar
  3. Chlorophyll
  4. Water vapour
Correct answer: D
Water evaporates from the moist mesophyll cells and escapes as water vapour through open stomata. This is why leaves lose water quickly at midday.
Use after: Model Building phase — Revising the Lesson 1 phenomenon model with leaf cross-section, water and water vapour arrows
Q7. Kale plants in a garden wilt at midday even though the soil is moist, and they recover by the next morning. Which explanation best uses leaf structure?
  1. The cuticle has become thinner, so light can no longer enter
  2. The palisade cells have stopped photosynthesising permanently and died
  3. Stomata are wide open in strong sun, so water vapour is lost faster than the xylem can supply water, and the cells lose turgor
  4. The soil has run out of water, so the roots cannot take in any more
Correct answer: C
In strong sun the stomata open wide and transpiration is fast. Water leaves faster than the xylem can replace it, so cells lose turgor and the leaf wilts. When the sun weakens, supply catches up and the leaf recovers.
Use after: End of lesson — Using structural evidence to explain shade causing pallor and slow growth in the sukuma wiki phenomenon
Q8. Kale plants moved into the shade of a mango tree stop wilting, but over several weeks their leaves become pale and growth slows. Which explanation fits what you learnt about leaf structure?
  1. The stomata in the shade are blocked, so water cannot leave the leaf
  2. The xylem stops carrying water, so the leaves turn yellow
  3. Less light reaches the palisade cells, so less photosynthesis happens and less food is made
  4. The cuticle gets thicker, so oxygen cannot leave the leaf
Correct answer: C
The palisade mesophyll holds most of the chloroplasts and needs light for photosynthesis. In shade it makes less food, so the plant grows more slowly and looks pale.
