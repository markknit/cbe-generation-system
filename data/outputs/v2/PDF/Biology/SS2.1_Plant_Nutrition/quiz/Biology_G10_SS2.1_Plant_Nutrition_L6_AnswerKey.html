Q1. In which part of the chloroplast do the light-independent reactions (the Calvin cycle) take place?
  1. In the stroma
  2. In the stomata on the leaf surface
  3. On the thylakoid membrane
  4. In the cell nucleus
Correct answer: A
The Calvin cycle takes place in the fluid-filled stroma. The thylakoid membrane is where the light-dependent reactions happen, and stomata are only the pores that let CO₂ into the leaf.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A — Annotated Diagram Walk (tracing the carbon story in red)
Q2. In the Calvin cycle, the enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide from the air. What does RuBisCO join the CO₂ onto?
  1. ATP, the energy carrier from the light reactions
  2. Glucose, which then splits into two parts
  3. G3P, a 3-carbon molecule
  4. RuBP, a 5-carbon acceptor molecule
Correct answer: D
RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to the 5-carbon acceptor RuBP, which forms two 3-carbon molecules of 3-PGA. G3P and glucose are products made later, not the acceptor.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B — Card-Sort Sequencing ('1 G3P used to build glucose' and '5 G3P used to regenerate RuBP' cards)
Q3. In one turn of the Calvin cycle, 6 molecules of G3P are produced. One G3P leaves the cycle to be used to build glucose. How many G3P molecules are used to regenerate RuBP so that the cycle can continue?
  1. 1
  2. 3
  3. 6
  4. 5
Correct answer: D
Of the 6 G3P made, 1 exits to make glucose, so the remaining 6 − 1 = 5 are used to regenerate RuBP.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B — Card-Sort Sequencing ('ATP and NADPH from light reactions arrive in stroma' card)
Q4. A group is sequencing the steps of the Calvin cycle. Where do the ATP and NADPH used in the Calvin cycle come from?
  1. They are made by the Calvin cycle itself using CO₂
  2. They are made in the light-dependent reactions and arrive in the stroma
  3. They are made when glucose is broken down at night
  4. They are taken in from the soil with water and minerals
Correct answer: B
The Calvin cycle does not make its own energy carriers. ATP and NADPH are made in the light-dependent reactions and move to the stroma, so this step must come before G3P is produced.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A — Collaborative Diagram Construction (the 'ATP + NADPH' bridge arrow from Box 1 to Box 2)
Q5. What do ATP and NADPH do in the Calvin cycle?
  1. They are the 5-carbon molecules that CO₂ is fixed onto
  2. They split water molecules to release oxygen
  3. They absorb sunlight in the stroma
  4. They provide the energy and hydrogen needed to reduce 3-PGA to G3P
Correct answer: D
ATP supplies energy and NADPH supplies hydrogen to turn 3-PGA into G3P. Splitting water and absorbing light belong to the light-dependent stage, and the 5-carbon acceptor is RuBP.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B — Think-Pair-Share Explanation (why Amina's shaded sukuma wiki grows slowly)
Q6. Amina's sukuma wiki under a mango tree receives only 20% of the sunlight it would get in the open, but it has plenty of CO₂ and water. Which chain of events best explains why it grows slowly?
  1. Less light → less CO₂ enters the leaf → no RuBP is made → the plant wilts
  2. Less light → the thylakoid stops working completely → water cannot reach the leaf → slower growth
  3. Less light → the Calvin cycle makes its own ATP instead → more glucose than usual → slower growth
  4. Less light → less ATP and NADPH → the Calvin cycle slows → less G3P and glucose → slower growth
Correct answer: D
Weaker light means fewer light-dependent reactions, so less ATP and NADPH reach the stroma. The Calvin cycle then makes less G3P and glucose, leaving less energy and fewer carbon skeletons for growth.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model-building: adding the 'Stroma — Calvin Cycle' chamber with ATP + NADPH arrows from the thylakoid
Q7. A learner is revising her photosynthesis model by adding a second chamber labelled 'Stroma — Calvin Cycle'. Which drawing is correct?
  1. CO₂ enters the stroma, and ATP and NADPH flow from the thylakoid to the stroma
  2. CO₂ and light both enter the stroma, and the stroma makes ATP and NADPH for the thylakoid
  3. CO₂ enters the stroma, and glucose flows from the stroma into the thylakoid to be turned into ATP
  4. CO₂ enters the thylakoid, and ATP and NADPH flow from the stroma to the thylakoid
Correct answer: A
CO₂ is fixed in the stroma. ATP and NADPH made in the thylakoid move to the stroma to power the Calvin cycle, so the arrows run from thylakoid to stroma.
