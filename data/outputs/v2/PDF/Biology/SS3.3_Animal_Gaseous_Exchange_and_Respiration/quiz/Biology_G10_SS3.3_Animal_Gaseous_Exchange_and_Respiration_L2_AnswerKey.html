Q1. During step-ups your breathing rate doubled, so your lungs had to move twice as much oxygen into your blood in the same time. Which set of features would make a respiratory surface best at doing this job?
  1. Large surface area, thin wall, moist surface and a rich blood supply
  2. Small surface area, thin wall, dry surface and a rich blood supply
  3. Large surface area, thick wall, dry surface and a poor blood supply
  4. Thick, hard wall with a small surface area and no blood supply
Correct answer: A
Gases diffuse quickly across a surface that is large, thin, moist and well supplied with blood. A thick, hard wall is a common misconception because it seems strong, but it slows diffusion.
Use after: Observe phase — Station A: Earthworm skin (cutaneous exchange)
Q2. An earthworm has no lungs or gills. How does it take in oxygen?
  1. Air enters through spiracles and travels along tracheal tubes
  2. Oxygen diffuses through its thin, moist skin into the capillaries just beneath it
  3. Air is breathed into millions of alveoli inside its body
  4. Oxygen is taken from the water through gill lamellae
Correct answer: B
The earthworm uses cutaneous exchange. Its skin is thin and kept moist by mucus, with a capillary network below the epidermis, so oxygen diffuses straight in.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: Fish gill lamellae
Q3. A fish gill has many thin, plate-like lamellae on each gill filament. What is the main advantage of having so many lamellae?
  1. They make the wall thicker so less water enters the blood
  2. They greatly increase the surface area for gas exchange
  3. They make the gill hard and strong so it does not break
  4. They keep the gill dry so oxygen can pass through
Correct answer: B
Many thin lamellae greatly increase the surface area in contact with water. More oxygen can diffuse into the blood at once.
Use after: Observe phase — Station C: Insect tracheal system
Q4. In an insect, oxygen enters through small openings called spiracles. How does the oxygen then reach the body cells?
  1. It diffuses through the insect's wet skin into the blood
  2. It dissolves in water and passes over gill lamellae
  3. Blood carries it from the lungs to the body cells
  4. Tracheal tubes branch into tracheoles that carry air directly to the body cells
Correct answer: D
An insect's tracheal system delivers air through spiracles, tracheal tubes and tracheoles that end right next to body cells. Blood is not the main carrier of oxygen.
Use after: Observe phase — Whole-class limewater demonstration (between Station C and Station D)
Q5. A teacher blows air from her lungs through a straw into clear limewater, and the limewater turns milky. What does this show?
  1. Exhaled air contains carbon dioxide
  2. Exhaled air contains no gas, only water vapour
  3. Exhaled air contains oxygen
  4. Exhaled air contains nitrogen only
Correct answer: A
Limewater turns milky when carbon dioxide is present. This shows that CO₂ leaves the body in exhaled air, so gas exchange is happening.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: master list 'Features of an Efficient Respiratory Surface' and connection paragraph
Q6. The wall of an alveolus is only one cell thick. How does this help gas exchange?
  1. It makes the wall strong enough to pump gases into the blood
  2. It keeps the alveolus dry so gases move faster
  3. It shortens the distance gases must diffuse, so they cross quickly
  4. It stores extra oxygen inside the wall
Correct answer: C
A very thin wall gives a short diffusion distance, so oxygen and carbon dioxide cross quickly. This is how breathing can double during exercise and still supply enough oxygen.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB update: green sticky note partial answers
Q7. After a breath, oxygen is in the air inside the alveoli. Which statement gives the best partial answer to the question 'Where does the oxygen go once it enters the body?'
  1. The oxygen stays in the alveoli until we breathe out
  2. The oxygen is changed into carbon dioxide inside the alveoli
  3. The oxygen diffuses across the thin alveolar wall into the dense network of capillaries
  4. The oxygen moves straight into the muscle cells through the lung wall
Correct answer: C
Evidence from the alveolus station shows a one-cell-thick wall and a dense capillary network. Oxygen diffuses from the alveolus into the blood, which then carries it away.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: zoom-in alveolus panel added to the class model
Q8. You add a zoom-in panel of an alveolus to your model, with arrows showing how gases move by diffusion. Which arrows are correct?
  1. Oxygen and carbon dioxide both move from the alveolus into the blood
  2. Oxygen moves from the blood into the alveolus, and carbon dioxide moves from the alveolus into the blood
  3. Oxygen moves from the alveolus into the blood, and carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the alveolus
  4. Oxygen and carbon dioxide both move from the blood into the alveolus
Correct answer: C
Oxygen diffuses from the air in the alveolus into the blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveolus and is breathed out.
