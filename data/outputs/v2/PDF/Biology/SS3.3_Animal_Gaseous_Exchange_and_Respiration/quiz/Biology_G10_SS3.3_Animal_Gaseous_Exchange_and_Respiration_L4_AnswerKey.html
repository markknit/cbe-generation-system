Q1. In a side-view drawing of the human chest, where is the diaphragm found?
  1. At the throat, at the top of the windpipe
  2. Between the two lungs, around the heart
  3. Along the inside of the ribs, between neighbouring ribs
  4. Just below the lungs, separating the chest from the abdomen
Correct answer: D
The diaphragm is a dome-shaped sheet of muscle below the lungs and above the abdomen. Drawing it at the throat is a common misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: animation, diaphragm row of the observation table (Timestamp 1)
Q2. What does the diaphragm do when you breathe IN (inhalation)?
  1. It stays still while only the ribs move
  2. It relaxes and moves upward
  3. It contracts and moves upward
  4. It contracts and flattens, moving downward
Correct answer: D
During inhalation the diaphragm contracts and flattens, moving down. This increases the volume of the thoracic cavity.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: animation, intercostal muscles row of the observation table (Timestamp 2)
Q3. During inhalation, what do the external intercostal muscles do, and what is the effect on the chest?
  1. They relax, so the ribs rise and the chest becomes bigger
  2. They contract and pull the ribs inward and downward, making the chest smaller
  3. They contract and raise the ribs upward and outward, making the chest bigger
  4. They relax, so the ribs drop and the chest becomes smaller
Correct answer: C
Contraction of the external intercostal muscles lifts the rib cage upward and outward. This adds to the increase in thoracic volume caused by the diaphragm.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: animation, pressure-volume graph (Timestamp 3)
Q4. When the volume of the thoracic cavity increases during inhalation, what happens to the air pressure inside the lungs, and why does air move in?
  1. Pressure falls, but air flows out because the lungs are now emptier
  2. Pressure stays the same, and the lungs pull air in by themselves
  3. Pressure falls slightly below atmospheric pressure, so air flows in from outside
  4. Pressure rises above atmospheric pressure, so air is pushed into the lungs
Correct answer: C
A larger volume gives a lower pressure. Air moves from the higher atmospheric pressure to the slightly lower pressure in the lungs. The difference is small (about 0.1 kPa in quiet breathing) but enough to move air.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: animation, labelled sequence naming the pleural cavity and pleural fluid
Q5. What is the pleural cavity?
  1. The fluid-filled space between the lung and the chest wall, which makes the lungs follow the chest's movements
  2. The muscular space between the stomach and the diaphragm
  3. The air space inside the lungs where gases are exchanged
  4. The tube that carries air from the nose to the lungs
Correct answer: A
The pleural cavity is a fluid-filled space between the visceral and parietal pleura. It transmits pressure changes so the lungs follow the chest wall.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: improvised bell-jar diaphragm model, scaffold questions Q1 and Q2 (pulling the base balloon down)
Q6. In a bottle model of breathing, a balloon is stretched over the cut base of the bottle, and two small balloons hang inside on a Y-shaped straw open to the outside air. You pull the base balloon downward and the small balloons inflate. Which explanation is correct?
  1. Pulling the base balloon down increases the volume inside the bottle, so the pressure falls below the outside air pressure and air flows into the small balloons
  2. The small balloons stretch and pull the air in by themselves
  3. Pulling the base balloon down increases the pressure inside the bottle, which pushes air into the small balloons
  4. Pulling the base balloon pushes extra air into the bottle, which fills the small balloons
Correct answer: A
Pulling the base down increases the volume around the small balloons and lowers the pressure there. The higher outside air pressure then pushes air in through the straw, as in the diaphragm flattening in the chest.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: teacher input answering 'What makes a breath happen — is it automatic?'
Q7. After 2 minutes of step-ups your breathing rate doubled. What mainly makes your breathing speed up?
  1. The diaphragm contracts and relaxes on its own without any signals from the brain
  2. Your blood runs out of nitrogen, so the brain tells the lungs to take in more
  3. The lungs get tired, so they pull in air more quickly by themselves
  4. Your muscles make more carbon dioxide, which makes the blood slightly more acidic, so the breathing centre in the brain sends faster signals to the diaphragm and intercostal muscles
Correct answer: D
Exercising muscles produce more carbon dioxide. Sensors detect the change, and the breathing centre (medulla) sends faster signals to the breathing muscles. The lungs do not pull air in by themselves.
