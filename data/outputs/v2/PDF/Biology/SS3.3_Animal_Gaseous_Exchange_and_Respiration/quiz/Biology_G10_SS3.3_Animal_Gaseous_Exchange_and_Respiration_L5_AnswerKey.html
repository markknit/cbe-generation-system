Q1. Oxygen moves from the air in the alveolus into the blood in the surrounding capillary. What makes oxygen move in this direction?
  1. Red blood cells push oxygen across the alveolar wall using energy
  2. The heart sucks oxygen out of the alveolus into the blood
  3. Oxygen moves from low concentration to high concentration so that the blood fills up
  4. Oxygen diffuses from a higher concentration in the alveolus to a lower concentration in the blood
Correct answer: D
Blood arriving at the lungs has less oxygen than the air in the alveolus, so oxygen diffuses down its concentration gradient from high to low. No pump or push is needed at this step.
Use after: Observe phase — Oxygen Journey table: Lung capillary row (What carries O₂? column)
Q2. After oxygen crosses into the lung capillary, which part of the blood mainly carries it around the body?
  1. Haemoglobin inside red blood cells, forming oxyhaemoglobin
  2. Plasma alone, with no help from any cells
  3. Platelets, which seal wounds
  4. White blood cells, which fight germs
Correct answer: A
Haemoglobin in red blood cells binds oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin, which is how most oxygen is transported. Naming haemoglobin or red blood cells is more precise than just saying 'blood'.
Use after: Observe phase — Reading: route of oxygenated blood (Oxygen Journey table: Heart row)
Q3. Which list shows the correct route of oxygen-rich blood after it leaves the lungs, until it reaches the capillaries in a leg muscle?
  1. Pulmonary vein → left side of the heart → aorta → arteries → muscle capillaries
  2. Pulmonary vein → right side of the heart → vena cava → muscle capillaries
  3. Pulmonary artery → left side of the heart → aorta → muscle capillaries
  4. Lung capillaries → straight to muscle capillaries, without passing through the heart
Correct answer: A
Oxygen-rich blood returns from the lungs in the pulmonary vein to the left side of the heart. The heart pumps it into the aorta and arteries, which lead to the muscle capillaries. The heart is a pump on the route, not the final destination.
Use after: Explain phase — Molecule Narration: the oxygen molecule enters the muscle cell
Q4. In the muscle, oxygen leaves the blood in the capillaries and enters the muscle cells. Why does oxygen move into the muscle cells?
  1. Haemoglobin stays in the capillary and forces oxygen out with the blood pressure
  2. Muscle cells use up oxygen in respiration, so there is less oxygen inside them than in the blood, and oxygen diffuses in down the concentration gradient
  3. The heart pushes oxygen directly through the cell membrane of each muscle cell
  4. Muscle cells have more oxygen than the blood, so oxygen is pulled in by the high concentration inside the cell
Correct answer: B
Working muscle cells use oxygen for respiration, so the oxygen concentration inside them stays lower than in the capillary blood. Oxygen therefore diffuses from the blood into the cells.
Use after: DQB phase — Claim-Evidence-Reasoning card on the Driving Question Board: 'Why does the heart beat faster?'
Q5. Achieng's pulse rose from 74 to 128 beats per minute after step-ups. Which claim and reasoning best explain why her heart beat faster?
  1. The heart beats faster so that haemoglobin carries more oxygen to the muscles each minute, because the muscles are using oxygen faster
  2. The heart beats faster to make more oxygen for the muscles to use
  3. The heart beats faster so that the lungs can stop working and rest during exercise
  4. The heart beats faster so that oxygen can stay inside the heart until it is needed
Correct answer: A
Haemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen, so more heartbeats per minute means more oxygen delivered per minute to muscles that are using it faster. The heart pumps oxygen but does not make it or store it.
Use after: Explain phase — Oxygen Journey table: CO₂ diffusion back into the blood and transport to the lungs (extension in the Model phase)
Q6. Muscle cells that are working hard produce carbon dioxide. What happens to this carbon dioxide?
  1. It diffuses from the muscle cells into the blood and is carried back to the lungs to be breathed out
  2. It is carried by the heart straight out of the body through the skin
  3. It turns into oxygen inside the red blood cells
  4. It stays in the muscle cells until the exercise ends
Correct answer: A
Carbon dioxide is at a higher concentration in the muscle cells than in the blood, so it diffuses into the capillaries. The blood carries it to the lungs, the reverse of the oxygen route, and it leaves the body when we breathe out.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative model revision: colour code for oxygenated (red) and deoxygenated (blue) blood
Q7. In a diagram of the circulatory system, oxygen-poor blood is usually drawn blue and oxygen-rich blood red. What is the colour of real oxygen-poor blood inside the body?
  1. Bright red
  2. Dark red
  3. Bright blue
  4. Purple-green
Correct answer: B
Blue is only a colour code used in diagrams. Real oxygen-poor blood is dark red, and veins only look blue through the skin.
