Q1. In Lesson 1, a learner who breathed 14 times per minute at rest breathed about 28 times per minute after 2 minutes of step-ups. Which explanation best matches the science learned in this unit?
  1. Learners breathe faster after exercise only because they feel tired
  2. The lungs become smaller during exercise, so they must fill more often
  3. Working muscle cells use more oxygen to make ATP and produce more carbon dioxide, so breathing speeds up to supply oxygen and remove CO₂
  4. The body needs to breathe faster to cool the blood in the lungs
Correct answer: C
Exercise increases ATP demand in muscle cells, so they use more oxygen and release more CO₂. Faster breathing supplies the oxygen and removes the CO₂. 'Because we are tired' is the naive Lesson 1 idea.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Gallery Walk, Evidence Stations 2 and 7 (respiratory surface and alveolus-capillary interface)
Q2. Which feature of the alveolus makes oxygen diffuse quickly from the air into the blood?
  1. Its thick, waterproof wall that holds the air in
  2. Its wall is only one cell thick, moist, and surrounded by capillaries with a rich blood supply
  3. Its lack of blood vessels, which keeps oxygen in the air sacs
  4. Its small surface area, which concentrates the oxygen
Correct answer: B
A very thin, moist wall, a large surface area and a rich capillary network keep the diffusion distance short and maintain the concentration gradient.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Gallery Walk, Evidence Station 3 (diaphragm and intercostal movement observation table and bottle model)
Q3. During inhalation, what happens to the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles?
  1. The diaphragm relaxes and flattens; the intercostal muscles contract so the ribs move down
  2. The diaphragm relaxes and arches upwards; the intercostal muscles relax so the ribs move down
  3. The diaphragm contracts and flattens; the intercostal muscles relax so the ribs move in
  4. The diaphragm contracts and flattens; the intercostal muscles contract and move the ribs up and out
Correct answer: D
On inhalation the diaphragm contracts and flattens while the intercostal muscles raise the ribs up and out. Chest volume increases and air pressure falls, so air flows in.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Gallery Walk, Evidence Station 5 (aerobic respiration equation)
Q4. Which equation correctly shows aerobic respiration in muscle cells?
  1. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃ + 2ATP
  2. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  3. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6CO₂ → 6O₂ + 6H₂O + ATP
  4. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP
Correct answer: D
Aerobic respiration uses glucose and oxygen to produce carbon dioxide, water and ATP. The first equation is lactic acid fermentation and the second is photosynthesis.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Final Explanation, component 3 (The Circulatory Link)
Q5. A learner writes the Final Explanation component 'The Circulatory Link'. Which statement is scientifically correct?
  1. Blood carries oxygen dissolved only in the plasma, and the heart slows down so the blood has more time to pick it up
  2. In the lungs, haemoglobin in red blood cells combines with oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin, and a faster heart rate carries it more quickly through the capillaries to the muscles
  3. Oxygen travels to the muscles through the windpipe, and blood only carries carbon dioxide away
  4. The heart pumps air from the lungs directly into the muscle cells
Correct answer: B
Haemoglobin picks up oxygen in the lungs to form oxyhaemoglobin. A faster heart rate speeds delivery to muscle capillaries, linking the breathing and circulatory systems.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB ceremony: Q2 'Why do legs burn even when you are breathing hard?'
Q6. During hard exercise, a learner is breathing very fast but still feels a burning sensation in the leg muscles. What is the best explanation?
  1. Oxygen is not reaching the leg muscle cells fast enough, so some cells break down glucose without oxygen and lactic acid builds up
  2. Fast breathing damages the leg muscles
  3. The muscles have run out of blood, so no respiration is happening
  4. Too much oxygen has reached the muscles and it is burning the muscle tissue
Correct answer: A
When oxygen supply cannot keep up with demand, muscle cells use anaerobic respiration (lactic acid fermentation). The lactic acid accumulates and causes the burning feeling, even though breathing is fast.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Part B: Akinyi transfer task, question 1 (first 8 km of steady swimming)
Q7. Akinyi, a swimmer from Kisumu, swims steadily for 8 km, turning to breathe every 3 strokes. Which description best fits her body during this steady swimming?
  1. Her breathing and heart rate increase, blood delivers oxygen on haemoglobin to her muscles, and her muscle cells mainly respire aerobically
  2. Her muscle cells respire only anaerobically, because she holds her breath with her face in the water
  3. Her heart rate falls so that her blood can carry more oxygen
  4. Her breathing rate and heart rate stay the same as at rest because the water cools her
Correct answer: A
Steady exercise raises breathing and heart rate, so enough oxygen reaches the muscles via haemoglobin. The muscle cells mainly use aerobic respiration to make ATP.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Part B: Akinyi transfer task, question 2 (final 2 km sprint, process, equation and burning sensation)
Q8. In the last 2 km, Akinyi sprints and her arm muscles burn. Which process and equation describe what is happening in her muscle cells?
  1. Lactic acid fermentation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃ + 2ATP, and the burning is caused by lactic acid building up
  2. Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂, and the burning is caused by glucose
  3. Lactic acid fermentation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃ + 2ATP, and the burning is caused by too much oxygen
  4. Aerobic respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP, and the burning is caused by carbon dioxide
Correct answer: A
When oxygen is insufficient during the sprint, muscle cells use anaerobic respiration (lactic acid fermentation), which releases a small amount of ATP. The lactic acid that accumulates causes the burning sensation.
Use after: End of lesson — Model Part B: Akinyi transfer task, question 3 (heavy breathing after she stops swimming)
Q9. Akinyi has stopped swimming but is still breathing very heavily. Why?
  1. She is breathing heavily only because she is embarrassed and tired
  2. She is repaying the oxygen debt: the extra oxygen is used to break down the lactic acid that built up and to restore her energy supplies
  3. Her lungs are damaged and cannot slow down
  4. Her muscle cells have stopped respiring and are waiting for oxygen to restart
Correct answer: B
After anaerobic respiration, extra oxygen is needed to oxidise the accumulated lactic acid and to replenish ATP stores. This is the oxygen debt, and it explains the heavy breathing after exercise stops.
