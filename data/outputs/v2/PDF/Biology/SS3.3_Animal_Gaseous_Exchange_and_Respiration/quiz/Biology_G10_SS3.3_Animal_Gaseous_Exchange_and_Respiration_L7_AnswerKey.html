Q1. A group mixes warm water, sugar and dried yeast in a bottle and stretches a balloon over the neck. After 10 minutes the balloon has started to inflate. Which gas is mainly inflating the balloon?
  1. Oxygen made by the yeast
  2. Carbon dioxide produced by the yeast during respiration
  3. Nitrogen released from the sugar
  4. Ethanol vapour that is lighter than air
Correct answer: B
Yeast respire the sugar and release carbon dioxide gas, which collects in the balloon. Yeast do not produce oxygen, and the sugar does not release nitrogen.
Use after: Observe phase — Yeast Balloon practical: teacher question 'Is there oxygen in this bottle? How could we make sure the CO₂ comes from respiration WITHOUT oxygen?'
Q2. A teacher wants to be sure that the CO₂ in a yeast balloon set-up comes from respiration WITHOUT oxygen. Which change to the set-up would help most?
  1. Add extra sugar and shake the bottle every minute
  2. Remove the balloon so that air can enter the bottle
  3. Use water that was boiled and cooled, and float a thin layer of cooking oil on top
  4. Use much hotter water, about 80 °C, so the yeast work faster
Correct answer: C
Boiled and cooled water has little dissolved oxygen, and the oil layer stops new air from dissolving in. Hot water would kill the yeast, and shaking or removing the balloon adds oxygen.
Use after: Observe phase — Observation Sheet: writing the word and balanced chemical equations for aerobic and anaerobic respiration
Q3. Which equation correctly shows anaerobic respiration in yeast?
  1. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ + energy
  2. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy
  3. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃ + energy
  4. C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ + energy
Correct answer: A
Yeast break glucose down without oxygen into ethanol and carbon dioxide, releasing a little energy. The equation has no oxygen on the left side, and 6 C, 12 H and 6 O are present on each side.
Use after: Observe phase — 'Burning legs' video clip: annotating what is happening in the runner's cells
Q4. During the final sprint of an 800 m race, a runner's muscle cells do not get enough oxygen. What does anaerobic respiration in these muscle cells produce?
  1. Lactic acid, with a small amount of energy
  2. Lactic acid, carbon dioxide and water
  3. Carbon dioxide and water, with a large amount of energy
  4. Ethanol and carbon dioxide
Correct answer: A
In animal muscle cells, glucose is partly broken down without oxygen into lactic acid, releasing only a little energy (about 2 ATP). Ethanol and CO₂ is the yeast pathway, and CO₂ and water come from aerobic respiration.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class side-by-side comparison table (Aerobic, Anaerobic animals, Anaerobic yeast)
Q5. Which statement correctly compares aerobic respiration with anaerobic respiration in muscle cells?
  1. Aerobic respiration releases less energy because it uses oxygen up
  2. Aerobic respiration releases much more energy from the same glucose because it breaks the glucose down completely
  3. Anaerobic respiration releases more energy because it is faster
  4. Both release the same amount of energy from one glucose molecule
Correct answer: B
With oxygen, glucose is broken down completely to CO₂ and water, releasing a lot of energy. Without oxygen, glucose is only partly broken down to lactic acid, so little energy is released.
Use after: Explain phase — Individual structured written explanation: why lactic acid builds up in leg muscles during intense step-ups
Q6. A learner writes an explanation of why lactic acid builds up in leg muscles during intense step-ups. Which explanation is scientifically correct?
  1. Oxygen reaches the muscle cells more slowly than they need it, so some glucose is broken down without oxygen and forms lactic acid
  2. Extra carbon dioxide in the blood is changed into lactic acid
  3. Oxygen is poisonous to muscles, so lactic acid is made to get rid of it
  4. The muscles run out of glucose, so they make lactic acid from fat
Correct answer: A
When the oxygen supply cannot meet the demand of working muscles, glucose is partly broken down by anaerobic respiration, and lactic acid is the product. Lactic acid is not made from fat or CO₂, and oxygen is not toxic to muscles.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative 'Body During Exercise' model revision: sentence defining oxygen debt
Q7. After a hard race, an athlete keeps breathing deeply and quickly for several minutes. Which statement best explains this using the idea of oxygen debt?
  1. The athlete needs extra oxygen to break down the lactic acid that built up during the race
  2. The athlete's lungs have been damaged by the lactic acid
  3. The athlete is breathing out the ethanol made during the race
  4. The athlete is removing the extra oxygen stored in the muscles
Correct answer: A
Oxygen debt is the extra oxygen needed after exercise to break down the accumulated lactic acid. Deep breathing after the race supplies this oxygen. Ethanol is made by yeast, not by human muscles.
