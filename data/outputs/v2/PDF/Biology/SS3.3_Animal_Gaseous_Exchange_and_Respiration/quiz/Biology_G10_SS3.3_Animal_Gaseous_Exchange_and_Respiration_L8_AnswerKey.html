Use after: Observe phase — Observe: structured reading 'Measuring the Body's Fuel Gauge' (definition of RQ and its formula)
Q1. Respiratory Quotient (RQ) is used to tell what fuel the body is burning. Which formula gives RQ?
  1. RQ = CO₂ produced × O₂ consumed
  2. RQ = CO₂ produced − O₂ consumed
  3. RQ = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ consumed
  4. RQ = O₂ consumed ÷ CO₂ produced
Correct answer: C
RQ is the ratio of carbon dioxide produced to oxygen consumed, with CO₂ on top. Inverting the ratio (O₂ ÷ CO₂) is the common error.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: mini-lecture on ugali, nyama choma and githeri RQ values ('What pattern do you notice about which food has the lowest RQ?')
Q2. Three foods are eaten in Kenya: ugali (maize starch), nyama choma (fat-rich meat) and githeri (beans and maize, mainly protein and carbohydrate). When each is respired, which food gives the LOWEST RQ?
  1. All three have the same RQ because all use oxygen
  2. Githeri, with an RQ of about 0.9
  3. Nyama choma, with an RQ of about 0.7
  4. Ugali, with an RQ of about 1.0
Correct answer: C
Fat has the lowest RQ (about 0.7). Carbohydrate is about 1.0 and protein about 0.9, so the foods give different RQ values.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Problem 2 (guided) – tea-farm worker doing light weeding
Q3. A Kericho tea-farm worker doing light weeding consumes 500 mL of O₂ and produces 350 mL of CO₂ per minute. What is the worker's RQ?
  1. 0.85
  2. 1.43
  3. 0.15
  4. 0.7
Correct answer: D
RQ = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ consumed = 350 ÷ 500 = 0.7. The value 1.43 comes from inverting the ratio.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Problem 3 (independent) – long-distance runner from Iten
Q4. A long-distance runner training in Iten consumes 2,000 mL of O₂ and produces 1,900 mL of CO₂ per minute. What is the runner's RQ, and which fuel is mainly being respired?
  1. 0.95, mainly carbohydrate
  2. 0.95, mainly fat
  3. 0.70, mainly fat
  4. 1.05, mainly carbohydrate
Correct answer: A
RQ = 1,900 ÷ 2,000 = 0.95. This is close to 1.0, so the runner is mainly respiring carbohydrate, like ugali. A fat-dominated RQ would be near 0.7.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Problem 4 (extension/discussion) – Nairobi Marathon RQ 0.97 to 0.85
Q5. At the start of the Nairobi Marathon a runner's RQ is 0.97. By the 30 km mark it has dropped to 0.85. What does this change show?
  1. The runner is using less oxygen than at the start, so the body has relaxed
  2. The runner is using more fat as fuel than at the start, so the fuel is a mixture
  3. The runner is now using only carbohydrate
  4. The runner has stopped respiring and is now using only protein
Correct answer: B
An RQ falling from near 1.0 towards 0.7 means the fuel is shifting from carbohydrate towards fat. An RQ of 0.85 shows a mixture of fuels.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: teacher links new RQ notes to breathing rate and oxygen demand with red-marker arrows
Q6. During a long run, a muscle cell uses more fat and less carbohydrate, so its RQ falls. What does this change mean for the cell's oxygen needs?
  1. The cell needs no O₂ at all because fat is burned anaerobically
  2. The cell needs slightly more O₂ for the same amount of energy, on top of the large rise in total energy demand
  3. The cell needs exactly the same O₂ as with carbohydrate
  4. The cell needs much less O₂, so breathing can slow down
Correct answer: B
Fat needs slightly more oxygen per unit of energy (roughly 5 to 10% more) than carbohydrate. This is a small extra on top of the large rise in total energy demand, so the lungs and heart keep working hard.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the RQ layer to the cumulative explanatory model (link back to the burning in the legs during the step-ups)
Q7. After 2 minutes of hard step-ups, a learner feels a burning sensation in the leg muscles. What explains this burning?
  1. Lactic acid made by anaerobic respiration in the muscles
  2. Too much O₂ entering the muscle cells
  3. Fat being burned, which always causes the burning feeling
  4. The RQ rising above 1.0 and damaging the muscle
Correct answer: A
The burning comes from lactic acid made by anaerobic respiration, as learned in Lesson 7. The fuel switch from carbohydrate towards fat is a different change, seen in long exercise such as a marathon.
