Use after: Predict phase — Journal prompt and think-pair-share: 'List everything a plant needs to stay alive' (board heading WHAT PLANTS NEED)
Q1. A Grade 10 class lists what a maize plant needs to stay alive and healthy. Which list contains the four essential needs of a plant?
  1. Sunlight, carbon dioxide, ready-made food and fertiliser only
  2. Mineral salts, sugar from the soil, water and wind
  3. Water, mineral salts, carbon dioxide and sunlight
  4. Water, ready-made food from the soil, oxygen and shade
Correct answer: C
Plants need water, mineral salts, carbon dioxide and light energy. They make their own sugar in the leaves and do not take food from the soil.
Use after: Observe phase — Evidence mapping: placing resource cards on the maize diagram (green star = entry point)
Q2. On a maize plant, where do the mineral salts that the plant needs enter the plant?
  1. Through the stem, from sunlight
  2. Through the leaves, from the air
  3. Through the roots, from the soil water
  4. Through the flowers, from the rain
Correct answer: C
Mineral salts are dissolved in soil water and are taken in by the roots, below the soil line. Placing them at the leaves is a common misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Evidence mapping: placing resource cards on the maize diagram; ENTRY POINT | USE POINT table
Q3. A group places the carbon dioxide card on its maize diagram. Where does the maize plant take in carbon dioxide?
  1. Through the roots, together with the mineral salts
  2. Through the stem, from the soil water
  3. Through the leaves, from the air
  4. Through the roots, from the soil
Correct answer: C
Carbon dioxide is a gas in the air. It enters the leaves through tiny openings called stomata, not the roots.
Use after: Explain phase — Structured reasoning frame: 'Water enters the maize plant at the ____ ... the plant must have a ____'
Q4. Water enters a maize plant at the roots, but the leaves need it. Which statement best explains why the plant must have a transport system?
  1. Water is only needed in the roots, so a transport system is not needed
  2. Water enters at one place and is needed at another, so something must carry it between them
  3. The transport system is needed only to make the plant stand upright
  4. Leaves can take in water directly from the air, so the roots are not important
Correct answer: B
The entry point (roots) and the use point (leaves) are different places. Something inside the plant must move the water from one to the other, and this is the transport system.
Use after: Explain phase — Structured reasoning frame extension: applying the same logic to sugars made in the leaf
Q5. The leaves of a maize plant make sugar using sunlight and carbon dioxide. The root cells also need sugar to stay alive. What must happen to the sugar?
  1. It stays in the leaf, because roots make their own sugar from soil water
  2. It moves up from the roots to the leaf
  3. It is carried from the leaf down through the stem to the roots
  4. It is taken in from the soil by the roots, so no movement is needed
Correct answer: C
Sugar is made in the leaf, but the root also needs it. It must be transported down the stem, which is another reason a transport system is needed. Plants do not take in food from the soil.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: orange sticky note 'GROWING UNDERSTANDING' about the Kibwezi wilting maize
Q6. Which statement about the Kibwezi maize is a correct cause-and-effect link, as a student might write on an orange sticky note?
  1. Mineral salts enter at the leaf, so the roots do not need to take in anything
  2. Plants make food in the roots, so nothing needs to move through the stem
  3. Water is in the soil, so the leaves always have enough water
  4. Water enters at the root and is needed at the leaf, so the plant needs a transport system
Correct answer: D
Water entering at the root and being needed at the leaf shows why a transport system is necessary. The other statements repeat the misconceptions that water in the soil means water in the leaf, that mineral salts enter at the leaf, and that food is made in the roots.
Use after: End of lesson — Whole-lesson revisit of the Kibwezi wilting maize; extension question and model sentence 'This model helps explain the Kibwezi wilting mystery because...'
Q7. A farmer in Kibwezi waters her maize in the morning, yet the leaves wilt in the afternoon. The leaves are losing water faster than the plant's transport system can deliver it from the roots. What is the best conclusion?
  1. The plant wilts because there is no water in the soil
  2. The plant can wilt even when the soil has water, because the leaves lose water faster than it is moved up from the roots
  3. The plant wilts because mineral salts have entered at the leaves
  4. The plant wilts because the leaves cannot make sugar
Correct answer: B
Water in the soil is not the same as water inside the leaf. If water is lost at the leaf faster than the transport system brings it from the root, the plant wilts.
