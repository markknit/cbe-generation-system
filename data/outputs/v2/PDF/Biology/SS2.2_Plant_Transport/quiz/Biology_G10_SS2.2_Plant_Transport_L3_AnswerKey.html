Q1. A farmer cuts across a maize stem and sees two different kinds of tubes. One kind carries water upward and the other carries something else. What are the two kinds of transport tubes in plants called?
  1. Phloem and stomata
  2. Stomata and root hairs
  3. Cortex and xylem
  4. Xylem and phloem
Correct answer: D
Xylem and phloem are the two vascular tissues that transport materials in plants. Stomata and root hairs are other plant structures, and the cortex is a layer of root tissue, not a transport tube.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity 2A: Whole-Plant Diagram Annotation (blue arrows for xylem)
Q2. In a maize plant, in which direction do water and mineral salts travel through the xylem?
  1. Upward from the soil through the root and stem to the leaves
  2. From the leaves down to the roots
  3. Only sideways from one leaf to another
  4. In both directions equally, like a two-way road
Correct answer: A
Xylem carries water and mineral salts upward in one direction: soil, root, stem, then leaf. This is why the blue arrows on the diagram all point upward.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity 2A: Whole-Plant Diagram Annotation (red arrows for phloem, including the maize cob as a sink)
Q3. A maize plant is growing a cob. The leaves make sugars by photosynthesis. Where does the phloem carry these manufactured food substances?
  1. From the leaves both downward to the roots and to the developing cob
  2. Only upward from the roots to the leaves
  3. From the soil to the leaves
  4. Only downward to the roots, never to the cob
Correct answer: A
Phloem carries manufactured food from the leaves to places that need it, such as the roots and the developing cob. It is bidirectional but mainly downward. The belief that phloem only goes down is a common misconception.
Use after: Explain phase — Activity 3A: Venn Diagram Compare & Contrast (type of cells, what is transported)
Q4. Which statement about xylem correctly compares it with phloem?
  1. Xylem carries water and mineral salts downward from the leaves.
  2. Xylem carries sugars and is made of living cells.
  3. Xylem and phloem carry exactly the same substances in the same direction.
  4. Xylem carries water and mineral salts and is made of dead, hollow cells that form continuous tubes.
Correct answer: D
Xylem is made of dead, hollow cells with no nucleus that form continuous tubes carrying water and mineral salts upward. Sugars are carried by phloem, so the other statements mix up the two tissues.
Use after: Explain phase — Activity 3C: Phenomenon Reconnect Discussion (what happens to the leaf if xylem is blocked)
Q5. The xylem in a maize stem becomes blocked, so water cannot reach the leaves. What will most likely happen to the leaves?
  1. They will lose water from their cells, become less firm and droop (wilt).
  2. They will make more food because the phloem works faster.
  3. They will stay the same because the soil still has water.
  4. They will grow firmer because they keep their sugars.
Correct answer: A
Blocked xylem means water cannot reach the leaf cells. The cells lose turgor, so the leaves droop and wilt. Water in the soil does not help if it cannot travel up the xylem.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model v.1: thought-bubble explanation of wilting (xylem, water, turgor pressure, wilting)
Q6. A student writes this explanation for the wilting maize: 'Insufficient water reaching leaf cells via xylem → ______ → wilting.' Which phrase correctly fills the blank?
  1. Xylem carrying water downward
  2. Roots growing too deep
  3. Too much sugar in the phloem
  4. Loss of turgor pressure in the leaf cells
Correct answer: D
When leaf cells do not get enough water, they lose turgor, meaning they are no longer firm like a well-filled balloon. This makes the leaf droop, which is wilting.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: reconnecting to the Kibwezi farmer's puzzle
Q7. A farmer in Kibwezi waters her maize soil in the morning, yet the plants wilt in the afternoon. Which statement shows the best understanding of the two transport tissues?
  1. Xylem carries water downward from the leaves to the soil, so the soil stays wet.
  2. Watering the soil means the leaves must have enough water, so the wilting must be caused by phloem.
  3. Water in the soil is carried to the leaves by the phloem, so the phloem must be blocked.
  4. Water in the soil is not the same as water inside the plant. It must travel up the xylem to the leaf cells, and if it does not arrive fast enough the leaves lose turgor and wilt.
Correct answer: D
Water in the soil is only useful if it reaches the leaf cells through the xylem, which carries it upward. If supply to the leaves is too slow, the leaf cells lose turgor and the plant wilts, even when the soil is wet.
