Use after: Observe phase — Step 1: external surface and squeeze test for wall thickness
Q1. A group squeezes the walls of a sheep heart gently. The wall of the left ventricle feels much thicker and firmer than the wall of the right ventricle. What is the best explanation?
  1. The right ventricle pumps blood to the whole body, so it needs a thinner wall to move faster.
  2. The left ventricle is closer to the lungs, so it needs a thicker wall to push blood a short distance.
  3. The left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body, so it needs to generate higher pressure.
  4. The left ventricle holds deoxygenated blood, which makes its wall thicker.
Correct answer: C
The left ventricle sends blood through the systemic circuit to the whole body, which needs greater pressure. The right ventricle only pumps blood to the nearby lungs, so its wall is thinner.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 4: locating and comparing the pulmonary artery and aorta
Q2. A learner says: 'Arteries always carry oxygenated blood and veins always carry deoxygenated blood.' Which statement about the vessels of the mammalian heart shows that this is wrong?
  1. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs.
  2. The pulmonary artery carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium.
  3. The pulmonary vein carries deoxygenated blood from the lungs to the right atrium.
  4. The aorta carries deoxygenated blood from the left ventricle to the body.
Correct answer: A
The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart to the lungs, and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood back. Whether a vessel is an artery or a vein depends on the direction of flow, not on the oxygen content.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 3: probing the atrioventricular valves and chordae tendineae
Q3. A learner probes the valve between the left atrium and the left ventricle of a sheep heart with a pencil. What is this valve called, and what does it do?
  1. The semilunar valve; it separates oxygenated blood from deoxygenated blood.
  2. The bicuspid valve; it lets blood flow from the aorta into the left ventricle.
  3. The tricuspid valve; it lets blood flow from the left ventricle back into the left atrium.
  4. The bicuspid valve; it stops blood flowing backwards from the left ventricle into the left atrium.
Correct answer: D
The bicuspid (mitral) valve is on the left side between the atrium and the ventricle. It closes when the ventricle contracts so that blood cannot flow backwards.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 5: colour-coded arrows tracing the pulmonary and systemic circuits
Q4. Learners trace the systemic circuit with a red pen. Which pathway is correct, starting from the left ventricle?
  1. Left ventricle → aorta → body tissues → vena cava → right atrium
  2. Right ventricle → aorta → body tissues → vena cava → left atrium
  3. Left ventricle → vena cava → body tissues → aorta → right atrium
  4. Left ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium
Correct answer: A
In the systemic circuit, oxygenated blood leaves the left ventricle through the aorta, delivers oxygen to the body tissues, and returns through the vena cava to the right atrium.
Use after: Explain phase — Circuit summary, sentence 3: why the septum makes the mammalian heart more efficient
Q5. In the mammalian heart, the septum completely separates the left and right sides. Why does this make the heart more efficient than a frog heart?
  1. The septum makes the heart beat faster, so more blood is made.
  2. The septum means a mammal has more blood in its body than a frog.
  3. The septum lets deoxygenated blood go straight to the body to save time.
  4. Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix, so the tissues receive fully oxygenated blood.
Correct answer: D
Because the septum stops mixing, the blood sent to the muscles is fully oxygenated, so more oxygen arrives per heartbeat. Efficiency depends on what happens to the blood, not on the amount of blood.
Use after: Explain phase — Comparison question: 'What is different about Kipchoge?' (think-pair-share)
Q6. In Lesson 5 we learned that mixed blood in the frog heart is acceptable. Why is mixed blood NOT good enough for a marathon runner such as Eliud Kipchoge?
  1. A frog's blood has no oxygen in it, but a mammal's blood does.
  2. Kipchoge has a special heart that other mammals do not have.
  3. A runner's muscles need a very high oxygen supply for hours, so the blood must reach them fully oxygenated.
  4. A frog's heart is much bigger than a human heart, so it can mix blood safely.
Correct answer: C
Frogs have low energy demands, so partly mixed blood is enough. A runner at 21 km/h for over two hours needs the maximum oxygen per heartbeat, and the four-chambered heart that every mammal has provides this.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative model revision: adding the four-chambered heart with named vessels
Q7. While drawing the mammalian heart in a cumulative model, a learner shows the pulmonary vein entering the right atrium. Which correction is right?
  1. The pulmonary vein should enter the left atrium, because it brings oxygenated blood from the lungs.
  2. The pulmonary vein should enter the aorta directly, because it carries blood to the body.
  3. The pulmonary vein should enter the right ventricle, ready to pump blood to the lungs.
  4. The pulmonary vein should enter the right atrium from the body, so no correction is needed.
Correct answer: A
The pulmonary vein brings blood that has just picked up oxygen in the lungs. This blood enters the left atrium, then passes to the left ventricle to be pumped to the whole body.
