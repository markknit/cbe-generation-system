Q1. In an earlier lesson you studied the transport system of the grasshopper. Which statement correctly describes it?
  1. It is an open system in which haemolymph flows freely around the organs in the body cavity.
  2. It is a closed system with a two-chambered heart made of one atrium and one ventricle.
  3. It is a single circuit in which blood goes from the heart to the gills, then to the body, then back to the heart.
  4. It is a closed system in which blood stays inside vessels and is pumped through a four-chambered heart.
Correct answer: A
The grasshopper has an open haemolymph system, so the fluid is not kept inside vessels all the time. The other choices describe closed systems of fish or mammals.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: Diagram Tracing (labelling the two-chambered fish heart)
Q2. How many chambers does the heart of a tilapia have?
  1. Three chambers: two atria and one ventricle
  2. One chamber: a simple tube with no atrium or ventricle
  3. Two chambers: one atrium and one ventricle
  4. Four chambers: two atria and two ventricles
Correct answer: C
The tilapia heart is two-chambered, with one atrium and one ventricle. Four chambers belong to the mammalian heart, and a simple tube-like heart is not what the fish diagram shows.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: Diagram Tracing (red and blue tracing of the single circuit)
Q3. Which sequence shows the path of blood in the single circulatory system of a tilapia, starting at the heart?
  1. Heart → gill capillaries → heart → body tissues → heart
  2. Heart → body tissues → gill capillaries → heart
  3. Heart → body tissues → heart, with the blood oxygenated inside the heart
  4. Heart → gill capillaries (oxygenated here) → body tissues → veins → back to the heart
Correct answer: D
Blood leaves the heart, is oxygenated at the gill capillaries, travels via the dorsal aorta to the body tissues, then returns by veins to the heart in one loop. Oxygenation happens at the gills, not in the heart, and blood does not return to the heart between the gills and the body.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity B: Reading + Data (the sentence about blood pressure after the gill capillaries)
Q4. In a tilapia, blood passes through the gill capillaries before going to the body tissues. What happens to the blood pressure after the gill capillaries, and what does this mean for the tissues?
  1. Blood pressure drops, but a second ventricle pumps the blood again before it reaches the tissues.
  2. Blood pressure drops significantly, so the tissues receive blood at a lower pressure than in a mammal.
  3. Blood pressure rises because the gills pump the blood, so the tissues receive blood at a higher pressure than in a mammal.
  4. Blood pressure stays the same as when it left the heart, because no pressure is lost in the gills.
Correct answer: B
The tilapia has only one ventricle, so blood is not pumped again after the gills. The pressure falls and the tissues receive blood at lower pressure than in a mammal.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Explanation Writing (one structural reason Kipchoge's system is more efficient)
Q5. Both a Nile tilapia and Eliud Kipchoge need oxygen delivered to their muscles. Which is one structural reason Kipchoge's circulatory system delivers oxygen more efficiently?
  1. In the tilapia heart, oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix, but in mammals they do not.
  2. In mammals, blood returns from the lungs to the heart and is pumped a second time at high pressure to the body, whereas in the tilapia blood goes to the body at lower pressure after the gills.
  3. His heart has only one chamber, so blood can move through it faster than in a fish.
  4. His blood flows in open spaces around the organs, so it reaches every cell directly.
Correct answer: B
The mammalian double circulation re-pressurises the blood after the lungs. In the tilapia's single circuit the pressure drops after the gills and is not restored. Only deoxygenated blood enters the fish heart, so mixing is not the reason.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Comparative Table Construction (Insect vs Fish columns: open/closed, blood vessel types)
Q6. Which statement correctly compares the transport system of a grasshopper with that of a tilapia?
  1. Both have closed systems with the same number of heart chambers.
  2. Both have open systems, but the tilapia has a heart with two chambers.
  3. The grasshopper has a closed system and the tilapia has an open system.
  4. The tilapia's blood stays inside vessels all the time, while the grasshopper's haemolymph flows in the open body cavity.
Correct answer: D
The tilapia has a closed system with a two-chambered heart and blood always in vessels. The grasshopper has an open system with haemolymph bathing the organs.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative Model Revision: (c) one-sentence 'efficiency rating' next to the fish system
Q7. A learner writes an 'efficiency rating' for the tilapia's circulatory system on their model. Which claim is best supported by the evidence from this lesson?
  1. As efficient as a mammal's system because both are closed systems with blood in vessels.
  2. More efficient than a mammal's system because the blood makes only one circuit around the body.
  3. More efficient than the grasshopper because blood stays in vessels, but less efficient than a mammal because blood pressure drops after the gills.
  4. Less efficient than the grasshopper because the tilapia heart has only one atrium.
Correct answer: C
The tilapia's closed system is more efficient than the grasshopper's open system. However, its single circuit has no second pumping after the gills, so pressure falls and it is less efficient than the mammal's double circulation.
