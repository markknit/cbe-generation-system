Q1. A data sheet shows the heart-rate recovery of an elite marathon runner (illustrative curve). His heart rate was 172 beats per minute (bpm) at the finish and about 100 bpm roughly 4 minutes later. By about how many bpm did it fall in those 4 minutes?
  1. 100 bpm
  2. 82 bpm
  3. 72 bpm
  4. 62 bpm
Correct answer: C
Reading the data: 172 - 100 = 72 bpm. This checks that learners can read values from the recovery data before predicting.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: Think-Write-Pair-Share 'I notice...' and 'I predict that the difference is caused by...'
Q2. Learners wrote 'I notice...' and 'I predict...' statements about two heart-rate recovery curves. Which of these is an OBSERVATION from the data rather than a prediction?
  1. The elite runner's blood carries more oxygen than the teenager's blood.
  2. The elite runner's heart rate was about 100 bpm four minutes after finishing.
  3. The elite runner has a bigger heart than the teenager.
  4. The teenager recovers slowly because he does not practise running.
Correct answer: B
An observation states what the data show. The heart rate being about 100 bpm after four minutes is read directly from the curve. The other choices are guesses about causes, which are predictions that still need testing.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: grasshopper, tilapia, frog and goat comparison
Q3. In the teacher's account, a tilapia in Lake Victoria, a frog in the Rift Valley wetlands and a goat at Gikomba market have hearts with different numbers of chambers. Which list gives the numbers of chambers correctly?
  1. Tilapia 4, frog 3, goat 2
  2. Tilapia 2, frog 4, goat 4
  3. Tilapia 3, frog 2, goat 4
  4. Tilapia 2, frog 3, goat 4
Correct answer: D
The account gives the tilapia a two-chambered heart, the frog three chambers and the goat four chambers, like humans. The grasshopper has no heart like Kipchoge's.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: pair talk 'Does having more heart chambers mean being a better animal?'
Q4. After hearing about the grasshopper, tilapia, frog and goat, a learner asks: 'Does having more heart chambers mean being a better animal?' At this point in the inquiry, which response is most appropriate?
  1. This has not been explained yet; it is a good question to investigate using evidence in later lessons.
  2. Only animals with four chambers need a transport system.
  3. No, the number of chambers makes no difference to any animal.
  4. Yes, more chambers always make an animal better.
Correct answer: A
This is the anchoring lesson, so the phenomenon is left unexplained. The class records the question and investigates it over the coming lessons rather than accepting or rejecting an answer now.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'My current explanation (Iteration 1)' and 'Claims to investigate' on the board
Q5. Two learners give different explanations for the same heart-rate data. What is the scientific way to decide between them?
  1. Choose the explanation given by the learner who speaks first.
  2. Find out which explanation the evidence supports best.
  3. Accept the teacher's guess without testing it.
  4. Choose the explanation that most learners prefer.
Correct answer: B
The lesson models linking claims to evidence: different explanations are normal, and the class investigates which one the evidence best supports. Popularity or order of speaking does not decide it.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: yellow 'notice' cards and blue 'wonder' cards on the Driving Question Board
Q6. The class Driving Question Board has two columns: 'What we notice' and 'What we wonder'. Which card belongs under 'What we wonder'?
  1. Omondi's heart rate was still 90 bpm fifteen minutes after jogging 400 m.
  2. Kipchoge's heart rate fell to about 100 bpm within about four minutes.
  3. Why does a grasshopper not have a pump like ours?
  4. A tilapia has a two-chambered heart.
Correct answer: C
A 'wonder' is a question about something not yet understood. The other three cards are facts from the data or the story, so they belong under 'What we notice'.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: 'My Transport System Model - Iteration 1' sketch of how substances move inside an animal
Q7. In the first lesson on Animal Transport, learners state a basic idea about why animals have transport systems. Which statement best describes the job of a transport system in an animal?
  1. It is found only in animals that have a heart with four chambers.
  2. It moves substances such as oxygen, nutrients and wastes around the body.
  3. It makes the animal run faster than other animals.
  4. It only keeps the animal's body warm.
Correct answer: B
The lesson's knowledge outcome is that animals need transport systems to move substances around their bodies. How different animals do this is left for later lessons.
