Use after: Observe phase — Activity 1: Translucent Spot Test
Q1. A learner rubs mashed groundnuts on a piece of brown paper bag and waits 5 minutes for it to dry. Which observation shows that the groundnuts contain lipids?
  1. A translucent (greasy) spot stays on the paper even after it is dry
  2. A wet spot appears but disappears completely once the paper dries
  3. The paper stays completely dry and unmarked
  4. The paper turns a purple colour where the food was rubbed
Correct answer: A
Lipids leave a permanent translucent spot that does not disappear on drying. Water-based foods leave a spot that dries and disappears.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity 2: Ethanol Emulsion Test
Q2. In the ethanol emulsion test, a learner dissolves a food sample in ethanol, then pours the clear ethanol layer into a test tube of distilled water. What result shows that lipid is present?
  1. The mixture stays clear and colourless
  2. A cloudy white emulsion forms
  3. The mixture bubbles and gives off heat
  4. The mixture turns purple
Correct answer: B
Lipids dissolve in ethanol but not in water, so when the ethanol is added to water the lipid comes out as tiny droplets, forming a cloudy white emulsion. A clear solution means no lipid.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity 1 and Activity 2: results table for ugali (A)
Q3. A group tests ugali paste (maize starch) with both the translucent spot test and the ethanol emulsion test. Which set of results would you expect?
  1. A permanent translucent spot and a cloudy white emulsion
  2. No permanent translucent spot but a cloudy white emulsion
  3. No permanent translucent spot and a clear solution in water
  4. A permanent translucent spot but a clear solution in water
Correct answer: C
Ugali is mostly starch and has very little lipid, so it should give a negative result in both tests. Two tests that agree make the evidence stronger.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity 3: Guided Reading on Vitamins and the Vitamin Summary Strip
Q4. Which row correctly matches a vitamin with its type and the deficiency disease it causes?
  1. Vitamin C: fat-soluble; deficiency causes scurvy
  2. Vitamin A: fat-soluble; deficiency causes night blindness
  3. B-complex vitamins: fat-soluble; deficiency causes beriberi
  4. Vitamin D: water-soluble; deficiency causes rickets
Correct answer: B
Vitamins A, D, E and K are fat-soluble, and lack of vitamin A causes night blindness. Vitamin C and the B-complex vitamins are water-soluble, and vitamin D is fat-soluble.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class discussion: essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamin absorption, and the Temperature Check on night blindness
Q5. A child eats a meal rich in vitamin A but has almost no fat in their diet. Why might the child still develop night blindness?
  1. Vitamin A is fat-soluble and needs dietary fat to be absorbed
  2. Vitamin A is a protein, so it cannot be used without amino acids
  3. Fat destroys vitamin A in the stomach
  4. Vitamin A is water-soluble and is washed out of the body too quickly
Correct answer: A
Vitamins A, D, E and K are fat-soluble and are absorbed only when fat is present in the diet. Without fat, the body cannot fully absorb vitamin A even from a vitamin-rich meal.
Use after: Model Building phase — Revising the Cumulative Chemical Model of Food: 'Lipids' section showing structure
Q6. At the chemical level, what is a lipid (fat) made of?
  1. Vitamins joined to a mineral salt
  2. A glycerol backbone joined to fatty acid chains
  3. Long chains of amino acids joined together
  4. Many glucose units joined in a long chain
Correct answer: B
A lipid such as a fat is made of one glycerol molecule joined to three fatty acids. Amino acid chains are proteins and glucose chains are starch.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: after the Cumulative Chemical Model revision and peer feedback
Q7. Which statement correctly describes the roles of lipids in living organisms?
  1. Lipids are harmful chemicals that the body does not need
  2. Lipids only provide vitamins and have no other role
  3. Lipids store energy and are part of cell membranes
  4. Lipids are the main chemicals used to build muscles and repair tissues
Correct answer: C
Lipids store energy, form part of cell membranes and help absorb fat-soluble vitamins. Building and repairing muscles is mainly the role of proteins, and calling fat 'unhealthy' is a common misconception.
