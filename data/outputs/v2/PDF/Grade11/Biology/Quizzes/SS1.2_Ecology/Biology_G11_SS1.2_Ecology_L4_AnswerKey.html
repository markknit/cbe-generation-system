Q1. A team is sampling the grass and weeds in a zone of the school field using a 1 m x 1 m quadrat. Why must they place the quadrat at random locations instead of where the grass looks nicest?
  1. So that every quadrat contains exactly the same number of plants
  2. So that the sample is not biased and represents the whole zone
  3. So that the counting is finished faster
  4. So that only the most common weed is counted
Correct answer: B
Random placement removes bias, so the small sampled area fairly represents the whole zone. Choosing spots by eye would over- or under-estimate the population.
Use after: Observe phase — Jigsaw Step 1 (expert groups): calculating total counted, mean per quadrat and density
Q2. A team places a 1 m x 1 m quadrat at 5 random spots in a sunny zone of the school field. The numbers of weed plants counted are 12, 8, 10, 15 and 5. What is the density of weeds in plants per m²?
  1. 10 plants per m²
  2. 5 plants per m²
  3. 15 plants per m²
  4. 50 plants per m²
Correct answer: A
Total counted = 12 + 8 + 10 + 15 + 5 = 50. Density = 50 plants / (5 quadrats x 1 m²) = 10 plants per m².
Use after: Observe phase — Jigsaw Step 1 (expert groups): estimated population = density x total area of the zone
Q3. In a damp zone of the school field, the mean number of grass tufts is 6 per 1 m x 1 m quadrat. The zone has a total area of 200 m². What is the estimated total number of grass tufts in the zone?
  1. 33 tufts
  2. 1200 tufts
  3. 120 tufts
  4. 206 tufts
Correct answer: B
Density = 6 tufts per m². Estimated population = density x total area = 6 x 200 = 1200 tufts.
Use after: Explain phase — Jigsaw Step 2 (home groups): question 3 on how the quadrat method gives an estimate and why it is only an estimate
Q4. A class uses quadrats to estimate that there are 1200 weed plants in a zone. Why is this number only an estimate?
  1. Weeds cannot be counted accurately because they are too small
  2. Quadrats can only be used on grass and not on weeds
  3. The area of the quadrat was not known
  4. Only a small part of the zone was sampled and the result is assumed to represent the whole zone
Correct answer: D
Quadrat sampling counts a small part of the habitat and scales it up. Chance and the number of quadrats used mean the true total may differ.
Use after: Explain phase — Jigsaw Step 2 (home groups): claim-evidence-reasoning statement on chart paper
Q5. Home groups compare the mean plant density of two zones on the school field: 42 plants per m² in a damp zone near a tap and 6 plants per m² in a heavily trampled zone. Which claim is best supported by this evidence?
  1. Trampling has been proved to be the only cause of the low density
  2. The distribution of plants is not uniform, because density was much higher in the damp zone than in the trampled zone
  3. The damp zone is healthier because more plants always means a healthier ecosystem
  4. The distribution of plants is uniform, because plants grow everywhere on the field
Correct answer: B
The numbers show a clear difference in density between zones, so plants are not evenly spread. The data show a pattern but do not prove a single cause, and more plants does not always mean a healthier ecosystem.
Use after: Explain phase — Jigsaw Step 2 (home groups): question 4 linking weeds and algae in the river to the field patterns
Q6. Along a Kenyan river, aquatic weeds and algae grow thickly in one stretch where fertiliser and sewage from nearby farms enter the water. Fish are fewer there. Which explanation fits best?
  1. Fish are fewer only because they are old and have no food
  2. Extra nutrients let algae and weeds boom, which can reduce oxygen in the water and harm the fish
  3. More weeds means more food and oxygen, so the fish there are healthier
  4. The weeds grow thickly only because the water is the same everywhere along the river
Correct answer: B
Nutrient enrichment causes algae and weeds to boom. This can lower oxygen levels and harm fish, so more plants is not always a sign of a healthy ecosystem.
Use after: End of lesson — Exit ticket: prompt 2 on two reasons why plant density may differ between two parts of a habitat
Q7. On the school field, plant density is much lower on a heavily used footpath than beside a tap. Which is the best explanation?
  1. Trampling and compacted soil damage plants and limit root growth, while the soil beside the tap has more water
  2. Plants need no water, so the tap makes no difference to density
  3. Seeds cannot grow anywhere that the soil is wet
  4. Plants are spread evenly, so the difference must be due only to counting mistakes
Correct answer: A
Environmental conditions such as trampling, soil compaction and water availability affect plant populations. Plants are not spread evenly, and these factors can explain the difference in density.
