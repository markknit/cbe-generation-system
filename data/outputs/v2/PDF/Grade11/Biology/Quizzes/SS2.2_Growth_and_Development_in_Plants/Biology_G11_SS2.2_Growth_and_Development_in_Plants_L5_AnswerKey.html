Q1. On Mr. Wafula's farm, a maize seedling grows taller each week. Which part of the plant is responsible for making the shoot longer?
  1. The vascular cambium in the stem
  2. The pith in the centre of the stem
  3. The bark around the stem
  4. The apical meristem at the shoot tip
Correct answer: D
Primary growth, which lengthens shoots and roots, happens at the apical meristems. The vascular cambium is involved in thickening, not lengthening.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: examining the maize H614, young Rosecoco bean and Grevillea/Cypress stem sections
Q2. A student looks at a stem section under a microscope. The vascular bundles are scattered through the stem and there is no ring of cambium. Which stem is most likely being viewed?
  1. Older Rosecoco bean stem with rings
  2. Grevillea robusta (silky oak)
  3. Cypress
  4. Maize H614
Correct answer: D
Maize is a monocot with scattered vascular bundles and no vascular cambium. Grevillea, Cypress and bean are dicots or woody plants with cambium in a ring.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: drawing and labelling the bean and Grevillea stem sections (locating the cambium)
Q3. In a stem section of Grevillea robusta, where is the vascular cambium found?
  1. Outside the bark
  2. Between the xylem and the phloem
  3. In the centre of the pith
  4. Inside the epidermis only, away from the vascular tissues
Correct answer: B
The vascular cambium is a thin layer between the xylem and the phloem, which allows it to add xylem inward and phloem outward.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: layered paper/plasticine ring model of secondary growth in Grevillea
Q4. Each year, the vascular cambium of a Grevillea tree divides. What does it add, and what is the result?
  1. Secondary xylem outward and bark inward, making roots longer
  2. Only phloem, so the stem stretches without making wood
  3. Secondary xylem inward and secondary phloem outward, increasing girth and forming annual rings
  4. Primary xylem and primary phloem, making the tree taller
Correct answer: C
Cambial division adds secondary xylem on the inside and secondary phloem on the outside. This thickens the trunk and produces annual rings. The idea that thickening is only stretching is a misconception.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning on why maize H614 has a wider stem than Rosecoco bean though it has no wood
Q5. Maize H614 has no vascular cambium and makes no wood, yet its stem widened from 6 mm to 22 mm. How can this be explained?
  1. Maize makes annual rings inside the stem
  2. The stem only looked wider because it was stretched
  3. The cambium is hidden and makes wood slowly
  4. The stem thickened by primary growth, through cell division and enlargement behind the apical meristem
Correct answer: D
Maize thickens by primary growth: cells divide and enlarge behind the apical meristem. It has no cambium, no true secondary growth and no annual rings.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: using the width data (6, 14, 22 mm) as evidence in the CER statement
Q6. The width of a maize H614 stem was 6 mm on Day 14, 14 mm on Day 28 and 22 mm on Day 42. By how many millimetres did the stem widen between Day 14 and Day 42?
  1. 16 mm
  2. 8 mm
  3. 22 mm
  4. 28 mm
Correct answer: A
Increase in width = width on Day 42 minus width on Day 14 = 22 - 6 = 16 mm.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the Day 1 drawings of maize H614 and Rosecoco bean, then Gallery Walk
Q7. A group is revising its Day 1 model of how Rosecoco bean grows. Its stem was 2 mm, 4 mm and 6 mm wide on Days 14, 28 and 42. Which statement should it add?
  1. The bean stem is thin because it has no vascular bundles
  2. The bean stem has scattered bundles and no cambium, like maize
  3. The bean stem thickens a lot because its cambium is as active as in a Grevillea tree
  4. The bean stem has a cambium, but it is only slightly active at the base, so thickening is small
Correct answer: D
Bean is a dicot with a ring of bundles and a thin cambium. It is herbaceous, so its cambial activity is limited and its stem stays thin, unlike a woody Grevillea.
