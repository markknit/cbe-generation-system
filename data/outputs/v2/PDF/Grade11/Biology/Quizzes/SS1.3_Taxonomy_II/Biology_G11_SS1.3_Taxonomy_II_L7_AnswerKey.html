Use after: Predict phase — Predict: message to a visitor from Kisumu (what kind of statements would work best)
Q1. A visitor from Kisumu has never seen the animals at your school. You can only send a written message so that the visitor names the same animal as you. Which statement would work best in the message?
  1. It is a beautiful animal.
  2. It is a big animal.
  3. It has six jointed legs.
  4. It is a useful animal for food.
Correct answer: C
Six jointed legs is a feature anyone can see and count, so everyone reads it the same way. Big, beautiful and useful are opinions that differ between people.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: swapping keys and identifying mystery animals
Q2. A group builds this part of a dichotomous key. 1a. Animal has a backbone: go to step 2. 1b. Animal has no backbone: go to step 3. 2a. Feathers present: chicken. 2b. Feathers absent, hair present: goat. 3a. Six jointed legs present: grasshopper. 3b. No legs, body covered by a hard shell: snail. A tester has an animal with no backbone and six jointed legs. Which animal does the key lead to?
  1. Goat
  2. Grasshopper
  3. Chicken
  4. Snail
Correct answer: B
No backbone leads to step 3, and six jointed legs leads to grasshopper. Following one choice at each step gives one answer.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: group records where the key failed or was ambiguous and fixes it
Q3. A group wrote this couplet: 'Animal is small' / 'Animal is big'. After swapping keys, the testing group got different answers from the authors. What is the main problem with this couplet?
  1. Animals never differ in size, so the feature is useless.
  2. The couplet should name the animal in the first statement.
  3. A couplet must have three choices, not two.
  4. Two people can disagree about what small and big mean, because the statements are not measurable or countable.
Correct answer: D
Small and big depend on what each person compares with. A good couplet uses observable, countable features, such as a backbone or the number of legs, so every user reaches the same answer.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: compare with the prepared key and sort animals into Chordata or Arthropoda
Q4. Learners sort animals into Phylum Chordata or Phylum Arthropoda. Which list contains only Arthropoda?
  1. Chicken, lizard, grasshopper
  2. Butterfly, earthworm, grasshopper
  3. Mosquito, butterfly, grasshopper
  4. Mosquito, butterfly, snail
Correct answer: C
Mosquito, butterfly and grasshopper are insects with jointed legs, so they are Arthropoda. The snail and earthworm are neither Arthropoda nor Chordata. Chicken and lizard are Chordata.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: virus diagrams and reading, table of virus characteristics
Q5. Which statement correctly describes a virus?
  1. It has a nucleus and mitochondria, like the cells of a goat.
  2. It is a very small bacterium that divides on its own in soil.
  3. It is a non-cellular particle with a protein coat around DNA or RNA, and it reproduces only inside a host cell.
  4. It carries out respiration and growth by itself outside a host.
Correct answer: C
Viruses have no cell structure and no nucleus or mitochondria. They have genetic material (DNA or RNA) inside a protein coat and can reproduce only inside a host cell.
Use after: Explain phase — CER: 'Is a virus a living organism?'
Q6. A learner claims that a virus is not a living organism. Which piece of evidence best supports this claim?
  1. A virus has genetic material, DNA or RNA.
  2. A virus can cause disease in maize and in people.
  3. A virus is very small.
  4. A virus has no cell structure and cannot reproduce or carry out metabolism outside a host cell.
Correct answer: D
Living things show characteristics such as reproduction and metabolism on their own. A virus has no cells and depends on a host cell for these. Small size, causing disease and having genetic material do not show that something is non-living.
Use after: Explain phase — Teacher probe: correct the misconceptions 'viruses are tiny bacteria' and 'antibiotics kill viruses'
Q7. A health worker says that antibiotics will not cure measles. What is the correct reason?
  1. Measles is caused by a very large bacterium that resists antibiotics.
  2. Antibiotics only work on diseases of plants.
  3. Measles is not caused by any organism or particle.
  4. Measles is caused by a virus, which is not a bacterium, and antibiotics work against bacteria.
Correct answer: D
Viruses are non-cellular particles that live and reproduce inside host cells, and they are not bacteria. Antibiotics target bacteria, so vaccines are used to prevent virus diseases like measles.
Use after: Model Building phase — Revise the class model: add a box for viruses showing their importance in health, agriculture and biotechnology
Q8. Learners add a box for viruses to the class model. Which example shows a useful (beneficial) importance of viruses?
  1. Bacteriophages being used to attack harmful bacteria
  2. Cassava mosaic disease reducing cassava yields on Kenyan farms
  3. Foot and mouth disease in cattle
  4. Newcastle disease killing chickens
Correct answer: A
Bacteriophages are viruses that infect and destroy bacteria, so they can be used against harmful bacteria. The other three examples are harmful viral diseases of crops and livestock.
