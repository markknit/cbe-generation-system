Q1. A learner finds an empty, split insect skin on a maize leaf. A larger grasshopper is hanging beside it. Which statement describes what the learner OBSERVED?
  1. The insect peeled off its skin like sunburnt skin
  2. The skin stretched until it became the larger insect
  3. The insect melted and rebuilt itself inside the skin
  4. The empty skin is smaller than the insect beside it
Correct answer: D
Only the first statement is something that can be seen directly. The other three are untested ideas (predictions) about how the insect got bigger, not observations.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: Think-Pair-Share and recording common ideas on the board
Q2. In the lesson, learners are asked to write a prediction about the empty skin. At this point, before the observation stations, which response best fits the teacher's instructions for ideas such as 'it peels like sunburn'?
  1. Tell the learner at once that the idea is wrong and give the correct explanation
  2. Record the idea in the learner's own words without correcting it, and test it later with evidence
  3. Ignore the idea and move on to the next learner
  4. Remove the idea from the board because it is not scientific
Correct answer: B
The teacher records ideas in learners' own words and does not correct them, so later evidence creates a need to know.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Station 2 housefly larvae, pupae and adults, and sorting Sequence B
Q3. At the observation stations, learners study a housefly in its stages: maggot (larva), a motionless puparium (pupa) and an adult fly. Which stage sequence did they sort for the housefly?
  1. Egg, larva, pupa, adult
  2. Egg, pupa, larva, adult
  3. Egg, nymph, nymph, adult
  4. Larva, egg, adult, pupa
Correct answer: A
The housefly follows complete metamorphosis with four stages in this order: egg, larva, pupa, adult.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Station 1 live grasshopper nymphs of different sizes plus an exuvia
Q4. Learners watch a young grasshopper nymph with a hand lens and compare it with an adult grasshopper. Which observation did the lesson lead them to record?
  1. The nymph looks like a small adult
  2. The nymph has no legs, like a maggot
  3. The nymph is a motionless resting stage
  4. The nymph looks nothing like the adult
Correct answer: A
Grasshopper nymphs look like small adults, unlike housefly maggots, which look very different from adult flies.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: sorting life cycle cards into Sequence A and Sequence B
Q5. A group sorts cards for a grasshopper and puts a pupa card between the nymph and adult cards. Which question should the teacher ask, as in the lesson?
  1. Why did you get this wrong?
  2. What evidence made you put this card here?
  3. Who in the group made this mistake?
  4. Can you copy the answer from another group?
Correct answer: B
The teacher does not correct sorting errors directly but asks learners what evidence they used, so that they can recheck their own thinking.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: writing the first Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) statement
Q6. A group has these observations: the housefly has a motionless pupa stage, but the grasshopper has no pupa stage. Which claim do these observations best support?
  1. All insects grow smoothly without changing shape
  2. Insects follow two developmental pathways
  3. All insects have exactly four life stages
  4. Insects grow by peeling like sunburnt skin
Correct answer: B
One insect has a pupa (4 stages) and the other has none (3 stages). This supports two pathways: complete and incomplete metamorphosis.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: building the comparative model with grasshopper and housefly columns
Q7. How many stages are in the life cycle of a grasshopper (incomplete metamorphosis), and how many are in the life cycle of a housefly (complete metamorphosis)?
  1. Grasshopper 3 stages, housefly 4 stages
  2. Grasshopper 4 stages, housefly 4 stages
  3. Grasshopper 3 stages, housefly 3 stages
  4. Grasshopper 4 stages, housefly 3 stages
Correct answer: A
Incomplete metamorphosis has egg, nymph, adult (3). Complete metamorphosis has egg, larva, pupa, adult (4).
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: writing two questions on sticky notes and clustering them
Q8. Which question about the empty skin is the best kind of question to post on the Driving Question Board (DQB)?
  1. Is the skin on a maize leaf?
  2. Did the insect leave the skin?
  3. Is the skin crunchy?
  4. How does an insect become bigger than its old hard skin?
Correct answer: D
A good DQB question starts with how or why and cannot be answered with yes or no.
Use after: End of lesson — Model: bridging question and exit ticket
Q9. At the end of the lesson, which question is still NOT explained and is left for the next lessons?
  1. Which insect has a larva stage?
  2. How many stages does a grasshopper have?
  3. Why does a grasshopper grow in sudden jumps instead of a little each day?
  4. Does a housefly have a pupa stage?
Correct answer: C
Learners have identified the stages and pathways, but the reason for growth in sudden jumps is the open question for Lesson 2.
