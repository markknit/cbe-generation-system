Q1. A student stands 40 m from the base of a mast and measures the angle from the ground up to the top as 50°. The 40 m is the side next to the 50° angle, and the mast's height is the side across from it. Which ratio links the 50° angle to the height?
  1. tan 50°, because it connects the opposite side and the adjacent side
  2. cos 50°, because 40 m is the side next to the angle
  3. tan 40°, because the student stands 40 m away
  4. sin 50°, because the height is the longest side of the triangle
Correct answer: A
The height is the opposite side and the 40 m is the adjacent side. The ratio opposite/adjacent is tan, and it does not involve the hypotenuse.
Use after: Observe phase — Part: switching from the 40° angle to the 50° angle and re-labelling O, A, H
Q2. In triangle ABC the right angle is at B, AB = 5 cm, BC = 12 cm and AC = 13 cm. When you look from angle C, which side is the OPPOSITE side?
  1. BC (12 cm), because the opposite side stays the same when you change the reference angle
  2. AC (13 cm)
  3. AB (5 cm)
  4. BC (12 cm)
Correct answer: C
The opposite side is directly across from the chosen angle. Across from C is AB. BC is adjacent to C, and AC is the hypotenuse. Switching the reference angle swaps the opposite and adjacent labels.
Use after: Observe phase — Class table on the board (angle, sin, cos, tan): checking the values pairs recorded
Q3. Learners computed ratios from right-angled triangles for different acute angles. Which of these values could NOT be the sine of an acute angle?
  1. 1.41
  2. 0.5
  3. 0.766
  4. 0.643
Correct answer: A
Sine is opposite ÷ hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is the longest side, so sin of an acute angle is always less than 1. A value of 1.41 is an error, for example from adding sin 40° and cos 40°.
Use after: Explain phase — SOH-CAH-TOA definitions copied into books
Q4. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm. For the angle θ, the side next to it (adjacent) is 6 cm and the side across from it (opposite) is 8 cm. What is cos θ?
  1. 0.75
  2. 0.8
  3. 0.6
  4. 1.33
Correct answer: C
cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse = 6/10 = 0.6. The value 0.8 is sin θ (O/H), 0.75 is A/O, and 1.33 is tan θ (O/A).
Use after: Explain phase — Guided example: the Nakuru mast problem, height = 40 × tan 50°
Q5. A student stands 40 m from the base of a mast and measures the angle of the top as 50°. Using tan 50° ≈ 1.192, what is the height of the mast, to 1 decimal place?
  1. 33.6 m
  2. 47.7 m
  3. 30.6 m
  4. 25.7 m
Correct answer: B
The 40 m is the adjacent side and the height is the opposite side, so tan 50° = height/40. Height = 40 × 1.192 ≈ 47.7 m. Dividing 40 by 1.192 is a common error.
Use after: Explain phase — Independent practice (a): Kisumu tree problem using tan
Q6. A surveyor in Kisumu stands 25 m from the base of a tree. She measures the angle of elevation of the top as 38°. Given tan 38° ≈ 0.781, what is the height of the tree, to 1 decimal place?
  1. 32.0 m
  2. 19.5 m
  3. 15.4 m
  4. 25.8 m
Correct answer: B
The 25 m is the adjacent side and the tree's height is the opposite side, so height = 25 × tan 38° = 25 × 0.781 ≈ 19.5 m. Dividing 25 by 0.781 gives the wrong answer, 32.0 m.
Use after: Explain phase — Independent practice (b): back-calculating θ from sin θ = 0.643 using tables
Q7. In a right-angled triangle, sin θ = 0.643 for an acute angle θ. The sine table shows that sin 40° = 0.643. What is the angle θ?
  1. 0.643°
  2. 64.3°
  3. 40°
  4. 50°
Correct answer: C
Find 0.643 in the sine column of the table and read the angle beside it, which is 40°. The ratio value is not the angle itself.
