Q1. A student stands 40 m from the base of a mast on level ground. She measures the angle of elevation of the top of the mast as 50°. Which calculation gives the height of the mast?
  1. h = 40 × sin 50° ≈ 30.6 m
  2. h = 40 × tan 50° ≈ 47.7 m
  3. h = 40 × cos 50° ≈ 25.7 m
  4. h = 40 ÷ tan 50° ≈ 33.6 m
Correct answer: B
The 40 m side is adjacent to 50° and the height is opposite, so tan 50° = h ÷ 40 and h = 40 × tan 50° ≈ 40 × 1.1918 ≈ 47.7 m.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Phase: worked example, Step 2 (identify known and unknown sides) and Step 3 (select ratio)
Q2. In the mast problem, the observer is 40 m from the base and the angle of elevation is 50°. Why is the tangent ratio the correct one to use?
  1. The 40 m side is opposite 50° and the height is adjacent to it.
  2. Tangent is always the ratio used for any height problem.
  3. The 40 m side is adjacent to 50° and the height is opposite 50°, and tangent links opposite and adjacent.
  4. The 40 m side is the hypotenuse, so tangent is needed.
Correct answer: C
Relative to the 50° angle, the known 40 m side is adjacent and the unknown height is opposite. Tangent is the only ratio that links these two sides, and the hypotenuse is not needed.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: Lake Victoria lighthouse problem, Diagram 1 (22° depression equals 22° elevation)
Q3. A coastguard officer stands at the top of a cliff and measures the angle of depression of a boat on the lake as 22°. What is the angle of elevation of the cliff top as seen from the boat, and why?
  1. 68°, because the two angles add up to 90°.
  2. 22°, because the angle of depression at the top and the angle of elevation at the boat are alternate angles between parallel lines.
  3. 22°, because the boat and the officer are at the same height.
  4. 158°, because the two angles lie on a straight line and add up to 180°.
Correct answer: B
The horizontal line at the cliff top is parallel to the water level. The angle of depression and the angle of elevation at the boat are alternate angles, so they are equal.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: Lake Victoria problem, part (a) height of the cliff above the water
Q4. A boat is 120 m horizontally from the foot of a cliff. The angle of elevation of the top of the cliff from the boat is 22°. What is the height of the cliff above the water, to one decimal place?
  1. 120 ÷ tan 22° ≈ 297.0 m
  2. 120 × tan 22° ≈ 48.5 m
  3. 120 × tan 38° ≈ 93.8 m
  4. 120 × sin 22° ≈ 45.0 m
Correct answer: B
The 120 m side is adjacent and the cliff height is opposite 22°, so h = 120 × tan 22° ≈ 120 × 0.4040 ≈ 48.5 m.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: Lake Victoria problem, part (c) height of the lighthouse itself
Q5. From a boat 120 m from the foot of a cliff, the angle of elevation of the top of the cliff is 22° and the angle of elevation of the top of a lighthouse standing on the cliff is 38°. The cliff is about 48.5 m high and the top of the lighthouse is about 93.8 m above the water. What is the height of the lighthouse itself, from the top of the cliff to its top?
  1. 45.3 m
  2. 142.3 m
  3. 93.8 m
  4. 48.5 m
Correct answer: A
The lighthouse height is the difference of the two heights: 120 tan 38° − 120 tan 22° ≈ 93.8 − 48.5 ≈ 45.3 m. The value 93.8 m is measured from the water, not from the cliff top.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building Phase: Final Model annotation of the complementary-angle relationship sin θ = cos(90° − θ)
Q6. A learner knows that sin 35° ≈ 0.574. Using the complementary-angle relationship, which angle has a cosine equal to sin 35°?
  1. 55°
  2. 145°
  3. 65°
  4. 35°
Correct answer: A
sin θ = cos(90° − θ), so sin 35° = cos(90° − 35°) = cos 55°.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building Phase: Final Model with SOH-CAH-TOA ratios and the identity tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
Q7. For an acute angle θ, sin θ = 0.6 and cos θ = 0.8. Using the identity tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ, what is tan θ?
  1. 1.33
  2. 1.4
  3. 0.48
  4. 0.75
Correct answer: D
tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ = 0.6 ÷ 0.8 = 0.75. The value 1.33 comes from dividing the wrong way round.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building Phase: Final Model special-angle table (30°, 45°, 60°)
Q8. A surveyor stands 30 m from the foot of a tower on level ground. The angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 45°. Using the exact value of tan 45°, what is the height of the tower?
  1. 15 m
  2. 30 m
  3. 52.0 m
  4. 21.2 m
Correct answer: B
tan 45° = 1 exactly, so h = 30 × tan 45° = 30 × 1 = 30 m.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB Review: answering the unit driving question on the Driving Question Board
Q9. Which statement best answers the unit driving question: "How can we use the angles and one side of a right-angled triangle to find any unknown height or distance?"
  1. Use Pythagoras' theorem, because it works for every height problem.
  2. Choose the trigonometric ratio (sine, cosine or tangent) that links the known angle and known side to the unknown side, then solve for it.
  3. Always use the sine ratio, whichever sides are known.
  4. Estimate the height by eye and check it with a ladder.
Correct answer: B
The unit shows that a known acute angle and one known side let us choose sin, cos or tan to find an unknown side. Pythagoras needs two sides, and no single ratio fits every case.
