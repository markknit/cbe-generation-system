Use after: Observe phase — Part 1: pouring sand or water from the cone model into the cylinder model (r = 7 cm, h = 12 cm)
Q1. A hollow cone and a hollow cylinder have the same radius and the same vertical height. The cone is filled to the brim with sand and poured into the cylinder again and again until the cylinder is full. How many cone-fuls of sand fill the cylinder?
  1. 4 cone-fuls
  2. 6 cone-fuls
  3. 3 cone-fuls
  4. 2 cone-fuls
Correct answer: C
A cone holds one third of a cylinder with the same base and height, so 3 cone-fuls fill the cylinder. In the lesson this was seen with r = 7 cm, h = 12 cm.
Use after: Observe phase — Part 1: calculating cylinder volume 1,848 cm³ and cone volume 616 cm³ for the matching models
Q2. A cone has radius 7 cm and vertical height 12 cm. Using π = 22/7, what is its volume?
  1. 88 cm³
  2. 1,848 cm³
  3. 924 cm³
  4. 616 cm³
Correct answer: D
V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓ × 22/7 × 7 × 7 × 12 = 616 cm³. Leaving out the ⅓ gives 1,848 cm³, which is the cylinder volume.
Use after: Observe phase — Part 1: checking that the cone and cylinder have the same radius and the same vertical height, not slant height
Q3. A cone has radius 7 cm, vertical height 12 cm and slant height about 13.9 cm. Which measurement of the cone is used for h in the formula V = ⅓πr²h?
  1. The diameter, 14 cm, because it is the widest part
  2. The sum of the two heights, 25.9 cm
  3. The slant height, 13.9 cm, because it runs along the cone's surface
  4. The vertical height, 12 cm, measured straight from the tip to the centre of the base
Correct answer: D
Volume uses the vertical (perpendicular) height, not the slant height. The prompt card in the lesson says: 'Volume uses h, not l.'
Use after: Observe phase — Part 2: checking the football volume (r = 10.5 cm) by arithmetic
Q4. A football is a sphere of radius 10.5 cm. Using V = 4/3πr³ and π = 22/7, what is its volume?
  1. 3,638.25 cm³
  2. 4,851 cm³
  3. 462 cm³
  4. 14,553 cm³
Correct answer: B
V = 4/3 × 22/7 × 10.5 × 10.5 × 10.5 = 4,851 cm³. Leaving out the 4/3 gives 3,638.25 cm³, and using r² instead of r³ gives 462 cm³.
Use after: Observe phase — Part 2: Turkana water tank frustum, big cone minus small cone
Q5. A water tank is shaped like a frustum. A complete big cone would have volume 12.936 m³. The small cone cut off from the top has volume 1.617 m³. What is the volume of the frustum?
  1. 11.319 m³
  2. 14.553 m³
  3. 1.617 m³
  4. 8 m³
Correct answer: A
Frustum = big cone − small cone = 12.936 − 1.617 = 11.319 m³ (11,319 litres). Adding the volumes is a common mistake.
Use after: Explain phase — Step (2): calculating the drum volume with V = πr²h
Q6. A cylindrical drum has radius 35 cm and height 105 cm. Using π = 22/7, what is the volume of the drum?
  1. 404,250 cm³
  2. 30,800 cm³
  3. 322,500 cm³
  4. 134,750 cm³
Correct answer: A
V = πr²h = 22/7 × 35 × 35 × 105 = 404,250 cm³. Dividing by 3 is the cone rule and does not apply to a cylinder.
Use after: Explain phase — Step (3): comparing the drum and box volumes and finding the difference in litres
Q7. The drum holds 404,250 cm³ and the box holds 322,500 cm³. 1,000 cm³ is 1 litre. How many more litres of maize does the drum hold than the box?
  1. 81.75 litres
  2. 726.75 litres
  3. 8.175 litres
  4. 81,750 litres
Correct answer: A
404,250 − 322,500 = 81,750 cm³. Dividing by 1,000 gives 81.75 litres.
Use after: Explain phase — Step (4): CER explanation for Mzee Kiplagat, distinguishing surface area from volume
Q8. Wanjiru used 30,800 cm² of iron sheet for a drum and also 30,800 cm² for a box. The drum holds 404,250 cm³ and the box holds 322,500 cm³. Which statement best explains this?
  1. The box holds less because boxes are always smaller than drums, whatever the shape.
  2. Surface area measures the sheet covering a solid and volume measures the space inside, so equal surface areas can give different volumes when the shapes differ.
  3. Surface area and volume measure the same thing, so the drum must have used more sheet.
  4. Equal surface area always means equal volume, so one of the volumes must have been measured wrongly.
Correct answer: B
Surface area is the material covering a solid and volume is the space inside. The shape decides how much space the same amount of material encloses, so the drum holds more.
