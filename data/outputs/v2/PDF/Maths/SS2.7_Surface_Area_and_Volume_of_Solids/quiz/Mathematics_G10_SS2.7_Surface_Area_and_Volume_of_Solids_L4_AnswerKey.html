Use after: Observe phase — Station A (sphere): finding r of the football from its circumference of about 66 cm
Q1. A football has a circumference of 66 cm. Using π = 22/7, what is its radius?
  1. 33 cm
  2. 10.5 cm
  3. 5.25 cm
  4. 21 cm
Correct answer: B
Circumference = 2πr, so r = 66 ÷ (2 × 22/7) = 10.5 cm. 21 cm is the diameter, and 33 cm is half the circumference.
Use after: Observe phase — Station A (sphere): covering half the ball with paper strips and counting circles' worth
Q2. Learners cover a ball with paper strips, peel them off and lay them flat. They find the strips from the whole ball cover about the same area as circles of the same radius as the ball. About how many such circles are needed?
  1. 3 circles
  2. 6 circles
  3. 2 circles
  4. 4 circles
Correct answer: D
Half the ball used about 2 circles' worth of strips, so the whole ball needs about 4. This is why the sphere's surface area is 4πr².
Use after: Explain phase — Part 1: claim 'SA of a sphere is 4 times the area of its great circle' and 4πr² = 1,386 cm²
Q3. A football has radius 10.5 cm. Using π = 22/7, what is its surface area (SA = 4πr²)?
  1. 132 cm²
  2. 693 cm²
  3. 346.5 cm²
  4. 1,386 cm²
Correct answer: D
SA = 4 × 22/7 × 10.5 × 10.5 = 1,386 cm². This is 4 × 346.5, the area of 4 great circles. 346.5 is only one circle, and 132 comes from forgetting to square r.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B (composite solid): computing the curved hemisphere area of the Nairobi packaging container
Q4. What is the curved surface area of a hemisphere of radius 7 cm (not counting the flat circular face)? Use π = 22/7.
  1. 616 cm²
  2. 308 cm²
  3. 462 cm²
  4. 154 cm²
Correct answer: B
A hemisphere's curved surface is half of a sphere's: 2πr² = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 308 cm². 616 is the whole sphere, and 154 is only one circle's area.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B (composite solid): marking the joined circle (r = 7 cm) as 'hidden'
Q5. A hemisphere is joined on top of a cylinder, and both have the same radius. A learner is finding the area of the outside surface that needs covering. What should be done about the circle where the two solids meet?
  1. Count it only if the cylinder has no base
  2. Count it once, because it belongs to the cylinder
  3. Leave it out, because it is hidden inside the joined solid and needs no covering
  4. Count it twice, once for each solid
Correct answer: C
The circle where the solids join is inside the composite solid, so it is not part of the outside surface. Only the visible parts are added.
Use after: Explain phase — Part 2: finishing the Nairobi container, 440 + 154 + 308 = 902 cm²
Q6. A container is a cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 10 cm with a closed base. A hemisphere of radius 7 cm sits on top of it. Using π = 22/7, what is the total outside surface area? (Curved cylinder wall = 440 cm², base circle = 154 cm², curved hemisphere = 308 cm².)
  1. 1,056 cm²
  2. 902 cm²
  3. 594 cm²
  4. 748 cm²
Correct answer: B
Add the visible parts only: 440 + 154 + 308 = 902 cm². 1,056 wrongly counts the hidden joined circle (154 cm²) a second time. 748 leaves out the base, and 594 leaves out the hemisphere.
Use after: Explain phase — Part 3: checking the method on the drum (r = 35 cm, h = 105 cm, closed), TSA = 30,800 cm²
Q7. A closed cylindrical drum has radius 35 cm and height 105 cm. Using π = 22/7, what is its total surface area?
  1. 23,100 cm²
  2. 26,950 cm²
  3. 7,700 cm²
  4. 30,800 cm²
Correct answer: D
Two circular faces: 2πr² = 2 × 22/7 × 35 × 35 = 7,700 cm². Curved wall: 2πrh = 2 × 22/7 × 35 × 105 = 23,100 cm². Total = 30,800 cm². Using only the curved wall, or only one base, gives the wrong totals.
Use after: End of lesson — Part 3 / Model: 'Both containers use the same sheet, so surface area does not tell us capacity'
Q8. A closed drum and a rectangular box are each made from 30,800 cm² of iron sheet, but the drum holds more maize. What does this show?
  1. The maize grains fit better in a rectangular box, so the box is wasting space
  2. The box must have been measured wrongly, since equal sheet should give equal capacity
  3. The drum must have a larger surface area than the box
  4. Surface area measures the material used, not the space inside, so equal surface area does not mean equal capacity
Correct answer: D
Surface area tells us how much sheet was used. Capacity depends on the shape, so two solids with the same surface area can hold different amounts.
