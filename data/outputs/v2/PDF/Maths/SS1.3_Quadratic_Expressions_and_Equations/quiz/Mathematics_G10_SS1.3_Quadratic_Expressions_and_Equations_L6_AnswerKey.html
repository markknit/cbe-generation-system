Q1. Mama Wanjiru's rectangular garden has a length that is 3 m more than its width. A garden 8 m wide has an area of 88 m², and a garden 10 m wide has an area of 130 m². She wants an area of exactly 108 m². What can you say about the width she needs?
  1. It is more than 10 m
  2. It is less than 8 m
  3. It cannot be estimated without more tables
  4. It is between 8 m and 10 m
Correct answer: D
The area rises as the width rises. 108 m² lies between 88 m² and 130 m², so the width must lie between 8 m and 10 m.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Step 1: write x(x + 3) = 108, expand and rearrange to standard form
Q2. The width of the garden is x m and the length is (x + 3) m. The area must be 108 m². Which is the equation written in standard form, equal to zero?
  1. x² + 3 − 108 = 0
  2. x² − 3x − 108 = 0
  3. x² + 3x − 108 = 0
  4. x² + 3x + 108 = 0
Correct answer: C
x(x + 3) = 108 expands to x² + 3x = 108. Subtracting 108 from both sides gives x² + 3x − 108 = 0.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Step 2: factorise using the sum-and-product method and check by expanding
Q3. Which is the correct factorised form of x² + 3x − 108? (Hint: find two numbers with sum 3 and product −108.)
  1. (x − 12)(x + 9)
  2. (x + 12)(x − 9)
  3. (x + 12)(x + 9)
  4. (x + 18)(x − 6)
Correct answer: B
12 + (−9) = 3 and 12 × (−9) = −108, so the factors are (x + 12)(x − 9). The other choices have the wrong sum or the wrong product.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Step 3: 'Mystery product cards' and zero-product property (product = 0 compared with product = 108)
Q4. A learner is solving (x + 12)(x − 9) = 108. She says, "So x + 12 = 108 or x − 9 = 108." Why is this wrong?
  1. Because the brackets must always be added, not multiplied
  2. Because x − 9 can never equal 108
  3. Because only a product equal to 0 tells us that one factor must be 0, and many pairs of numbers multiply to give 108
  4. Because 108 is not a multiple of 12
Correct answer: C
The zero-product property works only when the product is 0. Many pairs, such as 4 × 27 or 9 × 12, multiply to give 108, so neither bracket has to equal 108. That is why we rearrange to zero first.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'Which answer is the width, and how do you know?' and the grid-paper 9 m by 12 m garden
Q5. Solving x(x + 3) = 108 for the width x m of Mama Wanjiru's garden gives x = 9 or x = −12. Both values give an area of 108 when substituted into x(x + 3). Which statement is correct?
  1. The garden is 12 m wide and 9 m long, because −12 means 12 m
  2. The garden is 9 m wide and 12 m long, and x = −12 is rejected because a width cannot be negative
  3. Neither answer can be used, because the equation has two solutions
  4. The garden can be either 9 m wide or −12 m wide, because both answers work
Correct answer: B
9 × 12 = 108 and 12 = 9 + 3, so the width is 9 m and the length is 12 m. x = −12 satisfies the equation as arithmetic but cannot be a width, so we reject it using the context.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: trying x = 12 as a width (length 15, area 180) on grid paper
Q6. A learner looks at (x + 12)(x − 9) = 0 and says, "The 12 in the bracket is the width." Suppose the width of the garden were 12 m and the length 3 m more than the width. What would the area be?
  1. 36 m²
  2. 180 m²
  3. 144 m²
  4. 108 m²
Correct answer: B
If the width is 12 m, the length is 12 + 3 = 15 m. The area is 12 × 15 = 180 m², not 108 m², so 12 is not the width.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: testing the revised cumulative model 'Solving for a target area' with a new target of 70 m²
Q7. For a different garden, the width is x m and the length is (x + 3) m. The equation for the target area is x² + 3x − 70 = 0, which factorises as (x + 10)(x − 7) = 0. What is the width of this garden in metres?
  1. 7
  2. 10
  3. 3
  4. −10
Correct answer: A
By the zero-product property, x = −10 or x = 7. A width cannot be negative, so x = 7 m. The length is 7 + 3 = 10 m and the area is 7 × 10 = 70 m².
