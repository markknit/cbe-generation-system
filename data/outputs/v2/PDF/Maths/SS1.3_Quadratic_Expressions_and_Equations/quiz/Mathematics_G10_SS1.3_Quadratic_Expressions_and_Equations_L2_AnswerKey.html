Q1. Mama Wanjiru's garden is 9 m wide and 12 m long. Wafula says: "12² = (10 + 2)² = 10² + 2² = 104." What is wrong with his working?
  1. 12 should be written as 10 × 2.
  2. He missed the two middle strips, each 10 × 2, so (10 + 2)² is not 10² + 2².
  3. He did not use a calculator, so the answer cannot be trusted.
  4. 10² should be 20, not 100.
Correct answer: B
A square of side 10 + 2 is made of a 10 × 10 square, two 10 × 2 strips and a 2 × 2 square. Wafula left out the two strips (2 × 10 × 2 = 40), so 144, not 104, is the correct answer.

Q2. Learners fit these paper tiles together to make one big square: a 10 cm by 10 cm square, two 10 cm by 2 cm strips and a 2 cm by 2 cm square. What is the total area of the big square?
  1. 140 cm²
  2. 144 cm²
  3. 104 cm²
  4. 124 cm²
Correct answer: B
Add the pieces: 100 + 20 + 20 + 4 = 144 cm². This matches 12 × 12 = 144, because the side is 10 + 2 = 12 cm.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Part B: (a − b)² cut-out square, overlapping corner
Q3. A 10 cm by 10 cm square has a 2 cm wide strip cut away along its top edge and a 2 cm wide strip cut away along its right edge, leaving an 8 cm by 8 cm square. When we work out 100 − 20 − 20 + 4, why is the 4 added back?
  1. The 2 × 2 corner was never part of the original square.
  2. The 8 cm square needs 4 cm² extra to be complete.
  3. Every subtraction must be followed by adding the square of the small side without any reason.
  4. The 2 × 2 corner was removed twice, once with each strip, so it is added back once.
Correct answer: D
Each 10 × 2 strip includes the 2 × 2 corner, so cutting both strips takes the corner away twice. Adding 4 back once gives 100 − 40 + 4 = 64 cm², which equals 8 × 8.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: writing (a + b)² and (a − b)² as sums of pieces
Q4. The big square has side a + b and the cut-out square has side a − b (with a bigger than b). Which pair of identities is correct?
  1. (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² and (a − b)² = a² − 2ab − b²
  2. (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² and (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
  3. (a + b)² = a² + b² and (a − b)² = a² − b²
  4. (a + b)² = a² + ab + b² and (a − b)² = a² − ab + b²
Correct answer: B
The big square is a² + ab + ab + b² = a² + 2ab + b². In the cut-out square the two strips 2ab are removed and b² is added back once, giving a² − 2ab + b².
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: testing the identities on numbers (12², 8², 10², and other values of a and b)
Q5. Use (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b² with a = 10 and b = 3 to work out (10 − 3)². What is the answer?
  1. 91
  2. 109
  3. 31
  4. 49
Correct answer: D
(10 − 3)² = 100 − 2 × 10 × 3 + 9 = 100 − 60 + 9 = 49, which equals 7².
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: whiteboard quick check "Expand (x + 3)²"
Q6. Which is the correct expansion of (x + 3)²?
  1. x² + 3x + 9
  2. x² − 6x + 9
  3. x² + 9
  4. x² + 6x + 9
Correct answer: D
Using (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² with a = x and b = 3 gives x² + 2(3)x + 9 = x² + 6x + 9. The middle term 2ab = 6x must not be left out.

Q7. The square of side 10 has area 100. The square of side 12 has area 144. Where does the extra area come from when we build the 12 by 12 square from the 10 by 10 square?
  1. Only a 2 × 2 square is added, giving 4 more.
  2. Two 10 × 2 strips are added, giving 40 more, and nothing else.
  3. Two 10 × 2 strips and a 2 × 2 corner are added, giving 40 + 4 = 44 more.
  4. Two 2 × 2 squares are added, giving 8 more.
Correct answer: C
144 − 100 = 44. This is the two strips (2 × 10 × 2 = 40) plus the 2 × 2 corner (4), which are the 2ab and b² parts of the identity.
