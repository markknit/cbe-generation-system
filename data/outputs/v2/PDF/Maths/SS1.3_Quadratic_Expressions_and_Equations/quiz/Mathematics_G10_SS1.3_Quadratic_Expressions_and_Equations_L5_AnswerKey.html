Use after: Predict phase — Predict: learners write a first guess such as (2x + 3)(x + 1) and say how to check it
Q1. A learner guesses that 2x² + 7x + 3 factorises as (2x + 3)(x + 1). What happens when this guess is checked by expansion?
  1. It expands to 2x² + 4x + 3, so the guess is wrong
  2. It expands to 2x² + 7x + 3, so the guess is correct
  3. It expands to 2x² + 5x + 3, so the guess is wrong
  4. It expands to x² + 5x + 3, so the guess is wrong
Correct answer: C
(2x + 3)(x + 1) = 2x² + 2x + 3x + 3 = 2x² + 5x + 3. The middle term is 5x, not 7x, so the guess fails the expansion check.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B (grouping): splitting the middle term of 2x² + 7x + 3 with ac = 6
Q2. To factorise 2x² + 7x + 3 by grouping, you find ac = 2 × 3 = 6. Which split of the middle term 7x should you use?
  1. 6x + x
  2. 5x + 2x
  3. 2x + 3x
  4. 3x + 4x
Correct answer: A
The two numbers must multiply to ac = 6 and add to b = 7. Only 6 and 1 do this, giving 2x² + 6x + x + 3. The pair 5 and 2 adds to 7 but multiplies to 10, and 2 and 3 multiplies to 6 but adds to 5.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B (grouping): taking out common factors 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)
Q3. A learner has reached 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3). What is the full factorised form?
  1. 2x(x + 3)
  2. (2x + 3)(x + 1)
  3. 2x(x + 3) + x
  4. (2x + 1)(x + 3)
Correct answer: D
(x + 3) is a common factor of both terms. Taking it out leaves 2x + 1, so the result is (2x + 1)(x + 3). The 1 in the second group must not be dropped.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C (special cases): testing x² + 10x + 25 by tiles and by sum and product
Q4. Which is the correct factorisation of x² + 10x + 25?
  1. (x + 25)(x + 1)
  2. (x + 5)(x − 5)
  3. (x + 5)²
  4. (x + 10)(x + 15)
Correct answer: C
5 × 5 = 25 and 5 + 5 = 10, so x² + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)(x + 5) = (x + 5)². This is a perfect square, (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b². (x + 5)(x − 5) would give x² − 25.
Use after: Explain phase — Pair work: factorising 3x² + 10x + 3 (ac = 9, numbers 9 and 1)
Q5. A learner factorises 3x² + 10x + 3 using grouping. Which answer is correct?
  1. (3x − 1)(x − 3)
  2. (3x + 3)(x + 1)
  3. (x + 9)(x + 1)
  4. (3x + 1)(x + 3)
Correct answer: D
ac = 3 × 3 = 9, and 9 and 1 add to 10. So 3x² + 9x + x + 3 = 3x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = (3x + 1)(x + 3). Expanding (3x + 1)(x + 3) gives 3x² + 10x + 3.
Use after: Explain phase — Connecting back to the phenomenon: grouping x² + 3x − 108 into (x + 12)(x − 9)
Q6. Mama Wanjiru's garden expression is x² + 3x − 108. To factorise it by grouping, you need two numbers that multiply to −108 and add to 3. Which split of 3x is correct?
  1. 12x and −9x
  2. −12x and −9x
  3. 9x and −12x
  4. 12x and 9x
Correct answer: A
12 × (−9) = −108 and 12 + (−9) = 3. So x² + 12x − 9x − 108 = x(x + 12) − 9(x + 12) = (x − 9)(x + 12), the same brackets that sum and product give.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB review: choosing a method for 5x² − 20 and stating the class rules of thumb
Q7. A learner must factorise 5x² − 20. Which is the best first step?
  1. Take out the common factor 5 to get 5(x² − 4), then use the difference of two squares
  2. Say it cannot be factorised because there is no middle term
  3. Split the middle term into 5x and −4x
  4. Use sum and product, because the x² term is there
Correct answer: A
Always look for a common factor or a special case first. 5x² − 20 = 5(x² − 4) = 5(x − 2)(x + 2). A missing middle term does not stop factorising; it points to a difference of two squares.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative model revision: linking x² + 3x − 108 = (x + 12)(x − 9) to the garden's length and width
Q8. Mama Wanjiru's garden has an area of 108 m² and its length is 3 m more than its width. Since x² + 3x − 108 = (x + 12)(x − 9), which dimensions fit the garden?
  1. 6 m wide and 18 m long
  2. 8 m wide and 11 m long
  3. 9 m wide and 12 m long
  4. 10 m wide and 13 m long
Correct answer: C
The numbers 12 and 9 in the brackets are the length and width, and 9 × 12 = 108 with 12 being 3 more than 9. The 8 m by 11 m garden has area 88 m² and the 10 m by 13 m garden has area 130 m². The 6 m by 18 m garden has area 108 m² but its length is 12 m more than its width, not 3 m.
