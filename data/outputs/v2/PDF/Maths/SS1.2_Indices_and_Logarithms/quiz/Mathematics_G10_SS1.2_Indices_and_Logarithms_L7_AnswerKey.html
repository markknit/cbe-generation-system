Q1. In the SACCO loan problem, a learner wants to find log(1.045²). Using the product law log(a × b) = log a + log b, which result is correct?
  1. log 1.045 + 2
  2. (log 1.045)²
  3. 2 × log 1.045
  4. log 2.045
Correct answer: C
1.045² = 1.045 × 1.045, so log(1.045²) = log 1.045 + log 1.045 = 2 × log 1.045. This repeated addition is where the n in the power law comes from.

Q2. A Kericho SACCO member borrows Ksh 50,000 at 4.5% compound interest per annum for 12 years. Using the power law, log(1.045¹²) = 12 × log 1.045. From the four-figure tables, log 1.045 = 0.0191. What is log(1.045¹²)?
  1. 0.0016
  2. 0.2292
  3. 12.0191
  4. 1.2191
Correct answer: B
Power law: log(1.045¹²) = 12 × 0.0191 = 0.2292. Adding 12 to the log (instead of multiplying) or dividing by 12 are common errors.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher-led SACCO working, Steps 3 and 4 (antilog and final answer in Ksh)
Q3. A SACCO member borrows Ksh 50,000 at 4.5% compound interest per annum. After 12 years the amount owed is A = 50,000 × 1.045¹². Using tables, log(1.045¹²) = 0.2292 and antilog 0.2292 ≈ 1.695. What is the amount owed, to the nearest Ksh 10?
  1. Ksh 84,750
  2. Ksh 50,001.70
  3. Ksh 77,000
  4. Ksh 8,475
Correct answer: A
1.045¹² ≈ 1.695, so A = 50,000 × 1.695 = Ksh 84,750. Ksh 77,000 comes from treating the interest as simple interest (50,000 × 0.045 × 12 = 27,000, added to 50,000).

Q4. A square plot in Nakuru County has an area of 8,464 m². The side length is √8,464. Using the root law, log(√8,464) = (1/2) × log 8,464. If log 8,464 = 3.9276, what is log(√8,464)?
  1. 1.9638
  2. 7.8552
  3. 1.9276
  4. 0.9638
Correct answer: A
The square root means one half of the log: 3.9276 ÷ 2 = 1.9638. Doubling the log would give the log of 8,464², not of its square root.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: independent root-law working, Step 1 (find log 8,464 and its characteristic)
Q5. A learner wants to find log 8,464 from four-figure tables. What is the characteristic of log 8,464?
  1. 4
  2. 3
  3. 0
  4. 2
Correct answer: B
8,464 has 4 digits before the decimal point, so the characteristic is 4 − 1 = 3. Writing 2 or 4 shows a miscount of digits or a wrong rule.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: independent root-law working, Steps 3 and 4 (antilog and state answer with units)
Q6. A surveyor finds that the logarithm of the side of a square plot of area 8,464 m² is 1.9638. Antilog 1.9638 gives a number with digits 92.0. Which is the side length of the plot?
  1. 4,232 m
  2. 9.20 m
  3. 920 m
  4. 92.0 m
Correct answer: D
A characteristic of 1 means two digits before the decimal point, so the side is about 92.0 m. Check: 92² = 8,464. The value 4,232 comes from wrongly halving the area.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: pair explanation of the root law (Q2: which lesson's index law makes the root law work)
Q7. Why can we write log(√8,464) as (1/2) × log 8,464?
  1. Because log of a square root is always half of the number itself
  2. Because the quotient law says log(a/b) = log a − log b
  3. Because the square root of a number is the same as dividing the number by 2
  4. Because √8,464 = 8,464^(1/2), and the power law says log(aⁿ) = n × log a with n = 1/2
Correct answer: D
The fractional-index rule a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a from Lesson 3 lets us write √8,464 as 8,464^(1/2). The power law then brings the 1/2 to the front of the log.
