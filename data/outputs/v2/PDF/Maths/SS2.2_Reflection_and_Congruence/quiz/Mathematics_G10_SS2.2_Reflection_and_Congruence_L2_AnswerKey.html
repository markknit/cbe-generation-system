Q1. Two shapes are described as congruent. Which statement best explains what congruent means?
  1. They have the same shape, but one may be bigger than the other.
  2. They have the same shape and the same size.
  3. They are in the same position on the desk and face the same direction.
  4. They have the same area, even if their shapes are different, such as a 4 cm by 3 cm rectangle and a 6 cm by 2 cm rectangle.
Correct answer: B
Congruent shapes match in both shape and size. Position and direction can differ, and equal area alone is not enough.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Task 1: lopsided shape cut from folded paper, testing with the dot up and then flipped
Q2. A learner folds a sheet of paper, draws a lopsided letter F (no line of symmetry) and cuts through both layers to get pieces A and B. With a pencil dot facing up on both pieces, piece B slides and turns onto piece A and fits exactly. The learner then turns piece B over (dot face down). What happens?
  1. It becomes smaller than piece A, so it cannot fit.
  2. It still fits exactly on piece A without being turned back over, because the pieces are identical.
  3. It becomes a mirror copy of piece A and does not fit unless it is flipped back over.
  4. It fits only if piece A is also cut again to a new shape.
Correct answer: C
Flipping a shape with no line of symmetry gives its mirror copy. The size and shape stay the same, but the piece will not fit until it is flipped back.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Task 2: shape that HAS a line of symmetry (isosceles triangle or rectangle)
Q3. A learner cuts an isosceles triangle from folded paper to get two identical pieces. Piece B is turned over (dot face down). Why does it still fit exactly on piece A without being turned back?
  1. Because turning a piece over never changes how it looks, for any shape.
  2. Because the two pieces are different sizes.
  3. Because the triangle has a line of symmetry, so its flipped copy looks the same as the original.
  4. Because the triangle has three sides.
Correct answer: C
A shape with a line of symmetry looks the same when flipped, so the flipped copy fits too. This does not happen for shapes with no line of symmetry.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: classifying three pairs on the board, (iii) two circles of the same radius
Q4. A cook uses the same circular cutter to cut two chapatis. The two circles have the same radius. Which kind of congruence do the two circles have?
  1. Neither, because circles have no corners.
  2. Opposite congruence only, because every pair of copies is a mirror pair.
  3. Both direct and opposite congruence, because a circle has lines of symmetry and so looks the same when flipped.
  4. Direct congruence only, because circles can never be flipped.
Correct answer: C
One circle fits on the other by sliding, and it also fits after being flipped, because a circle has lines of symmetry.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: classifying three pairs on the board, (i) two identical lopsided triangles in the same orientation
Q5. Two identical lopsided triangles lie on a desk in the same orientation (neither is flipped). One can be slid onto the other. What kind of congruence is this?
  1. Opposite congruence, because the triangles are separate.
  2. They are not congruent, because they are in different places.
  3. Opposite congruence, because the triangles have no line of symmetry.
  4. Direct congruence, because one fits on the other by sliding and/or turning with no flip.
Correct answer: D
If one shape fits on the other by sliding and/or turning only, with no flip, the congruence is direct. Position on the desk does not matter.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: planes of symmetry of a box-shaped object (matchbox or brick-shaped box with three different edge lengths)
Q6. A teacher holds up a brick-shaped box whose length, width and height are all different. How many planes of symmetry does it have, where each plane cuts the box into two mirror-image halves?
  1. 3
  2. 6
  3. 1
  4. 2
Correct answer: A
There is one plane through the middle of the box parallel to each pair of faces, giving 3 planes. A plane parallel to one pair of faces does not make mirror halves along a diagonal, so there are no others.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: hypothesis box 'the image is a mirror copy of the object, so we think it is OPPOSITE congruent'
Q7. The hills of Homa Bay are reflected in the still water of Lake Victoria. The reflection has the same shape and size as the hills, but it is upside down, like a mirror copy. Which kind of congruence would you expect between the hills and their reflection?
  1. Direct congruence, because the reflection is the same size.
  2. Opposite congruence, because a mirror copy would need to be flipped over to fit the original.
  3. Direct congruence, because the reflection can be slid up onto the hills.
  4. Neither, because a reflection is only a picture.
Correct answer: B
A mirror copy has the same shape and size but is flipped, so the expected congruence is opposite. The lesson treats this as a hypothesis to be tested in later lessons.
