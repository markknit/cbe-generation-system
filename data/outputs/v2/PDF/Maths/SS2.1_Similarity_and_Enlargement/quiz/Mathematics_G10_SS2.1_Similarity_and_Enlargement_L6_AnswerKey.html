Q1. A large catering sufuria is geometrically similar to a small home sufuria. Every length on the large one is exactly 2 times the matching length on the small one (diameter 40 cm against 20 cm). How many times as much ugali does the large sufuria hold?
  1. 6 times as much
  2. 4 times as much
  3. 8 times as much
  4. 2 times as much
Correct answer: C
LSF = 2, so VSF = 2³ = 8. Doubling every length multiplies volume by 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, not 2 (length) or 4 (area).
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Similar Solids Data Card, Pair C (conical grain storage structures)
Q2. Two similar conical grain stores have these dimensions. Small: radius 0.5 m, height 1.2 m. Large: radius 1.5 m, height 3.6 m. What is the Volume Scale Factor (VSF) from the small store to the large store?
  1. 81
  2. 9
  3. 27
  4. 3
Correct answer: C
LSF = 1.5 ÷ 0.5 = 3, so VSF = 3³ = 27. The volumes confirm this: small = ⅓π(0.5)²(1.2) = 0.1π m³ and large = ⅓π(1.5)²(3.6) = 2.7π m³, and 2.7π ÷ 0.1π = 27.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Similar Solids Data Card, Pair B (cuboid water tanks) and early finishers' calculation of Tank 2 capacity in litres
Q3. A school has two cube-shaped water tanks. Tank 1 measures 1 m × 1 m × 1 m. Tank 2 is similar and measures 2 m × 2 m × 2 m. Given that 1 m³ holds 1 000 litres, how many litres can Tank 2 hold when full?
  1. 6 000 litres
  2. 4 000 litres
  3. 2 000 litres
  4. 8 000 litres
Correct answer: D
Volume of Tank 2 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 m³, and 8 × 1 000 = 8 000 litres. This is 8 times the 1 000 litres in Tank 1, matching VSF = 2³.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: guided questions Q1–Q3 on the relationship between LSF, ASF and VSF (Scale Factor Law)
Q4. Two similar solids have an Area Scale Factor (ASF) of 36. What is their Volume Scale Factor (VSF)?
  1. 216
  2. 6
  3. 1 296
  4. 108
Correct answer: A
ASF = (LSF)² = 36, so LSF = 6. Then VSF = 6³ = 216.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Kibera water harvesting tank problem (LSF = 4, small tank 500 litres)
Q5. A small cylindrical water harvesting tank in Kibera holds 500 litres. A geometrically similar large tank has a Length Scale Factor (LSF) of 4. How many litres does the large tank hold?
  1. 8 000 litres
  2. 2 000 litres
  3. 32 000 litres
  4. 6 000 litres
Correct answer: C
VSF = 4³ = 64, so the capacity is 500 × 64 = 32 000 litres. Using 4² = 16 gives 8 000 litres, which is the area scale factor error.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: revisiting the Lesson 1 water-tank capacity prediction and writing the 'VSF = (LSF)³' sticky note
Q6. One water tank is exactly twice as tall as a similar tank, yet it holds 8 times as much water. Which statement best explains this?
  1. Doubling the height doubles the volume, so the tank must be wider as well.
  2. The Volume Scale Factor is always 4 times the Length Scale Factor, so 2 × 4 = 8.
  3. Volume depends on three dimensions (length, width, height). Each is multiplied by 2, so the volume is multiplied by 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.
  4. Volume scales in the same way as area, so it is multiplied by 2² = 4 and the remaining factor of 2 comes from the water.
Correct answer: C
For similar solids every dimension is multiplied by k, so volume is multiplied by k × k × k = k³. For k = 2 this gives 8, resolving the water-tank puzzle.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: extending the Scale Factor Model with the Volume layer, and the Eldoret silo exit task
Q7. A cylindrical grain silo in Eldoret is similar to a model silo, and the Length Scale Factor from the model to the real silo is 5. The model holds 12 litres. How many litres does the real silo hold?
  1. 60 litres
  2. 1 500 litres
  3. 180 litres
  4. 300 litres
Correct answer: B
VSF = 5³ = 125, so the real silo holds 12 × 125 = 1 500 litres. Multiplying by 5 gives 60 litres and multiplying by 5² = 25 gives 300 litres, both of which are wrong.
