Q1. A school plan in Kisumu is drawn at scale 1 : 200, so every length on the plan is 1/200 of the real length. What is the ratio of the real area to the plan area for the same classroom floor?
  1. About 14 (the square root of 200)
  2. 400
  3. 40 000
  4. 200
Correct answer: C
Real lengths are 200 times the plan lengths, so the area ratio is the square of this: 200 × 200 = 40 000. Choosing 200 treats area as scaling in the same way as length.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: drawing and measuring table, row with L.S.F = 2 (2 cm × 3 cm rectangle)
Q2. A rectangle measures 2 cm by 3 cm. It is enlarged with a linear scale factor of 2. What is the area of the enlarged rectangle?
  1. 6 cm²
  2. 12 cm²
  3. 36 cm²
  4. 24 cm²
Correct answer: D
Both sides are multiplied by 2, so the image is 4 cm by 6 cm. Its area is 4 × 6 = 24 cm², which is 4 times the object area of 6 cm².
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: drawing and measuring table, row with L.S.F = ½ (6 cm × 4 cm rectangle)
Q3. A rectangle measures 6 cm by 4 cm. It is reduced to an image with linear scale factor ½. What is the ratio of the area of the image to the area of the object (image ÷ object)?
  1. 2
  2. 0.125
  3. 0.5
  4. 0.25
Correct answer: D
The image is 3 cm by 2 cm, so its area is 6 cm². The object area is 24 cm². The ratio is 6 ÷ 24 = 0.25, which equals (½)².
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Unga Pembe flour-packet poster problem
Q4. A shop poster is an enlargement of the front face of a 1 kg Unga Pembe packet. The poster has an area 4 times the area of the packet front. What is the linear scale factor from the packet to the poster?
  1. 2
  2. 8
  3. 16
  4. 4
Correct answer: A
A.S.F = (L.S.F)², so L.S.F = √4 = 2. Answering 4 confuses the area scale factor with the linear scale factor.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: applying A.S.F = (L.S.F)² to the Kisumu school plan and football pitch
Q5. On a plan drawn at scale 1 : 100, a plot has an area of 12 cm². What is the real area of the plot in m²? (1 m² = 10 000 cm²)
  1. 120 m²
  2. 12 m²
  3. 1 200 m²
  4. 0.12 m²
Correct answer: B
The area scale factor is 100² = 10 000, so the real area is 12 × 10 000 = 120 000 cm². Dividing by 10 000 gives 12 m². Multiplying by only 100 gives 1 200 cm², which is 0.12 m² and is wrong.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: writing the 'What We Now Know' statement
Q6. Which statement correctly completes the class 'What We Now Know' entry about similar figures?
  1. The area scale factor equals the square root of the linear scale factor.
  2. The area scale factor equals the square of the linear scale factor, so doubling lengths quadruples area.
  3. The area scale factor equals the linear scale factor.
  4. If the linear scale factor is 2, the area is also multiplied by 2.
Correct answer: B
The table data showed A.S.F = (L.S.F)²: L.S.F 2 gave 4, L.S.F 3 gave 9, and L.S.F ½ gave ¼. The other statements keep the misconception that area scales like length.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: adding area labels and the '× (L.S.F)²' arrow to the cumulative similarity diagram
Q7. On a similarity diagram, a figure has an area of 5 cm². A similar figure is an enlargement of it with linear scale factor 4. What area should be written on the enlarged figure?
  1. 20 cm²
  2. 25 cm²
  3. 80 cm²
  4. 320 cm²
Correct answer: C
The area scale factor is 4² = 16, so the new area is 5 × 16 = 80 cm². Multiplying by 4 gives 20 cm², which is the linear-scaling mistake.
