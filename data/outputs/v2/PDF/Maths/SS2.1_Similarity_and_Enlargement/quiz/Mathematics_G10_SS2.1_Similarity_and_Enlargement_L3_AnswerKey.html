Q1. Point P is 3 cm from the centre of enlargement O. P is enlarged with centre O and linear scale factor (L.S.F) k = 2. Where is the image P'?
  1. 6 cm from O, on the opposite side of O from P
  2. 5 cm from O, on the same ray as P
  3. 6 cm from O, on the same ray as P (beyond P)
  4. 3 cm from O, on the same ray as P
Correct answer: C
For a positive L.S.F the image lies on the same ray from O as the object, at a distance k × OP = 2 × 3 = 6 cm. Adding 2 cm instead of multiplying by 2 is a common error.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 3 (k = −2): marking A'' on the opposite side of O
Q2. Point A is 4 cm from the centre of enlargement O. A is enlarged with centre O and L.S.F k = −2. Where is the image A''?
  1. 6 cm from O, on the opposite side of O from A
  2. 8 cm from O, on the same side of O as A
  3. 2 cm from O, on the opposite side of O from A
  4. 8 cm from O, on the opposite side of O from A
Correct answer: D
The distance from O is |k| × OA = 2 × 4 = 8 cm. The negative sign means the image is measured through O to the opposite side.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 2: measuring corresponding sides and computing image ÷ object ratios
Q3. A pupil enlarges a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm using L.S.F = 2. She measures the image sides as 6 cm, 8 cm and 11 cm. Which side shows a construction or measuring error?
  1. The 3 cm side, because 6 ÷ 3 = 2 is too small
  2. The 4 cm side, because 8 ÷ 4 = 2 is too big
  3. The 5 cm side, because 11 ÷ 5 = 2.2, not 2
  4. No side has an error, because only the total perimeter needs to increase in an enlargement
Correct answer: C
In an enlargement every image side must be exactly k times its object side. The ratios are 2, 2 and 2.2, so the 5 cm side's image should be 10 cm, not 11 cm.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain prompt (2): what is different about the image when L.S.F is negative
Q4. A triangle is enlarged from centre O first with L.S.F = 2 and then with L.S.F = −2. What is the difference between the two images?
  1. The k = −2 image is on the same side of O and in the same orientation, but is twice as small
  2. Both images are the same size, but the k = −2 image is on the opposite side of O and inverted
  3. The k = −2 image is smaller than the k = 2 image
  4. The k = −2 image is on the same side of O as the object but inverted
Correct answer: B
The size of the image depends on |k|, so both images have sides twice as long. The negative sign only places the image on the opposite side of O and inverts it.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain prompt (3): L.S.F for the 1 : 200 Kisumu school plan and for finding real dimensions
Q5. On the school plan, the scale is 1 : 200. A classroom block measures 5 cm on the plan. What is its real length?
  1. 0.025 m
  2. 10 m
  3. 100 m
  4. 1 m
Correct answer: B
To go from plan to real building the L.S.F is 200. Real length = 5 × 200 = 1 000 cm = 10 m.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: writing the model statement 'image side = |k| × object side'
Q6. Side BC of a triangle is 6 cm long. The triangle is enlarged with a centre O and L.S.F k = −2. What is the length of the image side B''C''?
  1. 3 cm
  2. 12 cm
  3. −12 cm
  4. 4 cm
Correct answer: B
Lengths scale by the size of k: |−2| × 6 = 12 cm. A length cannot be negative, and the negative sign does not make the image smaller.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: 'Lengths — SOLVED ✓ | Areas — still to investigate | Volumes — still to investigate'
Q7. After constructing enlargements of a triangle with L.S.F k = 2 and k = −2, which statement is firmly established about lengths?
  1. The image sides are in the same ratio to the object sides only when k is positive
  2. Every image side equals k times the matching object side, so a negative k gives a shorter image
  3. Every image side equals |k| times the matching object side; the sign of k only shows which side of O the image lies on
  4. Doubling the lengths of a shape always doubles its area as well
Correct answer: C
The construction and measurements show that the length ratio is |k|, whatever the sign of k. What happens to area and volume has not been investigated yet, and area does not simply double when lengths double.
