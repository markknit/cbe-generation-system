Q1. A village elder draws a seven-sided plot of land with sides of different lengths. No single area formula you know fits the whole shape. What is the best first step to find its area?
  1. Say the area cannot be found because the shape is irregular
  2. Draw lines inside the plot to split it into triangles and quadrilaterals, find each area, then add them
  3. Measure only the longest side and square it
  4. Pick the longest side and multiply it by the shortest side
Correct answer: B
Any polygon can be decomposed into shapes whose areas we already know, such as triangles, rectangles and trapezoids. The total area is the sum of these parts.

Q2. The Kiambu Girls' Secondary School science block has an L-shaped footprint. It fits inside a rectangle 18 m long and 12 m wide. A rectangular notch 6 m by 7 m is missing from one corner. What is the area of the L-shape?
  1. 174 m²
  2. 216 m²
  3. 90 m²
  4. 258 m²
Correct answer: A
Subtract the notch from the full rectangle: 18 × 12 − 6 × 7 = 216 − 42 = 174 m².

Q3. One group finds the area of an L-shaped building by subtracting the notch from a big rectangle. Another group splits the L into two rectangles and adds them. Both groups work correctly. What will they find?
  1. The two answers will differ by the area of the notch
  2. The subtraction method gives a bigger area because it starts with a bigger rectangle
  3. The addition method gives a bigger area because it uses more shapes
  4. The two answers will be the same, because the area of a shape does not depend on how it is divided
Correct answer: D
Area is a property of the shape itself. Any correct decomposition must give the same total, so different answers mean an arithmetic error.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Phase: Nakuru pentagonal community garden worksheet
Q4. The Nakuru Community Organic Garden is a rectangle 15 m by 10 m. A triangle with base 10 m and height 4 m is added to one short side. A rectangular vegetable patch 3 m by 4 m is removed from the inside. What is the area of the garden?
  1. 182 m²
  2. 138 m²
  3. 170 m²
  4. 158 m²
Correct answer: D
Add the triangle and subtract the patch: 15 × 10 + ½ × 10 × 4 − 3 × 4 = 150 + 20 − 12 = 158 m².
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: Gallery Walk and teacher synthesis of the composite-polygon rule
Q5. Which statement correctly describes how to find the area of a composite polygon?
  1. Always add all the areas, even for a section that is cut out
  2. Add the areas of the component shapes, and subtract the area of any section that is removed
  3. Multiply the areas of all the component shapes together
  4. Find the area of the largest component shape only
Correct answer: B
The general rule is Area(composite) = sum of component areas, or whole area minus the removed area. Areas are never multiplied together, and a removed part must be subtracted.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board phase: calculating the Kericho total area and the equal share for each family
Q6. A group splits a plot into two triangles and a trapezoid with areas 300 m², 450 m² and 510 m². The total area is shared equally among three farming families. How much land does each family get?
  1. 420 m²
  2. 1 260 m²
  3. 510 m²
  4. 630 m²
Correct answer: A
Total area = 300 + 450 + 510 = 1 260 m². Each family gets 1 260 ÷ 3 = 420 m².
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board phase: shading and justifying the three equal sections of the Kericho plot
Q7. A village divides a plot fairly among three families. Which statement best describes a fair division of the land?
  1. Each family's section has the same number of sides
  2. Each family's section has the same area, even if the shapes are different
  3. Each family's section has the same perimeter
  4. Each family's section has one side of equal length
Correct answer: B
A fair division means equal areas. Sections can have different shapes, sides or perimeters and still have the same area.
