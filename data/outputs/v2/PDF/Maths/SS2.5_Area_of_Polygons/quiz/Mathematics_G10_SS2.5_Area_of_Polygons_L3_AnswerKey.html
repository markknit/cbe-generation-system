Q1. A triangular solar panel in Kisumu has two sides of 5 m and 7 m with an angle of 58° between them. The perpendicular height cannot be measured because the panel is bolted flat on a sloped roof. What is the best way to find its area?
  1. Use ½ × 5 × 7 = 17.5 m², because the two sides are enough to replace the base and height
  2. The area cannot be found unless the perpendicular height is measured
  3. Use the two sides and the angle between them in A = ½ab sin C
  4. Assume the height equals 5 m and use A = ½ × 7 × 5
Correct answer: C
A = ½bh needs h, which is unknown. But two sides and the included angle are known, so A = ½ab sin C can be used without measuring the height.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: structured derivation worksheet, Step 2 and Step 3 (sin C in terms of h and a, then make h the subject)
Q2. In a triangle, sides a and b meet at angle C. A perpendicular of length h is drawn from the end of side a to side b (extended if needed). This makes a right-angled triangle in which side a is the hypotenuse and angle C is one of the angles. Which expression gives h?
  1. h = a sin C
  2. h = b sin C
  3. h = a cos C
  4. h = a ÷ sin C
Correct answer: A
In the right-angled triangle, sin C = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = h/a, so h = a sin C. Using b means the wrong side has been taken as the hypotenuse.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: applying the derived formula to the Kisumu solar panel (calculator and tables)
Q3. A triangular panel has sides a = 5 m and b = 7 m with an included angle C = 58°. Given that sin 58° ≈ 0.8480, what is its area, to 2 decimal places?
  1. 29.68 m²
  2. 9.27 m²
  3. 17.50 m²
  4. 14.84 m²
Correct answer: D
A = ½ab sin C = ½ × 5 × 7 × 0.8480 ≈ 14.84 m². 17.50 m² forgets sin C, 29.68 m² forgets the ½, and 9.27 m² uses cos 58° instead of sin 58°.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: application problems (choosing the correct two sides and the included angle)
Q4. In triangle KLM, KL = 8 m, LM = 10 m, angle L = 35° and angle K = 80°. Which expression gives the area of the triangle using A = ½ab sin C?
  1. ½ × 8 × 10 × sin 80°
  2. ½ × 8 × 10 × cos 35°
  3. 8 × 10 × sin 35°
  4. ½ × 8 × 10 × sin 35°
Correct answer: D
The angle must lie between the two sides used. KL and LM meet at L, so the included angle is 35° and A = ½ × 8 × 10 × sin 35°. Using 80° picks an angle that is not between those sides.

Q5. A triangular maize plot near Kericho has two sides of 12 m and 9 m with an included angle of 72°. Given that sin 72° ≈ 0.9511, what is the area of the plot, to 2 decimal places?
  1. 16.69 m²
  2. 102.71 m²
  3. 54.00 m²
  4. 51.36 m²
Correct answer: D
A = ½ × 12 × 9 × sin 72° = 54 × 0.9511 ≈ 51.36 m². 54 m² leaves out sin C, 102.71 m² leaves out the ½, and 16.69 m² uses cos 72°.

Q6. One triangle inside a polygonal plot has two sides of 8.4 m and 11.2 m with an included angle of 65°. Its perpendicular height is not marked. Given that sin 65° ≈ 0.9063, what is its area, to 2 decimal places?
  1. 42.63 m²
  2. 19.88 m²
  3. 85.26 m²
  4. 47.04 m²
Correct answer: A
A = ½ × 8.4 × 11.2 × sin 65° = 47.04 × 0.9063 ≈ 42.63 m². 47.04 m² forgets sin C, 85.26 m² forgets the ½, and 19.88 m² uses cos 65°.

Q7. A plot is split into triangles to find its total area. For one triangle, the perpendicular height cannot be measured, but two sides and the angle between them are known. Which statement best describes how A = ½ab sin C helps?
  1. It works only for right-angled triangles, so it cannot help with this triangle
  2. It means the plot no longer needs to be split into triangles
  3. It always gives a larger area than A = ½bh, so it is used for bigger triangles
  4. It allows that triangle's area to be found without the height, so the plot's total area can still be found by adding the triangle areas
Correct answer: D
A = ½ab sin C works for any triangle when two sides and the included angle are known. So every triangle in a decomposed polygon can be solved and the areas added.
