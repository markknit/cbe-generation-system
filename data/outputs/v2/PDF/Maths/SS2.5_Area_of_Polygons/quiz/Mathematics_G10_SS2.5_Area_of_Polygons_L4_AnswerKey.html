Q1. A village plot in Kericho is a heptagon (a polygon with 7 sides). A learner chooses one corner (vertex) and draws diagonals from it to every other corner it can reach, so that the plot is split into non-overlapping triangles. How many triangles are formed?
  1. 5
  2. 6
  3. 7
  4. 4
Correct answer: A
A diagonal from one vertex splits an n-sided polygon into n - 2 triangles, so 7 - 2 = 5. The common error is thinking the number of triangles equals the number of sides.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Step 2: measuring the base and the corresponding perpendicular height of each triangle
Q2. A learner wants to find the area of a triangle on the scaled plot drawing using A = ½ × base × height. Which measurement should they use as the height?
  1. The length of the side next to the chosen base
  2. The perpendicular distance from the chosen base to the opposite vertex
  3. The length of the longest side of the triangle
  4. Half the length of the chosen base
Correct answer: B
The height must be measured at right angles (perpendicular) to the chosen base, up to the opposite vertex. Using a slanted side overestimates the area.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Steps 3 and 4: calculating triangle areas in cm² and converting with the scale (1 cm² = 25 m²)
Q3. On a scale drawing of a land plot, 1 cm represents 5 m. One triangle inside the plot has a base of 8 cm and a perpendicular height of 5 cm on the drawing. What is the real area of this triangle on the ground?
  1. 20 m²
  2. 500 m²
  3. 250 m²
  4. 100 m²
Correct answer: B
Area on the drawing = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm². Since 1 cm = 5 m, 1 cm² = 25 m², so the real area is 20 × 25 = 500 m². Multiplying by only 5 gives the wrong answer of 100 m².
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, written question Q2: why Group A and Group B totals differ
Q4. Two groups measured the same heptagon plot drawing. Group A got a total area of 1 240 m² and Group B got 1 270 m². What is the most likely reason for the small difference?
  1. Small measurement errors, such as reading the ruler at an angle or not measuring the height exactly perpendicular to the base
  2. Group B must have used 7 triangles while Group A used 5
  3. The formula A = ½bh gives a different answer each time it is used
  4. The area of a heptagon changes depending on which group measures it
Correct answer: A
The polygon's true area is fixed, and the formula is the same for everyone. Small differences come from measuring errors such as parallax or a height that is not exactly perpendicular.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, written question Q3: one family receives one-third of the total area
Q5. The total area of a plot is 1 260 m². It must be shared equally among three farming families. What area should each family receive?
  1. 630 m²
  2. 1 260 m²
  3. 3 780 m²
  4. 420 m²
Correct answer: D
Each family gets one-third of the total: 1 260 ÷ 3 = 420 m². Multiplying by 3 or dividing by 2 are common slips.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: class discussion generalising the pentagon, hexagon and heptagon results to (n - 2) triangles
Q6. A pentagon (5 sides) splits into 3 triangles and a hexagon (6 sides) splits into 4 triangles when all diagonals are drawn from one vertex. Using the same pattern, how many triangles will a polygon with 9 sides split into?
  1. 7
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 9
Correct answer: A
The pattern is number of triangles = n - 2, where n is the number of sides. For 9 sides, 9 - 2 = 7 triangles.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: writing the general statement 'Area of heptagon = A(T1) + A(T2) + ... + A(T5)' on the poster
Q7. A heptagon is split into five non-overlapping triangles T1 to T5 with bases b₁ to b₅ and perpendicular heights h₁ to h₅. Which statement correctly gives the area of the heptagon?
  1. Area = ½b₁h₁ × ½b₂h₂ × ½b₃h₃ × ½b₄h₄ × ½b₅h₅
  2. Area = ½ × (b₁ + b₂ + b₃ + b₄ + b₅) × (h₁ + h₂ + h₃ + h₄ + h₅)
  3. Area = ½b₁h₁ + ½b₂h₂ + ½b₃h₃ + ½b₄h₄ + ½b₅h₅
  4. Area = ½ × 7 × b₁ × h₁
Correct answer: C
The area of the polygon equals the sum of the areas of its non-overlapping triangles, and each triangle's area is ½ × base × height. Adding all the bases and all the heights first, or multiplying the areas, gives the wrong result.
