Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher asks 'What is the angle the minute hand turns through every 15 minutes?'
Q1. The minute hand of a clock moves from 12:00 to 12:15. Through what angle, and in which direction, does it turn about the centre of the clock?
  1. 90° clockwise
  2. 180° clockwise
  3. 90° anticlockwise
  4. 15° clockwise
Correct answer: A
The hand makes a full 360° turn in 60 minutes, so in 15 minutes it turns 15/60 × 360 = 90°. Clock hands move clockwise.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: 'What stays the same across all four images? What changes?'
Q2. A minute hand turns about the centre of a clock from 12:00 to 12:30. Which statement about the hand is true?
  1. The hand slides sideways to a new position without turning.
  2. The hand flips over like a mirror image and its length changes.
  3. The hand stays the same length and every point on it stays the same distance from the centre.
  4. The hand becomes longer because it has moved further round.
Correct answer: C
Rotation keeps the shape and size of an object, so distances from the fixed centre do not change. Only the direction changes. Flipping is reflection and sliding is translation.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: rotate A(0,5), B(0,3), C(0,1) to the 12:15 position
Q3. The tip of a clock's minute hand is at A(0, 5) at 12:00. The centre of the clock is the origin (0, 0). Using the rule (x, y) → (y, −x), where is the tip at 12:15, after a 90° clockwise rotation?
  1. (−5, 0)
  2. (0, −5)
  3. (5, 5)
  4. (5, 0)
Correct answer: D
Apply (x, y) → (y, −x) to (0, 5): the new point is (5, −0) = (5, 0). This is the 3 o'clock position. (−5, 0) is the anticlockwise answer.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: rotate the hand to the 12:45 position (270° clockwise = 90° anticlockwise)
Q4. At 12:00 a point C(0, 1) lies on a clock's minute hand, with the centre at the origin. At 12:45 the hand has turned 270° clockwise, which is the same as 90° anticlockwise, with the rule (x, y) → (−y, x). Where is the image of C?
  1. (0, −1)
  2. (−1, 0)
  3. (1, 0)
  4. (0, 1)
Correct answer: B
Apply (x, y) → (−y, x) to (0, 1): the new point is (−1, 0). This is the 9 o'clock position. (1, 0) comes from using the clockwise rule by mistake.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: determine centre and angle of rotation using perpendicular bisectors of PP' and QQ'
Q5. Triangle PQR is rotated to give image P'Q'R'. You want to find the centre of rotation by construction. Which method is correct?
  1. Draw the angle bisector at P. The centre is where it meets the side QR.
  2. Join P to P' and Q to Q'. The centre is where the two lines meet.
  3. Find the midpoint of PP'. That midpoint is the centre.
  4. Draw the perpendicular bisectors of PP' and QQ'. The centre is where they meet.
Correct answer: D
The centre is the same distance from a point and its image, so it lies on the perpendicular bisector of each pair PP' and QQ'. The centre is where the bisectors intersect.
Use after: Explain phase — Part C: order of rotational symmetry of the regular hexagon, rectangle and letter 'S'
Q6. What is the order of rotational symmetry of the letter 'S' (drawn in its usual shape)?
  1. 6
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 4
Correct answer: C
The letter S looks exactly the same after a half-turn (180°) about its centre and again after a full turn, so its order is 2. A quarter-turn (90°) does not fit it back onto itself, so it cannot be order 4.
Use after: Explain phase — Part D: axis and order of rotational symmetry of a triangular prism
Q7. A triangular prism has two equilateral triangle ends. It is turned about the axis that joins the centres of the two ends. What is the order of rotational symmetry about this axis?
  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 3
  4. 4
Correct answer: C
An equilateral triangle fits onto itself every 120°. So the prism fits onto itself 360 ÷ 120 = 3 times in one full turn about this axis.
Use after: Explain phase — Part E: congruence from rotation (pairs of triangles on a grid)
Q8. Triangle ABC is rotated 90° about a point to give triangle A'B'C'. Which statement is correct?
  1. Triangle A'B'C' is a mirror image of triangle ABC.
  2. Only the angles stay equal, while the side lengths change.
  3. The two triangles are congruent: all sides and angles are equal.
  4. Triangle A'B'C' is larger because it has turned.
Correct answer: C
Rotation preserves lengths and angles, so the image is congruent to the object. It is a direct congruence with the same orientation type, not a mirror image like a reflection.
