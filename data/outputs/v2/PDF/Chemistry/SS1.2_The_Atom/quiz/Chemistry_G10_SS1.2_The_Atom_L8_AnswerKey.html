Q1. Chlorine has atomic number 17. Which is its full s and p electron configuration?
  1. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁴
  2. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s⁵
  3. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵
  4. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹ 3p⁶
Correct answer: C
Fill in Aufbau order: 2+2+6+2 = 12 electrons, leaving 5 for 3p, so 3p⁵. The other options either have the wrong total or put too many electrons in 3s (maximum 2).
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Paired Systematic Configuration Practice (elements 11–20)
Q2. Calcium has atomic number 20. Which is its correct full electron configuration?
  1. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁸
  2. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s²
  3. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s¹
  4. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d²
Correct answer: B
After 3p⁶ is full (18 electrons), the next orbital in the Aufbau order is 4s, which takes the last 2 electrons. 3d does not fill before 4s, 4s¹ would be potassium, and 3p holds a maximum of 6 electrons.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Colour-Coded Group Pattern Discovery (Group 1 column)
Q3. Lithium (1s² 2s¹), sodium (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹) and potassium (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s¹) are all in Group 1. What do their configurations have in common?
  1. Each has one electron in an outermost p subshell
  2. Each has the same total number of electrons
  3. Each has exactly one electron in its outermost s subshell
  4. Each has the same number of occupied energy levels
Correct answer: C
All three end in ns¹, which is why they are in the same group. They have different numbers of energy levels (2, 3 and 4) and different total electrons (3, 11 and 19), and none ends in a p subshell.

Q4. Magnesium has the configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s². Which element has the same type and number of outermost electrons as magnesium?
  1. Calcium, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s²
  2. Chlorine, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵
  3. Sodium, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹
  4. Aluminium, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p¹
Correct answer: A
Magnesium and calcium both end in ns² (Group 2). Sodium ends in ns¹, while aluminium and chlorine have outermost electrons in a p subshell.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Peer-Teaching Carousel (explaining how many electrons are in each subshell)
Q5. Sulfur has atomic number 16 and the configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁴. How many electrons are in its 3p subshell?
  1. 4
  2. 6
  3. 16
  4. 2
Correct answer: A
Subtract the electrons in the inner subshells from the total: 16 − (2 + 2 + 6 + 2) = 4 electrons remain in 3p.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Think-Pair-Share: Phenomenon Reconnection (Na and K configurations side by side)
Q6. Sodium (…3s¹) and potassium (…4s¹) both have one electron in their outermost s orbital, yet sodium gives a yellow-orange flame and potassium gives a violet flame. Which statement best explains the different colours?
  1. Potassium has more outermost electrons than sodium, so it gives a different colour
  2. Sodium's outermost electron is in a p subshell and potassium's is in an s subshell
  3. The colour depends only on how hot the flame is, not on the type of atom
  4. The outermost electron is in a different energy level in each atom, so the energy released as light differs and gives a different wavelength
Correct answer: D
Both have ns¹, but the outermost electron is in level 3 for sodium and level 4 for potassium. The different energy gaps give photons of different energy and wavelength, so the colours differ.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative Model Annotation: drawing the potassium diagram beside sodium
Q7. A learner draws a model of a potassium atom (atomic number 19, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s¹). Which description of the diagram is correct?
  1. Three energy levels are occupied, and the outermost electron is in the 3p subshell
  2. Four energy levels are occupied, and the outermost electron is in the 4s subshell
  3. Four energy levels are occupied, and the outermost electron is in the 3d subshell
  4. Three energy levels are occupied, and the outermost electron is in the 3s subshell
Correct answer: B
The configuration uses levels 1, 2, 3 and 4, and the last electron enters 4s. This higher energy level is what makes potassium's flame colour differ from sodium's.
