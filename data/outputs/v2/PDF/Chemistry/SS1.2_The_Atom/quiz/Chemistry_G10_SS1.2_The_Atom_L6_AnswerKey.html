Q1. At a school lab in Kisumu, copper wire, table salt and potassium chloride are each put in the SAME Bunsen flame at the SAME temperature. The flame turns green, yellow and lilac-violet respectively. What best explains the different colours?
  1. The electrons in each type of atom are arranged differently, so each releases a different amount of energy as light
  2. The flame becomes hotter for some substances than for others
  3. Some substances burn more of the Bunsen gas than others
  4. The atoms of each substance are different sizes, and only size decides the colour
Correct answer: A
The temperature is the same, so the difference must come from inside the atoms. Different electron arrangements mean different energy is released as light, giving different colours.

Q2. In a sodium atom, two electrons are both in Shell 2 but have different energies. What does this show that Bohr's shell model cannot explain?
  1. Electrons can only exist in Shell 1
  2. Shell 2 is made of sub-regions (2s and 2p) with different energies, so a shell is not one uniform region
  3. The nucleus changes the energy of electrons by moving around
  4. Electrons in the same shell must always have exactly the same energy
Correct answer: B
Animation 1 shows that a shell contains sub-regions called orbitals (2s and 2p) with different energies. Bohr's model treats each shell as having a single energy.

Q3. Which statement correctly describes the shapes of s and p orbitals?
  1. Both s and p orbitals are flat rings around the nucleus
  2. s orbitals are dumbbell-shaped and p orbitals are spherical
  3. s orbitals are spherical and there is only one dumbbell-shaped p orbital in each shell
  4. s orbitals are spherical, and there are three dumbbell-shaped 2p orbitals lying along the x, y and z axes
Correct answer: D
An s orbital is a sphere. Each p sub-shell has three dumbbell-shaped orbitals (px, py, pz) pointing along the three axes.

Q4. A student writes in the observation table: '2p orbitals hold a maximum of 2 electrons.' What is the correct maximum number of electrons in the whole 2p sub-shell? (Each p orbital holds 2 electrons, and there are three 2p orbitals.)
  1. 3
  2. 2
  3. 8
  4. 6
Correct answer: D
Each of the three 2p orbitals (2px, 2py, 2pz) holds 2 electrons. So 3 × 2 = 6 electrons in the 2p sub-shell. The value 2 is the capacity of a single p orbital.

Q5. A sodium atom has 11 electrons (Z = 11). The orbitals fill in the order 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s. In which orbital is sodium's outermost electron?
  1. 2p
  2. 3s
  3. 3p
  4. 4s
Correct answer: B
1s holds 2, 2s holds 2 and 2p holds 6, which makes 10 electrons. The 11th electron goes into 3s, the next orbital in the order.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: card sort, applying the sorted table to fill orbitals for K (Z=19)
Q6. A potassium atom has 19 electrons (Z = 19). Using the filling order 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s, which orbital holds its outermost electron?
  1. 3p
  2. 4s
  3. 3s
  4. 2p
Correct answer: B
1s (2) + 2s (2) + 2p (6) + 3s (2) + 3p (6) = 18 electrons. The 19th electron goes into 4s, in energy level 4.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: 'Then vs Now' card and sentence frame on why sodium and potassium give different flame colours
Q7. Sodium's outermost electron is in the 3s orbital and potassium's is in the 4s orbital. Which statement best explains why sodium gives a yellow-orange flame and potassium gives a violet flame?
  1. Sodium is heated more than potassium in the flame
  2. All electrons release the same energy, but our eyes see it differently
  3. The electrons are in different orbitals at different energy levels, so they release different amounts of energy as different colours of light
  4. Potassium atoms have no electrons that can absorb energy
Correct answer: C
A heated electron jumps to a higher orbital and releases energy as light when it falls back. Different orbitals mean a different energy gap, so a different colour of light.

Q8. Which electron arrangement correctly shows a sodium atom (11 electrons) in the orbital model?
  1. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3p¹
  2. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹
  3. 1s² 2s² 2p⁵ 3s²
  4. 1s² 2s² 3s² 3p⁵
Correct answer: B
Fill in order: 1s² (2) + 2s² (2) + 2p⁶ (6) = 10 electrons, and the last electron goes into 3s¹. The other choices fill 3s before 2p is full, put the electron in 3p before 3s, or skip 2p.
