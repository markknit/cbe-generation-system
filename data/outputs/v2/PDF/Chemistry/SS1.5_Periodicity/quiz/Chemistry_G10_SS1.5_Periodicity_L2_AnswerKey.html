Use after: Observe phase — Stage 1: Lithium with water (teacher demonstration), testing the gas with a lighted splint
Q1. A teacher holds an inverted test tube over lithium reacting with cold water and then puts a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube. The splint goes out with a squeaky pop. Which gas has been collected?
  1. Oxygen
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Hydrogen
  4. Steam
Correct answer: C
Group I metals react with cold water to give a metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas. Hydrogen burns with a squeaky pop when tested with a lighted splint.
Use after: Observe phase — Stage 1: Lithium with water, recording the colour change of the indicator and pH of the solution
Q2. Three drops of universal indicator are added to the water in a trough. A small piece of lithium reacts completely with the water. What will the indicator show about the final solution?
  1. It stays green because the solution is neutral
  2. It does not change colour because lithium does not dissolve
  3. It turns red because hydrogen gas makes the solution acidic
  4. It turns blue or purple because the solution is alkaline
Correct answer: D
Lithium reacts with water to form lithium hydroxide, which dissolves to make an alkaline solution. Universal indicator shows blue or purple (high pH) in an alkali.
Use after: Observe phase — Stage 3: Potassium with water (teacher demonstration only), safety narration about storage under oil
Q3. Potassium is stored under oil in the laboratory. What is the main reason for this?
  1. It reacts with moisture in the air, so the oil keeps air and water away
  2. The oil stops it from becoming a non-metal
  3. The oil keeps it cool because it melts at room temperature
  4. Potassium dissolves in oil and this makes it safe to carry
Correct answer: A
Potassium is so reactive that even moisture in the air begins to react with it. Storing it under oil keeps air and water away from the metal.
Use after: Explain phase — Activity 1: Electron configuration diagrams for Li, Na and K
Q4. The electron arrangements are Li (2,1), Na (2,8,1) and K (2,8,8,1). What do all three atoms have in common?
  1. One electron in the outermost shell
  2. The same number of electron shells
  3. The same number of protons in the nucleus
  4. The same total number of electrons
Correct answer: A
Each atom ends with a 1 in its electron arrangement, so each has one outer-shell electron. This is the electron they lose when they react. The number of shells, protons and total electrons all differ.
Use after: Explain phase — Activity 2: Linking configuration to ionisation energy (bar chart of Li, Na, K data strip card)
Q5. The first ionisation energy of lithium is 520 kJ/mol and that of potassium is 419 kJ/mol. How much less energy (in kJ/mol) is needed to remove the outer electron from potassium than from lithium?
  1. 77
  2. 101
  3. 24
  4. 939
Correct answer: B
Subtract the values: 520 - 419 = 101 kJ/mol. Less energy is needed for potassium, so it loses its outer electron more easily and is more reactive.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Sketch: caption 'Reactivity increases down Group I because...'
Q6. A learner writes the caption: 'Reactivity increases down Group I because potassium has more protons than lithium, so it holds its electron more tightly.' Which statement best corrects this idea?
  1. Going down the group, atoms become heavier, so they move faster in water
  2. Going down the group, the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded by inner shells, so it is lost more easily
  3. Going down the group, atoms gain more outer electrons, so they react more
  4. Going down the group, ionisation energy increases, so the outer electron is lost more easily
Correct answer: B
More shells place the outer electron further away and increase shielding. This reduces the pull of the nucleus, lowers ionisation energy and makes the electron easier to lose. The learner's statement about protons is a misconception because the extra distance and shielding outweigh the extra protons.
Use after: Explain phase — Activity 3: Writing balanced equations with state symbols (peer-check)
Q7. Which equation correctly represents the reaction of sodium with cold water, with balancing and state symbols?
  1. 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + 2H2(g)
  2. 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g)
  3. 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + O2(g)
  4. Na(s) + H2O(l) → NaOH(aq) + H2(g)
Correct answer: B
Sodium and water give sodium hydroxide solution and hydrogen gas. Counting atoms: 2 Na, 4 H and 2 O on each side. The other equations are unbalanced or give the wrong gas.
