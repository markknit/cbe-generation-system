Use after: Observe phase — Investigation A — Magnesium in Cold Water
Q1. A student places a 3 cm strip of pre-cleaned magnesium ribbon into a test tube of cold water and watches it for 5 minutes. Which observation is most likely?
  1. The ribbon skims across the water and burns with an orange flame, like sodium
  2. The ribbon stays completely unchanged, like aluminium, with no gas at all
  3. The ribbon fizzes so vigorously that it dissolves within seconds
  4. Only a few slow bubbles form over several minutes, and the solution turns slightly alkaline
Correct answer: D
Magnesium reacts very slowly with cold water, giving a few slow hydrogen bubbles and a weakly alkaline solution of magnesium hydroxide. It is much less vigorous than sodium.
Use after: Observe phase — Teacher demonstration — Calcium in Cold Water, and the ranking of reactions with sodium placed in the ranking
Q2. A teacher drops a small piece of calcium into cold water and the class compares it with magnesium in cold water and sodium in cold water. Which ranking, from MOST vigorous to LEAST vigorous, is correct?
  1. Sodium, calcium, magnesium
  2. Magnesium, calcium, sodium
  3. Calcium, sodium, magnesium
  4. Sodium, magnesium, calcium
Correct answer: A
Sodium is the most vigorous of all. Calcium reacts faster than magnesium but less violently than sodium, and magnesium is the slowest in cold water.
Use after: Observe phase — Graphing task — atomic radius and first ionisation energy graphs down Group II
Q3. Going down Group II from beryllium to barium, which pair of trends is correct?
  1. Atomic radius increases and first ionisation energy increases
  2. Atomic radius decreases and first ionisation energy decreases
  3. Atomic radius increases and first ionisation energy decreases
  4. Atomic radius decreases and first ionisation energy increases
Correct answer: C
Each element down the group has an extra electron shell, so atoms get bigger. The outer electrons are farther from the nucleus and easier to remove, so first ionisation energy falls.
Use after: Observe phase — Graphing task — melting point graph and circling the exception at calcium
Q4. The melting points of Group II metals are: Be 1287 °C, Mg 650 °C, Ca 842 °C, Sr 777 °C, Ba 727 °C. Which statement describes the trend correctly?
  1. Melting point increases steadily from Be to Ba
  2. Melting point decreases smoothly with every element lower than the one before
  3. Melting point stays about the same for all Group II metals
  4. Melting point decreases overall from Be to Ba, but not smoothly, because calcium is higher than magnesium
Correct answer: D
The overall trend is a decrease from 1287 °C to 727 °C. Calcium (842 °C) is higher than magnesium (650 °C), so the fall is not smooth.
Use after: Explain phase — Writing balanced chemical equations for each reaction observed (dilute acid)
Q5. Which is the correct balanced equation for magnesium reacting with dilute hydrochloric acid?
  1. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l)
  2. Mg(s) + HCl(aq) → MgCl(aq) + H₂(g)
  3. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
  4. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H(g)
Correct answer: C
Magnesium forms Mg²⁺, so it needs two chloride ions (MgCl₂). Hydrogen is released as the diatomic gas H₂, and both sides have 1 Mg, 2 H and 2 Cl.
Use after: Explain phase — Two-Electron Tax sensemaking frame — Na and Mg electron configuration comparison
Q6. Sodium (2,8,1) and magnesium (2,8,2) are neighbours in Period 3. Which statement best explains why magnesium is less reactive than sodium with cold water?
  1. Magnesium has only one outer electron, so there is less to lose
  2. Magnesium has fewer protons than sodium, so it holds its electrons less strongly
  3. Magnesium atoms must lose two electrons, and its outer electrons are held more strongly by a greater nuclear charge, giving a higher ionisation energy
  4. Magnesium has one more electron shell than sodium, so its outer electron is shielded more
Correct answer: C
Magnesium has one more proton in the same number of shells, so its outer electrons are pulled harder and it must lose two electrons. This gives a higher ionisation energy and less reactivity than sodium.
Use after: Explain phase — Three-sentence explanation: going from magnesium to calcium down Group II, reactivity increases because…
Q7. Calcium (2,8,8,2) reacts more vigorously with cold water than magnesium (2,8,2), even though calcium has more protons. Which explanation is correct?
  1. Calcium has fewer outer electrons than magnesium, so it loses them more easily
  2. Calcium has more protons, so it pulls its outer electrons more strongly and loses them more easily
  3. Calcium has a higher first ionisation energy than magnesium, so it reacts faster
  4. Calcium has more inner shells, so more shielding and a greater distance from the nucleus make its outer electrons easier to remove
Correct answer: D
Down the group, extra inner shells increase shielding and distance, which outweighs the extra protons. Outer electrons are lost more easily (lower ionisation energy), so reactivity increases.
Use after: Model Building phase — Task 1 — Add Magnesium and Calcium to the electron-shell model diagram
Q8. Which electron arrangement is correct for a calcium atom (atomic number 20)?
  1. 2,8,10
  2. 2,8,2
  3. 2,8,8,1
  4. 2,8,8,2
Correct answer: D
Calcium has 20 electrons, arranged 2, 8, 8, 2. It has two outer electrons and three filled inner shells, as for other Group II elements.
