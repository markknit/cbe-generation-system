Use after: Predict phase — Predict card: 'Why might the same chemical be both useful and dangerous?'
Q1. Paracetamol relieves pain and fever, yet taking too much of it is dangerous. Which statement best explains why the same medicine can be both useful and harmful?
  1. Medicines that work faster are always safer, so a bigger dose gives quicker relief with no harm
  2. Only medicines made in factories are harmful, because natural substances are always safe
  3. The amount taken matters: the right dose helps, but more than the safe limit can damage the liver
  4. If a medicine is useful for one illness, it cannot harm any part of the body
Correct answer: C
The lesson stresses that a useful chemical becomes dangerous when the safe amount is exceeded; above 4 g of paracetamol in 24 hours the liver can be damaged. More medicine does not mean faster relief, and 'natural' does not mean safe.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2 - Medicine facts card: calculating 8 x 500 mg = 4000 mg = 4 g
Q2. An adult paracetamol label says: take 1 to 2 tablets of 500 mg every 4 to 6 hours, with a maximum of 8 tablets in 24 hours. What is the maximum total amount of paracetamol, in grams, that an adult may take in 24 hours?
  1. 4 g
  2. 40 g
  3. 0.4 g
  4. 400 g
Correct answer: A
8 tablets x 500 mg = 4000 mg. Since 1000 mg = 1 g, 4000 mg = 4 g.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2 - teacher question: 'If a learner takes 10 tablets of 500 mg in one day...'
Q3. The safe adult maximum of paracetamol is 4000 mg in 24 hours. A learner takes 10 tablets of 500 mg each in one day. By how many mg does this go over the safe limit?
  1. 5000 mg
  2. 4000 mg
  3. 500 mg
  4. 1000 mg
Correct answer: D
10 x 500 = 5000 mg taken. The limit is 4000 mg, so 5000 - 4000 = 1000 mg over the limit, which can damage the liver.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2 - re-observing F fizzing in dilute vinegar and G giving no fizz
Q4. A teacher puts an antacid tablet in dilute vinegar and it fizzes. A paracetamol tablet in dilute vinegar does not fizz. What is the best conclusion from this evidence?
  1. Paracetamol is stronger than the antacid because it does not fizz
  2. The antacid relieves pain and fever, while paracetamol relieves heartburn
  3. Both tablets do the same job because both are tablets
  4. The antacid reacts with acid, so it can neutralise excess stomach acid, while paracetamol does not neutralise acid
Correct answer: D
Fizzing with vinegar (an acid) shows the antacid reacts with acid, which is why it relieves heartburn. Paracetamol gave no reaction and relieves pain and fever by a different action.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 3 - teacher-led sulphur (D) demonstration
Q5. In a teacher-led demonstration, a very small amount of sulphur is heated. It melts and burns with a blue flame and a sharp, choking smell. Why is the demonstration done at a safe distance, with goggles, by an open window?
  1. Sulphur is a magnetic substance that can jump towards the learners
  2. The sharp smell shows that the sulphur has dissolved in the air
  3. Sulphur burning makes sulphur dioxide, a sharp choking gas, so burning it needs care
  4. The blue flame shows the sulphur has turned into sand, which is dangerous
Correct answer: C
Burning sulphur forms sulphur dioxide, which has a sharp choking smell and is harmful to breathe. Industries that use burning reactions must therefore handle them carefully.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class debrief: 'Salt A and sugar H both dissolve. How does that help a food or sugar factory such as Mumias or Nzoia?'
Q6. Sugar dissolves completely in water. A sugar factory such as Mumias or Nzoia wants to get solid sugar from a sugar-and-water solution. Which statement is correct?
  1. Dissolved sugar cannot be filtered out, so the water must be evaporated to leave the sugar behind
  2. A magnet will pull the sugar out, as it does with iron filings
  3. Adding more water will make the sugar solid again
  4. Filtering will work, because sugar is a solid like sand
Correct answer: A
Dissolved particles pass through filter paper, so filtering cannot separate sugar from the solution. Evaporating the water leaves the sugar behind. Sand, which does not dissolve, can be filtered.
Use after: Model Building phase — Building model v2: 'Industries and uses' layer with arrows from properties to industries
Q7. Learners are adding an 'Industries and uses' layer to their class model, with arrows from a property to an industry that uses it. Which arrow is correct?
  1. Burns with a blue flame and a sharp smell, so sulphur is safe to sniff directly
  2. Dissolves in water, so salt and sugar can be removed from water by a magnet
  3. Pulled by a magnet, so sugar is used to make paracetamol
  4. Reacts with acid, so the antacid is used in medicine to relieve heartburn
Correct answer: D
The antacid reacts with and neutralises excess stomach acid, so the property 'reacts with acid' links to the medicine industry. The other arrows join a property to a wrong use or an unsafe action.
