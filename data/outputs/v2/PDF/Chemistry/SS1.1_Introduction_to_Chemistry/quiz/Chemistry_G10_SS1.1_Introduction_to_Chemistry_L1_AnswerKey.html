Use after: Predict phase — Predict: individual prediction and group prediction table (Substance, Prediction, Reason)
Q1. Before any test, Otieno writes in his notebook: "Sulphur (D) will float on water because it looks light." What is this statement at this point in the lesson?
  1. An observation of sulphur floating on water
  2. A scientific explanation of why sulphur floats
  3. A prediction with a reason, which still has to be tested
  4. A result that proves how sulphur behaves in water
Correct answer: C
The learners have not put anything in water yet, so the statement is a prediction with a reason. Predictions are recorded before evidence and tested later.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: recording substances A-H in the observation table
Q2. Learners are at a tray with eight substances in a school laboratory in Kisumu. Which is the correct way to observe them?
  1. Smell each container closely to tell the substances apart
  2. Skip the goggles because most of the substances are ordinary things from home
  3. Wear goggles and use only your eyes and a hand lens
  4. Taste a tiny amount of the white grains to be sure they are salt
Correct answer: C
In the observation activity learners wear goggles, do not taste, and do not sniff from the container. They use their eyes and the hand lens only.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: observation table for substances A-H
Q3. Learners record what substance E, iron filings, looks like on the Kisumu tray. Which description fits what they would record?
  1. A clear liquid
  2. A yellow powder
  3. A white tablet
  4. Grey and gritty grains
Correct answer: D
In the observation table, E (iron filings) is described as grey and gritty. Sulphur is the yellow powder and the tablets are white.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: two different sorts using sorting cards A-H
Q4. One group sorts the eight substances into "powders" and "not powders". A second group sorts the same eight into "things I use in food" and "other things". Salt appears in a group in both sorts. What does this show?
  1. One of the groups must have made a mistake, because a substance can only belong in one group
  2. Substances can be sorted only by colour
  3. The same substance can belong to different groups, depending on the rule used to sort
  4. Only the teacher can decide which group a substance belongs in
Correct answer: C
Each group chose its own rule and then a second rule. The same substances can be grouped in many ways by the visible characteristics chosen. There is no single "right" grouping yet.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'Is it enough to look at a substance to know what it will do?' discussion
Q5. Salt (A), sugar (H) and sand (C) all look like small grains. After sorting and comparing them, what can the class say at this point in the lesson?
  1. Because they look alike, all three will behave in the same way
  2. Looking alike does not tell us how they will behave, so tests are needed
  3. Looking is enough to know exactly what a substance will do
  4. Because they look alike, all three will vanish in water
Correct answer: B
The class has only looked, sorted and predicted. Appearance alone cannot show how a substance behaves, so they need tests (planned for Lesson 2).
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: posting and clustering questions, including Wanjiru's question
Q6. Wanjiru asks: "The antacid tablet and the paracetamol tablet both look like white tablets, so why does one help heartburn and the other help pain?" At this point in the lesson, what is the best response?
  1. It is because one tablet is whiter than the other
  2. It is because one tablet is bigger than the other
  3. It has not been explained yet, so we write it as a question on the Driving Question Board
  4. It is because both are white tablets, so they do the same job
Correct answer: C
Lesson 1 is for noticing and wondering, and no explanation is given. Wanjiru's puzzle is recorded on a sticky note for the Driving Question Board and followed up in later lessons.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'What I notice / What puzzles me' T-chart
Q7. A group fills in a "What I notice / What puzzles me" T-chart about the Kisumu tray. Which entry belongs under "What I notice"?
  1. Why is too much medicine dangerous?
  2. F and G are both white tablets
  3. Can salt be taken out of water?
  4. Why might sulphur burn?
Correct answer: B
An observation is something learners can see on the tray, such as both tablets being white. The other three entries are questions about things not yet known or tested.
