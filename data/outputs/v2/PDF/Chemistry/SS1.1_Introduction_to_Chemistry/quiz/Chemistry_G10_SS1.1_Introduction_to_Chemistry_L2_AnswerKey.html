Q1. A learner stirs 2 g of table salt into 50 mL of water and the salt vanishes, leaving a clear liquid. Which statement is correct?
  1. The salt has dissolved and can be recovered by evaporating the water
  2. The salt has been destroyed, so it cannot be got back
  3. The salt has melted because the water is warm
  4. The salt has sunk to the bottom where it cannot be seen
Correct answer: A
Dissolving does not destroy a substance. The salt is mixed through the water and evaporation brings it back (2.7 g was recovered from mixture M).
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: water test (sand C and sulphur D)
Q2. A learner adds 1 level spatula of sand, and another adds 1 level spatula of sulphur powder, to separate beakers of water at about 25 C and stirs gently. Which result is correct?
  1. Sand sinks and stays visible, while sulphur floats and is not wetted
  2. Both sand and sulphur dissolve to give clear solutions
  3. Sand dissolves and sulphur sinks
  4. Sand floats and sulphur dissolves
Correct answer: A
Neither substance dissolves. Sand sinks and stays visible, while sulphur floats on top and refuses to wet. Powders that look alike do not behave alike.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: mixture M separation (trying to filter a salt solution)
Q3. A salt solution is poured through filter paper into a clean beaker. What passes through the filter paper into the beaker?
  1. Only the water, because the salt is caught on the paper
  2. Both the water and the dissolved salt
  3. Nothing passes through because the paper blocks the solution
  4. Only the salt, because the water stays behind
Correct answer: B
Dissolved salt particles are mixed among the water particles and are too small to be caught by filter paper. This is why salt needs evaporation, not filtration.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: mixture M separation (magnet, filtration, evaporation)
Q4. Mixture M is a dry mixture of iron filings, sand and salt. Which order of steps separates all three substances?
  1. Use a magnet to remove the iron, add water and filter out the sand, then evaporate the filtrate to recover the salt
  2. Add water and filter, then use a magnet on the filtrate, then evaporate the sand
  3. Filter the dry mixture, then use a magnet on the salt, then add water to the sand
  4. Evaporate the dry mixture, then use a magnet, then filter
Correct answer: A
Iron is magnetic, so it is removed first from the dry mixture. Sand does not dissolve, so it is filtered out of the water, and the dissolved salt is recovered by evaporating the filtrate.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: mixture M separation (mass table 9 g in, 8.4 g out)
Q5. A group started with 9 g of mixture M. They recovered 2.9 g of iron filings, 2.8 g of dry sand and 2.7 g of salt. How many grams were lost during the separation?
  1. 1.2 g
  2. 8.4 g
  3. 0.6 g
  4. 0.3 g
Correct answer: C
Total recovered = 2.9 + 2.8 + 2.7 = 8.4 g. Loss = 9 - 8.4 = 0.6 g, which can be explained by spillage or material sticking to the equipment.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: heat test (teacher demonstration)
Q6. Which property correctly separates sulphur from sand when each is heated in a dry test tube?
  1. Sulphur dissolves in water but sand does not
  2. Sulphur is attracted by a magnet but sand is not
  3. Sulphur melts at about 115 C and burns with a blue flame, while sand shows no change
  4. Sulphur does not change on heating, but sand melts and burns
Correct answer: C
Sulphur melts to a runny liquid and then burns with a blue flame and a choking smell. Sand does not change. Sulphur does not dissolve and is not magnetic.
Use after: Explain phase — Second step: particle pictures of solid, liquid and gas (melting of sulphur, paracetamol and sugar)
Q7. Solid sulphur is heated until it melts at about 115 C. Which statement best describes what happens to its particles?
  1. The particles stop moving and stay in fixed positions
  2. The particles disappear and new particles are made
  3. The particles gain energy and move more, staying close together as a liquid
  4. The particles move apart until they fill the whole test tube like a gas
Correct answer: C
In a solid the particles are tightly packed. With heat they move more and the solid becomes a liquid, where the particles are still close together but able to move around.
Use after: Explain phase — Second step: particle pictures (smell of sulphur dioxide spreading as a gas)
Q8. The class smells the sharp, choking sulphur dioxide gas from across the laboratory. Which particle idea best explains this?
  1. Gas particles are close together and slide slowly over each other
  2. Gas particles are heavier than air, so they must sink to the floor and stay there
  3. Gas particles are far apart and move independently, so they spread to fill the space
  4. Gas particles are tightly packed in a fixed shape, so they stay near the flame
Correct answer: C
Gas particles are far apart and move independently, so a gas spreads out to fill the whole container or room. Solids and liquids do not spread like this.
Use after: Explain phase — Third step: CER paragraph (physical versus chemical properties)
Q9. Which of the following is a CHEMICAL property of a substance?
  1. Iron filings being attracted by a magnet
  2. Sulphur burning with a blue flame
  3. Sugar melting at about 186 C
  4. Salt dissolving in water
Correct answer: B
Burning and reacting with acid are chemical properties. Magnetism, melting point and solubility are physical properties.
Use after: Explain phase — Third step: using properties to answer Wanjiru's puzzle about the antacid (F) and paracetamol (G)
Q10. An antacid tablet fizzes when dropped into vinegar, but a paracetamol tablet does not. What does this evidence show?
  1. Paracetamol has been destroyed by the vinegar
  2. Both tablets must dissolve in the same way because they look alike
  3. The two tablets are the same substance because both are white tablets
  4. The antacid reacts with acid but paracetamol does not, so they are different substances with different properties
Correct answer: D
Fizzing with acid is a chemical property of the antacid, and paracetamol shows no reaction. Substances that look alike can have different properties because of what they are made of.
