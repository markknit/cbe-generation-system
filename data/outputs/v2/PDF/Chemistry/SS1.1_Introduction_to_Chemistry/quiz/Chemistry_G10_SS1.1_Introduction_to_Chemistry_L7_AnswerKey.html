Q1. A pharmaceutical scientist at a Kisumu company tests whether an antacid tablet can relieve heartburn. Which property of the tablet is the scientist mainly testing?
  1. The colour of flame it gives when burnt
  2. How it reacts with acid
  3. Whether it floats on water
  4. Whether a magnet attracts it
Correct answer: B
An antacid works by neutralising excess stomach acid, so the key property is its reaction with acid. Magnetism, floating and flame colour do not explain heartburn relief.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: research on analytical chemist or laboratory technician (separating mixture M: magnet, filtration, evaporation)
Q2. A laboratory technician has a mixture of salt, sand and iron filings in water. She pulls out the iron filings with a magnet and then filters out the sand. What should she do next to get the dissolved salt back from the filtrate?
  1. Add more water and stir
  2. Filter the filtrate a second time
  3. Evaporate the water from the filtrate
  4. Use the magnet again on the filtrate
Correct answer: C
Salt is dissolved, so it passes through the filter paper. Evaporating the water leaves the salt behind as a solid.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: research on analytical chemist or laboratory technician (2.9 g, 2.8 g and 2.7 g recovered and 0.6 g lost out of 9 g)
Q3. An analytical chemist separates 9 g of mixture M. She recovers 2.9 g, 2.8 g and 2.7 g of the three solids, and 0.6 g is lost during the separation. What is the total mass recovered?
  1. 8.4 g
  2. 9.0 g
  3. 9.6 g
  4. 8.0 g
Correct answer: A
Total recovered = 2.9 + 2.8 + 2.7 = 8.4 g. This agrees with 9 g - 0.6 g lost = 8.4 g.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph on how pharmaceutical and analytical careers explain why F relieves heartburn and G relieves pain
Q4. Wanjiru asked why an antacid relieves heartburn but paracetamol relieves pain. Which statement gives the correct claim a pharmacist could make?
  1. Antacid neutralises excess stomach acid, while paracetamol relieves pain and fever and does not neutralise acid
  2. Both tablets relieve heartburn because both neutralise stomach acid
  3. Paracetamol relieves heartburn better because it is stronger
  4. Antacid relieves pain and fever, while paracetamol neutralises stomach acid
Correct answer: A
An antacid reacts with and neutralises excess stomach acid. Paracetamol relieves pain and fever but does not neutralise acid, so the two tablets do different jobs.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph using MEDICINE FACTS (8 tablets of 500 mg = 4 g in 24 hours)
Q5. An adult may take a maximum of 8 paracetamol tablets of 500 mg each in 24 hours. What is this maximum in grams? (1 g = 1000 mg)
  1. 0.04 g
  2. 40 g
  3. 0.4 g
  4. 4 g
Correct answer: D
8 x 500 mg = 4000 mg. Dividing by 1000 gives 4 g. Taking more than this can damage the liver.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: stereotype discussion 'What is a gender stereotype?'
Q6. Which of these best describes a gender stereotype?
  1. A fact about which careers men and women are trained for
  2. A scientific law that explains why some people are better at chemistry
  3. A fixed idea about what girls or boys can or should do
  4. A rule that only one gender may work in a laboratory
Correct answer: C
A gender stereotype is a fixed idea about what girls or boys can or should do. It is an opinion and not a scientific fact or a rule.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: stereotype discussion and plenary presentations on who can do these careers
Q7. Otieno says, 'Chemistry jobs like pharmacist or analytical chemist are only for boys.' Which reply is best?
  1. He is right, because most chemists in the past were men
  2. He is wrong only for pharmacists, but right for analytical chemists
  3. A stereotype can limit career choice, but it does not change who can do chemistry; girls and boys can both work in these careers
  4. He is right, because girls are not good at separating mixtures
Correct answer: C
A stereotype is a fixed idea that may discourage some learners, but it does not decide who can do chemistry. The career profiles showed women and men working as chemists, so the claim has no evidence behind it.
