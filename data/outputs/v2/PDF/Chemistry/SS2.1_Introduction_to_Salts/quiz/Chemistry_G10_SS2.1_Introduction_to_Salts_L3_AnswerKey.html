Q1. Zinc granules are added to dilute sulphuric acid in a conical flask. What will you observe?
  1. The solution turns blue and a white solid forms at once
  2. Nothing happens because zinc is a solid
  3. Bubbles of gas form on the zinc, which slowly dissolves
  4. A black solid dissolves and the solution turns blue
Correct answer: C
Zinc reacts with dilute sulphuric acid to give zinc sulphate solution and hydrogen gas, so bubbles appear and the zinc dissolves. The blue colour belongs to the copper(II) oxide reaction, not zinc.
Use after: Observe phase — Experiment A: collecting hydrogen and doing the pop test
Q2. A learner collects a gas over water from the reaction of zinc with dilute sulphuric acid. She brings a burning splint to the mouth of the test tube and hears a squeaky 'pop'. Which gas is present?
  1. Oxygen
  2. Hydrogen
  3. Ammonia
  4. Carbon dioxide
Correct answer: B
Hydrogen burns explosively with a squeaky pop when a burning splint is brought to it. Oxygen relights a glowing splint, and carbon dioxide puts the flame out.
Use after: Observe phase — Experiment A: adding excess zinc until the reaction stops, then filtering
Q3. In the preparation of zinc sulphate, excess zinc granules are added to the dilute sulphuric acid until the reaction stops. Why is excess zinc used?
  1. To turn the solution blue so that it is easy to see
  2. To make sure all the acid is used up, so the filtrate contains only zinc sulphate solution
  3. To make the zinc sulphate insoluble so that it can be filtered
  4. To make the reaction produce more hydrogen gas
Correct answer: B
Excess metal makes sure that all the acid reacts. The leftover zinc is then filtered off, which leaves a filtrate with no acid in it. Zinc sulphate stays dissolved and is soluble.
Use after: Observe phase — Experiment B: preparing copper sulphate from black CuO and dilute sulphuric acid
Q4. Black copper(II) oxide powder is added to dilute sulphuric acid and the mixture is warmed gently and stirred. What do you observe?
  1. The solution stays colourless and a brown solid forms
  2. Bubbles of hydrogen gas form and the black powder stays unchanged
  3. The black powder turns white and a pop is heard
  4. The black powder dissolves and the solution turns blue
Correct answer: D
Copper(II) oxide reacts with the acid to form copper sulphate solution, which is blue, and water. No gas is produced, so there are no bubbles and no pop.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 2 (equation writing): Reaction B, CuO + H₂SO₄
Q5. Which equation correctly represents the reaction of copper(II) oxide with dilute sulphuric acid?
  1. CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Cu(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) + O₂(g)
  2. CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)
  3. CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₃(aq) + H₂O(l)
  4. CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l)
Correct answer: D
A metal oxide plus an acid gives a salt plus water. The equation is balanced: Cu 1=1, S 1=1, H 2=2 and O 5=5. Hydrogen gas is not made in this reaction, and the sulphate ion is SO₄²⁻, not SO₃²⁻.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 2 (equation writing): identifying the acid, the metal and the salt product in each equation
Q6. Which statement best describes how a salt such as zinc sulphate is formed from an acid?
  1. The hydrogen in the acid is replaced by a metal ion
  2. The sulphate part of the acid is replaced by oxygen
  3. The acid gains extra hydrogen atoms from the metal
  4. The whole acid molecule is destroyed and only water remains
Correct answer: A
A salt forms when the hydrogen of an acid is fully or partly replaced by a positive ion, such as a metal ion or the ammonium ion. In H₂SO₄ the hydrogen is replaced by Zn²⁺ to give ZnSO₄.
Use after: DQB phase — Step 3 (phenomenon connection) and the Driving Question Board orange cards
Q7. A learner says: 'Calcium ammonium nitrate fertiliser clumped in the storeroom because it is a salt made from an acid.' Table salt and washing soda are also soluble salts made from acids, but they behaved differently in the same air. What is the best conclusion?
  1. Knowing how a salt is made does not explain the clumping, so we must investigate how each salt behaves with water in the air
  2. The statement is correct, because all salts made from acids take in water from the air
  3. The statement is wrong, because CAN is not made from an acid
  4. The statement is correct, because soluble salts always absorb water from the air
Correct answer: A
Table salt, washing soda and CAN are all soluble salts made by acid reactions, yet they behave differently. How a salt is made therefore cannot explain the clumping, and the salts' own properties and the air must be investigated.
Use after: Model Building phase — Speech bubble on the CAN fertiliser bag in the cumulative class model
Q8. Ammonium nitrate (NH₄NO₃) is the soluble salt in CAN fertiliser. Which pair of reactants could be used to make it, by the same type of acid reaction used in today's lesson?
  1. Hydrochloric acid and copper(II) oxide
  2. Sodium chloride and water
  3. Sulphuric acid and zinc metal
  4. Nitric acid and ammonia solution (a base)
Correct answer: D
Ammonium nitrate contains the nitrate ion from nitric acid and the ammonium ion from ammonia. The hydrogen of nitric acid is replaced by the ammonium ion when the acid is neutralised by the base.
