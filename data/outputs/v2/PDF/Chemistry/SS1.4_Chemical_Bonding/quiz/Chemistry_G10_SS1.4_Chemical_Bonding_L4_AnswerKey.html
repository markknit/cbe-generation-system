Q1. Carbon has the electron arrangement 2,4. Using this, how many covalent bonds can one carbon atom form to reach a stable noble gas arrangement?
  1. 4
  2. 8
  3. 6
  4. 2
Correct answer: A
Carbon has 4 valence electrons and needs 8 for a full outer shell, so it shares 4 electrons by forming 4 covalent bonds (8 - 4 = 4).
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: ARES simulation and Observation Table (O₂ double bond, lone pairs per atom)
Q2. In an oxygen molecule (O₂) the two atoms share a double bond. Each oxygen atom has 6 valence electrons. How many lone pairs does each oxygen atom have in O₂?
  1. 4
  2. 2
  3. 1
  4. 3
Correct answer: B
Each oxygen shares 2 of its electrons in the double bond, leaving 6 - 2 = 4 non-bonding electrons, which is 2 lone pairs.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity B: guided dot-and-cross construction, board pass check for CH₄
Q3. Which description of a correct dot-and-cross diagram for methane (CH₄) is right?
  1. Carbon has 2 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs, and only 2 hydrogens are attached
  2. Carbon has 4 bonding pairs and no lone pairs, and each hydrogen shares 1 pair
  3. Carbon has 4 bonding pairs and 1 lone pair, and each hydrogen has 8 electrons
  4. Carbon transfers 4 electrons to 4 hydrogen atoms to form ions
Correct answer: B
Carbon shares its 4 valence electrons with 4 hydrogen atoms, giving 4 bonding pairs and no lone pairs. Each hydrogen has 2 electrons, like helium.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: dative covalent bonds (NH₄⁺ from NH₃ and H⁺)
Q4. In the ammonium ion (NH₄⁺), a hydrogen ion (H⁺, which has no electrons) joins an NH₃ molecule. Which statement correctly describes the new N–H bond?
  1. The hydrogen ion donates both electrons of the shared pair to nitrogen
  2. Nitrogen transfers one electron completely to the hydrogen ion
  3. Nitrogen donates both electrons of its lone pair to form the shared pair
  4. Nitrogen and the hydrogen ion each give one electron to the shared pair
Correct answer: C
H⁺ has no electrons, so nitrogen supplies both electrons of the shared pair from its lone pair. This is a dative covalent bond.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: teacher's explanation of the dative bond arrow in NH₄⁺
Q5. After the dative bond has formed in NH₄⁺, how does it compare with the other three N–H bonds in the ion?
  1. It is weaker, because only one atom supplied the electrons
  2. It is stronger, because nitrogen gave both electrons
  3. It is identical to the others; the arrow only shows where the electrons came from
  4. It is ionic, because nitrogen has donated electrons
Correct answer: C
Once formed, a dative covalent bond is the same as an ordinary covalent bond. The arrow only records the origin of the shared pair.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Bonding Comparison Card, why graphite conducts but diamond cannot
Q6. In graphite each carbon atom forms 3 covalent bonds in a flat hexagonal layer. Which statement correctly explains why graphite conducts electricity?
  1. Graphite contains positive and negative ions that move when a current flows
  2. Each carbon uses 3 of its 4 valence electrons in bonds, and the 4th is delocalised and free to move
  3. Each carbon forms 4 bonds, so extra electrons are pushed out of the layer
  4. Each carbon has a lone pair that moves between the layers
Correct answer: B
Three of carbon's 4 valence electrons are in bonding pairs. The 4th electron is not held in a bond; it is delocalised and carries charge through the layers.
Use after: Model Building phase — Covalent bonding panel: diamond tetrahedral unit labelled 'no free electrons → insulator'
Q7. In diamond each carbon atom forms 4 covalent bonds in a 3-D tetrahedral network. Why is diamond an electrical insulator?
  1. Diamond contains ions that are locked in position
  2. All 4 valence electrons of every carbon are held in bonding pairs, so none are free to move
  3. Diamond is too hard for electrons to pass through
  4. Diamond carbon atoms have lone pairs that cannot move
Correct answer: B
Each carbon uses all 4 valence electrons in four bonds, so there are no delocalised electrons to carry charge.
Use after: Explain phase — Part C: dot-and-cross comparison of NaCl and covalent diagrams (T-chart IONIC | COVALENT)
Q8. Which pair of statements correctly compares sodium chloride (ionic) with methane (covalent)?
  1. Both NaCl and CH₄ form by sharing electrons, but NaCl shares more pairs
  2. In NaCl electrons are transferred to form ions, and in CH₄ electrons are shared in pairs
  3. Both NaCl and CH₄ form by transferring electrons, but CH₄ makes bigger ions
  4. In NaCl electrons are shared, and in CH₄ electrons are transferred
Correct answer: B
Sodium transfers an electron to chlorine, forming ions in a lattice. In methane, carbon and hydrogen atoms share electron pairs to form discrete molecules.
