Q1. Solid sodium chloride (NaCl) does not conduct electricity, but a solution of NaCl in water does. Which statement best explains this?
  1. In solution the NaCl forms small molecules that carry electric current
  2. In the solid the delocalised electrons are trapped, but in water they are released
  3. In the solid the ions are held in fixed positions, but in solution the ions are free to move and carry charge
  4. In the solid there are no ions, but dissolving in water creates ions from nothing
Correct answer: C
NaCl is made of Na+ and Cl- ions held in a rigid lattice, so charge cannot flow. When dissolved (or molten) the ions are free to move and carry the current.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: teacher question to Wanjiku about sugar not conducting even in solution
Q2. Sugar does not conduct electricity as a solid or when dissolved in water. What does this tell us about sugar?
  1. It is an ionic compound whose ions cannot move in water
  2. It is a giant metallic structure whose electrons are locked in place
  3. It is a simple molecular substance made of neutral molecules, with no ions or free electrons to carry charge
  4. It has delocalised electrons that are too weak to carry current
Correct answer: C
Sugar consists of neutral molecules, so it forms no ions in solution and has no free electrons. Therefore it cannot conduct in any state.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: sentence-frame scaffold for aluminium (giant metallic) and check on using 'delocalised electrons'
Q3. Aluminium, used for mabati roofing, conducts electricity even as a solid. Which explanation is correct?
  1. Its positive ions are free to move through the solid
  2. It contains Al+ and Cl- ions that move when heated
  3. Its delocalised electrons are free to move through the lattice of metal ions
  4. Its atoms share electrons in pairs to form molecules that carry charge
Correct answer: C
Metals have a lattice of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons. These electrons can move in the solid state and carry the current. Ions in a solid metal stay in fixed positions.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: sentence-frame scaffold for SiO2 (giant covalent) – conductivity and melting point
Q4. Sand (SiO2) is a giant covalent structure. Which pair of properties does it have, and why?
  1. It does not conduct and has a very high melting point, because it has no free ions or electrons and many strong covalent bonds must be broken
  2. It conducts when solid and has a low melting point, because its electrons are free to move
  3. It does not conduct and has a low melting point, because its molecules are held by weak forces
  4. It conducts only when dissolved and has a high melting point, because it contains ions
Correct answer: A
In SiO2 all outer electrons are held in strong covalent bonds throughout the network, so there are no free charge carriers. A lot of energy is needed to break these bonds, giving a very high melting point.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: graphite resolution and cold-call exit question 'why does graphite conduct but diamond does not'
Q5. Both diamond and graphite are made only of carbon, yet graphite conducts electricity and diamond does not. What is the best explanation?
  1. Graphite contains metal ions that carry the current
  2. Graphite has more protons in its carbon atoms than diamond
  3. In graphite each carbon forms three bonds, leaving one delocalised electron per atom free to move along the layer; in diamond all four electrons are held in bonds
  4. Diamond has ions that are locked in place, but graphite has free ions
Correct answer: C
Graphite carbon atoms use only three electrons for bonding in flat layers, leaving one delocalised electron each. In diamond each carbon forms four bonds, so there are no free electrons.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: teacher's two-panel diagram of diamond and graphite (layers held by van der Waals forces)
Q6. Graphite is soft enough to write with, even though the bonds inside each of its layers are strong. Why is it soft?
  1. The layers are held to each other only by weak van der Waals forces, so they slide over each other easily
  2. It has delocalised electrons, which make any solid soft
  3. The covalent bonds inside the layers are weak
  4. Its carbon atoms are bonded in a 3-D tetrahedral network
Correct answer: A
Strong covalent bonds exist only within the layers. The forces between layers are weak van der Waals forces, so layers slide off easily and leave a mark.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB phase: green 'We Can Now Explain' sticky notes (teacher flags notes missing 'free-moving ions')
Q7. A student wrote on a green sticky note: 'NaCl conducts because it is ionic.' What important idea is missing to make this a complete explanation?
  1. NaCl conducts because its molecules carry charge
  2. NaCl conducts because it has delocalised electrons
  3. NaCl conducts only when its ions are free to move, that is, when it is dissolved in water or molten
  4. NaCl conducts in the solid state because the ions move through the crystal
Correct answer: C
Being ionic is not enough. Conduction needs mobile ions, which are only free in solution or in the molten state, not in the solid lattice.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model phase: exit ticket Part 3 (circle the bond type that explains the conductivity of graphite)
Q8. Which bond type explains the anomalous electrical conductivity of graphite?
  1. Ionic bond
  2. Hydrogen bond
  3. Metallic bond between metal ions
  4. Covalent bond with delocalised electrons
Correct answer: D
Graphite is a giant covalent structure in which each carbon uses three electrons in covalent bonds, and the fourth electron is delocalised along the layer and carries charge.
