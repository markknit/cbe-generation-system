Use after: Predict phase — Predict phase: annotating the original three predictions after the demonstrations (circle matches, cross out mismatches)
Q1. Before any demonstration, a student wrote: "Both samples are 100% carbon with the same number of protons, neutrons and electrons, so both will leave the same mark on a ceramic tile and both will light a bulb." After the scratch test and the conductivity test, what can be said about this prediction?
  1. It was supported by the scratch test but not by the conductivity test
  2. It cannot be checked, because predictions are never compared with observations
  3. It was supported, because both samples behaved in the same way in both tests
  4. It was not supported, because the two samples behaved differently in both tests
Correct answer: D
Graphite smeared grey while diamond scratched the tile, and graphite lit the bulb while diamond did not. The samples differed in both tests, so the prediction of identical behaviour was not supported.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe phase: Demo 1 — Scratch Test on the ceramic tile
Q2. In the scratch test, a graphite stick and a diamond chip were each pressed firmly across a white ceramic tile. Which description records only what was observed?
  1. Diamond left a grey smear, while graphite left a white scratch in the tile
  2. Both samples left a grey smear and the tile was not damaged
  3. Graphite left a broad grey smear, while diamond left a white scratch in the tile itself
  4. Neither sample left any mark on the tile
Correct answer: C
The graphite smeared grey on the tile, while the diamond gouged a white scratch into the tile surface and was itself undamaged.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe phase: Demo 2 — Conductivity Test with the bulb-and-battery tester
Q3. A simple 6 V battery-and-bulb conductivity tester was touched to a graphite stick and then to a diamond chip. What was observed?
  1. The bulb did not light with either sample
  2. The bulb lit brightly with graphite but did not light at all with diamond
  3. The bulb lit with diamond but did not light with graphite
  4. The bulb lit with both samples
Correct answer: B
Graphite completed the circuit and the bulb lit brightly. Diamond did not light the bulb at all.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe phase: completing the structured Observation Table (Property | Diamond | Graphite | My Surprise Rating)
Q4. Students were told to write observations in the Observation Table, not explanations. Which of these statements is an observation rather than an explanation?
  1. The bulb lit because graphite is black and so it is a better material
  2. The bulb lit because graphite is cheaper than diamond
  3. The bulb did not light because diamond is too shiny
  4. The bulb lit when the probes touched the graphite stick
Correct answer: D
Saying that the bulb lit when the probes touched graphite describes what the eyes saw. The other statements use "because" to give a reason that the demonstration did not show.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: Group Explanation Attempt and gallery walk
Q5. Both the diamond chip and the graphite stick were labelled "100% Carbon — no other elements." Which statement best describes the puzzle the class was left with?
  1. The two samples behaved differently because one was a solid and the other was not
  2. The diamond must contain an extra element that the graphite does not have
  3. The graphite must contain more protons than the diamond
  4. Two samples made only of carbon behaved very differently in hardness, conduction and appearance, and the class cannot yet explain why
Correct answer: D
The paradox is that two pure-carbon samples showed very different properties. At this stage no group has a complete explanation, which is what makes it a puzzle. The other choices are misconceptions that the labels contradict.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: Group Explanation Attempt written in groups of 4
Q6. During the Group Explanation Attempt, the teacher said: "A good scientific explanation attempt is one that is logical and based on evidence — not one that is correct." Which explanation attempt best fits this advice?
  1. Refusing to write anything until the correct answer is known
  2. A paragraph that uses the scratch and conductivity observations as evidence and is honest about what it cannot yet explain
  3. Copying the answer from a textbook without using the observation table
  4. Choosing whichever idea most students in the class like best
Correct answer: B
A good attempt links reasoning to the observed evidence, even if it is incomplete. Copying, refusing to try or voting does not use the evidence.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB phase: writing one question per sticky note and posting them on the Driving Question Board
Q7. Students posted questions on the Driving Question Board (DQB) that the demonstration raised but did NOT answer. Which question belongs on the DQB?
  1. What colour was the sticky note I used?
  2. Does the graphite stick make the bulb light?
  3. I wonder why diamond scratches the tile but graphite only leaves a smear
  4. Was the graphite stick taken from an HB pencil?
Correct answer: C
A good DQB question is about the phenomenon and is still unanswered. The question about the bulb was answered by the demonstration, the HB pencil was stated in the lesson, and the sticky note colour has nothing to do with the puzzle.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model phase: drawing the Initial Model and storing it in the Model Envelope
Q8. At the end of the lesson, each student draws an Initial Model of the carbon atoms inside diamond and graphite, signs and dates it, and the models are stored in Model Envelopes. Why are the models kept?
  1. So students can compare them with their final model at the end of the unit and see how their thinking has changed
  2. So they can be marked for correctness in this lesson
  3. So the class can copy the best drawing as the right answer
  4. So they can be thrown away once the DQB is complete
Correct answer: A
The envelope holds a "before" model so that growth in understanding can be seen when it is returned at the end of the unit. The drawings are not marked as right or wrong today.
