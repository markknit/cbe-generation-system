Q1. Diamond and graphite are both made only of carbon atoms. Diamond does not conduct electricity at all. Which statement best explains why?
  1. Diamond has delocalised electrons, but they are trapped between layers
  2. Each carbon atom uses all four outer electrons in strong covalent bonds, so no electrons are free to move
  3. Diamond atoms have no electrons
  4. Diamond has ionic bonds, and ionic solids never conduct
Correct answer: B
In diamond each carbon forms four covalent bonds in a tetrahedral network, so all outer electrons are held in bonds. With no free-moving charged particles, diamond cannot conduct.

Q2. Graphite from a pencil lights a bulb when connected in a circuit. Which feature of its structure explains this?
  1. Each carbon atom forms four bonds in a tetrahedral network
  2. Graphite contains metal atoms mixed in with the carbon
  3. Each carbon atom forms three bonds in flat hexagonal layers, leaving one delocalised electron per atom free to move
  4. Graphite is made of positive and negative ions that move when connected
Correct answer: C
Each carbon in graphite uses only three outer electrons for bonding in hexagonal layers. The fourth electron is delocalised and carries charge through the layers.

Q3. A group presents a 3-D model of a sodium chloride (NaCl) lattice. Which description of the bonding and a matching property is correct?
  1. Van der Waals forces: weak forces between ions make NaCl soft and easy to melt
  2. Ionic bonds: strong attractions between oppositely charged ions in a giant lattice give a high melting point
  3. Covalent bonds: shared electron pairs between separate NaCl molecules give a low melting point
  4. Metallic bonds: a sea of delocalised electrons lets solid NaCl conduct electricity
Correct answer: B
NaCl is a giant ionic lattice. Many strong electrostatic attractions between Na+ and Cl- ions need a lot of energy to break, so the melting point is high.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class consolidation: completing the unit comparison table (giant covalent row)
Q4. Silicon dioxide (SiO2), the main compound in sand used in construction, is a continuous network of atoms joined by strong covalent bonds throughout the whole structure. In the class comparison table, which structure type does it belong to, and why?
  1. Giant metallic, because it is hard and has a high melting point
  2. Simple molecular, because it is made of covalent bonds
  3. Giant covalent, because strong covalent bonds link all the atoms in one continuous network
  4. Giant ionic, because it contains a metal and a non-metal
Correct answer: C
A substance whose atoms are all joined by covalent bonds in a continuous network is giant covalent, like diamond. It is not simple molecular, because there are no small separate molecules.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board final revision: answering the graphite and pencil question with evidence
Q5. Graphite is soft enough to write with, even though the bonds between carbon atoms inside each layer are strong. What explains this?
  1. The covalent bonds inside the layers are weaker than those in diamond
  2. Graphite has fewer protons than diamond
  3. The delocalised electrons break the layers apart when pressed
  4. Each layer is held to the next by weak Van der Waals forces, so layers slide off easily
Correct answer: D
Strong covalent bonds hold atoms within a layer. Only weak Van der Waals forces hold the layers together, so layers slide and rub off onto paper.
Use after: Model Building phase — Exit ticket: Section 3, property to use (Kenyan context)
Q6. An aluminium sufuria (cooking pot) heats up quickly on a jiko and food cooks evenly. Which property of metallic bonding best explains why the metal conducts heat well?
  1. Metal atoms share electrons in fixed pairs between two atoms only
  2. A sea of delocalised electrons moves freely through the lattice of metal ions and carries energy
  3. Weak Van der Waals forces between layers let heat pass through
  4. Positive and negative ions are held in a rigid lattice with no free particles
Correct answer: B
Metals have delocalised electrons that move freely among positive metal ions. They carry thermal energy quickly, which makes metals suitable for cookware.
Use after: End of lesson — Exit ticket: Section 4, The Carbon Paradox (three-sentence explanation)
Q7. Which statement is the best three-part summary of the Carbon Paradox for diamond and graphite?
  1. Diamond has more protons than graphite, so it is harder and does not conduct
  2. Graphite has more electrons than diamond, so it conducts
  3. Diamond has ionic bonds and graphite has covalent bonds, so their properties differ
  4. Both contain only carbon, but diamond has four bonds per atom in a 3-D network, while graphite has three bonds per atom in layers with one delocalised electron, so their properties differ
Correct answer: D
Diamond and graphite have identical atoms. The difference in properties comes only from how the atoms are bonded: a tetrahedral network in diamond, and layers with delocalised electrons in graphite.
