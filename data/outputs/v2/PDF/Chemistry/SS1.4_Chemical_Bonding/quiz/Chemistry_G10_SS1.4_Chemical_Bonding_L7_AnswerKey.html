Q1. Two white crystalline solids look alike: table salt (NaCl) and white sugar. Which statement classifies them correctly by structure and bonding?
  1. NaCl is a simple molecular structure; sugar is a giant ionic structure
  2. Both are simple molecular structures because both dissolve in water
  3. Both are giant ionic structures because both are white crystals
  4. NaCl is a giant ionic structure; sugar is a simple molecular structure
Correct answer: D
NaCl is a lattice of oppositely charged ions (giant ionic). Sugar is made of separate covalently bonded molecules held together by weak intermolecular forces (simple molecular). Appearance alone cannot decide the structure type.
Use after: Predict phase — Individual prediction: Column 1 ('Predicted Structure Type') of the Evidence Prediction Table, Unknown D (aluminium foil pellet)
Q2. A strip of aluminium foil is predicted to conduct electricity even as a solid. Which reason is correct?
  1. Its molecules are free to move and carry charge
  2. It contains ions that are free to move through the solid
  3. Its atoms share electrons in covalent bonds that hold all electrons tightly in place
  4. Its delocalised electrons are free to move through the giant metallic structure
Correct answer: D
Metals have a giant metallic structure with delocalised electrons that move freely and carry charge. Aluminium has no mobile ions or separate molecules in the solid.

Q3. Sand (silicon dioxide, a giant covalent structure) has a melting point far higher than sugar (a simple molecular substance). What is the best particle-level explanation?
  1. Sand has stronger intermolecular forces between its molecules than sugar has
  2. Sand is grey-white and sugar is white, so the colour makes the difference
  3. Melting sand needs a huge amount of energy to break the many strong covalent bonds throughout the network, but melting sugar only needs weak forces between molecules to be overcome
  4. Melting sugar breaks the covalent bonds inside each sugar molecule, which are weaker than the bonds in sand
Correct answer: C
In a giant covalent structure every atom is joined to its neighbours by strong covalent bonds that must be broken to melt it. In sugar only the weak intermolecular forces between molecules are overcome, so the melting point is much lower.
Use after: Observe phase — Written predictions of electrical conductivity (solid, and dissolved or molten) for each structural type, with the prompt 'What would have to be free and moving?'
Q4. A group predicts that solid NaCl will not light a bulb in a conductivity tester, but that a solution of NaCl in water will. What reason should they give?
  1. In the solid the ions are fixed in the lattice, but in solution the ions are free to move and carry charge
  2. NaCl is a simple molecular substance, so only its solution conducts
  3. In the solid there are no charged particles at all, but dissolving creates new ones
  4. Water itself carries the current, so the NaCl does not matter
Correct answer: A
Conduction needs charged particles that are free and moving. Solid NaCl has ions locked in a lattice; dissolving frees them to move.
Use after: Observe phase — Circulating question: 'Kericho tea farmers dissolve fertiliser salts in water every season. Which of your unknowns do you predict will behave the same way?'
Q5. Kericho tea farmers dissolve fertiliser salts in water every season. Which of these four substances would you predict to dissolve in water in a similar way to a giant ionic fertiliser salt?
  1. Graphite from a pencil
  2. Table salt (NaCl)
  3. Fine dry sand (SiO₂)
  4. Aluminium foil
Correct answer: B
NaCl is a giant ionic compound, like fertiliser salts, and is predicted to dissolve in water. Sand and graphite are giant covalent and aluminium is giant metallic, so none are predicted to dissolve.
Use after: Explain phase — Graphite discussion: targeted cold-call on which property differs for graphite versus diamond and sand (bonus unknowns E and F)
Q6. Diamond and graphite are both giant covalent structures made only of carbon. Which property is predicted to be different, and why?
  1. Electrical conductivity, because diamond contains free ions that graphite lacks
  2. Solubility in water, because graphite dissolves as its layers are held by weak forces
  3. Electrical conductivity, because each carbon in graphite uses three bonds, leaving one delocalised electron free to move between layers
  4. Melting point, because graphite has no covalent bonds
Correct answer: C
Graphite carbon atoms form three bonds in flat layers, leaving one delocalised electron each that can move and carry charge. Diamond's four bonds per carbon leave no free electrons, so it does not conduct.
Use after: Explain phase — Peer review: Claim–Evidence–Reasoning (CER) protocol, circling cells where the justification is only an observation
Q7. During a peer review, a group must choose the justification that gives particle-level REASONING for diamond's very high melting point, not just an observation. Which one is it?
  1. Diamond is very hard and sparkles
  2. Diamond has a high melting point because it is a solid at room temperature
  3. Diamond has a high melting point because the Nairobi jeweller's tool is made of it
  4. Diamond has a high melting point because each carbon is covalently bonded to four others in a 3-D network, and a lot of energy is needed to break these strong bonds
Correct answer: D
Good reasoning links bond type to structure to property. Only this choice names the covalent bonds, the 3-D network and the energy needed. The others only describe or restate observations.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: ORANGE card 'Testable Claim I am now confident about'
Q8. Which learner's ORANGE card is the best testable claim linked to bond type for Lesson 8?
  1. Sand is grey-white and gritty.
  2. How do we measure a melting point?
  3. I think Unknown A will dissolve.
  4. I predict that sand will not dissolve in water because it is a giant covalent structure with strong bonds throughout the network.
Correct answer: D
A good testable claim follows the format 'I predict that [substance] will [result] because [bond-type reason]'. The other choices are a bare guess, a procedural question and an observation.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative Bonding Concept Model: adding the Property Prediction Ring, Giant Covalent node (diamond and graphite)
Q9. On the Property Prediction Ring of the Bonding Concept Model, a group fills in the Giant Covalent node for diamond and graphite. Which pair of predictions is correct?
  1. Both have a high melting point; diamond conducts electricity but graphite does not
  2. Both have a high melting point, and both conduct electricity
  3. Both have a high melting point; diamond does not conduct electricity but graphite does
  4. Both have a low melting point; diamond does not conduct but graphite does
Correct answer: C
Both are giant covalent with strong bonds throughout, so both have high melting points. Only graphite has delocalised electrons, so only graphite is predicted to conduct. The two conductivity boxes must differ.
