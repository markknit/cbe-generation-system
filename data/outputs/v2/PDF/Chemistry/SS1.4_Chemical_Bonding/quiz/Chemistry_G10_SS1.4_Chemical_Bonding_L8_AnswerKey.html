Q1. A group tests sodium chloride (NaCl) with a conductivity tester. The bulb stays off when the probes touch solid NaCl, but it glows brightly when the probes are dipped in a NaCl solution. What has changed between the two tests?
  1. The water has added extra electrons to the NaCl
  2. The NaCl has become a metal, so electrons can now flow
  3. The sodium and chloride atoms have joined to form molecules
  4. The ions in NaCl are now free to move in the water and carry charge
Correct answer: D
In solid NaCl the ions are locked in the lattice, so no charge can flow. When dissolved, the ions are free to move and carry current, so the bulb lights.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: Conductivity Testing (dissolved samples) after Station A solubility results
Q2. Sugar (sucrose) is stirred into 50 mL of distilled water and dissolves completely. The conductivity tester is then dipped into the sugar solution. What result is expected?
  1. The bulb stays off because sugar did not dissolve
  2. The bulb glows brightly, like NaCl solution, because sugar dissolved
  3. The bulb stays off because sugar is a simple molecular compound and forms no free ions in solution
  4. The bulb glows dimly because sugar is a metal
Correct answer: C
Sugar is a simple molecular compound. It dissolves as whole molecules, with no mobile ions or electrons, so the solution does not conduct.
Use after: Observe phase — Station A: Solubility Testing (SiO₂ sand) and Station C: Melting Point Data Cards
Q3. Fine sand from a Kenyan river bed is mostly SiO₂, a giant covalent structure. A spatula of sand is stirred in 50 mL of water for 30 seconds. Which description best matches what is expected for SiO₂?
  1. It does not dissolve, but it melts easily
  2. It does not dissolve, leaving a residue, and it has a very high melting point
  3. It dissolves to form a clear solution and has a low melting point
  4. It dissolves and conducts electricity in solution
Correct answer: B
Giant covalent structures such as SiO₂ are generally insoluble in water. Their strong covalent bonds throughout the structure give very high melting points.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: Conductivity Testing (solid samples)
Q4. A group tests five substances as solids with a conductivity tester: NaCl crystals, sugar crystals, dry SiO₂ sand and aluminium foil. Which one is expected to light the bulb in the solid state?
  1. Aluminium foil
  2. NaCl crystals
  3. Sugar crystals
  4. Dry SiO₂ sand
Correct answer: A
Aluminium has a giant metallic structure with free-moving electrons, so it conducts when solid. The other three do not conduct as solids.
Use after: Observe phase — Station C: Melting Point Data Cards (diamond and graphite cards) and Station B critical connection
Q5. Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon. Data cards show diamond has a melting point above 3,500 °C. Graphite does not melt at normal pressure and sublimes at about 3,650 °C. In the conductivity test, graphite lights the bulb but diamond does not. Which statement matches this evidence?
  1. Both conduct, because both are made of the same atoms
  2. Graphite melts at a much lower temperature than diamond because it is soft
  3. Diamond conducts and graphite does not, because diamond is harder
  4. Both have extremely high melting points, but only graphite conducts electricity
Correct answer: D
Both carbon forms have extremely high melting points, but only graphite conducts. This is the carbon paradox: same atoms, different property.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: Conductivity Testing (beaker of water alone as a control)
Q6. At Station B, a beaker of the water alone (with no substance dissolved) is tested with the conductivity tester as a control. Why is this control test done?
  1. To warm the probes before testing the other solutions
  2. To dissolve any residue left on the probes
  3. To show that all water conducts electricity better than solids
  4. To make sure the bulb lighting with a solution is due to the dissolved substance and not the water itself
Correct answer: D
A control gives a baseline. If the water alone does not light the bulb, a bright bulb in a solution can be attributed to the dissolved substance.
Use after: Explain phase — Pattern Statement task, Starter 1 (after the master data table is built)
Q7. From the class master table, a learner completes this Pattern Statement: 'Substances that conduct electricity in solution but not as solids seem to have ____ bonds.' Which word correctly fills the gap?
  1. Ionic
  2. Metallic
  3. Giant covalent
  4. Simple molecular
Correct answer: A
Ionic compounds such as NaCl do not conduct as solids because their ions are fixed. They conduct in solution because the ions become free to move. Metals conduct as solids.
Use after: Model Building phase — Bonding Concept Model update: 'Giant Covalent' row, diamond/graphite two-cell sub-table
Q8. In the 'Giant Covalent' row of your model, you draw a two-cell table for diamond and graphite. Which entry best explains why graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not?
  1. Diamond contains no carbon, while graphite does
  2. In graphite, each carbon atom has one delocalised electron free to move between the layers. In diamond, all four outer electrons are held in bonds
  3. Graphite has more protons in its atoms than diamond
  4. Graphite is a metal, while diamond is a simple molecular compound
Correct answer: B
Graphite has three bonds per carbon atom in flat layers, leaving one delocalised electron per atom free to move. Diamond has four bonds per carbon atom in a 3-D network, so no electrons are free.
