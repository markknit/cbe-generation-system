Use after: Predict phase — Predict: written prediction 'What do you predict happens to the electrons in a metal like aluminium?'
Q1. In sodium chloride the electrons are transferred, and in water they are shared between two specific atoms. In a piece of aluminium foil, where every atom is the same element, what is the best description of what happens to the outer (valence) electrons?
  1. They are released into a shared pool and move freely throughout the whole structure
  2. They stay on their own aluminium atom and do not move
  3. They are transferred from one aluminium atom to another aluminium atom and stay there
  4. They are shared in pairs between two specific neighbouring aluminium atoms
Correct answer: A
In metallic bonding, atoms release their valence electrons into a pool of delocalised electrons that belong to no single ion. This is the model students meet in the explanation, and it corrects the 'electrons stay on their own atom' misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Demonstration step (3): conductivity tester on aluminium foil, solid NaCl and candle wax
Q2. A conductivity tester (cells, torch bulb and two crocodile-clip leads) is used to test three solids in turn: aluminium foil, solid sodium chloride (NaCl) and candle wax. Which result is correct?
  1. The bulb lights for all three solids
  2. The bulb lights for aluminium foil only
  3. The bulb lights for aluminium foil and solid NaCl, but not for the candle wax
  4. The bulb lights for solid NaCl only, because it contains ions
Correct answer: B
Aluminium has delocalised electrons that carry charge. Solid NaCl has ions locked in the lattice and candle wax is a simple molecular substance with no free charged particles, so neither conducts.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 4, Question A: 'Using the sea-of-electrons model, explain why metals conduct electricity'
Q3. Using the sea-of-electrons model, which statement best explains why metals conduct electricity?
  1. Metals contain negative ions that are attracted to the battery
  2. The positive metal ions are free to move through the wire and carry the current
  3. Electrons are held in fixed positions between pairs of atoms and pass the charge along
  4. The delocalised electrons are free to move and carry charge when a voltage is applied
Correct answer: D
The positive ions stay in the lattice. The delocalised electrons move freely throughout the metal and carry charge when a voltage is applied.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 4, Question B: 'When we bend copper wire, the layers of positive ions slide past each other but the wire does not break. Why not?'
Q4. A copper wire is bent into a curve and does not break, while a salt crystal would shatter if struck. Which statement best explains why the copper wire does not break?
  1. The layers of ions cannot move at all, so the wire only appears to bend
  2. Sliding the layers brings positive ions next to each other, but the repulsion is too weak to matter
  3. The bonds between particular pairs of copper atoms break and then reform in the new position
  4. The sea of electrons surrounds the ions in all directions, so there is no directional bond to break and the attraction continues when the ions slide
Correct answer: D
Metallic bonding is non-directional. The electron sea keeps attracting the ions after the layers slide, whereas in an ionic crystal sliding brings like charges together and the crystal cracks.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 4, Question C: 'Why do metals generally have high melting points compared to simple molecular substances like iodine?'
Q5. Metals such as iron (melting point 1538 °C) have much higher melting points than iodine (114 °C). Which statement best explains this using the metallic bonding model?
  1. Iron has weak forces between its particles, but they are packed more closely than in iodine
  2. There are many positive ions and many delocalised electrons, so the strong electrostatic attraction between them needs a lot of energy to overcome
  3. Iron has only a few positive ions, so they are held together tightly
  4. Iron atoms are joined in pairs by covalent bonds, and these bonds are very hard to break
Correct answer: B
Strong electrostatic attraction between the lattice of positive ions and the sea of delocalised electrons requires much energy to overcome. This is a stronger interaction than the forces between iodine molecules.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 3 board diagram: 4x4 grid of circles with a cloud of dots labelled 'delocalised electrons (sea of electrons)'
Q6. A student must draw a labelled 2-D diagram of metallic bonding in aluminium. Which diagram is correct?
  1. A regular grid of positive aluminium ions with a cloud of small dots, labelled delocalised electrons, around and between all the ions
  2. A grid of neutral atoms, each with its own electrons kept inside its own circle
  3. A grid of alternating positive and negative ions, with no electrons shown
  4. Two aluminium atoms joined by a shared pair of dots and crosses between them
Correct answer: A
A correct diagram has labelled positive ions in a lattice and the delocalised electrons shown as a cloud or distributed dots. The other options show covalent-style sharing, an ionic lattice, or electrons that are not delocalised.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB update: linking delocalised electrons to the graphite conductivity question from Lesson 1
Q7. Graphite is a covalent solid made only of carbon, yet it conducts electricity. Using what you have learned about delocalised electrons, which explanation is best?
  1. Each carbon atom contributes one delocalised electron that is free to move along the layer and carry charge
  2. Each carbon atom is held in a fixed bond by all four of its electrons, which lets charge pass
  3. Graphite conducts because the carbon atoms are packed in a 3-D tetrahedral network
  4. Graphite contains positive and negative ions that move when a voltage is applied
Correct answer: A
In graphite each carbon forms three bonds, leaving one delocalised electron per atom free to move in the layer. A 3-D network where all four electrons are held in bonds, as in diamond, does not conduct.
Use after: Model Building phase — Three-column table 'Three Bond Types — Evidence Summary' (BOND TYPE / HOW ELECTRONS BEHAVE / PROPERTIES EXPLAINED)
Q8. Which row of the 'Three Bond Types' summary table correctly describes how electrons behave in each type of bonding?
  1. Ionic: electrons shared between two atoms. Covalent: electrons transferred. Metallic: electrons delocalised
  2. Ionic: electrons transferred from metal to non-metal. Covalent: electrons shared between two specific atoms. Metallic: electrons delocalised in a sea
  3. Ionic: electrons delocalised. Covalent: electrons transferred. Metallic: electrons shared between two specific atoms
  4. Ionic: electrons transferred. Covalent: electrons delocalised. Metallic: electrons shared between two specific atoms
Correct answer: B
Ionic bonding involves complete transfer of electrons, covalent bonding involves sharing between specific atoms, and metallic bonding involves delocalised electrons moving throughout the lattice.
