Q1. Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon. In diamond, how many other carbon atoms is each carbon atom bonded to, and how are the atoms arranged?
  1. Four, in flat layers held together by weak forces
  2. Two, in long single chains
  3. Three, in flat hexagonal layers
  4. Four, in a 3-D tetrahedral network
Correct answer: D
Each carbon in diamond forms four strong covalent bonds to four neighbours, giving a rigid 3-D tetrahedral network. Three bonds in flat hexagonal layers describes graphite.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2: Giant Covalent Structures (Diamond & Graphite), animation of graphite's layered structure
Q2. A learner watches an animation of graphite and sees electrons moving along the layers. Which statement correctly explains why these free electrons are there?
  1. Graphite is black, and black substances always contain free electrons
  2. All four outer electrons of each carbon atom are used in bonds, so the electrons shown are extra ones from outside
  3. Each carbon atom gives away one electron to form C⁺ ions arranged in a lattice
  4. Each carbon atom uses three outer electrons in bonds, and the fourth is delocalised and free to move along the layer
Correct answer: D
In graphite each carbon forms three bonds within its hexagonal layer. The fourth outer electron is delocalised and can move along the layer, which is why graphite conducts electricity.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 1: Ionic Bonding (NaCl lattice) animation
Q3. In an animation of sodium chloride (NaCl) forming, what happens to the valence electron of each sodium atom?
  1. It stays on sodium and becomes delocalised among all the sodium atoms
  2. It is shared equally between the sodium and chlorine atoms
  3. It is transferred to a chlorine atom, forming Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions that attract each other
  4. It is lost completely and not taken up by any other atom
Correct answer: C
Sodium transfers one valence electron to chlorine, giving Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. Their electrostatic attraction builds the lattice and explains the high melting point.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 3: Metallic Bonding (Aluminium) electron-sea animation
Q4. An electron-sea animation of aluminium shows a lattice of positive ions surrounded by delocalised electrons. How does this explain why aluminium conducts electricity?
  1. The delocalised electrons are free to move through the lattice and carry charge
  2. The metal contains Al³⁺ and Cl⁻ ions that move when a voltage is applied
  3. The positive ions move through the metal and carry the charge
  4. The metal atoms share electrons in fixed pairs, which pass charge along the bonds
Correct answer: A
In metallic bonding the positive ions stay in fixed positions while the delocalised electrons move freely, so they carry electric charge through the metal.
Use after: Explain phase — Discussion Question 2: NaCl simulation vs. SiO₂ in the solubility investigation
Q5. In the solubility investigation, NaCl dissolved in water but SiO₂ (sand) did not. Which explanation matches what the simulations showed?
  1. SiO₂ has delocalised electrons, which stop it from dissolving
  2. NaCl has weak bonds, but SiO₂ has no bonds at all
  3. NaCl is an ionic lattice that water can pull apart into ions, while SiO₂ is a giant covalent network of strong bonds that water cannot break
  4. NaCl is a giant covalent network and SiO₂ is an ionic lattice
Correct answer: C
Water molecules can pull Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions out of the ionic lattice. The strong covalent bonds throughout SiO₂ cannot be broken by water, so sand stays undissolved.
Use after: Explain phase — Discussion Question 1: graphite simulation vs. conductivity results of graphite and diamond
Q6. In the conductivity test, graphite lit the bulb but diamond did not. Why does diamond not conduct electricity?
  1. Diamond atoms have fewer protons than graphite atoms
  2. All four outer electrons of each carbon atom are held in bonds, so there are no free electrons to carry charge
  3. Diamond is transparent, so charge passes straight through without stopping
  4. Diamond is too hard for electricity to enter it
Correct answer: B
In diamond each carbon uses all four outer electrons in strong covalent bonds, so no electrons are free to move. Diamond and graphite have the same number of protons because both are pure carbon.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: adding a 'Model Limitations' box to the Carbon Bonding Model
Q7. A learner adds a 'Model Limitations' box to their bonding model. Which entry names a real limitation of a bonding simulation?
  1. Simulations show electrons as dots, but real electrons behave more like probability clouds
  2. Simulations show that diamond and graphite have different numbers of protons
  3. Simulations prove that chemical bonds do not really exist
  4. Simulations cannot show any structure of a substance
Correct answer: A
All models simplify reality. Showing electrons as dots is a simplification of their quantum behaviour. The other statements are false, since bonds exist, both substances are pure carbon, and simulations do show structures.
