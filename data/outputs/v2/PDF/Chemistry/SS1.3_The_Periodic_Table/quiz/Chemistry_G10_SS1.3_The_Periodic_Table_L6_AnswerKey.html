Q1. A sodium atom has the electron arrangement 2,8,1. To become stable like a noble gas, what does it do, and what ion does it form?
  1. It gains 7 electrons and forms an ion with a 7- charge
  2. It gains 1 electron and forms an ion with a 1- charge
  3. It loses its 1 outer electron and forms an ion with a 1+ charge
  4. It loses its 1 outer electron and forms an ion with a 1- charge
Correct answer: C
Sodium has 1 outer electron, so losing it leaves the full level 2,8 like neon. It then has 11 protons and 10 electrons, so the charge is 1+.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Phase, Step 2: repeating the counter activity for Cl (adding one counter to make 2,8,8, with 17 protons and 18 electrons)
Q2. A chlorine atom has 17 protons and the arrangement 2,8,7. It gains 1 electron to form a chloride ion, Cl-. How many electrons does the chloride ion have?
  1. 16
  2. 19
  3. 17
  4. 18
Correct answer: D
The atom has 17 electrons, the same as its protons. Gaining 1 electron gives 17 + 1 = 18 electrons, arranged 2,8,8 like argon. The protons stay at 17, so the charge is 17 - 18 = -1.

Q3. Which is the correct electron arrangement in s and p notation for the sodium ion, Na+? (A sodium atom is 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s1.)
  1. 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2
  2. 1s2 2s2 2p6
  3. 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s1
  4. 1s2 2s2 2p5
Correct answer: B
Na+ forms when the atom loses its 3s1 electron. Only the electrons change, so Na+ is 1s2 2s2 2p6, the same as neon.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Phase, Step 4: circling which noble gas (Ne or Ar) each ion matches
Q4. Which group of ions all have the same electron arrangement as neon (1s2 2s2 2p6)?
  1. F-, Cl-, Ca2+, Na+
  2. Na+, Cl-, K+, Ca2+
  3. Mg2+, Cl-, O2-, K+
  4. Na+, Mg2+, O2-, F-
Correct answer: D
Na+, Mg2+, O2- and F- all have 1s2 2s2 2p6, which is the neon arrangement. Cl-, K+ and Ca2+ have 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6, which is the argon arrangement.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: using the rule to predict, without counters, the ions formed by Li, Be, N and S
Q5. A sulfur atom has the electron arrangement 2,8,6. Using the rule for forming ions, which ion does it form?
  1. S2+, because it loses 2 electrons
  2. S6-, because it gains 6 electrons
  3. S2-, because it gains 2 electrons to make 2,8,8
  4. S6+, because it loses its 6 outer electrons
Correct answer: C
Sulfur is a non-metal with 6 outer electrons. Gaining 2 electrons fills the outer level (2,8,8, like argon), giving an ion with a 2- charge. It is in group 16, which forms 2- ions.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: challenge question 'Na+ and Ne have the same arrangement. Are they the same thing? Why not?'
Q6. A sodium ion (Na+) has 11 protons and 10 electrons. A neon atom has 10 protons and 10 electrons. They have the same electron arrangement. Why are they different particles?
  1. Na+ has more electrons than neon
  2. Na+ has no nucleus but neon does
  3. They are the same particle because their arrangements match
  4. Na+ has 11 protons in its nucleus but neon has 10 protons
Correct answer: D
The two particles have the same 10 electrons, but Na+ has 11 protons and neon has 10. Different proton numbers make them different particles, and a Na+ ion has a 1+ charge while neon is neutral.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB Phase: moving the note 'Why does sodium react but neon does not?' to the 'Answered' section with an evidence statement
Q7. Sodium reacts vigorously with water, but neon does not react at all. Which explanation is best?
  1. Neon has one outer electron that it cannot lose
  2. Neon has fewer protons than sodium, so it cannot react
  3. Sodium has a full outer level, so it is unstable
  4. Sodium has one outer electron that is easily lost to reach a stable arrangement, but neon already has a full outer level
Correct answer: D
Sodium (2,8,1) easily loses its single outer electron to reach the stable 2,8 arrangement. Neon (2,8) already has a full outer level, so it has no tendency to lose or gain electrons.
