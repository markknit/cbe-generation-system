Q1. Four atoms have these electron arrangements: Ne 2,8; Na 2,8,1; Mg 2,8,2; Cl 2,8,7. Which atom is the most stable, meaning the least likely to react?
  1. Na 2,8,1, because it has only one outer electron
  2. Ne 2,8, because its outer energy level is already full
  3. Cl 2,8,7, because it has seven outer electrons
  4. Mg 2,8,2, because it has two outer electrons
Correct answer: B
Neon has a full outer energy level (an octet), so it has no tendency to lose or gain electrons. The other atoms have outer levels that are not full.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Electron Counter Card Sort, Step 2 (building Mg and completing the lose/gain table)
Q2. A magnesium atom has the electron arrangement 2,8,2. Which statement about how it reaches a full outer level is correct?
  1. It loses 2 electrons, because that is fewer moves than gaining 6
  2. It gains 2 electrons, because it has 2 outer electrons
  3. It gains 6 electrons, because gaining is always easier than losing
  4. It loses 8 electrons so that all its electrons are removed
Correct answer: A
Mg can lose its 2 outer electrons and be left with the full level 2,8, like neon. Gaining 6 electrons would need far more moves.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Electron Counter Card Sort, Step 2 (building O and counting electrons to gain)
Q3. An oxygen atom has the electron arrangement 2,6. How many electrons must it gain to fill its outer energy level to 8?
  1. 4
  2. 8
  3. 6
  4. 2
Correct answer: D
The outer level needs 8 electrons and oxygen has 6, so it must gain 8 - 6 = 2 electrons. This gives the Ne-like arrangement 2,8.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Electron Counter Card Sort, Step 3 (comparing with the Ne and Ar mats)
Q4. A sodium atom (2,8,1) loses its one outer electron. What electron arrangement does it end up with?
  1. 2,8, the same as neon
  2. 2,8,1, it stays the same
  3. 2,7, the same as fluorine
  4. 2,8,8, the same as argon
Correct answer: A
When Na loses its outer electron, the full second level becomes the new outer level, giving 2,8. This is the same arrangement as neon.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning sticky note on why Na loses 1 and Cl gains 1
Q5. A chlorine atom has the electron arrangement 2,8,7. Which is the best reason why chlorine gains 1 electron instead of losing 7?
  1. Gaining 1 electron gives a full outer level (2,8,8) with the fewest electrons moved
  2. Chlorine atoms want electrons because they are greedy
  3. Chlorine cannot lose electrons because it is used to disinfect water
  4. Chlorine needs to fill all its energy levels with electrons in total
Correct answer: A
Gaining 1 electron gives the stable arrangement 2,8,8 like argon. Losing 7 would take far more moves. Atoms do not literally want things; they become more stable with a full outer level.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: whole-class discussion on why Ne and Ar do not react while Na and K react vigorously
Q6. Neon and argon do not react at all, but sodium reacts vigorously with water. Which explanation is correct?
  1. Neon and argon have no electrons, so they cannot react
  2. Neon and argon already have a full outer level, while sodium has one outer electron it can lose to become stable
  3. Sodium has more electrons in total than neon and argon
  4. Neon and argon are heavier than sodium, so they react more slowly
Correct answer: B
Ne (2,8) and Ar (2,8,8) already have full outer levels, so there is no need to change. Na (2,8,1) can reach a stable arrangement by losing one electron.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: vocabulary on the board (stable, full outer level, octet, duplet) and He as a stable atom
Q7. A helium atom has only 2 electrons (1s2), yet it is a stable noble gas. Why is helium stable?
  1. Its only energy level is full with 2 electrons (a duplet)
  2. It has more electrons than neon
  3. It has no outer electrons at all
  4. It needs 8 outer electrons and gets them from other atoms
Correct answer: A
The first energy level holds a maximum of 2 electrons, so helium's outer level is full. Helium is stable with a duplet, not an octet.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the Electron Arrangement Model (does the model explain why Li, Na and K react alike?)
Q8. Lithium, sodium and potassium have very different masses, but all react vigorously with water. What best explains why they behave alike?
  1. They all have full outer energy levels
  2. They all have one outer electron that they can lose to reach a stable arrangement
  3. They all have 3 energy levels
  4. They all have the same mass number
Correct answer: B
Li, Na and K are in the same group, so each has one outer electron to lose. They reach a stable arrangement in the same way, so they behave alike despite their different masses.
