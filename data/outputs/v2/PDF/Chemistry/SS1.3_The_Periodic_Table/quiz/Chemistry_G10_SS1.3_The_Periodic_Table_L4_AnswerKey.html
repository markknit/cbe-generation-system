Q1. Lithium (A_r 7), sodium (A_r 23) and potassium (A_r 39) have very different masses, yet all three react vigorously with water. Which statement best explains why they behave alike?
  1. They each have the same total number of electrons
  2. They each have 1 electron in their outer energy level
  3. They each have a full outer energy level
  4. They have similar masses
Correct answer: B
Li (2,1), Na (2,8,1) and K (2,8,8,1) each have 1 outer electron, so they belong to the same family and behave alike. Their masses and total electron numbers are different, and a full outer level would make them unreactive.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: teacher demonstration or simulation of Li, Na and K in water
Q2. In a demonstration, small pieces of lithium, sodium and potassium are each placed in water. Which order lists them from slowest to fastest reaction?
  1. Lithium, sodium, potassium
  2. All three react at the same speed
  3. Sodium, lithium, potassium
  4. Potassium, sodium, lithium
Correct answer: A
Li fizzes steadily, Na melts and darts about, and K burns with a lilac flame and is very vigorous. Reactivity of the alkali metals increases down the group: Li < Na < K.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Data cards 1 and 2 (Mg and Ca; Ne and Ar), where the teacher points out that Ar and Ca have the same mass
Q3. Argon (A_r 40) and calcium (A_r 40) have the same mass. Calcium reacts with water, but argon does not react at all. What does this show?
  1. Calcium reacts because it has a smaller mass than argon
  2. Elements with the same mass always react in the same way
  3. Argon is simply a slow reactor that needs more time
  4. Mass alone does not decide how an element behaves
Correct answer: D
Ar and Ca have equal masses but opposite behaviour, so mass cannot be what decides reactivity. The difference comes from the outer electrons: Ar (2,8,8) has a full outer level and Ca (2,8,8,2) does not.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Data card 3 (Cl2 in water treatment and fluoride in Rift Valley groundwater; halogens)
Q4. Chlorine is used to disinfect drinking water in Nairobi and Mombasa. Its electron arrangement is 2,8,7. Which family does chlorine belong to, and how many outer electrons does it have?
  1. Halogens, with 7 outer electrons
  2. Alkali metals, with 1 outer electron
  3. Noble gases, with a full outer level
  4. Alkaline earth metals, with 2 outer electrons
Correct answer: A
The arrangement 2,8,7 shows 7 outer electrons, which places chlorine in group 17, the halogens, with fluorine (2,7).
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER statement and written question (1) 'Why are Na and Mg, neighbours in period 3, so different?'
Q5. Sodium (2,8,1) and magnesium (2,8,2) are neighbours in period 3, yet they behave differently. What is the best reason?
  1. Sodium has 1 outer electron and magnesium has 2 outer electrons
  2. Magnesium has no outer electrons
  3. Sodium and magnesium are in different periods
  4. Sodium has a much greater mass than magnesium
Correct answer: A
Neighbours across a period have different numbers of outer electrons (1 and 2), so they belong to different families. Their masses are almost the same (23 and 24) and both are in period 3.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: written question (3) predicting the reactivity of rubidium (Rb)
Q6. Rubidium (Rb) lies directly below potassium in group 1. Which prediction is best supported by the evidence from Li, Na and K?
  1. Rb is more reactive than K, because it has 1 outer electron and reactivity increases down group 1
  2. Rb does not react with water, because it is in a lower period
  3. Rb is as reactive as Li, because all group 1 metals react at the same speed
  4. Rb is less reactive than K, because heavier metals react more slowly
Correct answer: A
Rb has the same outer electron count as Li, Na and K, so it is an alkali metal. The trend Li < Na < K continues down the group, so Rb should be more reactive than K.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the 'Electron Arrangement Model' poster with family names, group numbers and outer electron counts
Q7. Which row correctly matches a chemical family with its group number and its number of outer electrons?
  1. Noble gases, group 18, 7 outer electrons
  2. Halogens, group 17, 1 outer electron
  3. Alkali metals, group 2, 2 outer electrons
  4. Alkaline earth metals, group 2, 2 outer electrons
Correct answer: D
Alkaline earth metals such as Mg (2,8,2) and Ca (2,8,8,2) are in group 2 and have 2 outer electrons. Alkali metals are group 1 with 1, halogens are group 17 with 7, and noble gases are group 18 with a full outer level.
