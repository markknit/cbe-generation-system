Q1. Mrs Achieng mixes vinegar with baking soda and the mixture fizzes. The gas is bubbled through limewater and the limewater turns milky. Which gas is produced?
  1. Oxygen
  2. Hydrogen
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Air from the cup
Correct answer: C
Limewater turning milky is the test for carbon dioxide. Vinegar is an acid reacting with a carbonate-type substance, which gives carbon dioxide.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation B (metal): magnesium ribbon + dilute hydrochloric acid with lighted splint test
Q2. A 2 cm strip of magnesium ribbon is placed in dilute hydrochloric acid. Bubbles form. A lighted splint is held at the mouth of the tube and a squeaky pop is heard. Which gas is present?
  1. Hydrogen
  2. Steam
  3. Oxygen
  4. Carbon dioxide
Correct answer: A
Hydrogen burns with a squeaky pop when a lighted splint is brought near. Acids react with reactive metals such as magnesium to give a salt and hydrogen.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation B: magnesium in water as a control (no bubbles)
Q3. A group places magnesium ribbon in water as well as in dilute hydrochloric acid. The water tube has no bubbles. Why is the water tube included in the investigation?
  1. To show that the fizzing needs an acid and does not happen with water alone
  2. To make the magnesium react faster in the acid
  3. To collect extra gas for the splint test
  4. To show that water also produces hydrogen
Correct answer: A
A control shows what happens without the acid. Since water gives no bubbles, the acid must be the cause of the gas in the other tube.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: completing word equations (magnesium + hydrochloric acid) and the general pattern acid + metal
Q4. Which word equation correctly shows what happens when magnesium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid?
  1. Magnesium + hydrochloric acid → magnesium oxide + oxygen
  2. Magnesium + hydrochloric acid → magnesium chloride + hydrogen
  3. Magnesium + hydrochloric acid → magnesium chloride + water + carbon dioxide
  4. Magnesium + hydrochloric acid → magnesium chloride + oxygen
Correct answer: B
The general pattern is acid + metal → salt + hydrogen. Magnesium and hydrochloric acid give magnesium chloride (the salt) and hydrogen gas.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: word equation for vinegar + sodium hydrogencarbonate and the pattern acid + carbonate
Q5. Vinegar (ethanoic acid) reacts with sodium hydrogencarbonate (baking soda). Which set of products is correct?
  1. A salt and hydrogen only
  2. A salt and carbon dioxide only
  3. A salt, water and carbon dioxide
  4. A salt, water and oxygen
Correct answer: C
The general pattern is acid + carbonate (or hydrogencarbonate) → salt + water + carbon dioxide. This explains the fizzing and the milky limewater.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Wanjiru's question, why soap does not give fizz with magnesium
Q6. Soap solution has a pH of 10. Why does magnesium ribbon not give bubbles of gas when placed in soap solution, while it does in dilute hydrochloric acid?
  1. Soap is too slippery for the magnesium to touch
  2. Soap solution is not an acid, so it does not supply the H+ (aq) ions needed for the reaction
  3. Magnesium only reacts with substances that taste sour
  4. Soap solution contains too much water for any reaction
Correct answer: B
Soap solution is basic and contains mainly OH- ions, not an excess of H+ ions. The reaction with magnesium needs H+ (aq) ions from an acid.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the particle drawings to show H+ ions in both reactions
Q7. Magnesium reacts with hydrochloric acid, and baking soda reacts with vinegar. What is the same in both reactions that makes them happen?
  1. Both acids supply OH- (aq) ions that take part in the reaction
  2. Both acids supply H+ (aq) ions that take part in the reaction
  3. Both reactions produce oxygen gas
  4. Both reactions only happen when a lighted splint is present
Correct answer: B
Both reactions depend on the H+ (aq) ions released by the acid. The H+ ions react with the metal or the carbonate to give a gas.
