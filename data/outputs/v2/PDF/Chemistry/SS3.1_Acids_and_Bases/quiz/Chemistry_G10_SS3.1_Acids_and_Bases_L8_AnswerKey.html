Q1. Four solutions, each of concentration 0.1 M, were tested with universal indicator. The pH values were: hydrochloric acid 1, ethanoic acid 3, ammonia solution 11, sodium hydroxide 13. Which list puts them in order from lowest pH to highest pH?
  1. Ethanoic acid, hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide, ammonia
  2. Hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide, ethanoic acid, ammonia
  3. Sodium hydroxide, ammonia, ethanoic acid, hydrochloric acid
  4. Hydrochloric acid, ethanoic acid, ammonia, sodium hydroxide
Correct answer: D
Ordering by pH value from lowest to highest gives 1 (HCl), 3 (ethanoic acid), 11 (ammonia), 13 (NaOH). Acids are below 7 and bases above 7.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher demonstration with torch bulb, battery and carbon electrodes
Q2. A teacher dips a torch bulb circuit with carbon electrodes into 0.1 M hydrochloric acid and then into 0.1 M ethanoic acid. The bulb glows brightly in the hydrochloric acid but only dimly in the ethanoic acid. What does this show?
  1. Ethanoic acid contains no ions at all
  2. Hydrochloric acid contains more water than ethanoic acid
  3. The 0.1 M hydrochloric acid contains many more ions than the 0.1 M ethanoic acid
  4. Ethanoic acid is more concentrated than hydrochloric acid
Correct answer: C
A brighter bulb means more ions are present to carry current. Both solutions are 0.1 M, so the difference is that hydrochloric acid ionises almost completely while ethanoic acid ionises only partly.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: degree of ionisation particle drawing for HCl and ethanoic acid
Q3. 0.1 M hydrochloric acid has pH 1 and 0.1 M ethanoic acid has pH 3. Both have the same concentration. Why is the pH of hydrochloric acid lower?
  1. More hydrochloric acid has dissolved in the water than ethanoic acid
  2. Hydrochloric acid is more concentrated than ethanoic acid
  3. Ethanoic acid does not form any H+ (aq) ions in water
  4. Almost all hydrochloric acid molecules ionise, giving many more H+ (aq) ions than ethanoic acid, where only a few molecules ionise
Correct answer: D
Strength depends on the degree of ionisation. HCl ionises almost completely, giving a high H+ (aq) concentration and low pH. Ethanoic acid ionises only partly, so it has fewer H+ (aq) ions.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: separating strength from concentration (dilute strong acid versus concentrated weak acid)
Q4. Solution X is 0.001 M hydrochloric acid (very watery). Solution Y is 5 M ethanoic acid (very concentrated). Which statement is correct?
  1. Strong acids are always concentrated and weak acids are always dilute
  2. X is a weak acid because it is dilute, and Y is a strong acid because it is concentrated
  3. Hydrochloric acid is still the strong acid even though X is dilute, and ethanoic acid is still the weak acid even though Y is concentrated
  4. Y must contain the strong acid because it is more concentrated
Correct answer: C
Strength is about how much an acid ionises in water, while concentration is how much is dissolved. Strong does not mean concentrated and weak does not mean dilute.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: using the pH graph and the 10-fold rule to compare lemon juice and vinegar
Q5. Lemon juice has pH 2.0 and vinegar has pH 2.8. Each pH unit is a 10-fold change in H+ (aq) concentration. About how many times more H+ (aq) does lemon juice have than vinegar?
  1. About 6.3 times
  2. About 8 times
  3. About 10 times
  4. About 1.4 times
Correct answer: A
The difference is 2.8 - 2.0 = 0.8 pH units. The ratio of H+ (aq) concentrations is 10^0.8, which is about 6.3.

Q6. A pharmacist in Nairobi gives a patient with hyperacidity an antacid containing magnesium hydroxide instead of sodium hydroxide. Why is magnesium hydroxide the better choice?
  1. Magnesium hydroxide is more concentrated than sodium hydroxide, so it works faster
  2. Sodium hydroxide cannot neutralise stomach hydrochloric acid
  3. Magnesium hydroxide is a mild base. It dissolves only slightly, so the pH stays near 10 and the stomach does not become strongly basic, while sodium hydroxide is corrosive
  4. Magnesium hydroxide is a strong acid, so it removes the extra acid quickly
Correct answer: C
Magnesium hydroxide is only slightly soluble, so few OH- (aq) ions are free and the pH cannot rise much above 10. Sodium hydroxide gives pH 13 at 0.1 M and is corrosive, so it is unsafe to swallow.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: drawing ions and whole molecules in four beakers (0.1 M HCl, ethanoic acid, NaOH, ammonia)
Q7. A learner draws a particle diagram of 0.1 M ammonia solution, a weak base. Which drawing is correct?
  1. H+ (aq) ions only, because the solution is a base
  2. Almost every ammonia molecule has changed into ions, giving a large number of OH- (aq) ions
  3. A few OH- (aq) ions together with many whole ammonia molecules that have not ionised
  4. No ions at all, only whole ammonia molecules
Correct answer: C
A weak base ionises only partly in water, so the solution has a few OH- (aq) ions and many intact molecules. This is why ammonia has pH 11, lower than NaOH at pH 13.
