Q1. Four clear cups on a teacher's bench hold lemon juice, vinegar, baking soda solution and soap solution. They all look similar. Which is the safe way to find out how they differ?
  1. Taste a little from each cup and compare
  2. Touch each liquid to feel which is slippery
  3. Use measurements such as pH values and the teacher's given observations, without tasting or touching
  4. Decide by colour alone, since clear liquids are all the same
Correct answer: C
Unknown chemicals must never be tasted or touched. Measured data such as pH and the teacher's read-out observations let us compare the products safely, and clear liquids are not necessarily the same.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: studying the pH graph and putting the products in order from lowest to highest pH
Q2. A pH meter at 25 °C gave these readings: lemon juice 2.0, vinegar 2.8, pure water 7.0, baking soda solution 8.3, soap solution 10.0. Which list shows the products in order from LOWEST to HIGHEST pH?
  1. Lemon juice, vinegar, baking soda, pure water, soap
  2. Vinegar, lemon juice, pure water, soap, baking soda
  3. Lemon juice, vinegar, pure water, baking soda, soap
  4. Soap, baking soda, pure water, vinegar, lemon juice
Correct answer: C
Ordering the numbers from smallest to largest gives 2.0 (lemon juice), 2.8 (vinegar), 7.0 (water), 8.3 (baking soda) and 10.0 (soap).
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: marking which products are below and above the water line at 7
Q3. Pure water has a pH of 7.0 and is used as the reference line. Lemon juice is 2.0, vinegar is 2.8, baking soda solution is 8.3 and soap solution is 10.0. Which pair of products both have a pH ABOVE 7?
  1. Vinegar and baking soda
  2. Baking soda and soap
  3. Lemon juice and vinegar
  4. Lemon juice and soap
Correct answer: B
Only baking soda (8.3) and soap (10.0) lie above the water line at 7. Lemon juice (2.0) and vinegar (2.8) lie below it.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: calculating differences between pH values (soap and baking soda)
Q4. Soap solution has a pH of 10.0 and baking soda solution has a pH of 8.3. What is the difference between their pH values?
  1. 18.3
  2. 1.7
  3. 2.3
  4. 0.7
Correct answer: B
Subtract the smaller value from the larger one: 10.0 - 8.3 = 1.7.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: 'Which is furthest from water?' prompt while reading the pH graph
Q5. Pure water has a pH of 7.0. Lemon juice is 2.0, vinegar is 2.8, baking soda solution is 8.3 and soap solution is 10.0. Which product has a pH furthest from the pH of pure water?
  1. Baking soda, because it is the closest to soap
  2. Vinegar, because it is used for pickling
  3. Soap, because it has the highest number
  4. Lemon juice, because 7.0 - 2.0 = 5.0 is the biggest gap from water
Correct answer: D
The gaps from 7.0 are lemon juice 5.0, vinegar 4.2, baking soda 1.3 and soap 3.0, so lemon juice is furthest. Learners who pick soap assume the highest number is the furthest from water.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: claim-evidence-reasoning hypotheses on why lemon juice and vinegar differ from soap
Q6. Lemon juice (pH 2.0) tastes sour and soap solution (pH 10.0) feels slippery. At this early stage of the unit, which statement is the best description of what we know?
  1. We have noticed a pattern between pH values and taste or feel, but we have not yet explained why the products behave differently
  2. A higher pH number means a product is more sour, so the graph already explains everything
  3. Lemon juice is sour because it is clear and soap is slippery because it is white
  4. Soap is slippery only because it contains oil, so the pH does not matter
Correct answer: A
The graph shows a pattern (sour products are below 7, slippery ones above 7), but the reason is deliberately not explained yet. It will be built across the unit, and the hypotheses are tested later.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: learners write two questions each on sticky notes and sort them
Q7. The driving question is: 'Why are patients with hyperacidity issues given antacid medications?' Which learner question would best fit on the Driving Question Board under it?
  1. How much does a bar of soap cost at the market?
  2. What colour is the antacid bottle?
  3. What is in an antacid that helps a patient after eating spicy nyama choma?
  4. Is lemon juice sold in Kisumu market?
Correct answer: C
A good DQB question asks 'what' or 'why/how' about the phenomenon and connects to the driving question. The antacid question does both, while the others are about colour, price or where things are sold.
