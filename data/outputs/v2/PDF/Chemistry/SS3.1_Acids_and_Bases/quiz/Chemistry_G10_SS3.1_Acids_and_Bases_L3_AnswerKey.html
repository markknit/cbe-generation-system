Q1. Pure water from a tap is added to the sorting table for acids, neutral substances and bases. Which column does pure water belong in, and why?
  1. Base column, because water does not taste sour
  2. Acid column, because water is a liquid we drink like lemon juice
  3. Neutral column, because pure water has a pH of 7.0
  4. It does not belong in any column, because water has no pH
Correct answer: C
Pure water is neutral with pH 7.0, which is neither below 7 (acid) nor above 7 (base). Water does have a pH value, and not tasting sour does not make it a base.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 1, red cabbage extract added to the five labelled test tubes
Q2. Red cabbage extract is purple in pure water. A learner adds 2 cm3 of this extract to lemon juice. What colour will the mixture be?
  1. Green-yellow
  2. Purple, the same as in water
  3. Blue
  4. Red or pink
Correct answer: D
Red cabbage extract turns red or pink in acids such as lemon juice. Blue is the colour in weak bases, and purple means neutral.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 2, universal indicator in a second set of tubes compared with the pH colour chart
Q3. Five solutions are tested with universal indicator. The colours are: red-orange (pH 2), orange (pH 3), green (pH 7), blue (pH 8) and blue-purple (pH 10). Which solution is the most basic?
  1. The blue-purple solution at pH 10
  2. The blue solution at pH 8
  3. The red-orange solution at pH 2
  4. The green solution at pH 7
Correct answer: A
The higher the pH above 7, the more basic the solution, so pH 10 (blue-purple) is the most basic. The pH 2 solution is the most acidic and the pH 7 solution is neutral.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: comparing indicators, question (a) 'Why is the sorting the same with all indicators but the colours differ?'
Q4. Red cabbage extract turns lemon juice pink and soap solution blue-green. Universal indicator turns lemon juice red-orange and soap solution blue-purple. The colours are different, but both indicators sort lemon juice as an acid and soap as a base. Why?
  1. One of the indicators is wrong and the other is correct
  2. Each indicator has its own set of colours, but every indicator changes colour according to whether a substance is acidic, neutral or basic
  3. The indicators change the pH of the substances, so the colours differ
  4. Indicators only work on the colour of the substance, not on whether it is acidic or basic
Correct answer: B
Different indicators use different colour schemes, but they all respond to the same property, which is whether the substance is acid, neutral or base. So the sorting is the same even though the colours differ.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: claim-evidence-reasoning statement comparing lemon juice (pH 2.0) and vinegar (pH 2.8)
Q5. A pH meter gives lemon juice a pH of 2.0 and vinegar a pH of 2.8. Which statement is correct?
  1. Lemon juice and vinegar are equally acidic, because both are acids
  2. Lemon juice is more acidic, because its pH is lower and further below 7
  3. Vinegar is a base, because its pH is not a whole number
  4. Vinegar is more acidic, because 2.8 is a larger number than 2.0
Correct answer: B
Below pH 7, a lower pH means a more acidic substance. Lemon juice (2.0) is lower than vinegar (2.8), so it is more acidic. Both are acids, but they are not equally acidic.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: sticky notes 'What we now know' and 'New questions' (Otieno's question about lemon being sour)
Q6. A learner has a completed table showing that lemon juice is an acid with pH 2.0, and that it turns red cabbage extract pink and universal indicator red-orange. She asks: 'Why is lemon juice sour?' What can be said about this question?
  1. The table already answers it, because a low pH is the reason for the sour taste
  2. The question cannot be answered by science, so it is removed from the board
  3. The table shows that lemon juice is acidic but not why it is sour, so it is kept as a question on the Driving Question Board
  4. The table answers it, because lemon juice turns red cabbage extract pink
Correct answer: C
The table classifies substances using evidence, but it does not explain what makes an acid sour. That is a new 'why' question to park on the board and answer in later lessons.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: first draft particle model using red and blue placeholder dots for the five beakers
Q7. In a first draft particle model, red dots stand for 'whatever makes a substance acidic' and blue dots stand for 'whatever makes it basic'. Lemon juice is drawn with many red dots. How should a beaker of pure water (pH 7.0) be drawn?
  1. With equal numbers of red and blue dots, and very few of each
  2. With many red dots and no blue dots
  3. With many blue dots and no red dots
  4. With no dots at all, because water has no pH
Correct answer: A
Water is neutral, so the acid-making and base-making dots are balanced. Water has pH 7.0 and is not strongly acidic or basic, so the dots are few and equal.
