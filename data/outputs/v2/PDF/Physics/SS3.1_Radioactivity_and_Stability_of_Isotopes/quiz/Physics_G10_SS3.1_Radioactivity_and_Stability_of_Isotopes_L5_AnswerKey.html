Q1. A uranium-238 nucleus (nucleon number 238, proton number 92) decays by emitting an alpha particle (nucleon number 4, proton number 2). Which daughter nucleus forms?
  1. Protactinium-238 (nucleon number 238, proton number 91)
  2. Thorium-236 (nucleon number 236, proton number 90)
  3. Thorium-234 (nucleon number 234, proton number 90)
  4. Plutonium-242 (nucleon number 242, proton number 94)
Correct answer: C
Nucleon number and proton number are both conserved. 238 - 4 = 234 and 92 - 2 = 90, and Z = 90 is thorium.

Q2. Radium-226 (nucleon number 226, proton number 88) is found in pitchblende ore with uranium. It decays by alpha emission. What is the nucleon number of the daughter nucleus?
  1. 222
  2. 224
  3. 230
  4. 226
Correct answer: A
An alpha particle carries away 4 nucleons, so the daughter has 226 - 4 = 222 nucleons. The daughter is radon-222.

Q3. Carbon-14 (nucleon number 14, proton number 6), used to date charcoal and bone, decays by beta-minus emission. Which equation is correctly balanced?
  1. ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ⁰₋₁e
  2. ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₆C + ⁰₋₁e
  3. ¹⁴₆C → ¹³₇N + ⁰₋₁e
  4. ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₅B + ⁰₋₁e
Correct answer: A
Nucleon number stays 14. The proton number must balance: 6 = 7 + (-1). So the daughter is nitrogen-14, with proton number 7.

Q4. Cobalt-60 decays by beta-minus emission to an excited nickel-60 nucleus. The excited nucleus then emits a gamma ray and becomes a nickel-60 nucleus with less energy. What happens to the nucleon number and proton number of the nickel nucleus when it emits the gamma ray?
  1. The nucleon number stays the same and the proton number rises by 1
  2. The nucleon number falls by 4 and the proton number falls by 2
  3. The nucleon number falls by 1 and the proton number stays the same
  4. Neither number changes; the nucleus only loses excess energy
Correct answer: D
A gamma ray is energy, not a particle with mass or charge. Nucleon number and proton number stay the same and the nucleus de-excites.

Q5. Uranium-238 has 146 neutrons and 92 protons, so N/Z = 146/92 = 1.59. After alpha decay the daughter thorium-234 has N/Z = 144/90 = 1.60. What can you conclude about this single alpha step?
  1. The N/Z ratio hardly changes, so this single alpha step does not necessarily bring the nucleus closer to the belt of stability
  2. It makes the nucleus completely stable
  3. It moves the nucleus much closer to the belt of stability
  4. The N/Z ratio doubles, so the nucleus moves far from the belt
Correct answer: A
The ratio changes only from 1.59 to 1.60. Alpha decay mainly reduces the size of a heavy nucleus. Thorium-234 is still unstable and decays further.

Q6. In beta-minus decay a neutron in the nucleus changes into a proton, and an electron is emitted. Thorium-234 decays to protactinium-234 this way. How do the neutron number (N) and proton number (Z) of the nucleus change?
  1. N and Z both stay the same
  2. N decreases by 1 and Z increases by 1
  3. N increases by 1 and Z decreases by 1
  4. N decreases by 2 and Z decreases by 2
Correct answer: B
One neutron becomes one proton, so N falls by 1 and Z rises by 1. The nucleon number stays constant. This moves a neutron-rich nucleus down and to the right, towards the belt of stability.

Q7. Pitchblende ore left in complete darkness next to photographic film exposes the film, even though the film is in a closed container. Which explanation is best?
  1. Alpha particles from uranium-238 pass easily through the container and expose the film
  2. The uranium atoms lose heat energy, and the heat darkens the film
  3. The rock stores sunlight and releases it slowly as light
  4. Uranium nuclei decay into daughter nuclei, some of which emit beta and gamma radiation that pass through the container, while alpha particles are stopped
Correct answer: D
Uranium-238 decays by balanced nuclear equations into unstable daughters such as thorium-234 and protactinium-234, which emit beta and gamma radiation. These penetrate the container and expose the film; alpha particles are stopped.
Use after: End of lesson — Uranium decay series chart (homework or opening of Lesson 6): counting 8 alpha and 6 beta steps from U-238 to Pb-206
Q8. Uranium-238 (proton number 92) decays through a long series to stable lead-206 (proton number 82). The series has 8 alpha decays. Each alpha decay lowers the proton number by 2, and each beta-minus decay raises it by 1. How many beta-minus decays are in the series?
  1. 8
  2. 6
  3. 4
  4. 10
Correct answer: B
After 8 alpha decays the proton number is 92 - 8 × 2 = 76. To reach 82, the series needs 82 - 76 = 6 beta-minus decays.
