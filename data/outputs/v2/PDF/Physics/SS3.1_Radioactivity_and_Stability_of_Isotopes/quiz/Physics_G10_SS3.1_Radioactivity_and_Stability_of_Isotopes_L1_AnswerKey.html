Q1. A photographic film was sealed in a lightproof box next to a piece of pitchblende (uranium ore) in a dark room. When the film was developed, it showed a clear image. No light, heat or sound was involved. Which statement is supported by this observation alone?
  1. The film was exposed by sound made by the rock.
  2. A hidden lamp inside the box lit up the film.
  3. Something from the rock reached the film even though no light could enter the box.
  4. Heat from the rock burned the film.
Correct answer: C
The observation only shows that something invisible passed from the rock to the film. The cause is not yet explained, and light, heat and sound were ruled out.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: cold-call predictions scribed on the 'Before' column of the class anchor chart
Q2. During the prediction activity, learners' ideas about what came out of the rock are written word-for-word in the 'Before' column of the class anchor chart. What happens to these predictions?
  1. They are kept without correction and revisited in later lessons as new evidence is gathered.
  2. Only the predictions that turn out to be correct are kept.
  3. They are marked right or wrong straight away by the teacher.
  4. They are erased once the teacher gives the correct answer.
Correct answer: A
The predictions are a record of the class's starting ideas. They are not corrected on the day and are revisited every lesson, as the teacher says.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: completing the N column of the 10-nuclide data table ('Show me how you calculated N for Uranium-238')
Q3. Uranium-238 has atomic number Z = 92 and mass number A = 238. How many neutrons (N) are in one nucleus of uranium-238?
  1. 146
  2. 330
  3. 92
  4. 238
Correct answer: A
Neutron number N = A − Z = 238 − 92 = 146.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: reading 'Inside the Rock' and plotting Carbon-12 (Z = 6, N = 6) and Carbon-14 (Z = 6, N = 8) on the N vs Z graph
Q4. Carbon-12 (mass number 12) and carbon-14 (mass number 14) both have atomic number Z = 6. Which statement correctly compares them?
  1. They have the same number of neutrons but different numbers of protons.
  2. They have the same number of protons (6) but different numbers of neutrons (6 and 8).
  3. Carbon-14 has 14 protons and carbon-12 has 12 protons.
  4. They are different elements because their mass numbers are different.
Correct answer: B
Z = 6 means 6 protons in both. N = A − Z gives 6 neutrons in carbon-12 and 8 neutrons in carbon-14, so they are isotopes of the same element.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: groups shade the belt of stability on the pre-printed N vs Z graph and circle the outsiders
Q5. Learners plotted many nuclides on a graph with neutron number N on the y-axis and proton number Z on the x-axis, with the line N = Z drawn. Which description best matches where the stable nuclides cluster?
  1. In a band that curves above the N = Z line for heavier elements.
  2. In a band that curves below the N = Z line for heavier elements.
  3. Spread evenly all over the graph with no pattern.
  4. Exactly on the N = Z line for every element.
Correct answer: A
The stable nuclides form a band called the belt of stability. For heavier elements the band moves above the N = Z line because stable heavy nuclei have more neutrons than protons.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Kericho runners isotope analogy and the teacher's 'limit of the analogy' statement
Q6. The Kericho runners analogy says isotopes are like two runners where one carries an extra weight. Carbon-13 has 6 protons and 7 neutrons, and it is a stable isotope of carbon. What does this show about the analogy?
  1. Neutrons only make a nucleus lighter, never heavier.
  2. Extra neutrons do not automatically make a nucleus unstable, so the 'extra weight' idea has limits.
  3. Carbon-13 must really be radioactive, because it has extra neutrons.
  4. Any isotope with more neutrons than carbon-12 is unstable.
Correct answer: B
Carbon-13 has more neutrons than carbon-12 but is stable. Stability depends on the balance of forces in the nucleus, not just on carrying extra neutrons.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: writing three sticky-note questions and posting them in the columns of the Driving Question Board
Q7. In the Driving Question Board activity, learners write questions about pitchblende and radioactivity that they cannot yet answer. Which question is the most investigable?
  1. Do you think the pitchblende rock is beautiful?
  2. Why do scientists like rocks?
  3. Is radioactivity scary?
  4. How does the darkening of a film change when the same ore is placed closer to or further from it?
Correct answer: D
This question can be tested by changing one variable (distance) and observing the film. The other questions are matters of opinion or are too vague to investigate.
