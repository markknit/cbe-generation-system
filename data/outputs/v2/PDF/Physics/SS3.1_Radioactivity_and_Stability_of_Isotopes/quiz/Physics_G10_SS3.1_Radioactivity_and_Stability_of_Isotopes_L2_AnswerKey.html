Q1. Alpha, beta and gamma radiation are each directed at sheets of paper, aluminium foil and lead. Which list shows the radiations from LEAST penetrating to MOST penetrating?
  1. Alpha, gamma, beta
  2. Alpha, beta, gamma
  3. Gamma, beta, alpha
  4. Beta, alpha, gamma
Correct answer: B
Alpha is stopped by paper, beta needs aluminium foil, and gamma is only reduced by thick lead. So penetrating power increases from alpha to beta to gamma.
Use after: Observe phase — Evidence Table check: ionising ability versus penetrating power (misconception pause)
Q2. Alpha particles are the MOST strongly ionising radiation, yet a sheet of paper stops them. What is the best explanation?
  1. They lose their energy quickly because they ionise very many atoms along a short path
  2. Alpha particles are much lighter than beta particles, so they are easily stopped
  3. Strong ionisation means an alpha particle has extra energy left over to keep travelling through matter
  4. Alpha particles have no charge, so they are easily blocked by solid materials
Correct answer: A
Each ionisation uses up some of the particle's energy. An alpha particle ionises so many atoms that it runs out of energy within a very short distance. Ionising ability and penetrating power are therefore opposite.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Nuclear Decay Equations, unguided equation (1) Radium-226
Q3. Radium-226 (atomic number 86 + 2 = 88, mass number 226) undergoes alpha decay. Which daughter nucleus is formed?
  1. Radon-222 (atomic number 86)
  2. Francium-226 (atomic number 87)
  3. Thorium-230 (atomic number 90)
  4. Radon-226 (atomic number 86)
Correct answer: A
An alpha particle removes 4 from the mass number and 2 from the atomic number. This gives mass number 226 - 4 = 222 and atomic number 88 - 2 = 86, which is radon-222.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Nuclear Decay Equations, unguided equation (2) Carbon-14
Q4. Carbon-14 (atomic number 6, mass number 14) undergoes beta-minus decay. Which daughter nucleus is formed?
  1. Boron-14 (atomic number 5)
  2. Nitrogen-14 (atomic number 7)
  3. Carbon-13 (atomic number 6)
  4. Beryllium-10 (atomic number 4)
Correct answer: B
Beta-minus emission raises the atomic number by 1 and leaves the mass number unchanged. This gives atomic number 7 and mass number 14, which is nitrogen-14.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Connecting Back to the Rock, comparing U-238 (146/92) with Th-234 (144/90)
Q5. Uranium-238 (atomic number 92) emits an alpha particle and becomes thorium-234 (atomic number 90). How many neutrons does a thorium-234 nucleus have?
  1. 142
  2. 144
  3. 90
  4. 146
Correct answer: B
Neutrons = mass number - atomic number = 234 - 90 = 144. Uranium-238 had 146 neutrons, so it lost 2 neutrons and 2 protons in the alpha particle.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Connecting Back to the Rock, blue arrows for beta decay on the N vs Z plot
Q6. On a graph of N (neutrons, vertical axis) against Z (protons, horizontal axis), a nucleus undergoes beta-minus decay. How does its position move?
  1. Up and to the left, because N increases by 1 and Z decreases by 1
  2. It does not move, because the mass number stays the same
  3. Down and to the right, because N decreases by 1 and Z increases by 1
  4. Down and to the left, because N decreases by 2 and Z decreases by 2
Correct answer: C
In beta-minus decay a neutron changes into a proton. So N goes down by 1 and Z goes up by 1, which is a diagonal step down and to the right. The first option describes alpha decay.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Connecting Back to the Rock, link back to the film in the lightproof box
Q7. A piece of pitchblende is placed on photographic film inside a lightproof paper-and-card box. The film is exposed. Which explanation fits the evidence on penetrating power?
  1. Alpha particles from uranium-238 pass through the box and expose the film
  2. Only gamma radiation exists in pitchblende, so no other radiation is emitted
  3. Beta and gamma radiation from the unstable daughter nuclei pass through the box, while alpha is stopped by paper or card
  4. The film is exposed by light that leaks into the box, not by radiation
Correct answer: C
Alpha particles are stopped by paper or card, so they cannot reach the film. Beta and gamma radiation, emitted by daughters such as thorium-234 and protactinium-234, are penetrating enough to get through and expose it.

Q8. Uranium-238 has N/Z = 146/92 ≈ 1.59. After one alpha decay it becomes thorium-234 with N/Z = 144/90 = 1.60. Which statement best fits this evidence about the uranium-238 decay chain?
  1. This first alpha step does not necessarily bring the nucleus closer to the belt, but the whole chain ends at stable lead-206
  2. Every alpha emission moves a nucleus closer to the belt of stability, so the chain stops after one step
  3. The chain never ends because beta emission always makes nuclei less stable
  4. The chain ends at thorium-234 because gamma emission makes it stable
Correct answer: A
The N/Z ratio does not move closer to the belt after the first alpha step (1.59 to 1.60). Alpha emission mainly reduces the size of a heavy nucleus, and the full chain of alpha and beta decays finally reaches stable lead-206.
