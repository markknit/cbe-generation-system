Q1. You have one 9 V bulb, a 9 V battery pack and some wires. What is the minimum you need to make the bulb light?
  1. One wire from the + terminal of the pack to the bulb
  2. Two wires that both join the bulb to the + terminal only
  3. Two wires that make a complete loop: from the + terminal through the bulb to the - terminal
  4. One wire joining the + terminal directly to the - terminal, with the bulb left out
Correct answer: C
A bulb lights only when it is part of a complete closed loop that includes the source, so the path must leave one terminal, pass through the bulb and return to the other terminal. One wire is not enough because there is no return path.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation 1: arrangement (d), a switch added, tested open and closed
Q2. A 9 V pack, a switch and a 9 V bulb are joined by wires in one loop. The switch is open and the bulb is off. Why is the bulb off?
  1. The bulb needs a second battery pack to light
  2. The wires are too long for the current to reach the bulb
  3. The open switch leaves a gap, so the loop is not complete (an open circuit)
  4. The pack has no electricity until the switch is closed
Correct answer: C
An open switch breaks the loop, making an open circuit, so the bulb goes out. Closing the switch completes the loop and the bulb lights.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation 2: opening the loop at a wire, the bulb and the switch
Q3. A group has a bulb that is lit in a complete loop with a 9 V pack. They break the loop at different places: first they remove one wire, then they put the wire back and open the switch, then they put the switch back and remove the bulb from its holder. What do they see?
  1. The bulb goes out wherever the loop is broken
  2. The bulb gets brighter when the loop is broken at the switch
  3. The bulb goes out only when the wire is removed
  4. The bulb goes out only when the break is next to the battery pack
Correct answer: A
In a single loop a break anywhere has the same effect: the circuit is open and the bulb goes out. The position of the break does not matter.
Use after: Explain phase — Introducing the standard symbols and redrawing the working circuit as a circuit diagram
Q4. In a circuit diagram, which symbol is used for a bulb?
  1. A circle with the letter V inside
  2. One long thin line next to one short thick line
  3. A circle with a cross inside
  4. A circle with the letter A inside
Correct answer: C
A bulb is drawn as a circle with a cross. A circle with A is an ammeter, a circle with V is a voltmeter, and a long line with a short line is a cell.
Use after: Explain phase — Tracing the series diagram: 'If one bulb were removed from each, where would the loop be broken?'
Q5. Three identical bulbs are connected one after another in a single loop with a 9 V pack (in series). One bulb is removed from its holder. What happens to the other two bulbs, and why?
  1. They stay lit, because each bulb has its own loop
  2. They stay lit but get dimmer, because the loop is shorter
  3. They go out, because the removed bulb used up all the current
  4. They go out, because there is only one loop and removing a bulb breaks it
Correct answer: D
Series means one loop. Removing any bulb opens the circuit, so all the bulbs go out. This is why a whole old string of Christmas lights can go dark when one bulb fails.
Use after: Explain phase — Tracing the parallel diagram and writing an explanation for the Nairobi classroom tubes
Q6. Three identical bulbs are each connected on a separate branch across the same 9 V pack (in parallel). One bulb is removed. What happens to the other two bulbs, and why?
  1. They stay lit, because the pack has no need of a loop
  2. They go out, because the removed bulb was the first in the path
  3. They go out, because the loop is broken
  4. They stay lit, because each of them is still part of a complete loop with the pack
Correct answer: D
Parallel means branches. Removing one bulb breaks only its own branch, and the other branches still form complete loops with the pack. This is like the Nairobi classroom where one tube blew and the other three stayed lit.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: sorting notes into 'We now know' and 'We still wonder'
Q7. After Lesson 2, the class knows that a bulb needs a complete closed loop with a source. Which question on the Driving Question Board should go under 'We still wonder'?
  1. Does removing a bulb from a series loop break the loop?
  2. Does an open switch stop the bulb from lighting?
  3. Does a bulb need a complete loop to light?
  4. Why are bulbs in series dimmer than bulbs in parallel when both have complete loops?
Correct answer: D
Today's work showed that a complete loop is needed and that a break stops the bulb. A complete loop does not yet explain why the brightness is different, so that question is still to be explained in later lessons.
