Q1. Brightness of a bulb depends on how fast it transfers electrical energy. Which two quantities must you multiply to find this rate (the power)?
  1. The current through the bulb and the time it is on
  2. The resistance of the bulb and the time it is on
  3. The current through the bulb and the p.d. across it
  4. The p.d. of the battery and the number of bulbs
Correct answer: C
Power is the rate of energy transfer, P = IV: the current through the component multiplied by the p.d. across it. Time is needed for energy (E = Pt), not for power.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: calculating P = IV for one series bulb
Q2. In a series circuit, one 30 Ω bulb has 3.0 V across it and a current of 0.10 A through it. What is the power of this bulb?
  1. 0.03 W
  2. 30 W
  3. 0.30 W
  4. 3.1 W
Correct answer: C
P = IV = 3.0 V x 0.10 A = 0.30 W. Dividing instead of multiplying gives 30, and adding gives 3.1.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: comparing the power of a series bulb and a parallel bulb (ratio of 9)
Q3. One identical bulb in a series circuit has 3.0 V across it and 0.10 A through it. One bulb in the parallel circuit has 9.0 V across it and 0.30 A through it. How many times more power does the parallel bulb have than the series bulb?
  1. 6 times
  2. 3 times
  3. 9 times
  4. 27 times
Correct answer: C
Parallel bulb: 9.0 x 0.30 = 2.7 W. Series bulb: 3.0 x 0.10 = 0.30 W. The ratio is 2.7 / 0.30 = 9.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: claim-evidence-reasoning on why series bulbs are dim and parallel bulbs are bright
Q4. Three identical bulbs are connected in series to a 9 V battery and glow dimly. Which statement best explains why they are dim?
  1. Each bulb gets only 3.0 V of the 9 V and 0.10 A, so its power is only 0.30 W.
  2. Each bulb has a much higher resistance when it is in series.
  3. The battery has become weaker because it is connected in series.
  4. The first bulb uses up most of the current, so little is left for the others.
Correct answer: A
The 9 V is shared between three bulbs, so each has 3.0 V across it and 0.10 A through it. Its power is 0.30 W, much less than the 2.7 W of a bulb connected directly across 9 V. The bulb resistance stays the same and current is not used up.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: cost problem (a), four 36 W tubes for 5 h
Q5. A Nairobi classroom has four 36 W tubes working on the 240 V mains. All four are on for 5 hours. How much energy do they use?
  1. 0.72 kWh
  2. 720 kWh
  3. 0.144 kWh
  4. 7.2 kWh
Correct answer: A
Total power = 4 x 36 = 144 W = 0.144 kW. Energy = 0.144 kW x 5 h = 0.72 kWh.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: what happens to the cost if one of the four tubes blows
Q6. One of the four 36 W tubes in a classroom blows. The other three stay lit for 5 hours. Electricity costs KSh 25 per kWh (a teaching figure). What is the cost of running the three tubes?
  1. KSh 18.00
  2. KSh 4.50
  3. KSh 13.50
  4. KSh 54.00
Correct answer: C
Power = 3 x 36 = 108 W = 0.108 kW. Energy = 0.108 x 5 = 0.54 kWh. Cost = 0.54 x 25 = KSh 13.50.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: cost problem (b), 60 W bulb compared with 9 W LED
Q7. A 60 W filament bulb is on for 5 hours. Electricity costs KSh 25 per kWh (a teaching figure). What does it cost?
  1. KSh 75
  2. KSh 0.30
  3. KSh 7.50
  4. KSh 1.13
Correct answer: C
Energy = 0.060 kW x 5 h = 0.300 kWh. Cost = 0.300 x 25 = KSh 7.50. A 9 W LED for the same time costs only about KSh 1.13.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: Achieng's transfer question on the 12 V solar system and 36 W light
Q8. A 12 V solar system powers a 36 W light. Using P = IV, what current does the light draw?
  1. 48 A
  2. 432 A
  3. 0.33 A
  4. 3 A
Correct answer: D
I = P / V = 36 W / 12 V = 3 A.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: showing that the series current is 0.10 A everywhere on the model
Q9. Three identical bulbs are in series. The current through the first bulb is 0.10 A. What is the current through the third bulb?
  1. Less than 0.10 A, because the first two bulbs have used up some current
  2. 0.10 A, the same as everywhere else in the series loop
  3. 0.30 A, because the current adds up along the loop
  4. 0.03 A, because each bulb uses up some current
Correct answer: B
Current is not used up in a series loop. It is 0.10 A everywhere. What changes at each bulb is the energy transferred per coulomb (the p.d. across it).
