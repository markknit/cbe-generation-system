Q1. Three identical 30 Ω bulbs are connected on separate branches (in parallel) across a 9 V battery pack. What is the potential difference (p.d.) across each bulb?
  1. 4.5 V
  2. 27 V
  3. 9.0 V
  4. 3.0 V
Correct answer: C
Each branch is connected directly across the battery terminals, so every parallel bulb has the full 9.0 V. The 3.0 V value is the carry-over from the series circuit, where the 9 V is shared among three bulbs.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 3, ammeter placed in series in each branch
Q2. A 30 Ω bulb is on its own branch directly across a 9 V battery pack. What current flows in that branch?
  1. 0.60 A
  2. 0.90 A
  3. 0.30 A
  4. 0.10 A
Correct answer: C
I = V/R = 9.0/30 = 0.30 A. The 0.10 A is the series value and 0.90 A is the total battery current for three such branches.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 3, ammeter placed at the battery (expect 0.90 A)
Q3. Three identical bulbs each carry 0.30 A on separate parallel branches. What does an ammeter placed at the battery read?
  1. 0.10 A
  2. 0.30 A
  3. 0.90 A
  4. 0.60 A
Correct answer: C
Branch currents add up in a parallel circuit: 0.30 + 0.30 + 0.30 = 0.90 A. The current is not shared out as in a series circuit.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: step (3) effective resistance, 1/R = 3/30
Q4. Three 30 Ω bulbs are connected in parallel. Using 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3, what is the effective resistance of the combination?
  1. 90 Ω
  2. 30 Ω
  3. 10 Ω
  4. 0.10 Ω
Correct answer: C
1/R = 1/30 + 1/30 + 1/30 = 3/30, so R = 30/3 = 10 Ω. This is smaller than any one bulb because the extra branches give the current more paths.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 4, removing one bulb and re-reading the meters
Q5. Three identical 30 Ω bulbs are lit on separate parallel branches across a 9 V pack. The battery current is 0.90 A. One bulb is removed. What happens?
  1. The other two bulbs share the 9 V, so each has 4.5 V and the battery current falls to 0.60 A.
  2. The other two bulbs go out because the circuit is broken.
  3. The other two bulbs get brighter and the battery current stays at 0.90 A.
  4. The other two bulbs stay at 9.0 V and 0.30 A each, and the battery current falls to 0.60 A.
Correct answer: D
Each remaining branch is still connected across the battery, so it keeps 9.0 V and 0.30 A. Only the total current falls: 0.30 + 0.30 = 0.60 A.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: working the Nairobi classroom tubes (0.60 A falling to 0.45 A)
Q6. A classroom has 4 identical fluorescent tubes in parallel across 240 V. Each tube is rated 36 W, so each draws 0.15 A. One tube blows. What is the total current from the supply now?
  1. 0.45 A
  2. 0.15 A
  3. 0.60 A
  4. 0 A
Correct answer: A
The other three tubes still have 240 V and 0.15 A each, so the total is 3 × 0.15 = 0.45 A (the same as 3 × 36/240). The total falls from 0.60 A but not to zero.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: written paragraph on why the Christmas-light string (series) goes dark but the classroom lights stay on
Q7. In an old string of Christmas lights, the bulbs are connected one after another in series. One bulb fails and the whole string goes dark. Why?
  1. The failed bulb takes the whole p.d., leaving none for the other bulbs.
  2. The bulbs are in a single loop, so the failed bulb breaks the circuit and no current flows anywhere.
  3. The remaining bulbs get too much p.d. and all burn out at once.
  4. The failed bulb uses up all the current before it reaches the others.
Correct answer: B
In series there is only one path for the charge. A failed bulb opens the loop, so the current is zero everywhere, unlike a parallel circuit where each branch has its own path.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: step (4) power link, 2.7 W in parallel compared with 0.30 W in series
Q8. A 9.0 V, 30 Ω bulb on a parallel branch carries 0.30 A, while the same bulb in a series circuit carries 0.10 A and has 3.0 V across it. What is the power of the bulb in the parallel branch, and how does this compare with the series bulb?
  1. 0.30 W, the same as the series bulb
  2. 0.90 W, three times the series bulb
  3. 2.7 W, which is 9 times the series bulb's 0.30 W
  4. 27 W, because the bulb has 9.0 V and the series current is 3.0 A
Correct answer: C
P = V × I = 9.0 × 0.30 = 2.7 W. The series bulb has P = 3.0 × 0.10 = 0.30 W, so the parallel bulb is 9 times greater and so is much brighter.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: recording Kipchirchir's battery question and linking it to the 0.90 A vs 0.10 A readings
Q9. Kipchirchir says: 'The battery will get used up faster in parallel.' Three identical bulbs draw 0.90 A from the same battery when in parallel, but 0.10 A when in series. What is the best response to his idea?
  1. He is right, because each bulb in parallel has only 3.0 V.
  2. He is wrong, because the battery current is the same in both circuits.
  3. He is partly right, because the parallel circuit draws a larger current from the battery (0.90 A compared with 0.10 A).
  4. He is wrong, because current is used up in each bulb so both circuits drain the battery equally.
Correct answer: C
The measured battery current is 9 times larger in parallel, so more charge flows from the battery each second and it would run down faster. The reasons that he is wrong or that p.d. is 3.0 V do not match the data.
Use after: End of lesson — Explain: KICD extension, metre bridge worked example and homework (R = 10 Ω, l = 60 cm)
Q10. In a metre bridge, an unknown resistor X is in one gap and a known resistor R = 10 Ω is in the other. The galvanometer reads zero when the jockey is at l = 60 cm from the X end. Using X = R × l/(100 − l), what is X?
  1. 6.0 Ω
  2. 60 Ω
  3. 15 Ω
  4. 6.7 Ω
Correct answer: C
X = 10 × 60/(100 − 60) = 10 × 60/40 = 15 Ω. The value 6.7 Ω comes from swapping l and (100 − l).
