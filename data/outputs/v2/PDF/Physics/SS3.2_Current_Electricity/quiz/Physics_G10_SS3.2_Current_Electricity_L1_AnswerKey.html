Q1. In class, three identical bulbs are connected one after another in a single loop to a 9 V battery pack (series). What do learners observe about the brightness of the bulbs?
  1. The first bulb is brightest and the last bulb is the dimmest
  2. The bulbs glow dim
  3. The bulbs glow brightly
  4. The bulbs do not light at all
Correct answer: B
In the series demonstration the three identical bulbs all glow dim. The observation does not show the first bulb brighter than the last.
Use after: Observe phase — Demo A: unscrewing one bulb in the series circuit
Q2. Three identical bulbs are connected in series to a 9 V pack. Mr. Otieno unscrews the middle bulb. What do learners observe?
  1. All the bulbs go out
  2. The other two bulbs become brighter
  3. Only the bulb next to the battery stays lit
  4. The other two bulbs stay lit at the same brightness
Correct answer: A
In Demo A, unscrewing one bulb in the series circuit makes all the bulbs go out.
Use after: Observe phase — Demo B: three identical bulbs on separate branches with one bulb unscrewed
Q3. Three identical bulbs are connected on separate branches (parallel) to a 9 V pack, and one bulb is unscrewed. What do learners observe about the other two bulbs?
  1. They stay lit at the same brightness as before
  2. They become dimmer
  3. They go out
  4. They flicker and then go out
Correct answer: A
In Demo B, the two remaining bulbs stay lit at the same brightness as before when one is removed.
Use after: Observe phase — Demo B: Mr. Otieno says that nothing has changed except how the wires are arranged
Q4. Which statement about the two bench circuits is correct? Both use the same type of 9 V battery pack and three identical bulbs.
  1. The brightness is different because the parallel circuit uses a stronger battery
  2. The brightness is the same, but the parallel bulbs stay lit longer
  3. The series bulbs glow brighter because they are connected one after another
  4. The parallel bulbs glow brighter than the series bulbs, even though only the connection pattern is different
Correct answer: D
The same battery and identical bulbs were used. Only the arrangement changed, and the parallel bulbs were brighter.
Use after: Observe phase — Looking at the old string of Christmas lights and linking it to the bench circuits
Q5. An old string of Christmas lights goes completely dark when one bulb fails. Which of the two bench circuits does it behave like?
  1. The parallel circuit, because the bulbs are dim
  2. The parallel circuit, because the other bulbs stay lit
  3. Neither circuit, because bulbs in Christmas lights are different
  4. The series circuit, because all the bulbs go out when one is removed
Correct answer: D
Because the whole string goes dark when one bulb fails, it behaves like the series circuit. The classroom tubes behave like the parallel circuit.
Use after: Observe phase — Looking at the classroom lighting: switching off one tube while the others stay lit
Q6. During evening prep in a Nairobi classroom, one of four fluorescent tubes blows out and the other three stay lit. Which bench circuit does the classroom lighting behave like?
  1. Both circuits behave the same way
  2. The series circuit
  3. The parallel circuit
  4. Neither circuit
Correct answer: C
In the parallel circuit the other bulbs stay lit when one is removed, as the classroom tubes do.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: posting questions such as Wanjiru's 'Why are the series bulbs so dim?'
Q7. At the end of Lesson 1, a learner asks: "Why are the series bulbs so dim?" What is the best response in this lesson?
  1. The battery pushes harder in the parallel circuit
  2. We have not yet gathered evidence to explain this, so we put it on the Driving Question Board to investigate
  3. The first bulb uses up the current, so the others get less
  4. Bulbs in series share the light between them
Correct answer: B
This is the anchoring lesson, so the cause is deliberately not explained. The question goes on the Driving Question Board until evidence is collected. The other choices are untested ideas that the teacher does not confirm.
