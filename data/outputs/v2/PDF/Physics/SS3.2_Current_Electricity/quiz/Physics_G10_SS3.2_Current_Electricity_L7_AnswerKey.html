Q1. A 9 V battery is connected to one 30 Ω bulb. Two more identical bulbs are then added in the same single loop (in series). What happens to the current from the battery, and why?
  1. It increases, because more bulbs can carry more current
  2. It stays the same, because the battery p.d. is still 9 V
  3. It decreases, because the first bulb uses up some of the current
  4. It decreases, because the total resistance of the loop increases
Correct answer: D
Adding bulbs in series adds resistance (30 Ω becomes 90 Ω), so by V = IR the current falls from 0.30 A to 0.10 A. The current is not used up.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 1: measure the current at three points (before bulb 1, between bulbs 1 and 2, after bulb 3)
Q2. Three identical bulbs are connected in series to a 9 V battery. An ammeter reads 0.10 A before bulb 1. What will the ammeter read between bulb 1 and bulb 2?
  1. 0.07 A, because bulb 1 has used up some current
  2. 0.05 A, because the current is shared equally between the bulbs
  3. 0.10 A, because the current is the same everywhere in a series loop
  4. 0.00 A, because bulb 1 stops the current
Correct answer: C
There is only one path, so the same charge passes each point every second. Current is not used up by the bulbs.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 2 and Step 3: measure the p.d. across each bulb and add the three p.d.s to compare with the battery
Q3. Three identical bulbs are connected in series to a 9 V battery. A voltmeter is connected across one bulb. What does it read?
  1. 3.0 V, because the 9 V is shared equally between three identical bulbs
  2. 0.10 V, because the current is 0.10 A
  3. 9.0 V, because each bulb gets the full battery p.d.
  4. 4.5 V, because the battery p.d. is shared between two bulbs
Correct answer: A
Identical bulbs in series share the battery p.d. equally: 9 V ÷ 3 = 3.0 V each, and 3.0 + 3.0 + 3.0 = 9.0 V.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 4: remove one bulb (unscrew bulb 2) and observe all bulbs go out
Q4. In a series circuit of three bulbs, bulb 2 is unscrewed. All the bulbs go out and the ammeter reads 0 A. What is the correct explanation?
  1. The other two bulbs now have too much resistance for current to flow
  2. The battery has run out of charge because bulb 2 was removed
  3. The loop is open, so there is no complete path and the current is zero
  4. Bulb 2 was using all the current, so the others now get none
Correct answer: C
A series circuit has one path only. Removing a bulb breaks the loop, so current cannot flow anywhere, which is also why one dead bulb makes an old Christmas-light string go dark.
Use after: Explain phase — Steps 1 to 3: use V = IR to find R(total) = 90 Ω and show R(total) = R1 + R2 + R3
Q5. A 9 V battery is connected to three identical 30 Ω bulbs in series. What is the current from the battery?
  1. 3.0 A
  2. 0.33 A
  3. 0.30 A
  4. 0.10 A
Correct answer: D
R(total) = 30 + 30 + 30 = 90 Ω. Then I = V/R = 9/90 = 0.10 A.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 4: calculate power in each bulb, P = IV, and compare with the rating of 2.7 W
Q6. In a series circuit, each 30 Ω bulb has 3.0 V across it and 0.10 A through it. Each bulb is rated 2.7 W at its full 9 V. What power does each bulb actually have, and what does this mean for its brightness?
  1. 0.30 W, which is much less than 2.7 W, so the bulb glows dim
  2. 0.03 W, so the bulb is too weak to light at all
  3. 2.7 W, so the bulb glows at full brightness
  4. 0.90 W, because power is 9 V × 0.10 A, so it glows brighter than normal
Correct answer: A
P = IV = 0.10 × 3.0 = 0.30 W. This is 9 times smaller than 2.7 W, so the bulb glows dim.
Use after: Explain phase — Exit-ticket question: two 30 Ω resistors in series on a 9 V battery
Q7. Two 30 Ω resistors are connected in series to a 9 V battery. What is the current in the circuit?
  1. 0.10 A
  2. 0.30 A
  3. 0.15 A
  4. 0.075 A
Correct answer: C
R(total) = 30 + 30 = 60 Ω. I = V/R = 9/60 = 0.15 A.
Use after: Explain phase — KICD extension: teacher demonstration of the potential-divider example (60 Ω and 30 Ω on 9 V)
Q8. A 60 Ω resistor and a 30 Ω resistor are connected in series to a 9 V battery to form a potential divider. What is the p.d. across the 60 Ω resistor?
  1. 9.0 V
  2. 3.0 V
  3. 6.0 V
  4. 4.5 V
Correct answer: C
R(total) = 90 Ω, so I = 9/90 = 0.10 A. The p.d. across the 60 Ω resistor is V = IR = 0.10 × 60 = 6.0 V (and 3.0 V across the 30 Ω).
