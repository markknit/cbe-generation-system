Q1. Proxima Centauri is about 40 trillion km from Earth. The fastest spacecraft ever built, the Parker Solar Probe, reaches about 700,000 km/h. Roughly how long would the journey take at that speed?
  1. About 65,000 years
  2. About 6,500 years
  3. About 650 years
  4. About 65 years
Correct answer: B
Time = distance ÷ speed = 40 000 000 000 000 ÷ 700 000 ≈ 57 million hours. Dividing by 24 × 365 = 8 760 hours per year gives about 6,500 years, so travelling there is not practical and telescopes are needed.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Component 1: ray diagram of a convex lens refracting parallel rays to a focal point
Q2. In a refracting telescope, parallel rays of light from a distant star pass through a convex objective lens. Where does the lens bring these rays together to form the image?
  1. At the star, where the light started
  2. At the focal point, one focal length away from the lens
  3. Nowhere; the rays stay parallel and spread out evenly
  4. On the surface of the objective lens itself
Correct answer: B
A convex lens refracts parallel rays so they converge at the focal point, which is one focal length from the lens. The eyepiece then magnifies this image. Labelling the focal point on the lens itself is a common error.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Component 1: comparing the refracting and reflecting telescope diagrams
Q3. Why are the world's biggest modern optical telescopes reflecting telescopes (using mirrors) and not refracting telescopes (using lenses)?
  1. Mirrors detect radio waves, which lenses cannot
  2. Lenses cannot bend light at all
  3. Mirrors can be made very large and still keep their shape, while very large lenses are heavy and sag under their own weight
  4. Light travels faster after it reflects from a mirror than after it passes through a lens
Correct answer: C
Large lenses are heavy and sag, which spoils the image. Mirrors can be much larger and still hold a precise shape. Lenses do bend light (refraction), and mirrors do not detect radio waves or speed light up.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Component 2: video on the Hubble Space Telescope and the Hubble Deep Field
Q4. The Hubble Deep Field image shows thousands of galaxies in a patch of sky about the size of a grain of sand held at arm's length. What does this image give us as evidence?
  1. The universe contains an enormous number of galaxies, even in a tiny part of the sky
  2. An astronaut travelled to these galaxies and photographed them up close
  3. All the objects in the image are stars inside our own Milky Way
  4. The patch of sky was empty, so space is mostly nothing
Correct answer: A
Seeing thousands of galaxies in such a tiny patch shows that the universe is vast and full of galaxies. The evidence was gathered remotely by a telescope, not by travelling there.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: three-column comparison table (refracting, reflecting, radio telescope)
Q5. The MeerKAT telescope in South Africa, part of the Square Kilometre Array project, is a radio telescope. Which statement about radio telescopes is correct?
  1. They use large dish antennas to collect radio waves from galaxies and hydrogen clouds
  2. They must be launched into space to work
  3. They collect sound waves travelling through space
  4. They use large lenses to bend visible light to a focal point
Correct answer: A
Radio telescopes use large dish antennas, not lenses or mirrors, to detect invisible radio waves emitted by galaxies and hydrogen clouds. Radio waves are not sound waves, and MeerKAT works from the ground in Africa.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: dotted timeline arrows from each telescope back to cosmic history
Q6. The James Webb Space Telescope detects infrared light from the first stars and galaxies, which formed over 13 billion years ago. What does this tell us about light and distance?
  1. The early universe is very close to Earth
  2. The telescope travels back in time to collect the light
  3. Light from very distant objects takes so long to reach us that we see those objects as they were long ago
  4. Light from distant objects shows us how they look today
Correct answer: C
Light takes time to travel across space, so the light from very distant objects left them long ago. Telescopes therefore look back in time. Each telescope arrow in the model should point to the epoch of the objects it observes, not always to the Big Bang.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: sticky note beginning "Scientists gather evidence by..."
Q7. Complete the sentence in the best way: "Scientists gather evidence about distant stars and galaxies by..."
  1. waiting for the stars to move close enough to Earth to touch
  2. sending astronauts to visit each star and bring back samples
  3. using telescopes to collect light and other radiation, such as radio waves, that travel to us from distant objects
  4. looking carefully with the naked eye, because it works as well as any instrument
Correct answer: C
Distant objects cannot be visited, so scientists collect the light and other radiation that reaches Earth, using refracting, reflecting and radio telescopes on Earth and in space.
