Q1. The Moon travels around Earth and does not fly off into space in a straight line. Which statement explains this best?
  1. Earth's gravity pulls the Moon inward while the Moon keeps moving sideways, so it follows a curved path
  2. Earth's magnetic field holds the Moon in place
  3. Gravity pulls the Moon inward and an equal outward force pushes it away, so the two forces cancel
  4. The Moon's own gravity pushes it away from Earth, and this keeps it at a steady distance
Correct answer: A
The only real force on the Moon is Earth's gravity pulling it inward. Because the Moon is also moving sideways, the pull bends its path into an orbit instead of letting it fall in or fly away. No outward force is needed.

Q2. A tide table for Mombasa Old Port shows these readings for Day 1: 00:12 h 3.1 m, 06:24 h 0.4 m, 12:48 h 3.0 m, 18:36 h 0.5 m. What pattern do the readings show?
  1. One high tide per day, so the sea is high once every 24 hours
  2. Two high tides per day, roughly 12 hours apart
  3. Four high tides per day, roughly 6 hours apart
  4. The water level stays almost the same all day
Correct answer: B
The heights are high (3.1 m and 3.0 m) at 00:12 h and 12:48 h, which is about 12 hours apart. The low readings of 0.4 m and 0.5 m fall between them, so there are two high tides each day.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Source B, Earth-Moon tidal bulge diagram (observation task 3 and the 'Why are there TWO tidal bulges?' question)
Q3. Water on Earth bulges on the side facing the Moon and also on the opposite side at the same time. What explains the bulge on the far side?
  1. The Moon pulls Earth itself more strongly than it pulls the far-side water, so the far-side water is left behind and bulges outwards
  2. Earth's spin throws water off only the side that faces away from the Moon
  3. The Sun pushes the far-side water away from the Moon
  4. The Moon pushes the far-side water away from itself
Correct answer: A
Gravity gets weaker with distance. The near-side water is pulled most, Earth's centre is pulled less, and the far-side water is pulled least. Earth moves towards the Moon more than the far-side water does, so the far-side water is left behind and forms a second bulge.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Explanation 2, inverse-square ratio activity, Problem A
Q4. The Moon is moved to twice its present distance from Earth. Its mass does not change. By what factor does the gravitational force between Earth and the Moon change?
  1. It becomes 8 times weaker
  2. It becomes 4 times weaker
  3. It becomes 2 times weaker
  4. It stays the same, because the masses have not changed
Correct answer: B
Gravitational force is proportional to 1/d². If d doubles, the force becomes 1/2² = 1/4 of its original value, so it is 4 times weaker.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Explanation 2, inverse-square ratio activity, Problem B (i)
Q5. The Sun has 27 million times the mass of the Moon and is about 390 times farther from Earth. Use F proportional to M divided by d² to compare their pulls on Earth. How does the Sun's pull compare with the Moon's pull?
  1. The Moon pulls harder, by about 390 times
  2. The Sun pulls about 178 times harder
  3. The Sun pulls about 69,000 times harder
  4. The Sun pulls about 27 million times harder
Correct answer: B
Ratio = 27,000,000 ÷ 390² = 27,000,000 ÷ 152,100 ≈ 178. The Sun pulls on Earth about 178 times harder than the Moon does (roughly 180 times).
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Explanation 2, Problem B (ii), tidal effect and the M divided by d-cubed rule
Q6. Tidal effects depend on the difference in pull between the near and far sides of Earth. This difference is proportional to M divided by d³. The Sun has 27 million times the Moon's mass and is about 390 times farther away. Compared with the Moon's tidal effect, the Sun's tidal effect is about:
  1. 0.46 times as big, so about 46 percent of the Moon's
  2. zero, because the Sun is too far away to cause tides
  3. 69,000 times bigger, because the Sun is so massive
  4. 180 times bigger, because the Sun pulls much harder
Correct answer: A
Ratio = 27,000,000 ÷ 390³ = 27,000,000 ÷ 59,319,000 ≈ 0.46. The Sun's tidal effect is about 46 percent of the Moon's, so the Moon raises the bigger tides, but the Sun's effect is not zero.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Explanation 3, Kenya connection (Mombasa and Lake Victoria contrast)
Q7. The Moon pulls on the water of Lake Victoria just as it pulls on the Indian Ocean at Mombasa. Why does Lake Victoria show almost no tide?
  1. Lake Victoria is too far from the Sun to have tides
  2. The Moon only pulls on salt water, not fresh water
  3. The Moon's gravity cannot reach inland Africa
  4. The lake is so small that the Moon's pull is almost the same across all of it, so the difference in pull is tiny
Correct answer: D
Tides come from the difference in the Moon's pull across a water body. Across a small lake that difference is tiny, so any tide is only a few millimetres to about a centimetre. Rain, wind and seiches cause larger changes.
