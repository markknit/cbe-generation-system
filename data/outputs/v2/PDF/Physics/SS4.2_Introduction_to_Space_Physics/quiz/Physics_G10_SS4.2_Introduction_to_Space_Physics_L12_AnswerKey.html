Q1. In Lesson 1, a learner wrote: "The night sky above Nairobi has always looked exactly like this, and it has always existed." Which statement best corrects this idea?
  1. Scientists estimate the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in the Big Bang, and stars and planets have formed and changed since then.
  2. The stars were made at the Big Bang and have stayed exactly the same ever since.
  3. The night sky is only a few thousand years old, because that is how long people have watched it.
  4. Everything in the sky was made at the same moment about 4.6 billion years ago, and nothing has changed since.
Correct answer: A
The anchoring phenomenon is that the sky looks unchanging, but the evidence shows a universe about 13.8 billion years old that keeps changing. Stars are born, evolve and die, and planets formed much later.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Phase: Evidence Audit, checklist item 2 (CMB radiation as evidence for the Big Bang)
Q2. Scientists detect faint microwave radiation coming from every direction in space, called the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). What is this radiation evidence for?
  1. It is leftover energy from the very hot early universe, which supports the Big Bang.
  2. It is heat from the Sun that has bounced off the planets.
  3. It is light from the Milky Way's brightest stars, which supports stellar evolution.
  4. It is radio signals sent by satellites orbiting the Earth.
Correct answer: A
The CMB is the cooled remnant of radiation from the hot, dense early universe. It is key evidence for the Big Bang.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Phase: Evidence Audit, checklist item 3 (Hubble's redshift observations and expanding universe)
Q3. Hubble observed that the light from distant galaxies is shifted towards the red end of the spectrum (redshift). What does this observation show?
  1. The Earth is at the exact centre of the universe.
  2. Galaxies are moving away from us, so the universe is expanding.
  3. The distant galaxies are on fire and glowing red.
  4. Distant galaxies are moving towards us and are getting closer.
Correct answer: B
Redshift means light waves are stretched because the source is moving away. Galaxies moving away from us is evidence that the universe is expanding.
Use after: Explain phase — Observe Phase checklist item 4 and Model Phase Task 1, part (c): stellar evolution and heavier elements
Q4. Where did the carbon, oxygen and iron atoms in your body come from?
  1. They were all made in the first few minutes after the Big Bang.
  2. They were made inside the Earth's core and rose to the surface.
  3. They were made inside stars and scattered into space when massive stars exploded as supernovae.
  4. They were made by the Sun and sent to Earth as sunlight.
Correct answer: C
Heavier elements such as carbon, oxygen and iron were forged in stellar cores and spread by supernovae. Only the hydrogen came mainly from the Big Bang.
Use after: Explain phase — Observe Phase checklist item 7 (Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation and orbital mechanics) and Model Phase Task 1, part (e)
Q5. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation says the force of gravity between two bodies depends on their masses and the distance between them. If the distance between two bodies is doubled, with the masses unchanged, what happens to the gravitational force?
  1. It becomes four times stronger.
  2. It stays the same.
  3. It becomes half as strong.
  4. It becomes one quarter as strong.
Correct answer: D
Gravitational force is proportional to 1/r². Doubling r gives 1/2² = 1/4 of the original force.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: Final Explanation presentations (types of telescopic and electromagnetic evidence)
Q6. Scientists cannot travel to distant stars and galaxies. Which method do they mainly use to study them?
  1. Digging for rocks that fell from the stars to the ground.
  2. Collecting samples of star material with rockets.
  3. Collecting and analysing electromagnetic radiation such as visible light and radio waves using telescopes.
  4. Measuring the sounds that stars make in space.
Correct answer: C
Distant objects are studied by detecting the electromagnetic radiation they send us, such as visible light, radio waves and the CMB, using telescopes.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB Phase: traffic-light categorisation of Driving Question Board questions
Q7. On the Driving Question Board, a class marks a question YELLOW. What does a yellow dot mean?
  1. The question was wrong and should be removed.
  2. It is a frontier question that no one can answer yet.
  3. We have partial evidence, but the question needs more investigation.
  4. We can fully answer it with evidence from our unit.
Correct answer: C
Green means answered with evidence, yellow means partly answered and needing more investigation, and red means still open.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Phase Task 1: Individual Written Reflection, part (d) (formation of the Solar System)
Q8. According to the unit, how did our Solar System form?
  1. From a collapsing cloud (nebula) of gas and dust about 4.6 billion years ago.
  2. From one giant star that split into the Sun and the planets.
  3. The Sun formed first and then captured planets that came from other galaxies.
  4. Directly from the Big Bang about 13.8 billion years ago.
Correct answer: A
The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing nebula. This was much later than the Big Bang, and the nebula contained material from earlier stars.
Use after: End of lesson — Model Phase Task 1, part (g) and Task 2: Whole-Class Synthesis of the Final Explanation
Q9. A person standing in the Rift Valley on a clear night looks up at the stars. Which statement best describes what they are seeing?
  1. A fixed pattern of lights that has never changed since the Big Bang.
  2. Reflected light from the Moon only.
  3. The stars exactly as they are at this very moment.
  4. Ancient light that left objects long ago, giving a snapshot of a universe that is changing over billions of years.
Correct answer: D
Light takes time to travel, so we see stars as they were in the past. The night sky is a snapshot of a dynamic, evolving universe, not a static one.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Phase Task 2: Whole-Class Synthesis (sentence-by-sentence Final Explanation)
Q10. A group is building a causal chain from the Big Bang to the night sky above Nairobi. Which order is correct?
  1. Big Bang, then first stars and galaxies form, then massive stars end as supernovae and scatter heavier elements, then the Solar System forms.
  2. First stars form, then the Big Bang, then supernovae, then the Solar System forms.
  3. Big Bang, then the Solar System forms, then the first stars, then supernovae.
  4. Solar System forms, then the Big Bang, then first stars, then supernovae.
Correct answer: A
Hydrogen clouds collapse into the first stars and galaxies after the Big Bang. Supernovae then scatter heavier elements, and later a nebula collapses to form the Solar System.
