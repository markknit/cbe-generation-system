Q1. A Jiko stool stays steady when a 20-litre jerrycan of water is placed over its legs. It topples when the same jerrycan is placed so that it hangs out beyond one leg at the corner. Which explanation best fits this?
  1. At the corner, the jerrycan's weight acts farther from the leg that acts as the pivot, so its turning effect is larger.
  2. A heavy load always makes a stool topple, wherever it is placed.
  3. The jerrycan becomes heavier when it is placed at the corner.
  4. The stool only topples because the person nudged it.
Correct answer: A
The weight of the jerrycan is the same in both positions. Only its distance from the pivot (the leg) changes, so the turning effect (moment) changes.
Use after: Observe phase — Metre rule and pencil pivot investigation: moving the left 500 g mass to 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 cm
Q2. A metre rule is balanced on a pencil pivot at the 50 cm mark. A 500 g mass hangs on the right, 30 cm from the pivot. An identical 500 g mass hangs on the left, 20 cm from the pivot. What happens?
  1. The right side goes down because its mass is farther from the pivot.
  2. The rule stays balanced because both masses are 500 g.
  3. It is impossible to tell without converting the masses to newtons.
  4. The left side goes down because the left mass is closer to the pivot.
Correct answer: A
The two forces are equal, but the right mass is farther from the pivot. It has the larger moment, so the right side goes down.
Use after: Observe phase — Door/gate demonstration: pushing near the hinge vs near the outer edge
Q3. At a school gate, Wanjiru pushes with the same force first near the hinge and then near the outer edge. Why does it feel harder to push near the hinge?
  1. Near the hinge the distance from the pivot is smaller, so the same force gives a smaller turning effect.
  2. The turning effect does not depend on where you push, so it only feels harder.
  3. Near the hinge the force is smaller, so the gate resists more.
  4. Near the hinge the gate becomes heavier.
Correct answer: A
The hinge is the pivot. Moment = force × distance, so the same force at a smaller distance gives a smaller moment and the gate is harder to turn.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 4 worked example (a): mama pushing a mkokoteni cart
Q4. A mama pushes a mkokoteni cart handle with a force of 40 N. The handle is 0.8 m from the wheel axle, which acts as the pivot. What is the moment of her force about the axle?
  1. 0.02 N·m
  2. 50 N·m
  3. 40.8 N·m
  4. 32 N·m
Correct answer: D
Moment = F × d = 40 N × 0.8 m = 32 N·m. Dividing or adding the numbers is a common error.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 4 worked examples (b) and (c): school gate pushed with 25 N at different distances from the hinge
Q5. A student pushes a school gate with a force of 25 N at a point 30 cm from the hinge. What is the moment of the force about the hinge in N·m?
  1. 7.5 N·m
  2. 55 N·m
  3. 30 N·m
  4. 750 N·m
Correct answer: A
Convert 30 cm to 0.3 m first. Moment = 25 N × 0.3 m = 7.5 N·m. Using 30 gives 750, which is in N·cm and not the SI unit.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 3 of the derivation: Units for moment
Q6. A force is measured in newtons (N) and a perpendicular distance is measured in metres (m). In what unit is the moment of the force given?
  1. N
  2. N/m
  3. Newton-metre (N·m)
  4. kg·m
Correct answer: C
Moment = force × distance, so the unit is newtons × metres = N·m.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 5 phenomenon reconnection: Jiko stool moment calculation (200 N jerrycan)
Q7. A jerrycan overhangs the corner of a Jiko stool. To work out the moment of its weight that tends to topple the stool, which distance should be used as d?
  1. The height of the stool above the floor.
  2. The distance from the jerrycan to the opposite corner of the seat.
  3. The distance from the jerrycan's weight to the centre of the stool.
  4. The perpendicular distance from the line of action of the jerrycan's weight to the nearest supporting leg (the pivot).
Correct answer: D
The stool begins to turn about the nearest leg, so that leg is the pivot. d is the perpendicular distance from the weight's line of action to that pivot.
