Q1. A tall three-legged stool has a heavy maize sack placed on one corner of its seat, and the stool is given a small nudge. Which condition tells you the stool will topple?
  1. The nudge is bigger than the weight of the sack.
  2. The vertical line through the combined centre of gravity falls outside the base of support.
  3. The stool is tall, so it always topples when nudged.
  4. The sack is heavy, so the stool must fall.
Correct answer: B
An object topples when the vertical line through the combined centre of gravity of the stool and load falls outside the base of support. The weight then gives an unbalanced moment that turns the stool over. 'It is heavy' or 'it is tall' are vague and are not the deciding condition.
Use after: Observe phase — Live demonstration: (C) container at the corner of the wide flat model rocks but returns upright
Q2. A wide, low four-legged stool carries a full jerrycan at one corner with a small overhang. When nudged, it rocks and then returns upright. What is the best explanation?
  1. The vertical line through the centre of gravity falls outside the base, but the legs push the stool back.
  2. The stool is heavy, so no moments act on it.
  3. The jerrycan weighs less on a four-legged stool than on a three-legged stool.
  4. The wide base keeps the vertical line through the combined centre of gravity inside the base of support, so the stool returns upright.
Correct answer: D
On a wide base the vertical line through the combined centre of gravity stays inside the base of support even when the stool tilts. The weight's moment then turns the stool back to upright, which is stable equilibrium.
Use after: Explain phase — Group model presentations: moment arm annotations (teacher draws a perpendicular on the board)
Q3. A force of 50 N pushes on a door at an angle. Which distance is used with the force to calculate the moment about the hinge?
  1. The full width of the door.
  2. The height of the hand above the floor.
  3. The shortest (perpendicular) distance from the hinge to the line along which the force acts.
  4. The distance along the slanted path from the hinge to the hand.
Correct answer: C
Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. Using the total or slanted distance is the common error of confusing the moment arm with total distance.
Use after: Model Building phase — Summative problem set, Question 2: school gate moment
Q4. A student pushes a school gate with a force of 40 N at a point 0.8 m from the hinge. The force is perpendicular to the gate. What is the moment of the force about the hinge?
  1. 0.02 N·m
  2. 50 N·m
  3. 40.8 N·m
  4. 32 N·m
Correct answer: D
Moment = force × perpendicular distance = 40 N × 0.8 m = 32 N·m.
Use after: Model Building phase — Summative problem set, Question 3: see-saw balance
Q5. A see-saw is balanced on its pivot. A child of weight 300 N sits 1.2 m from the pivot. A second child of weight 400 N sits on the other side. How far from the pivot must the second child sit for the see-saw to balance?
  1. 0.9 m
  2. 1.2 m
  3. 0.3 m
  4. 1.6 m
Correct answer: A
By the Principle of Moments, clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment. 300 × 1.2 = 360 N·m, so 400 × d = 360 and d = 0.9 m. The heavier child sits closer to the pivot.
Use after: Model Building phase — Summative problem set, Question 5(a): jerrycan-on-stool clockwise moment
Q6. A 20-litre jerrycan of water weighs 200 N. Its centre is 0.15 m beyond the pivot leg of a three-legged stool, so it tends to turn the stool clockwise about that leg. What is the moment of the jerrycan about the pivot leg?
  1. 1333 N·m
  2. 200.15 N·m
  3. 300 N·m
  4. 30 N·m
Correct answer: D
Moment = force × perpendicular distance = 200 N × 0.15 m = 30 N·m.
Use after: Model Building phase — Summative problem set, Question 5(c): topples or remains stable, justified with the Principle of Moments
Q7. A three-legged stool weighs 30 N, and its centre of gravity is 0.1 m on one side of a pivot leg. A 200 N jerrycan has its centre 0.15 m on the other side of that leg. The jerrycan's moment is 30 N·m. The stool's weight gives a moment of 3 N·m the other way. What happens when the stool is nudged?
  1. It stays stable, because the stool's centre of gravity is close to the leg.
  2. It topples, because the clockwise moment of 30 N·m is greater than the anticlockwise moment of 3 N·m.
  3. It stays stable, because the stool's moment of 3 N·m cancels the jerrycan's moment.
  4. It topples, because 30 N·m minus 3 N·m is 27 N·m, which means the moments are balanced.
Correct answer: B
The Principle of Moments gives balance only when the moments are equal. Here 30 N·m is much greater than 3 N·m, so there is a large unbalanced clockwise moment and the stool topples.
Use after: Model Building phase — Summative problem set, Question 4: double-decker bus design
Q8. A double-decker bus is built with heavy parts such as the engine and chassis in the lower deck and a lighter upper deck. Why does this make the bus less likely to topple when it tilts on a bend?
  1. It lowers the centre of gravity, so the vertical line through it stays inside the base of support for larger tilts.
  2. It removes all turning effects on the bus.
  3. It makes the wheels grip the road better, so the bus can never tilt.
  4. It makes the bus weigh less, so there is less moment to turn it over.
Correct answer: A
Keeping the heavy parts low lowers the centre of gravity. The bus can then tilt further before the vertical line through the centre of gravity leaves the base of support, so it is more stable. Lowering the centre of gravity does not reduce the weight.
