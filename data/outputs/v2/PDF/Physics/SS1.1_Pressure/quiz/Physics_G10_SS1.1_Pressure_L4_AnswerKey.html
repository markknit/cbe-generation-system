Q1. Mama Wanjiru in Kisumu pushes with the same 40 N force on a sharp knife and on a blunt knife. The sharp knife slices a tomato easily, but the blunt knife only squashes it. Why?
  1. The sharp knife has a smaller edge area, so the same force gives a larger pressure
  2. The sharp knife has a larger area, so the pressure is spread out more
  3. The sharp knife gives the tomato a bigger force than the blunt knife
  4. The blunt knife is pushed with a smaller force than the sharp knife
Correct answer: A
Pressure = force / area. The force is the same (40 N), but the sharp edge has a much smaller area, so the pressure on the tomato is larger.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: groups remove all three tapes at once and measure the jet distances
Q2. A tin is filled with water. It has three small holes at 5 cm, 10 cm and 15 cm below the water surface. All the tape is removed at the same time. Which jet of water squirts out the farthest?
  1. The jet from the 15 cm hole, because the water is deepest there
  2. All three jets land the same distance away
  3. The jet from the 10 cm hole, because it is in the middle
  4. The jet from the 5 cm hole, because it is closest to the air
Correct answer: A
The lowest hole (15 cm deep) has the greatest pressure, so its jet pushes out the farthest. The idea that the top hole is farthest because it is near the air is a common misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher introduces the pressure values 500 Pa, 1,000 Pa and 1,500 Pa for the three depths
Q3. For water in a can, the pressure is 500 Pa at a depth of 5 cm and 1,500 Pa at a depth of 15 cm. How many times larger is the pressure at 15 cm than at 5 cm?
  1. 1,000 times
  2. 2 times
  3. 10 times
  4. 3 times
Correct answer: D
Divide the pressures: 1,500 Pa / 500 Pa = 3. The pressure at 15 cm is 3 times the pressure at 5 cm.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: dam diagram comparing 100 kPa at 10 m with 300 kPa at the base
Q4. Water in Masinga Dam is 30 m deep. The water pressure is 100 kPa at a depth of 10 m and 300 kPa at the base. Where does the dam wall need to be strongest?
  1. At the base, where the pressure is largest
  2. It needs the same strength everywhere because the water is the same
  3. At the top, where the pressure is smallest
  4. At a depth of 10 m only
Correct answer: A
The base has the greatest pressure (300 kPa), so the wall must be thickest and strongest there.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph on why pressure increases with depth
Q5. Why is the water pressure greater at a deeper point in a liquid?
  1. There is more water above a deeper point, so more weight presses down there
  2. The holes at the bottom of a container are always bigger
  3. Deeper water is closer to the air, which pushes harder
  4. Deeper water is a different kind of liquid
Correct answer: A
A deeper point has a taller column of water above it. This extra water has more weight, so the pressure there is larger.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: question (b) on why water pushes sideways out of the holes
Q6. Water in a can is pulled down by gravity, yet it squirts sideways out of a hole in the wall of the can. What does this show?
  1. The can must have been tilted so the water could flow sideways
  2. Liquid pressure acts only downwards, so the sideways jet is caused by the tape
  3. Water only pushes sideways when the hole is near the top
  4. Liquid pressure acts on the container walls and pushes outwards through any opening
Correct answer: D
A liquid presses on the walls of its container. When a hole is opened, the water pushes outwards through it, so the jet leaves sideways.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: revised class model with the can of water and the dam wall
Q7. A group draws a model of a dam wall holding back water 30 m deep. Which drawing best shows the idea that liquid pressure increases with depth?
  1. Arrows pointing only downwards, with a wall of the same thickness
  2. Longer arrows near the top, with the wall thicker at the top
  3. Arrows of the same length at every depth, with a wall of the same thickness
  4. Longer arrows near the base, with the wall thicker at the base
Correct answer: D
Pressure is larger at greater depth, so the arrows are longer near the base. The wall is built thicker at the bottom to withstand this.
