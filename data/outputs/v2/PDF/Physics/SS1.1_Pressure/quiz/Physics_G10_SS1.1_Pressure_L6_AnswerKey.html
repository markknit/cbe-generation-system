Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Building the water column (comparing the 1 m² and 2 m² bases)
Q1. A column of water 10 m high stands on a base of 1 m² and gives a pressure of 100,000 Pa at the bottom. A second column of water is also 10 m high but stands on a base of 2 m². It holds twice as much water. What is the pressure at the bottom of the second column?
  1. 200,000 Pa, because there is twice as much water
  2. 400,000 Pa, because both the weight and the area have doubled
  3. 50,000 Pa, because the force is shared between two square metres
  4. 100,000 Pa, the same, because the extra weight is spread over twice the area
Correct answer: D
Weight doubles to 200,000 N but the area also doubles to 2 m², so P = 200,000 / 2 = 100,000 Pa. The area cancels.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Building the water column (steps V, m, W for the 2 m² base)
Q2. A column of water has a base area of 2 m² and a height of 10 m. The density of water is 1,000 kg/m³ and g = 10 N/kg. What is the weight of the water in the column?
  1. 2,000 N
  2. 20,000 N
  3. 100,000 N
  4. 200,000 N
Correct answer: D
V = 2 x 10 = 20 m³, m = 1,000 x 20 = 20,000 kg, W = 20,000 x 10 = 200,000 N.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Building the water column (doing the derivation with letters)
Q3. In the derivation, learners reach W = ρAhg for the weight of a water column with base area A and height h. They then use P = F/A with F = W. What happens next, and what does it show?
  1. A cancels, leaving P = ρgh, so the pressure depends only on density and depth
  2. Nothing cancels, so P = ρA²hg and a wider column gives more pressure
  3. h cancels, leaving P = ρg, so the pressure depends only on density
  4. ρ cancels, leaving P = Ahg, so the pressure depends on the size of the column
Correct answer: A
P = W/A = ρAhg / A = ρgh. The area A cancels, so only ρ, g and h matter.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Using the equation (Lake Victoria at 10 m, 20 m and 40 m)
Q4. Using ρ = 1,000 kg/m³ and g = 10 N/kg, what is the pressure from the water alone at a depth of 40 m in Lake Victoria?
  1. 4 kPa
  2. 4,000 kPa
  3. 40 kPa
  4. 400 kPa
Correct answer: D
P = ρgh = 1,000 x 10 x 40 = 400,000 Pa. Dividing by 1,000 gives 400 kPa.
Use after: Explain phase — Task 2: Explain why the pressure does not depend on the width of Lake Victoria
Q5. Lake A is 5 km wide and Lake B is 50 km wide. Both contain fresh water. At a point 20 m below the surface in each lake, how does the pressure from the water compare?
  1. Greater in Lake B, because it holds more water
  2. Cannot be compared unless the volume of each lake is known
  3. The same in both lakes, because P = ρgh depends only on density and depth
  4. Greater in Lake A, because the water is more squeezed together
Correct answer: C
P = ρgh has no width or total volume in it. Both lakes have the same ρ, g and h, so the pressure is the same.
Use after: Explain phase — Task 4: short practice (cooking oil, ρ = 920 kg/m³, at 10 m depth)
Q6. A farmer in a Kenyan market stores cooking oil with density 920 kg/m³ in a tall tank. Using g = 10 N/kg, what is the pressure from the oil alone at a depth of 10 m?
  1. 920 kPa
  2. 9.2 kPa
  3. 92 kPa
  4. 100 kPa
Correct answer: C
P = ρgh = 920 x 10 x 10 = 92,000 Pa = 92 kPa. The 100 kPa answer comes from using the density of water by mistake.
Use after: Explain phase — Task 1: Explain why the wall of a dam like Masinga is built thicker at the bottom
Q7. The wall of a dam such as Masinga is built thicker at the bottom than at the top. Which explanation is correct?
  1. The water at the bottom has a greater density than the water at the top
  2. The water at the top pushes harder because it is closer to the air
  3. Pressure increases with depth (P = ρgh), so the water pushes harder on the wall at the bottom
  4. The dam holds more water sideways at the bottom, so the pressure is the same everywhere
Correct answer: C
Pressure P = ρgh grows with depth h, as the column of water above is taller and heavier. A thicker base wall can withstand the larger pressure (for example 300 kPa at 30 m compared with 100 kPa at 10 m).
