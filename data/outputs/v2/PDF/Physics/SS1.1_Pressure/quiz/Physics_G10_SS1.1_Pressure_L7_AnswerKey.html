Use after: Predict phase — Predict: cup of water turned upside down over cardboard
Q1. Mama Wanjiru fills a cup to the brim with water, covers it with cardboard, and turns it upside down while holding the cardboard. She then removes her hand. The water stays in the cup and the cardboard does not fall. What holds the cardboard up?
  1. Air outside the cup pushes up on the cardboard
  2. The cardboard is stuck to the water like glue, so nothing pushes on it
  3. The cardboard is too light for gravity to pull it down
  4. A vacuum inside the cup pulls the cardboard upward
Correct answer: A
Air has weight and presses on all surfaces, so the air below pushes up on the cardboard with a large force. A vacuum does not pull, and gravity acts on the cardboard whatever its mass.
Use after: Observe phase — Data card: Mombasa, Kisumu, Nairobi, Mount Kenya altitude and pressure graph
Q2. A data card shows atmospheric pressure at four places: Mombasa 101 kPa, Kisumu 89 kPa, Nairobi 83 kPa, Mount Kenya summit (5,199 m) 54 kPa. Which statement best describes the pattern?
  1. Atmospheric pressure is highest in Nairobi
  2. Atmospheric pressure decreases as altitude increases
  3. Atmospheric pressure increases as altitude increases
  4. Atmospheric pressure is the same everywhere in Kenya
Correct answer: B
Mombasa is at sea level and has the highest pressure (101 kPa). Pressure falls through Kisumu and Nairobi to 54 kPa on Mount Kenya, so pressure decreases with altitude.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2: crushed can (teacher demo)
Q3. A little water is boiled in an empty soda can. The can is quickly turned upside down into a basin of cold water, and it collapses with a bang. Which is the best explanation?
  1. The steam inside the can pushes the walls inwards
  2. The steam cools and the pressure inside drops, so the air outside pushes the can in
  3. The cold water sucks the can inwards
  4. The cold water is heavier than the can and crushes it
Correct answer: B
Steam pushed most of the air out. When it cools and condenses, the pressure inside falls, so the greater atmospheric pressure outside pushes the can inwards. Suction does not pull.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Step 2: calculate the force of air on the glass mouth
Q4. The mouth of a glass has an area of 40 cm² (0.004 m²). Atmospheric pressure at Mombasa is 101,000 Pa. What upward force does the air exert on the mouth of the glass? (Use F = P × A.)
  1. 4,040 N
  2. 25,250,000 N
  3. 404 N
  4. 40.4 N
Correct answer: C
F = P × A = 101,000 Pa × 0.004 m² = 404 N upward.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Step 2: compare air force (404 N) with the weight of the water
Q5. The glass holds 250 g of water. Taking g = 10 N/kg, what is the weight of the water?
  1. 2.5 N
  2. 250 N
  3. 25 N
  4. 0.25 N
Correct answer: A
250 g = 0.25 kg, so W = 0.25 × 10 = 2.5 N. This is far less than the 404 N of air force on the cardboard, so the cardboard stays in place.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Step 3: ears popping from Mombasa to Nairobi
Q6. On a bus trip from Mombasa up to Nairobi, a passenger's ears pop. Which explanation is correct?
  1. The air pressure outside gets higher as the bus climbs and squeezes the ear until the pressures are equal
  2. The air pressure outside gets lower as the bus climbs, so the higher pressure in the middle ear pushes out until the pressures are equal
  3. The air stops pressing on the ear at high altitude, so the ear pops
  4. The air gets heavier as the bus climbs, so it pushes harder on the ear
Correct answer: B
Atmospheric pressure falls with altitude, so the pressure trapped in the middle ear is higher than outside. It pushes out until the pressures equalise, which causes the pop.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Step 3: drinking straw
Q7. A learner drinks uji through a straw. Which statement correctly explains why the liquid rises up the straw?
  1. Sucking lowers the pressure in the straw, so atmospheric pressure on the surface of the liquid pushes it up
  2. Sucking creates a pulling force that lifts the liquid straight up
  3. The liquid is lighter than air, so it floats up the straw
  4. The straw pushes the liquid upward when it is squeezed by the lips
Correct answer: A
Sucking reduces the pressure inside the straw. The atmospheric pressure acting on the liquid in the cup is then greater, so it pushes the liquid up. Suction does not pull.
Use after: DQB phase — Link task: weather, wind and Indian Ocean trade
Q8. Weather reports talk about high and low pressure. If the air pressure is high in one place and low in a nearby place, what happens to the air?
  1. It moves from the region of lower pressure to the region of higher pressure
  2. It moves straight down to the ground in both places
  3. It stays still, because air cannot move sideways
  4. It moves from the region of higher pressure to the region of lower pressure, and we call this wind
Correct answer: D
Differences in air pressure make air flow from higher pressure towards lower pressure. This moving air is wind, which includes the seasonal kaskazi and kusi winds used by dhows along the Kenyan coast.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: add 'Fluids press on surfaces' and the annotation about the two knives
Q9. Mama Wanjiru pushes a sharp knife and a blunt knife with the same force of 40 N. Air presses on both knives equally. What explains why the sharp knife cuts better?
  1. The sharp knife is pushed with a bigger force
  2. The sharp knife has more air pushing on it
  3. The sharp knife has a much smaller area at the edge, so the same force gives a greater pressure
  4. The blunt knife has less atmospheric pressure acting on it
Correct answer: C
The force is the same and air presses equally on both knives, so atmospheric pressure is not the difference. The sharp edge has a smaller area, so P = F/A is greater.
