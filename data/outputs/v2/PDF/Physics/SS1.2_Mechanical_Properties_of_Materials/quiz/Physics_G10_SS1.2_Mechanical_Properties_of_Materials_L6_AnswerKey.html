Q1. A model old rope has spring constant k = 210 N/m and its elastic limit is at an extension of 2.1 m. The largest force it can carry elastically is 441 N. A heavy sack pulls on this rope with a force of 784 N. Which prediction is best supported by this data?
  1. The rope is safe, because its elastic limit extension of 2.1 m is larger than the 0.92 m extension of the new rope.
  2. The rope was pushed beyond its elastic limit, so it will be permanently deformed and may snap.
  3. The rope will stretch more than the new rope, so it must be the stronger rope and will survive.
  4. The rope will return to its original length, because all ropes obey Hooke's Law whatever the load.
Correct answer: B
784 N is about 1.8 times the 441 N the old rope can carry elastically, so the load exceeds its elastic limit and permanent deformation is expected. Stretching more does not mean stronger, and the extensions of two different ropes cannot be compared this way.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Task 2: calculating the extension of rope A under the 784 N load using x = F/k
Q2. A model new rope has spring constant k = 850 N/m. A load of 784 N hangs from it and the rope obeys Hooke's Law. What is its extension?
  1. 0.92 m
  2. 1.08 m
  3. 0.54 m
  4. 666 400 m
Correct answer: A
Using Hooke's Law, x = F/k = 784 ÷ 850 ≈ 0.92 m. Dividing k by F gives 1.08 m, and multiplying F by k is not Hooke's Law.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Task 2: stored energy Ep = ½kx² for rope A
Q3. A model rope has spring constant k = 850 N/m and is stretched by an extension x = 0.92 m. Using Ep = ½kx², how much elastic potential energy is stored in it?
  1. 721 J
  2. 360 J
  3. 784 J
  4. 391 J
Correct answer: B
Ep = ½kx² = ½ × 850 × 0.92² ≈ 360 J. Leaving out the square on x gives 391 J, and using the force value 784 as the energy is a common slip.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Task 1: plotting Ep against x from the Lesson 4 spring data
Q4. A student stretches a spring that obeys Hooke's Law by different extensions x and calculates the elastic potential energy Ep = ½kx² each time. What shape is the graph of Ep (vertical axis) against x (horizontal axis)?
  1. A straight line through the origin, because force is proportional to extension
  2. A curve that gets steeper and steeper (a parabola), because Ep depends on x²
  3. A curve that slopes downwards, because the spring loses energy as it stretches
  4. A horizontal straight line, because the spring constant k stays the same
Correct answer: B
Since Ep = ½kx², Ep increases with the square of x, so the graph is a parabola that gets steeper. Only the force-extension graph (F = kx) is a straight line.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Task 3: comparing 784 N with the 441 N at rope B's elastic limit
Q5. For a rope with k = 210 N/m, Hooke's Law would give an extension of 784 ÷ 210 = 3.73 m under a 784 N load. However, the rope's elastic limit is at an extension of 2.1 m (a force of 441 N). Why is 3.73 m not a trustworthy measurement of the rope's extension?
  1. Hooke's Law does not work for ropes made of natural fibre at any load.
  2. The rope would stretch less than 3.73 m because heavier loads make ropes stiffer.
  3. The calculation is wrong because k should be divided by the force instead.
  4. Hooke's Law only holds up to the elastic limit, and 784 N is beyond it, so force and extension are no longer proportional.
Correct answer: D
Hooke's Law applies only within the elastic limit. Beyond it the rope is permanently deformed, so 3.73 m only shows what a rope that kept obeying Hooke's Law would do.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, Worked Example 1: matatu suspension spring on Thika Road, part (b)
Q6. A matatu suspension spring has k = 12 000 N/m. When four passengers board, the spring is compressed by x = 0.23 m. Using Ep = ½kx², how much elastic potential energy is stored in the spring?
  1. 1380 J
  2. 2744 J
  3. 635 J
  4. 317 J
Correct answer: D
Ep = ½kx² = ½ × 12 000 × 0.23² ≈ 317 J. Forgetting the ½ gives 635 J, and forgetting to square x gives 1380 J.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, Worked Example 2: bungee cord at a Nairobi adventure park on Ngong Road, part (a)
Q7. A person of mass 65 kg jumps from a platform on a bungee cord and falls 20 m to the lowest point, where they are momentarily at rest. Take g = 9.8 N/kg. The gravitational potential energy lost equals the energy now stored in the cord. How much energy is stored in the cord?
  1. 1300 J
  2. 6370 J
  3. 12 740 J
  4. 637 J
Correct answer: C
Energy lost = mgh = 65 × 9.8 × 20 = 12 740 J. The whole 20 m fall counts, not only the 10 m the cord stretches, so using 10 m (6370 J) is wrong.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: board diagram of the force-extension graph (Hooke's Law region, elastic limit, plastic deformation, breaking point)
Q8. On a force-extension graph for a rope, what does the elastic limit mark?
  1. The start of the graph, where the extension is zero
  2. The point where the rope breaks into two pieces
  3. The point where the rope stores no energy
  4. The largest force or extension beyond which the rope is permanently deformed
Correct answer: D
The elastic limit is the 'point of no return': beyond it the material does not return to its original shape. Breaking happens later, at the breaking point.
Use after: End of lesson — Model: Final Explanation letter to the Kericho Farmer (material recommendation and safe working load)
Q9. A farmer wants a rope for a bridge that must safely carry a 784 N load again and again. Rope A has k = 850 N/m with an elastic limit at an extension of 1.5 m. Rope B has k = 210 N/m with an elastic limit at an extension of 2.1 m. Which recommendation is best?
  1. Rope B, because its elastic limit extension of 2.1 m is larger, so it is the stronger rope.
  2. Rope B, because it has the smaller spring constant and so stretches more before snapping.
  3. Rope A, because the largest force it can carry elastically is about 1275 N, which is above 784 N.
  4. Either rope, because both obey Hooke's Law so both can carry any load safely.
Correct answer: C
Rope A's elastic-limit force is F = kx = 850 × 1.5 = 1275 N, above 784 N, so it stays elastic. Rope B's is only 210 × 2.1 = 441 N, so 784 N would deform it permanently.
