Q1. A 30 cm steel ruler is heated from 25 °C to 200 °C. Which prediction best matches what happens and why?
  1. It stays exactly the same length, because steel is too hard to change
  2. It becomes slightly longer, because its particles vibrate more and the average distance between them increases
  3. It becomes shorter, because the particles lose energy and move closer together
  4. It becomes several centimetres longer, because heating makes the particles themselves bigger
Correct answer: B
Solids expand when heated because the particles vibrate with larger amplitude and the average spacing grows. For a 30 cm ruler the change is only a fraction of a millimetre, so the expansion is small and the particles do not change size.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: bar-and-gauge experiment, describing the relationship between ΔT and ΔL in the data table
Q2. In a bar-and-gauge experiment, a metal rod is heated and a dial gauge gives these readings: when the temperature rise ΔT is 10 °C, the length change ΔL is 0.1 mm; at 20 °C it is 0.2 mm; at 30 °C it is 0.3 mm; at 40 °C it is 0.4 mm. Which statement describes the pattern?
  1. ΔL changes randomly with no pattern
  2. ΔL stays the same whatever the temperature rise
  3. ΔL increases in proportion to ΔT: doubling the temperature rise doubles the length change
  4. ΔL decreases as the temperature rise increases, so the rod shortens
Correct answer: C
Each time ΔT doubles, ΔL doubles (0.1 to 0.2 mm, 10 to 20 °C), so ΔL is directly proportional to ΔT. This is the pattern that leads to ΔL = αL₀ΔT.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Part A: deriving ΔL = αL₀ΔT and defining α (units K⁻¹)
Q3. The coefficient of linear expansivity (α) of a material is best described as:
  1. the total length change of any rod of that material, measured in metres
  2. the fractional change in length per unit temperature rise, measured in K⁻¹
  3. the length of a rod at 0 °C, measured in metres
  4. the temperature at which a solid begins to expand, measured in K
Correct answer: B
From ΔL = αL₀ΔT, α = ΔL/(L₀ΔT). It is the change in length per unit original length per degree of temperature rise, so its SI unit is K⁻¹.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Part B: Bridge Girder Problem
Q4. A steel bridge girder is 25 m long at a fitting temperature of 20 °C. In full sun its temperature reaches 65 °C. For steel, α = 11 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹. By how much does the girder expand?
  1. 17.875 mm
  2. 0.012375 mm
  3. 12.375 mm
  4. 123.75 mm
Correct answer: C
ΔT = 65 − 20 = 45 °C. ΔL = αL₀ΔT = 11 × 10⁻⁶ × 25 × 45 = 0.012375 m = 12.375 mm. Using 65 °C as ΔT gives the wrong value of 17.875 mm.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Part C: Kenya Power Problem (homework), part (i)
Q5. A Kenya Power overhead aluminium cable near Kisumu is 200 m long at 15 °C. On a hot afternoon its temperature rises to 70 °C. For aluminium, α = 23 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹. What is the increase in length of the cable?
  1. 0.0253 m
  2. 2.53 m
  3. 0.322 m
  4. 0.253 m
Correct answer: D
ΔT = 70 − 15 = 55 °C. ΔL = αL₀ΔT = 23 × 10⁻⁶ × 200 × 55 = 0.253 m, which is about 25 cm. Using 70 °C as ΔT gives the wrong value of 0.322 m.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Part C: Kenya Power Problem (homework), part (ii) explaining the sag
Q6. Kenya Power overhead cables look more saggy on a hot afternoon than on a cool morning. Which explanation is correct, and why do engineers allow for the sag?
  1. The cable becomes longer when hot but the pylons stay fixed, so the extra length hangs down; engineers allow sag so the cable does not snap or damage the pylons when it contracts in cold weather
  2. The cable gets heavier when hot, so it hangs lower; engineers allow sag to make the cable stronger
  3. The cable contracts when hot and pulls the pylons together; engineers allow sag to save money on cable
  4. The pylons expand much more than the cable, so the cable hangs lower; engineers allow sag so it is easier to repair
Correct answer: A
Heating lengthens the cable but the pylons stay the same distance apart, so the extra length forms a sag. A cable strung tight would be pulled very hard when it contracts in cool weather and could snap or damage the pylons.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: clustering evidence, Theme B 'Expansion forces can be large enough to damage structures'
Q7. Railway engineers leave small gaps between lengths of steel rail. Suppose rails were laid end to end with no gaps on a very hot day in Kisumu. What would most likely happen?
  1. The rails would melt, because they gain energy from the sun
  2. The rails would expand with nowhere to go, pushing on each other with large forces and buckling
  3. The rails would stay exactly the same, because steel is too hard to expand
  4. The rails would shrink and pull apart, because metals contract when hot
Correct answer: B
Steel expands when heated, so rails with no gaps push against each other with very large forces and buckle. This links to the cracked bottle, where expansion also produced forces large enough to damage a hard material.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding Panel 3 (linear expansion of a solid) to the cumulative particle model
Q8. Which statement correctly explains, at particle level, why a metal rod gets longer when heated?
  1. New particles are added to the rod as it heats up
  2. The particles move faster in straight lines through the rod, like gas particles, and push the ends outwards
  3. The particles vibrate with larger amplitude about their positions, so the average distance between them increases
  4. The particles themselves become bigger when heated
Correct answer: C
In a solid the particles stay in a lattice and vibrate. More heat means greater vibration amplitude and a larger average separation, so the rod lengthens. The particles stay the same size and are not moving freely like gas particles.
