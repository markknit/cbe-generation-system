Use after: Observe phase — Station A: Liquid-in-Glass Thermometer
Q1. A liquid-in-glass thermometer has a red alcohol column inside a thin glass tube. Why does the column rise when the bulb is placed in warm water?
  1. The alcohol expands as its temperature rises, so it moves up the narrow tube
  2. The alcohol becomes heavier and sinks into the bulb
  3. The alcohol evaporates and fills the tube with gas
  4. The glass shrinks and squeezes the alcohol upwards
Correct answer: A
The thermometric property of a liquid-in-glass thermometer is the expansion of the liquid. The liquid expands with temperature and can only move up the narrow capillary tube.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: Clinical Thermometer (constriction as a design feature)
Q2. A clinical thermometer has a narrow kink (constriction) in the glass tube just above the bulb. What is the purpose of this constriction?
  1. It allows the thermometer to measure temperatures below 0 °C
  2. It makes the liquid expand more quickly when it is heated
  3. It stops the liquid column from falling back, so the reading stays after the thermometer is removed from the patient
  4. It stops the liquid from expanding past 42 °C
Correct answer: C
The constriction holds the liquid column in place when the bulb cools, so the nurse can read the highest temperature reached.
Use after: Observe phase — Station C: Bimetallic Strip teacher-supervised demo
Q3. A bimetallic strip is made of brass bonded to iron. When it is heated, it bends so that the iron is on the inside of the curve and the brass is on the outside. What does this show?
  1. Brass and iron expand by exactly the same amount
  2. Brass expands more than iron for the same temperature rise
  3. Brass contracts while iron expands
  4. Iron expands more than brass
Correct answer: B
The metal that expands more ends up on the outside of the curve. Brass is on the outside, so it expands more than iron.
Use after: Explain phase — Teacher introduces THERMOMETRIC PROPERTY formally after the gallery debrief
Q4. What is a thermometric property?
  1. The unit printed on the scale of a thermometer
  2. The material that a thermometer is made from
  3. The lowest temperature that a thermometer can read
  4. A physical property that changes continuously and reproducibly with temperature, such as the length of a liquid column
Correct answer: D
A thermometric property is any physical property that varies measurably and predictably with temperature. Examples are liquid expansion and the bending of a bimetallic strip.
Use after: Explain phase — Kenyan weather data card: converting Marsabit 38 °C to Kelvin
Q5. The afternoon temperature in Marsabit peaks at 38 °C. What is this temperature in kelvin, using T(K) = T(°C) + 273?
  1. -235 K
  2. 235 K
  3. 311 K
  4. 38 K
Correct answer: C
T(K) = 38 + 273 = 311 K.
Use after: Explain phase — Kenyan weather data card: converting the freezer -18 °C to Kelvin
Q6. A freezer runs at -18 °C. What is this temperature in kelvin, using T(K) = T(°C) + 273?
  1. -255 K
  2. 291 K
  3. 255 K
  4. 237 K
Correct answer: C
T(K) = -18 + 273 = 255 K. Adding 18 instead of subtracting it gives 291 K, which is a common error.
Use after: Explain phase — Kenyan weather data card: Celsius–Kelvin conversions (Kelvin to Celsius)
Q7. On a hot afternoon in Kisumu, a thermometer calibrated in kelvin reads 300 K. What is this temperature in degrees Celsius, using T(K) = T(°C) + 273?
  1. 33 °C
  2. 573 °C
  3. 27 °C
  4. -27 °C
Correct answer: C
T(°C) = T(K) - 273 = 300 - 273 = 27 °C.
Use after: Explain phase — Q2 of the data card analysis: is 255 K close to absolute zero?
Q8. A learner converts -18 °C to 255 K and says, "255 K is very close to absolute zero because it is a freezing temperature." Which response is correct?
  1. The learner is wrong, because 255 K is still 255 kelvin above absolute zero (0 K), which is a large difference
  2. The learner is right, because absolute zero is -18 °C
  3. The learner is wrong, because absolute zero is 255 K
  4. The learner is right, because any temperature below 0 °C is close to absolute zero
Correct answer: A
Absolute zero is 0 K (about -273 °C). A freezer at 255 K is still far above it, so the particles still have a lot of kinetic energy.
Use after: Explain phase — Q3 of the data card analysis: particle-level meaning of temperature (kinetic energy)
Q9. Which statement correctly describes what temperature tells us at the particle level?
  1. Temperature is the total amount of heat in a body, so a bucket of water at 30 °C has a higher temperature than a cup of water at 30 °C
  2. Temperature is the number of particles in a body
  3. Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles, so particles move faster at 291 K than at 255 K
  4. Temperature is the size of the particles, which shrink as a body cools
Correct answer: C
Temperature relates to the average kinetic energy of the particles. A bucket and a cup at the same temperature have the same average kinetic energy, even though the bucket holds more thermal energy.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative model revision: ΔT = -36 K annotation
Q10. Water at Nairobi temperature (18 °C, which is 291 K) is cooled in a freezer at -18 °C (255 K). By how many kelvin does its temperature fall?
  1. 36 K
  2. 309 K
  3. 546 K
  4. 18 K
Correct answer: A
The temperature drop is 291 - 255 = 36 K. Equivalently, 18 - (-18) = 36 degrees, and a 1 °C change equals a 1 K change.
