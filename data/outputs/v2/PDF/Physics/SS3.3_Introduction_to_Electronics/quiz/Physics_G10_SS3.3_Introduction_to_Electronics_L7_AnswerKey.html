Q1. Mr Otieno, an electrician in Nairobi, holds a copper cable covered in PVC plastic while it carries current at 240 V. Which idea best explains why the PVC cover makes the cable safe to hold?
  1. PVC has a very large energy gap (above about 5 eV), so almost no electrons can move through it
  2. PVC has a small energy gap, so it conducts only a little current
  3. PVC has overlapping energy bands, so its electrons move only in one direction
  4. PVC has no electrons at all, so it has zero conduction
Correct answer: A
Insulators such as PVC, glass and dry wood have a large energy gap (above about 5 eV), so almost no electrons are free to move. It is 'almost none', not zero, conduction.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: class poster of the five-material test (copper bright; glass, dry wood and plastic strip off)
Q2. In the five-material test, a bulb lit brightly with a copper wire in the circuit. Which statement correctly explains why copper conducts so well?
  1. Copper has zero resistance at room temperature because it is a superconductor
  2. Copper has holes that carry current because it is doped with boron
  3. Copper has a large energy gap of about 1.1 eV, so electrons are easily pushed across it
  4. Copper has overlapping energy bands (0 eV gap), so its outer electrons are free to move through the metal
Correct answer: D
In metals like copper the bands overlap (0 eV gap), so outer electrons are free to move. Copper still has a small resistance (1.7 x 10^-8 ohm metre) and is not a superconductor at room temperature.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Group A (Garissa solar plant, 50 MW) case card on silicon p-n junctions
Q3. At the Garissa solar plant, the silicon in the panels has a p-type layer and an n-type layer. Which statement about the p-type layer is correct?
  1. It is pure silicon with a 0 eV energy gap
  2. It is silicon doped with phosphorus, which adds extra free electrons
  3. It is silicon that has been given a positive charge overall
  4. It is silicon doped with boron, which creates holes that act as charge carriers
Correct answer: D
p-type silicon is doped with boron, which leaves holes as the majority carriers. Phosphorus doping gives n-type silicon with extra free electrons, and doped silicon stays electrically neutral.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Group B (microchips in phones, M-Pesa and computers) case card, one-way behaviour like the 1N4007 diode
Q4. A 1N4007 diode is connected in a simple circuit with a bulb. The bulb lights when the diode is connected one way round and stays off when it is reversed. What property of the diode does this show?
  1. The diode conducts only when it is cooled below its critical temperature
  2. The diode has zero resistance in both directions
  3. The diode is an insulator that breaks down when turned round
  4. The p-n junction conducts in one direction only
Correct answer: D
A diode is made by joining p-type and n-type silicon, and the p-n junction conducts one way only. This one-way action is the basis of solar cells and microchips.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Group C (Kenyatta National Hospital MRI, Dr Wekesa) case card on NbTi magnets and Tc
Q5. At Kenyatta National Hospital, the NbTi magnet in an MRI machine is cooled by liquid helium at 4.2 K. NbTi has a critical temperature (Tc) of 9.2 K. Why is this cooling needed?
  1. Cooling below Tc gives the NbTi zero resistance, so large current flows without energy lost as heat
  2. Cooling raises the energy gap so the magnet becomes an insulator
  3. Cooling makes NbTi work as a diode so the current flows one way
  4. Cooling adds holes to the NbTi, making it p-type
Correct answer: A
A superconductor has zero resistance only below its critical temperature. At 4.2 K, which is below 9.2 K, NbTi carries a large current to make a strong field (about 1.5 T to 3 T) without losing energy as heat.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'Why does it work?' concept chain and the question on silicon being about 10^11 times more resistive than copper
Q6. Silicon is about 10^11 times more resistive than copper, yet it is used in solar panels at Garissa and in phone chips. Which statement best explains why it is so useful?
  1. Silicon is a conductor with overlapping bands, so its resistance does not change
  2. Silicon has zero resistance at room temperature when it is doped
  3. Silicon has a small energy gap (1.1 eV), and doping with phosphorus or boron raises its conductivity in a controlled way
  4. Silicon has an energy gap above 5 eV, so it conducts better than copper once doped
Correct answer: C
Silicon's small gap (1.1 eV) means it conducts a little, and doping greatly raises conductivity in a controlled way. Joining p and n types gives diodes and transistors.

Q7. A learner says: 'Silicon doped with phosphorus is negatively charged because it has extra free electrons.' Which response is correct?
  1. Incorrect, because phosphorus atoms also add extra positive charge in their nuclei, so the doped silicon stays electrically neutral overall
  2. Correct, but only when it is joined to p-type silicon
  3. Incorrect, because phosphorus doping removes electrons and creates holes
  4. Correct, because each extra electron gives the whole piece a negative charge
Correct answer: A
Each phosphorus atom brings an extra electron and an extra proton, so n-type silicon has more free electrons as carriers but remains electrically neutral overall.
