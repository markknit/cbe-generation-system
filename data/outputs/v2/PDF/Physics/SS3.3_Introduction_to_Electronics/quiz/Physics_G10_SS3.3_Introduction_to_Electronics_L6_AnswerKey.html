Q1. In the class model of electrons moving through copper wire, what causes copper to have a small resistance at room temperature?
  1. The plastic covering stops the electrons from entering the copper
  2. Electrons are held tightly in the atoms and cannot move at all
  3. Copper atoms have no electrons, so current passes through the gaps
  4. Electrons collide with the vibrating copper atoms and some energy becomes heat
Correct answer: D
In the Lesson 1 model, moving electrons collide with atoms, which gives resistance and turns some electrical energy into heat. Copper has free electrons, so its resistance is small but not zero.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: data card activity where groups convert temperatures using K = C + 273
Q2. Liquid nitrogen boils at -196 C. Using K = C + 273, what is this temperature in kelvin?
  1. 77 K
  2. 196 K
  3. 469 K
  4. -77 K
Correct answer: A
K = C + 273 = -196 + 273 = 77 K. Adding 196 instead of subtracting it gives the wrong value of 469 K.

Q3. A YBCO disc has Tc of about 92 K and an NbTi coil has Tc of 9.2 K. Liquid nitrogen is at 77 K and liquid helium is at 4.2 K. Which statement is correct?
  1. Liquid helium is too cold for NbTi, so NbTi needs liquid nitrogen
  2. Liquid nitrogen cools NbTi below its Tc because 77 K is a very low temperature
  3. Liquid nitrogen is not cold enough for NbTi, but it cools YBCO below its Tc
  4. Liquid nitrogen cannot make YBCO superconducting because 77 K is above 9.2 K
Correct answer: C
77 K is below the Tc of YBCO (about 92 K), so YBCO becomes superconducting. 77 K is above the Tc of NbTi (9.2 K), so NbTi needs liquid helium at 4.2 K.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: building the CER statement 'A superconductor has zero resistance below Tc'
Q4. A YBCO disc is cooled in liquid nitrogen at 77 K, below its Tc, and a small magnet floats above it. What does this show about the disc?
  1. The disc has become a superconductor with zero resistance
  2. The disc has become an insulator
  3. The disc has a larger resistance than copper
  4. The disc has melted and become a liquid
Correct answer: A
Below its critical temperature a superconductor has zero resistance and shows the Meissner effect, so a magnet levitates. It is not an insulator.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: answering 'How does Dr Wekesa's MRI machine use NbTi?'
Q5. Dr Wekesa's MRI machine uses NbTi coils with Tc of 9.2 K. Why must the coils be kept at 4.2 K with liquid helium?
  1. Liquid helium makes the current bigger by pushing electrons through the coils
  2. The coils must be above 9.2 K so that the resistance is zero
  3. The coils must stay below 9.2 K to keep zero resistance, so a large current flows without energy loss
  4. Liquid helium is needed to turn the NbTi into copper
Correct answer: C
NbTi has zero resistance only below its Tc of 9.2 K. At 4.2 K the coils carry a large current without losing energy as heat and give a field of about 1.5 T to 3 T.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: discussing why household wiring still uses copper and PVC
Q6. Why does Mr Otieno still wire houses in Nairobi with copper and PVC instead of superconductors?
  1. Room temperature is far above the Tc of the superconductors listed (4.2 K, 9.2 K and about 92 K), so they would need cooling
  2. Copper has zero resistance at room temperature, which is better than any superconductor
  3. Superconductors only work with a magnet above them
  4. Superconductors are insulators, so current cannot pass through them
Correct answer: A
Room temperature (about 293 K) is much higher than every Tc in the lesson, so these materials would not be superconducting without costly cooling. Superconductors are not insulators.

Q7. During an MRI scan the NbTi coil at 4.2 K warms up to 12 K. Its Tc is 9.2 K. What happens?
  1. The resistance becomes smaller, so the magnetic field becomes stronger
  2. The resistance stays zero because the coil is still very cold
  3. The coil becomes an insulator and the current stops completely
  4. The resistance returns, so the magnet stops working properly and energy is lost as heat
Correct answer: D
12 K is above the Tc of 9.2 K, so the NbTi is no longer a superconductor. Its resistance returns, so the large current cannot flow without loss and the magnet stops working.
