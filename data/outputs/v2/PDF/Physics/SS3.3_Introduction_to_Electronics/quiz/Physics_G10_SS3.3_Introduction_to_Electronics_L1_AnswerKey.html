Use after: Observe phase — Observe: build the test circuit and close the gap with a clip-to-clip touch
Q1. Grade 10 learners build a test circuit with two 1.5 V dry cells, a 3 V torch bulb and crocodile clips. They touch the clips together first, and the bulb lights. Why do they do this check before testing any material?
  1. To warm the bulb so it lights faster
  2. To be sure the cells, bulb and wires work, so that an off bulb later is due to the material
  3. To clean the clips so the materials conduct better
  4. To make the cells stronger before the test
Correct answer: B
Touching the clips together shows the circuit itself works. If the bulb later stays off, learners can say the tested material is the reason and not a flat cell or a loose wire.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: testing the materials in the fixed order and recording results in the table
Q2. In the 3 V test circuit, a group tests copper wire, glass, dry wood and a plastic strip one after another. Which result did the groups record?
  1. Copper and dry wood lit the bulb, glass and plastic did not
  2. All four materials lit the bulb, but copper was the brightest
  3. Glass and plastic lit the bulb dimly, copper left it off
  4. Copper lit the bulb brightly, while glass, dry wood and plastic left it off
Correct answer: D
Only copper lit the bulb (brightly). Glass, dry wood and plastic left it off, so 1 of the 4 ordinary materials let current flow.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: testing the silicon diode forward-connected, then reversed
Q3. A silicon diode (1N4007) is connected in the 3 V test circuit with the 3 V bulb. What do learners observe when they connect it one way and then reverse it?
  1. The bulb stays off when connected forward and lights when reversed
  2. The bulb lights when the diode is connected forward and stays off when it is reversed
  3. The bulb stays off both ways, as it does with plastic
  4. The bulb lights both ways, as it does with copper
Correct answer: B
The diode lit the bulb when forward-connected and left it off when reversed. It behaved differently from all four ordinary materials.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: linking the results back to Mr Otieno's insulated wire
Q4. Mr Otieno, an electrician in Nairobi, holds a copper cable covered in PVC plastic while it carries current. Using only the results of today's bulb test, what can learners say about why this is safe?
  1. In the test, plastic left the bulb off while copper lit it, so the plastic cover does not let current flow easily
  2. Copper only carries current when nobody is holding the cable
  3. The plastic cover has no electrons inside it
  4. In the test, plastic lit the bulb better than copper did
Correct answer: A
The evidence is that copper lit the bulb and plastic did not. This supports a claim that the cover does not let current pass easily. The test does not show why, and that comes in later lessons.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: naming the puzzle (all materials are made of atoms with electrons, yet behave so differently)
Q5. Copper, glass, dry wood, plastic and silicon are all made of atoms that have electrons. Yet copper lights the bulb and plastic does not. At this point in the unit, which statement is correct?
  1. The difference is because plastic and wood have no electrons
  2. The difference is because copper atoms are bigger than plastic atoms
  3. The difference is because wood is natural, so it should conduct
  4. This has not been explained yet, so it is a puzzle we will investigate in the coming lessons
Correct answer: D
The lesson names a puzzle: the same building blocks give very different behaviour. The explanation is not given in this lesson, and learners should not claim one without evidence.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: writing questions on sticky notes and posting them under the Driving Question
Q6. A class is building a Driving Question Board about why some materials conduct and others do not. Which note is the best question to post, because it comes straight from today's test results?
  1. What is the price of copper wire in Nairobi?
  2. Who sold the dry cells to the school?
  3. What colour is the plastic on the cable?
  4. Why does the diode light the bulb in only one direction?
Correct answer: D
A good DQB question starts with why or how and comes from the evidence. The diode lighting the bulb one way only is an observation that learners cannot yet explain.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: drawing a first model of electrons in copper, plastic and the diode
Q7. Wanjiku draws her first model of copper and plastic using only today's evidence. Which model idea fits what the lesson asks learners to do?
  1. Drawing the model only after the teacher gives the correct answer
  2. Drawing electrons only in copper, because plastic has none
  3. Leaving out the diode, because models must explain everything
  4. Drawing atoms and electrons in both copper and plastic, showing where electrons move when the cells are connected, and noting what the model cannot explain yet
Correct answer: D
The first class model shows atoms and electrons in each material and links electron movement to the bulb lighting. It also records what it cannot explain yet, such as the diode, so it can be revised in later lessons.
