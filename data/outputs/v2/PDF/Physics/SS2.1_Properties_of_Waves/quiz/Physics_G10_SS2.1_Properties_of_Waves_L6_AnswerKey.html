Q1. A thin ray of light in air hits the surface of water at 45 degrees from the normal. What happens to the ray as it enters the water?
  1. It bends away from the normal, so the angle in water is more than 45 degrees
  2. It bends towards the surface of the water, so the angle from the normal stays at 45 degrees
  3. It bends towards the normal, so the angle in water is less than 45 degrees
  4. It goes straight on without bending
Correct answer: C
Water has a higher refractive index than air (1.33 against 1.00), so light slows down and bends towards the normal. Learners measured about 32 degrees from the normal in water.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 1: Refraction (calculate with Snell's law and compare with measured r)
Q2. A ray in air (n = 1.00) hits water (n = 1.33) at i = 45 degrees. Use n1 sin i = n2 sin r with sin 45 = 0.707. What is the value of sin r?
  1. 0.941
  2. 0.532
  3. 0.707
  4. 0.471
Correct answer: B
1.00 x 0.707 = 1.33 x sin r, so sin r = 0.707 / 1.33 = 0.532, which gives r = 32 degrees. Multiplying by 1.33 instead of dividing gives 0.941, and dividing by 1.50 gives 0.471, which is the glass value.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: practice problem 2 (find the speed of light in glass)
Q3. The speed of light in air is 3.0 x 10^8 m/s. Glass has a refractive index of 1.50. Using n = c/v, what is the speed of light in glass?
  1. 2.0 x 10^8 m/s
  2. 2.26 x 10^8 m/s
  3. 3.0 x 10^8 m/s
  4. 4.5 x 10^8 m/s
Correct answer: A
v = c/n = 3.0 x 10^8 / 1.50 = 2.0 x 10^8 m/s. Light is slower in glass than in air, so the answer cannot be bigger than 3.0 x 10^8 m/s.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: building the explanation using speeds (n = c/v)
Q4. Kiprono says glass bends light more than water because glass is harder. Which is the correct scientific reason that light bends more in glass than in water?
  1. Glass reflects most of the light, so the rest is bent more
  2. Glass is solid, and solids always bend light more than liquids
  3. Light travels faster in glass than in water, so it is pulled more towards the normal
  4. Light travels more slowly in glass (n = 1.50) than in water (n = 1.33), so it bends more towards the normal
Correct answer: D
A higher refractive index means slower light, and a bigger drop in speed gives more bending towards the normal. Hardness is not the cause of the bending.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2: Lenses (converging and diverging lens ray diagrams)
Q5. Parallel rays of light from a torch pass through a converging (convex) lens and then through a diverging (concave) lens. Which statement describes what each lens does to the rays?
  1. Both lenses bring the rays together at a focal point
  2. Both lenses spread the rays out, but by different amounts
  3. The converging lens spreads the rays out, and the diverging lens brings them to a focal point
  4. The converging lens brings the rays together at a focal point, and the diverging lens spreads them as if they came from a focal point behind the lens
Correct answer: D
A converging lens makes parallel rays meet at a focal point. A diverging lens spreads them so they appear to come from a focal point behind the lens.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: connecting lenses to uses (spectacles, short and long sight)
Q6. A learner in a Kisumu classroom can read a book clearly but cannot read the board from the back of the class. This is short sight. Which lens would help, and why?
  1. A converging lens, because it spreads the rays slightly
  2. A diverging lens, because it spreads the rays slightly so that the image falls on the retina
  3. No lens can help, because the problem is in the board and not in the eye
  4. A converging lens, because it brings distant rays together more strongly
Correct answer: B
Short sight is corrected with a diverging lens, which spreads the rays a little so that the image forms on the retina. Converging lenses are used to correct long sight.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the class echo model (key box: reflection returns the wave, refraction bends the wave that enters)
Q7. At Hell's Gate, learners shout at a cliff and hear their voices return after 0.50 s. Which statement correctly explains the returning shout?
  1. The sound wave is refracted by the cliff, and the bending sends it back to the learners
  2. The sound wave is reflected from the cliff, and reflection is what sends the wave back; refraction describes only the part that enters the cliff material
  3. Refraction and reflection are the same process, so either word can explain the echo
  4. The sound wave enters the cliff and Snell's law makes it come back out towards the learners
Correct answer: B
An echo is a reflected wave that returns to the listener. Refraction describes the part of a wave that goes into a new medium and bends there. It does not by itself explain the echo.
