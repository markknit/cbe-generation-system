Q1. A ray of light hits a plane mirror. The angle between the incoming ray and the normal is 40°. What angle will the reflected ray make with the normal?
  1. 40°
  2. 20°
  3. 80°
  4. 50°
Correct answer: A
By the first law of reflection, the angle of reflection r equals the angle of incidence i, and both are measured from the normal. So r = 40°.
Use after: Observe phase — Station A: plane mirror (measuring i and r from the normal)
Q2. In a mirror experiment, Kiprono measures the angle between the incoming ray and the mirror surface and gets 50°. What is the angle of incidence i?
  1. 140°
  2. 90°
  3. 50°
  4. 40°
Correct answer: D
The angle of incidence is measured from the normal, which is at 90° to the mirror. So i = 90° - 50° = 40°.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: ripples (ripple tank or PhET simulation)
Q3. Straight ripples in a ripple tank travel towards a straight barrier that is tilted. What do the learners see after the ripples reach the barrier?
  1. The ripples bounce off at an angle equal to the angle at which they arrived, and stay straight
  2. The ripples bend into circles and move in all directions
  3. The ripples stop at the barrier and disappear
  4. The ripples always return along exactly the same path, whatever the tilt
Correct answer: A
Water waves reflect from a barrier obeying the same law as light: the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence. The ripples change direction but keep their straight pattern.
Use after: Observe phase — Station A: plane mirror (checking the paper-plane and lifting the torch)
Q4. At a mirror, learners lay the incident ray, reflected ray and normal flat on paper. When they lift the torch slightly, the ray no longer stays on the paper. Which law of reflection does this check?
  1. The reflected ray is always on the same side of the normal as the incident ray
  2. The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal lie in the same plane
  3. The reflected ray is always shorter than the incident ray
  4. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection
Correct answer: B
When the ray is level with the paper, all three lines lie on one flat surface. This is the second law: the incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: applying the laws to Kiprono's sound ray at the Hell's Gate cliff (surface-angle probe)
Q5. A sound ray from Kiprono hits the Hell's Gate cliff. The ray makes an angle of 30° with the surface of the cliff. What is the angle of reflection r, measured from the normal?
  1. 30°
  2. 60°
  3. 120°
  4. 90°
Correct answer: B
The angle of incidence is measured from the normal, which is at 90° to the cliff. So i = 90° - 30° = 60°, and r = i = 60°.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'Does the sound need to come back along the same line it went?'
Q6. Wanjiru shouts and part of her sound ray hits the cliff exactly along the normal (i = 0°). What happens to that ray after reflection?
  1. It continues straight through the cliff
  2. It goes off at 90° to the cliff surface
  3. It is absorbed by the cliff and a new sound is made
  4. It returns along the same line it came from
Correct answer: D
If i = 0°, then r = 0° as well. The reflected ray leaves along the normal, so it goes back along its own path. Only a ray hitting along the normal returns along the same line.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding a reflection component to the class echo model (Hell's Gate scene)
Q7. Amina draws a sound ray hitting a cliff at a point, with a dashed normal at that point. The incident ray makes an angle of 35° with the normal. Which drawing of the reflected ray is correct?
  1. On the same side of the normal as the incident ray, at 35° to the normal
  2. Along the cliff surface, with no angle to the normal
  3. On the opposite side of the normal, at 35° to the normal, going back towards the listener
  4. On the opposite side of the normal, at 55° to the normal
Correct answer: C
The reflected ray lies on the opposite side of the normal from the incident ray, with r = i = 35°. It heads back towards the listener.
