Q1. In a ripple tank, two dippers touch the water at the same rate. Along some lines the water surface stays nearly flat, even though both dippers are still moving. What is happening along those lines?
  1. The waves from the two dippers always add to make bigger waves, so the flat water is a mistake
  2. The dippers are not sending any waves in those directions
  3. The waves from the two dippers bounce off each other and stop
  4. A crest from one dipper meets a trough from the other, so their displacements add up to almost zero
Correct answer: D
When waves meet, their displacements add (superposition). Where a crest meets a trough they cancel, which is destructive interference, so the water stays nearly flat.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Station 2: PhET Sound simulation with two speakers set to 340 Hz (confirming lambda = 340/340 = 1.0 m)
Q2. Two speakers play the same note of frequency 340 Hz. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s. What is the wavelength of the sound?
  1. 340 m
  2. 0.5 m
  3. 1.0 m
  4. 2.0 m
Correct answer: C
Use v = f x wavelength, so wavelength = v / f = 340 / 340 = 1.0 m.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: worked example (speakers at 340 Hz, listener 5.0 m and 5.5 m away)
Q3. Two speakers play a 340 Hz note, so the wavelength is 1.0 m. A learner stands 5.0 m from one speaker and 5.5 m from the other. What is the path difference, and what does the learner hear?
  1. 10.5 m, which is a whole number of wavelengths, so the sound is loud
  2. 5.5 m, which is more than one wavelength, so the sound is loud
  3. 0.5 m, which is half a wavelength, so the sound is quiet
  4. 0.5 m, which is half a wavelength, so the sound is loud
Correct answer: C
Path difference = 5.5 - 5.0 = 0.5 m, which is half a wavelength (0.5/1.0). Crest meets trough, so destructive interference gives a quiet spot.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: deciding what would happen at path difference 2.0 m (loud)
Q4. Two speakers play a 340 Hz note, so the wavelength is 1.0 m. A listener stands where the path difference from the two speakers is 2.0 m. What does the listener hear, and why?
  1. Quiet, because a larger path difference always makes the sound weaker
  2. Loud, because 2.0 m is half a wavelength
  3. Quiet, because the waves from the two speakers cancel whenever the path difference is more than 1.0 m
  4. Loud, because 2.0 m is two whole wavelengths, so crest meets crest
Correct answer: D
A path difference of 2.0 m is 2.0 / 1.0 = 2 whole wavelengths. The waves are in step, so constructive interference gives a loud sound.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: whole-class sensemaking (the rule in learners' own words) and Observe, Station 3: Kiprono's challenge (loud and quiet spots)
Q5. A learner walking across a room between two speakers playing the same note finds a quiet spot. Both speakers are still switched on. Which statement best explains the quiet spot?
  1. The waves from both speakers still arrive, but a crest from one meets a trough from the other and they cancel at that point
  2. The air at that spot blocks the sound from both speakers
  3. One of the speakers has stopped working
  4. The sound energy is destroyed completely and cannot be found anywhere in the room
Correct answer: A
Both waves still reach the spot, but their displacements cancel when they are half a wavelength out of step. The energy is not destroyed; it is redistributed to the loud places.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning, applying interference to the Kisumu hall (site C)
Q6. In the Kisumu school hall, the far wall is 12 m away and the echo delay is 0.07 s, which is less than 0.1 s. Which statement about the sound in the hall is correct?
  1. All the reflected waves cancel each other everywhere, so the hall should be silent
  2. The waves collide and stop, so the sound cannot reach the seats at the back
  3. The hall has no reflections, so the sound is loud only because of the speaker
  4. Reflected waves from the walls, floor and roof overlap with the original sound and add in some places and partly cancel in others
Correct answer: D
The echo arrives less than 0.1 s after the original, so reflected and direct waves overlap. They add in some places (loud) and partly cancel in others (quiet), which makes the sound loud and muddy.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: noise-cancelling headphones (bus engine rumble from Kisumu to Nairobi)
Q7. A noise-cancelling headphone must cancel a steady 340 Hz engine noise. The speed of sound is 340 m/s, so the wavelength of the noise is 1.0 m. How far out of step must the wave made by the headphone be?
  1. 1.0 m, which is one whole wavelength out of step
  2. 0 m, so the wave is exactly in step with the noise
  3. 0.5 m, which is half a wavelength out of step
  4. 340 m, which is the speed of sound
Correct answer: C
For destructive interference, the second wave must be half a wavelength out of step: 1.0 / 2 = 0.5 m. Then crest meets trough at the ear and the noise cancels.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: Hell's Gate panel (85 m, 0.50 s, single clean reflection) beside the Kisumu hall diagram
Q8. At Hell's Gate, a cliff 85 m away gives a clean echo after 0.50 s. In the Kisumu hall the sound is muddy. Which is the best reason for the difference?
  1. Sound travels faster in the open air, so no waves can meet
  2. At Hell's Gate there is a single reflection arriving after a clear delay, so there is little overlap, while in the hall many reflections overlap
  3. At Hell's Gate the reflected waves always cancel the original sound
  4. At Hell's Gate the cliff does not reflect sound
Correct answer: B
The cliff gives one clean reflection after 0.50 s, so it hardly overlaps the original shout. In the hall, many reflections arrive close together and overlap, adding and cancelling at different places.
