Q1. The class safe limit is 85 dB for up to 8 hours. Which of these sources is ABOVE the safe limit?
  1. Matatu with loud music (95 dB)
  2. Kisumu hall during assembly (85 dB)
  3. Ordinary conversation (60 dB)
  4. Whisper (30 dB)
Correct answer: A
95 dB is greater than 85 dB, so the matatu with loud music is above the limit. The Kisumu hall at 85 dB is exactly at the limit, and the other two are well below it.
Use after: Observe phase — Part 3 (calculation link): site C round trip 24 m, t = 2d/v
Q2. In the Kisumu hall, the far wall is 12 m away and the speed of sound is 340 m/s. Which statement gives the correct delay and what it means?
  1. 0.04 s, because the sound travels 12 m, so it is heard as a clear separate echo
  2. 28 s, because the sound is delayed a long time before it returns
  3. 0.07 s, which is less than 0.1 s, so the reflected sound merges with the original voice
  4. 0.07 s, which is more than 0.1 s, so it is heard as a clear separate echo
Correct answer: C
The sound travels there and back, so t = 2d/v = 24/340 = 0.07 s. This is less than 0.1 s, so the ear cannot separate the reflection from the original sound, and repeated reflections make it muddy (reverberation).
Use after: Explain phase — Design a noise-reduction treatment for the Kisumu hall
Q3. A school hall has hard painted concrete walls that reflect 85% of sound. Curtains reflect only 15% and foam only 10%. Why does hanging curtains and putting foam on the walls make the muddy sound clearer?
  1. They stop the speaker from producing sound, so the hall becomes silent
  2. They reflect more sound back to the speaker, so the voice is stronger
  3. They make sound travel faster, so the 0.07 s delay disappears
  4. They absorb much of the sound energy, so there are fewer repeated reflections and the reverberation is shorter
Correct answer: D
Soft materials absorb sound energy instead of reflecting it. With less energy reflected back and forth, the overlapping reflections die away quickly and the sound is clearer. The delay itself and the source do not change.
Use after: DQB phase — Final DQB review: 'Why is there a delay?' answered by t = 2d/v
Q4. At Hell's Gate, a learner shouts at a cliff and hears the echo 0.50 s later. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. How far away is the cliff?
  1. 680 m
  2. 170 m
  3. 85 m
  4. 17 m
Correct answer: C
The sound travels to the cliff and back, so d = vt/2 = 340 × 0.50 / 2 = 85 m. Using 170 m forgets that this is a round trip.
Use after: Model Building phase — Step 2: final model, site D Nairobi open grounds
Q5. On open school grounds, the nearest large hard wall is 300 m away, so the reflected sound returns after about 1.76 s. Yet the learner hears no echo. What is the best explanation?
  1. Sound travels more slowly over open ground, so it never returns
  2. The delay is too long for the human ear to detect any sound
  3. The sound spreads out over a large area and the grass and bushes absorb it, so the returning sound is too faint
  4. The delay is shorter than 0.1 s, so the echo merges with the shout
Correct answer: C
A longer delay does not mean a stronger echo. Strength depends on how much energy is reflected. Spreading and absorption by grass (about 10% reflected) leave the returning sound too faint to hear.
Use after: Model Building phase — Step 2: final model showing angle of incidence i equal to angle of reflection r from the normal
Q6. A sound ray hits a hard, smooth wall so that the angle between the ray and the normal is 30°. What is the angle between the reflected ray and the normal?
  1. 90°
  2. 30°
  3. 0°
  4. 60°
Correct answer: B
The law of reflection says the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal. So the reflected angle is also 30°.
Use after: End of lesson — Step 5: individual Final Explanation using claim, evidence and reasoning
Q7. Which statement best completes the Final Explanation of why some places give an echo and others do not?
  1. Soft surfaces such as foam and grass reflect sound best, so they give the clearest echoes
  2. An echo is heard only when the reflector is at least 17 m away and reflects enough sound energy
  3. The longer the delay, the stronger the echo, so far walls always give the loudest echo
  4. An echo is heard whenever sound meets any surface, whatever the distance
Correct answer: B
A distinct echo needs a delay of at least 0.1 s, which means a reflector at least 17 m away. The surface must also reflect enough energy, as hard smooth surfaces do. Soft surfaces absorb sound.
