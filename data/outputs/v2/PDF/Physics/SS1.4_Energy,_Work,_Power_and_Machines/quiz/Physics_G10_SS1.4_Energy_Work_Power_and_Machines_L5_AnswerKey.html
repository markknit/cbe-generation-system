Use after: Predict phase — Predict: writing 'What do you think we mean by the word work in physics?'
Q1. In physics, when is work done on an object?
  1. When a force acts on the object for a long time
  2. When a person feels tired from pushing or holding the object
  3. When a force moves the object in the direction of the force
  4. When a person is busy doing a job such as farming
Correct answer: C
Work is done only when a force moves an object in the direction of the force (W = Fd). Tiredness or being busy is the everyday meaning of 'work', not the physics meaning.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: 'Mama Kavata holds the bucket still for 10 seconds with her arms shaking. Does she do work on the bucket?'
Q2. Mama Kavata holds a 20 kg bucket still for 10 seconds. Her arms shake from the effort. How much work does she do on the bucket in the physics sense during this time?
  1. No work, because the bucket does not move
  2. Negative work, because the bucket is not rising
  3. Some work, because her arms are tired and shaking
  4. Some work, because she is pulling upwards with 200 N
Correct answer: A
Work needs movement in the direction of the force. The bucket moves 0 m, so W = 200 N x 0 m = 0 J, even though her muscles are tired.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Mini-Well lifting investigation (2 kg load raised 1 m)
Q3. In the Mini-Well investigation, a learner lifts a 2 kg load (weight 20 N) slowly and steadily 1 m from the floor onto a bench. How much work does the learner do on the load?
  1. 40 J
  2. 2 J
  3. 21 J
  4. 20 J
Correct answer: D
The lifting force equals the weight, 20 N, and the distance moved upwards is 1 m. W = Fd = 20 N x 1 m = 20 J.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: carrying the load 2 m horizontally at a steady height
Q4. A learner lifts a 2 kg load onto a bench, then carries it 2 m sideways at a steady height. During the sideways carry, the learner's lifting force is upwards but the motion is horizontal. How much work does the lifting force do on the load during the sideways carry?
  1. 40 J, because 20 N x 2 m = 40 J
  2. Negative work, because the force is opposite to the motion
  3. 0 J, because the force is at right angles to the motion
  4. 20 J, because the load is still being held up
Correct answer: C
The upward lifting force is at right angles to the horizontal motion, so there is no distance moved in the direction of the force. The work done by the lifting force is 0 J.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: bucket data table, computing F x d for 2 m, 4 m, 6 m and 8 m and comparing with the PE column
Q5. Mama Kavata lifts a bucket of weight 200 N steadily up through 4 m of the well. Using W = Fd, how much work does she do on the bucket over these 4 m?
  1. 204 J
  2. 800 J
  3. 50 J
  4. 1600 J
Correct answer: B
W = Fd = 200 N x 4 m = 800 J. This matches the gain in potential energy of the bucket at 4 m.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: sign table (gravity 200 N down while the bucket goes up)
Q6. Mama Kavata lifts a 20 kg bucket (weight 200 N) steadily up an 8 m deep well. What is the work done by gravity on the bucket during the lift?
  1. -200 J, because the weight is 200 N
  2. 0 J, because the bucket moves at steady speed
  3. +1600 J, because gravity acts on the bucket the whole time
  4. -1600 J, because gravity's force is opposite to the motion
Correct answer: D
Gravity pulls down with 200 N while the bucket moves up 8 m, so the force is opposite to motion. W = -200 N x 8 m = -1600 J.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: sign table, net work = +1600 + (-1600) = 0 J
Q7. During the steady 8 m lift of a 200 N bucket, the woman does +1600 J of work and gravity does -1600 J of work. What is the net work on the bucket, and what does it tell us?
  1. 3200 J, so the bucket's speed keeps increasing
  2. -1600 J, so the bucket loses energy
  3. 0 J, so the bucket's kinetic energy does not change and its speed stays constant
  4. 0 J, so nothing happened to the bucket
Correct answer: C
Net work = +1600 + (-1600) = 0 J. With no net work, KE and speed stay constant, even though the bucket's PE rises by 1600 J.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: lowering the bucket slowly 8 m by rope (signs reversed)
Q8. Mama Kavata now lowers the 200 N bucket slowly down 8 m using a rope. Her pull on the rope is upwards. What is the work done by the woman?
  1. -1600 J, because her force is up while the motion is down
  2. +1600 J, because she is still pulling with 200 N
  3. -200 J, because the force is 200 N
  4. 0 J, because the bucket is not being lifted
Correct answer: A
Her force is upwards and the bucket moves downwards, so force and motion are opposite. W = -200 N x 8 m = -1600 J. Negative work takes energy away from the bucket.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: quick problem on the farmer pushing a wheelbarrow
Q9. A farmer in the Rift Valley pushes a wheelbarrow with a steady force of 60 N along a straight path through 10 m, in the direction of the push. How much work does the farmer do on the wheelbarrow?
  1. 600 J
  2. 70 J
  3. 6 J
  4. 6000 J
Correct answer: A
The force and motion are in the same direction, so W = Fd = 60 N x 10 m = 600 J.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: clarifying the misconception that negative work means no work or wasted energy
Q10. A learner says: 'The work done by the woman on the bucket is negative, so no work is done and the energy is wasted.' Which statement best corrects this idea?
  1. Negative work means the force and motion are opposite, and it takes energy away from the object
  2. Negative work means the energy is lost as heat
  3. Negative work means the object has stopped moving
  4. Negative work means the force is smaller than the weight
Correct answer: A
A negative sign shows that force and motion are in opposite directions. Positive work gives energy to an object and negative work takes energy away; it does not mean no work or wasted energy.
