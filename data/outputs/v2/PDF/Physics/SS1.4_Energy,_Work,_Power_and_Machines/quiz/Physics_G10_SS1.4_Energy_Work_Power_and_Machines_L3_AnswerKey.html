Q1. A 20 kg bucket is released from the top of a well, 8 m above the water, and falls freely. Assume there is no friction or air resistance. As it falls, its PE goes down and its KE goes up. What happens to the TOTAL energy (KE + PE) of the bucket?
  1. It grows, because the bucket speeds up
  2. It shrinks, because energy is used up as the bucket falls
  3. It stays the same, because PE is changed into KE
  4. It becomes zero when the bucket reaches the water
Correct answer: C
In an ideal system with no friction, energy is only transformed from PE to KE, so the total stays constant. The idea that energy is 'used up' or 'created' by motion is the common misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: completing the free-fall table for the 20 kg bucket released from 8 m
Q2. A 20 kg bucket is held 6 m above the water in a well. Use g = 10 N/kg. What is its potential energy (PE = mgh) relative to the water?
  1. 1200 J
  2. 120 J
  3. 1176 J
  4. 400 J
Correct answer: A
PE = mgh = 20 x 10 x 6 = 1200 J. The value 1176 J comes from using g = 9.8 N/kg, but this class uses g = 10 N/kg.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: adding the KE + PE column to the free-fall table and graphing PE, KE and total against height
Q3. A 20 kg bucket is released from 8 m above the water, and there is no friction. When it is 2 m above the water, its PE is 400 J. Its total energy (KE + PE) is 1600 J at every height. What is its KE at 2 m? (Use g = 10 N/kg.)
  1. 1600 J
  2. 800 J
  3. 400 J
  4. 1200 J
Correct answer: D
Total = KE + PE, so KE = 1600 - 400 = 1200 J. The total of 1600 J comes from the starting PE of 20 x 10 x 8, which is 1600 J.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: PhET Pendulum Lab, recording PE, KE and total at the top, halfway down, bottom and halfway up
Q4. A pendulum bob of mass 0.5 kg is pulled aside so that it is 0.2 m above its lowest point, and is then released. Assume there is no friction. Use g = 10 N/kg. When the bob is halfway down (0.1 m above its lowest point), what is its KE?
  1. 1 J
  2. 0 J
  3. 1.5 J
  4. 0.5 J
Correct answer: D
Total energy = 0.5 x 10 x 0.2 = 1 J. At 0.1 m, PE = 0.5 x 10 x 0.1 = 0.5 J, so KE = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5 J.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: PhET Pendulum Lab, watching the total energy bar with friction set to NONE
Q5. In the PhET Pendulum Lab, with friction set to NONE, a group sets the mass to 0.5 kg and raises the bob 0.2 m. They read a total energy of 0.98 J at every position, but they had calculated 1 J. What is the best explanation?
  1. Some energy is used up every time the bob swings
  2. The simulation has lost 0.02 J of energy to friction
  3. The total energy changes during the swing, so the reading is not constant
  4. The simulation uses g = 9.8 N/kg, but we used g = 10 N/kg, so the total is constant but slightly different
Correct answer: D
0.5 x 9.8 x 0.2 = 0.98 J, while 0.5 x 10 x 0.2 = 1 J. This is a difference in the value of g, not a change in energy, and the total stays constant.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: applying the conservation principle to the Kitui phenomenon ('where did that 1600 J come from?')
Q6. Mwende lifts a 20 kg bucket 8 m up a well in Kitui, and the bucket gains 1600 J of PE. Energy cannot be created. Where does this 1600 J of energy come from?
  1. The chemical energy in her body, from food such as ugali and githeri
  2. The energy comes from nowhere, since the bucket only changes position
  3. The bucket makes its own energy as it moves upward
  4. The rope and pulley create the extra energy
Correct answer: A
By conservation of energy, the bucket's gain in PE is transferred from the chemical energy stored in Mwende's body, which comes from her food. A pulley cannot create energy.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: elastic potential energy demonstration with a rubber band launching a paper ball
Q7. A rubber band is stretched and then released, and it launches a small paper ball. Which statement best describes the energy changes?
  1. The energy is created at the moment of release
  2. Elastic potential energy stored in the stretched rubber band changes into kinetic energy of the paper ball
  3. The stretched rubber band has no stored energy, so the paper ball has KE only by chance
  4. Kinetic energy in the rubber band changes into elastic potential energy of the ball
Correct answer: B
A stretched or compressed object stores elastic potential energy. When released, this changes into kinetic energy, following the same conservation idea.
