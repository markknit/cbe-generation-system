Q1. In Lesson 3, a 20 kg bucket released from 8 m in an ideal fall (no energy losses) hit the water at 12.6 m/s. A real bucket is now dropped down Mama Kavata's well in Kitui from the same 8 m. Which prediction is best?
  1. It hits the water at exactly 12.6 m/s, because the formula always works
  2. It hits the water faster than 12.6 m/s, because the real well is deeper
  3. It hits the water slower than 12.6 m/s, because some of its energy is transferred to heat, sound and friction
  4. It hits the water slower than 12.6 m/s, because the energy is destroyed as it falls
Correct answer: C
In a real fall some energy goes to heat, sound and air/rope friction, so less is left as kinetic energy and the speed is lower. Energy is transferred, not destroyed.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: data card of the real fall of the 20 kg bucket (calculating real KE at the bottom)
Q2. In a real fall, a 20 kg bucket reaches the water at 11 m/s. What is its kinetic energy at the water? (KE = 1/2 mv²)
  1. 220 J
  2. 1600 J
  3. 1210 J
  4. 2420 J
Correct answer: C
KE = 1/2 × 20 × 11 × 11 = 1210 J. The wrong answers come from forgetting the half or the square, or from using the ideal value of 1600 J.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: filling in the Ideal versus Real table (difference = 1600 − 1210)
Q3. A 20 kg bucket starts from rest 8 m above the water. Using g = 10 N/kg, its PE at the top is 1600 J. In the real fall its KE at the water is 1210 J. How much energy was transferred to heat, sound and air/rope friction?
  1. 390 J
  2. 1600 J
  3. 1210 J
  4. 2810 J
Correct answer: A
Total energy stays 1600 J, so the energy transferred to the surroundings = 1600 − 1210 = 390 J.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: sliding demo (block on a rough floor, rubbing palms, 'Where did the motion energy of the block go when it stopped?')
Q4. A block slides across a rough floor and stops. Just before stopping it had kinetic energy. Which statement best describes where that energy went?
  1. It changed into gravitational potential energy of the block
  2. It was transferred to the floor, block and air as heat and sound
  3. It went back into the block as extra kinetic energy
  4. It was destroyed by friction
Correct answer: B
Friction transfers the block's kinetic energy to the surroundings as heat and sound, like the warmth felt when rubbing palms together. The energy is not destroyed.
Use after: Explain phase — Extra problem: 2 kg block sliding from rest down a smooth slope 0.5 m high
Q5. A 2 kg block slides from rest down a smooth slope 0.5 m high. There is no friction. Using g = 10 N/kg, what is its speed at the bottom? (v = sqrt(2gh))
  1. 3.2 m/s
  2. 1.6 m/s
  3. 10 m/s
  4. 5.0 m/s
Correct answer: A
PE at the top = 2 × 10 × 0.5 = 10 J, which becomes 10 J of KE. So v = sqrt(2 × 10 × 0.5) = sqrt(10) ≈ 3.2 m/s. The mass cancels.
Use after: Explain phase — Extra problem: the real case where the block reaches the bottom at 3.0 m/s
Q6. The same 2 kg block starts with 10 J of potential energy at the top of a slope. On a real, rough slope it reaches the bottom at 3.0 m/s. How much energy was transferred to heat and sound?
  1. 0 J
  2. 10 J
  3. 1 J
  4. 9 J
Correct answer: C
KE at the bottom = 1/2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 9 J. Energy transferred = 10 − 9 = 1 J.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-Evidence-Reasoning statement: 'The energy of the bucket was conserved' (correcting the 'energy destroyed' misconception)
Q7. A learner says: 'In the real fall of the bucket, the missing 390 J was destroyed by friction.' Which reply is correct?
  1. Energy is never destroyed: the 390 J was transferred to the surroundings as heat and sound, so 1600 J = 1210 J + 390 J
  2. Wrong: the 390 J was turned back into potential energy at the top of the well
  3. Wrong: the 390 J does not exist, because the real bucket has the same KE as the ideal bucket
  4. Correct: friction uses up energy, so the total energy at the bottom is only 1210 J
Correct answer: A
Total energy is conserved. The bucket's 1600 J is shared between 1210 J of KE and 390 J transferred to heat, sound and air/rope friction.
