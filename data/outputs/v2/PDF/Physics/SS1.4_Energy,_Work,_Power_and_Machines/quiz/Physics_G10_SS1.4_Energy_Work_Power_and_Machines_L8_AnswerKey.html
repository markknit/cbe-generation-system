Use after: Observe phase — Station 1 Pulley system: load 200 N, effort 125 N
Q1. At the pulley station, a 200 N bucket of water is lifted using a pulley system. The effort needed is 125 N. What is the mechanical advantage (MA = load/effort) of the pulley system?
  1. 1.6
  2. 2
  3. 0.625
  4. 80
Correct answer: A
MA = load / effort = 200 N / 125 N = 1.6. Dividing effort by load gives the inverted value 0.625, and 80 is the efficiency in per cent, not the MA.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2 Ramp: 50 kg sack loaded 1.0 m high with a 4 m plank
Q2. Two youths load a 50 kg sack of maize (weight 500 N) onto a lorry 1.0 m high using a 4 m plank as a ramp. They push with an effort of 200 N along the plank. Using efficiency = (MA/VR) x 100%, what is the efficiency of the ramp?
  1. 2.5%
  2. 160%
  3. 80%
  4. 62.5%
Correct answer: D
MA = 500/200 = 2.5 and VR = 4/1 = 4, so efficiency = (2.5/4) x 100% = 62.5%. The work output is 500 J and the work input is 800 J, which also gives 62.5%.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 3 Lever (crowbar): effort arm 1.2 m, load arm 0.3 m, load 400 N, effort 125 N
Q3. A farmer uses a crowbar to lift a heavy stone. The effort is 125 N and the load is 400 N. The effort arm is 1.2 m and the load arm is 0.3 m. What is the mechanical advantage of the crowbar?
  1. 0.3125
  2. 4
  3. 3.2
  4. 80
Correct answer: C
MA = load / effort = 400 N / 125 N = 3.2. The value 4 is the velocity ratio (1.2 m / 0.3 m), and 0.3125 comes from dividing effort by load.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 1 Pulley system: single fixed pulley alone has VR = 1
Q4. Mwende uses a single fixed pulley (one wheel fixed above the well) to lift a 200 N bucket. Which statement is correct?
  1. The velocity ratio is 2, so the effort is halved to 100 N.
  2. The pulley reduces the work needed to lift the bucket.
  3. The pulley gives the bucket extra energy so it rises more easily.
  4. The velocity ratio is 1, so the effort is still about 200 N and the pulley only changes the direction of the force.
Correct answer: D
A single fixed pulley has VR = 1, so the ideal effort equals the load (200 N). It only changes the direction, which lets Mwende pull down using her body weight.
Use after: Explain phase — Final Explanation (CER): 1600 J becomes PE and 400 J (20%) is transferred as heat by friction
Q5. A bucket gains 1600 J of potential energy. With the pulley system, Mwende pulls the rope with a 125 N effort over 16 m. How much of the energy she supplies is transferred as heat by friction in the pulleys and rope?
  1. 2000 J
  2. 1600 J
  3. 0 J, because energy cannot be lost
  4. 400 J
Correct answer: D
Work input = 125 N x 16 m = 2000 J. Output is 1600 J of PE, so 2000 - 1600 = 400 J is transferred as heat by friction. Energy is not destroyed, so it is conserved.
Use after: Explain phase — Final Explanation (CER): the pulley trades a smaller force for a longer distance (VR = 2, MA = 1.6)
Q6. A woman lifts a 20 kg bucket 8 m by hand. A child lifts the same bucket the same height using a pulley system. Which statement best explains why the pulley system feels easier?
  1. The pulley system gives the bucket extra energy.
  2. The bucket gains less potential energy with the pulley system.
  3. The pulley system needs a smaller force but over a longer distance, so the work is not reduced.
  4. The pulley system reduces the work needed, so less energy is used.
Correct answer: C
The bucket gains the same PE either way. A machine trades a smaller force for a longer distance (effort 125 N over 16 m instead of 200 N over 8 m) and never reduces the work needed. Friction means slightly more work is actually done.
Use after: Explain phase — Challenge: could an ideal pulley system with no friction have 100% efficiency?
Q7. An ideal pulley system with no friction has a velocity ratio of 2 and is used to lift a 200 N bucket. Its efficiency would be 100%, so MA = VR. What effort is needed?
  1. 100 N
  2. 200 N
  3. 400 N
  4. 125 N
Correct answer: A
With 100% efficiency, MA = VR = 2. Effort = load / MA = 200 N / 2 = 100 N. The real pulley system needs 125 N because of friction.
Use after: Model Building phase — Final model: source of energy (chemical energy from ugali or githeri in Mama Kavata or Mwende)
Q8. Mwende, a 10-year-old, lifts the bucket with the pulley system. Where does the energy she supplies come from originally?
  1. From chemical energy stored in food such as ugali and githeri
  2. From the friction in the rope
  3. From the pulley system, which creates energy
  4. From the potential energy of the bucket at the bottom of the well
Correct answer: A
The energy Mwende supplies comes from chemical energy in the food she has eaten. The pulley only transfers energy and does not create it, and friction transfers some energy to heat.
