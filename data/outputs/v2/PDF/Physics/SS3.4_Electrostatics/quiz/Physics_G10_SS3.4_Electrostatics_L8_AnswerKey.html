Q1. A gold-leaf electroscope has an insulating stopper where the metal rod passes into the case. What is the main job of this insulating stopper?
  1. It makes the leaf heavy so it hangs down
  2. It carries electrons from the leaf to the Earth
  3. It collects charge from the charged object
  4. It stops charge leaking away from the cap, stem and leaf
Correct answer: D
The stopper and case are insulators, so charge stays on the metal cap, stem and leaf instead of leaking away. The cap collects charge and the metal stem and leaf let it spread.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: particle-level diagram of the electroscope when the negative strip is near the cap
Q2. A rubbed polythene strip (negative) is brought near the cap of a neutral electroscope without touching it. The leaf lifts away from the stem. Which explanation is correct?
  1. The strip gives the leaf a positive charge, so it is pulled upward
  2. Electrons are pushed down the stem to the leaf, so the leaf and stem both have excess electrons and repel
  3. The leaf is attracted to the strip because the strip is magnetic
  4. Protons in the cap move down the stem into the leaf
Correct answer: B
The negative strip repels electrons in the metal down the stem to the leaf. The leaf and stem then have the same (negative) charge and like charges repel, so the leaf diverges.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 3, touch the strip to the cap (contact), remove the strip and record what the leaf does
Q3. A negatively charged polythene strip touches the cap of an electroscope and is then taken away. The leaf stays diverged. Why does it stay diverged?
  1. The strip is still pulling the leaf upward from a distance
  2. Protons moved from the strip onto the electroscope and are stuck there
  3. The leaf is stuck to the stem and cannot fall
  4. Electrons moved from the strip to the electroscope, so the leaf and stem keep excess electrons and repel
Correct answer: D
On contact, electrons pass from the strip to the electroscope. They stay on the leaf and stem, so repulsion continues after the strip is removed. The strip cannot be pulling the leaf because it is no longer there.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 6, repeat the contact charging with 40 strokes and compare with 20 strokes
Q4. The same electroscope is charged by contact with a polythene strip rubbed 20 strokes, and the leaf diverges. After it is discharged, it is charged again with a strip rubbed 40 strokes and the leaf diverges more. What does this show?
  1. The leaf diverges more only because the strip is longer
  2. The electroscope has become more sensitive after the first test
  3. More strokes make the leaf lighter
  4. More charge on the leaf and stem gives more repulsion and a bigger divergence
Correct answer: D
On the same electroscope, more strokes put more charge on the leaf and stem, so repulsion and divergence are greater. Divergence shows the amount of charge.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: sign test, with the perspex rod (positive) making the leaf diverge less
Q5. An electroscope is charged negatively and its leaf is diverged. A rubbed object is brought near the cap without touching it, and the leaf diverges less. What does this show about the charge on the object?
  1. It is neutral, so it has no effect on the leaf
  2. It has a negative charge, the same as the electroscope
  3. It has the opposite (positive) charge to the electroscope
  4. Nothing can be known about its charge
Correct answer: C
A positive object attracts electrons up toward the cap, so the leaf and stem have fewer excess electrons and the divergence falls. A decrease in divergence means the opposite sign.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: earthing, electrons flowing to the Earth through the finger
Q6. A negatively charged electroscope has its leaf diverged. A learner touches the cap with a finger and the leaf collapses. What happens?
  1. The finger adds more electrons, so the leaf repels more strongly
  2. Electrons flow from the electroscope through the finger to the Earth, leaving it neutral, so the leaf collapses
  3. Electrons stay on the leaf but the finger pushes the leaf down
  4. Protons flow from the Earth into the electroscope, so the leaf falls
Correct answer: B
Earthing lets the excess electrons flow through the finger to the Earth. With no excess charge on the leaf and stem there is no repulsion, so the leaf falls.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: adding the 'How do we know?' detector box showing the electroscope near the negative balloon
Q7. Amina rubs a balloon 20 strokes on dry hair and brings it near the cap of a neutral electroscope without touching it. The leaf rises. What can she correctly conclude from this alone?
  1. The balloon is magnetic
  2. The balloon is positively charged
  3. The balloon is charged, but the rise alone does not show its sign
  4. The balloon is negatively charged for certain
Correct answer: C
A leaf rising on a neutral electroscope shows only that the object is charged. The sign needs a sign test on an already charged electroscope.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: Amina's Mombasa question and testing the humidity idea with the electroscope
Q8. On a humid day in Mombasa, a charged electroscope's leaf slowly falls even though nobody touches the cap. What is the best explanation?
  1. The electrons turn into protons in humid air
  2. Charge leaks away through the damp air, so repulsion becomes weaker
  3. The leaf has become heavier because it has absorbed protons
  4. The Earth pulls harder on the leaf in humid places
Correct answer: B
Damp air lets charge escape from the electroscope. With less charge on the leaf and stem there is less repulsion, so the leaf collapses. This links to Amina's balloon question about her coastal home.
