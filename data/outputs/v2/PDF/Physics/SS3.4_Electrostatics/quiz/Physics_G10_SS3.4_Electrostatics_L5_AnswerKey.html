Q1. A neutral gold-leaf electroscope has its leaf hanging straight down. A polythene strip rubbed with wool (so it is negative) is touched to the metal cap and then taken away. What will the leaf do after the strip is removed?
  1. It stays raised, because charge has moved onto the electroscope
  2. It goes even higher once the strip is removed
  3. It stays down the whole time because polythene is an insulator
  4. It falls straight back down as soon as the strip is removed
Correct answer: A
Touching lets electrons move from the strip onto the metal cap, stem and leaf. The electroscope keeps this charge after the strip is removed, so the leaf stays diverged.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: bring the rubbed strip near the cap without touching, then take it away
Q2. A neutral electroscope has a negative polythene strip held close to its cap but NOT touching it. The leaf rises. The strip is then taken away. What does the leaf do, and why?
  1. It falls back down, because no charge was transferred to the electroscope
  2. It rises higher, because the strip is now further away
  3. It stays raised, because the strip left its charge behind
  4. It falls back down, because the strip's charge was used up
Correct answer: A
With no contact, no electrons were transferred, so the electroscope is still neutral overall. When the strip is removed the leaf falls back. Only contact leaves the leaf raised.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: step 4, discharging the electroscope by touching the cap with a finger
Q3. A electroscope has been charged by touching it with a negative polythene strip, and its leaf is raised. A learner then touches the metal cap with a finger. What do they see, and what does it show?
  1. The leaf collapses, because the extra charge is conducted away through the finger
  2. The leaf stays the same, because the charge is trapped on the leaf
  3. The leaf rises higher, because the finger adds more charge
  4. The leaf rises and falls repeatedly, because the finger is neutral
Correct answer: A
The body can conduct the extra electrons away from the electroscope. With no excess charge left, the leaf hangs down again. This is how the class discharged the electroscope between trials.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: question 2, what the free electrons do when the negative strip touches the metal cap
Q4. A negative polythene strip touches the metal cap of a neutral electroscope. Which statement correctly describes what moves?
  1. Positive charges move from the strip onto the cap
  2. Electrons move from the strip onto the cap and spread down the stem to the leaf
  3. Protons move from the electroscope onto the strip
  4. Electrons are created in the cap by the touch of the strip
Correct answer: B
In a solid only electrons move. The strip has extra electrons, and the metal has free electrons, so electrons move from the strip onto the metal and spread through the cap, stem and leaf. Charge is transferred, not created.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: questions 3 and 4, why the leaf stays diverged and what sign the electroscope now has
Q5. After a negative strip has touched a neutral electroscope and been removed, the leaf stays raised. What is the sign of the electroscope's charge, and why does the leaf rise?
  1. Positive, because the leaf lost electrons to the strip
  2. Negative, because the stem and leaf attract the strip even though it has gone
  3. Positive, because the stem and leaf attract each other
  4. Negative, because the stem and leaf both have extra electrons and like charges repel
Correct answer: D
Contact gives the SAME sign as the charged object. The stem and leaf both gain extra electrons and become negative, and like charges repel, so the leaf rises.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: exit question about a positive perspex rod touching a neutral electroscope
Q6. A positive perspex rod touches the cap of a neutral electroscope. What sign of charge does the electroscope gain, and which way do electrons move?
  1. Positive; electrons move from the rod to the electroscope
  2. Negative; electrons move from the rod to the electroscope
  3. Negative; electrons move from the electroscope to the rod
  4. Positive; electrons move from the electroscope to the rod
Correct answer: D
Contact gives the same sign, so the electroscope becomes positive. A positive rod has a shortage of electrons, so electrons move from the electroscope onto the rod, leaving the electroscope with a deficit.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: revising the particle model with the paper piece touching the balloon and then being repelled
Q7. A neutral paper piece jumps up to a negative balloon, touches it, and then falls off. Which explanation is correct?
  1. The paper piece lost electrons to the balloon, became positive and was repelled
  2. The paper piece became magnetic after touching the balloon and was pushed away
  3. The balloon's charge was used up when the paper touched it, so nothing held the paper
  4. The paper piece gained some electrons from the balloon, became negative like the balloon, and was repelled
Correct answer: D
On contact a few electrons move from the negative balloon onto the paper. The paper then has the same sign as the balloon, and like charges repel, so it falls off.
