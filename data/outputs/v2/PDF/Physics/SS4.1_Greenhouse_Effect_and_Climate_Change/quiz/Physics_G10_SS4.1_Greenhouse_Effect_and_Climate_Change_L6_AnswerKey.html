Q1. Which statement best describes what the ozone layer does for life on Earth?
  1. It produces the oxygen that animals breathe
  2. It absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation from the Sun before it reaches the surface
  3. It traps heat leaving Earth's surface, keeping the planet warm
  4. It reflects all sunlight back into space so Earth does not overheat
Correct answer: B
The ozone layer absorbs harmful UV-B radiation coming in from the Sun. Trapping outgoing infrared heat is the job of greenhouse gases, not ozone.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: notebook prompt 'Do you think ozone depletion and the greenhouse effect are the same thing, different things, or related things?'
Q2. A student says: 'Ozone depletion and the greenhouse effect are the same thing, because both are about gases in the atmosphere.' Which response is most accurate?
  1. The student is right: both are caused by the same gas doing the same job
  2. The student is wrong: ozone depletion is about reduced absorption of incoming UV-B in the stratosphere, while the greenhouse effect is about trapping outgoing infrared radiation in the troposphere
  3. The student is wrong: the greenhouse effect happens only in the stratosphere and ozone depletion only at the surface
  4. The student is right: thinning of the ozone layer is what makes the greenhouse effect stronger
Correct answer: B
They are distinct processes. Ozone depletion involves UV-B absorption in the stratosphere. The greenhouse effect involves infrared trapping in the troposphere. They interact but are not the same.
Use after: Observe phase — Source A: Khan Academy 'Ozone Layer and Ozone Hole' reading, Evidence Collection Table column 'What damages it and how'
Q3. CFC molecules rise to the stratosphere, where ultraviolet radiation releases chlorine atoms that destroy ozone. What is the key feature of how CFCs destroy ozone?
  1. CFCs only damage ozone over Antarctica and have no effect elsewhere
  2. The chlorine atom is regenerated after each reaction, so one CFC molecule can destroy many ozone molecules
  3. Each CFC molecule destroys exactly one ozone molecule and is then used up
  4. CFCs turn ozone into carbon dioxide, which then escapes into space
Correct answer: B
The destruction is catalytic: the chlorine is not used up and keeps destroying ozone molecules, so small amounts of CFCs do a lot of damage.
Use after: Observe phase — Source B: UNEP Ozone Data Portal, Nairobi UV-B irradiance graph annotation
Q4. UV-B measurements in Nairobi rose to a peak in the late 1990s and then slowly decreased. The Montreal Protocol, which cut the production of CFCs, began to take effect around that time. What is the best explanation for the fall in UV-B after 2000?
  1. More greenhouse gases in the troposphere absorbed the extra UV-B
  2. Less CFC in the atmosphere meant less ozone was destroyed, so the ozone layer slowly recovered and absorbed more UV-B
  3. Nairobi moved further from the equator, so it received less sunlight
  4. The Sun began giving out less UV-B after the 1990s
Correct answer: B
Reduced CFC production let ozone recover gradually. More ozone means more UV-B absorbed, so less reaches the ground. The decline lags behind the Protocol because CFCs stay in the atmosphere for a long time.
Use after: Observe phase — Source B: teacher question 'Is the change in Nairobi as big as the Antarctic ozone hole? Why not?'
Q5. The Antarctic ozone hole is very large, but the change in ozone and UV-B over Nairobi, near the equator, is much smaller. Why?
  1. Ozone loss was greatest near the poles and was small near the equator
  2. Nairobi is too high above sea level to be affected by UV-B
  3. Ozone depletion is caused only by cold weather, and Nairobi is warm
  4. CFCs are only released near the South Pole
Correct answer: A
Ozone loss was greatest near the poles and much smaller near the equator, so the UV-B change in Nairobi is much smaller than the Antarctic ozone hole.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: T-chart discussion, 'Which of these two phenomena best explains what they're experiencing?' (farmer in Embu and glacier at Mount Kenya)
Q6. Mount Kenya's Lewis Glacier has retreated greatly since 1934, and rains in Embu County have become unpredictable. Which atmospheric process is the primary driver of this warming and rainfall disruption?
  1. Ozone depletion alone, because UV-B directly melts the ice and changes the rains
  2. Neither, because the atmosphere has no effect on glaciers
  3. The greenhouse effect, because extra greenhouse gases trap more outgoing infrared radiation and warm the surface
  4. Both equally, because ozone depletion and the greenhouse effect are the same process
Correct answer: C
The greenhouse effect (trapped infrared) is the main driver of warming, glacier retreat and rainfall change. Ozone depletion is a separate process that raises UV-B at the surface.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: group sentence 'how do these two phenomena interact?' and Venn diagram overlap
Q7. Which statement correctly describes an interaction between ozone depletion and the greenhouse effect?
  1. Ozone depletion cools the troposphere, which stops the greenhouse effect
  2. Greenhouse gases remove CFCs from the stratosphere, so they never harm ozone
  3. Ozone molecules trap infrared radiation, which is the cause of the greenhouse effect
  4. CFCs destroy ozone and are also powerful greenhouse gases
Correct answer: D
CFCs link the two problems: they deplete stratospheric ozone and they also trap infrared heat in the atmosphere as greenhouse gases.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the stratospheric ozone layer and CFC annotations to the cumulative atmospheric model
Q8. A student is adding the ozone layer to their model of the atmosphere. Which drawing and note is correct?
  1. Ozone layer drawn in the troposphere, mixed in with the greenhouse gases, with a note that it absorbs infrared
  2. Ozone layer drawn at ground level, with a note that thinning of ozone traps more infrared heat
  3. Ozone layer drawn in the stratosphere, with a note that visible light is absorbed by ozone and cannot reach the surface
  4. Ozone layer drawn in the stratosphere (about 15–35 km), above the troposphere, with a note that fewer ozone molecules means more UV-B reaching the surface
Correct answer: D
Ozone is in the stratosphere (about 15–35 km), above the troposphere where greenhouse gases act. It absorbs UV-B, so thinner ozone means more UV-B at the surface, not extra warming from infrared.
