Use after: Observe phase — Part A — Graph Analysis: Keeling Curve (the 'What does ppm mean?' prompt)
Q1. The Keeling Curve shows atmospheric CO₂ rising from about 315 ppm in 1958 to over 420 ppm today. What does a reading of 420 ppm mean?
  1. There are about 420 CO₂ molecules in every million molecules of air
  2. The air contains 420 kg of CO₂ for every tonne of air
  3. CO₂ makes up 42% of the air
  4. Only one air molecule in every 420 is a CO₂ molecule
Correct answer: A
ppm means parts per million, so 420 ppm is 420 CO₂ molecules among every million air molecules. That is only about 0.04% of the air, which is why the percentage and 1-in-420 options are wrong.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A — Graph Analysis: 'Does correlation prove causation? What else would you need to know?'
Q2. Students notice that atmospheric CO₂ concentration and global average temperature anomaly have both risen steadily since about 1950. A student says, 'This proves that rising CO₂ causes the warming.' What is the best scientific response?
  1. The graphs show a correlation. To support causation we also need a mechanism and evidence, such as a simulation that changes only the greenhouse gas amount
  2. Two graphs that rise together always prove that one causes the other
  3. The graphs show that temperature rise causes the CO₂ rise, not the other way round
  4. Correlation can never be used as evidence in science
Correct answer: A
A correlation is useful evidence but does not prove causation by itself. The mechanism (greenhouse gases absorb IR) and a controlled simulation that changes one variable strengthen the causal claim.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B — PhET Recall: add 'Lots of greenhouse gases' and record the new temperature
Q3. In the PhET 'Greenhouse Effect' simulation, a student changes the atmosphere from 'Today's atmosphere' to 'Lots of greenhouse gases'. What happens to the equilibrium temperature, and why?
  1. It rises, because more of the infrared radiation from the surface is absorbed and re-emitted back toward the surface
  2. It stays the same, because only the energy from the Sun decides the temperature
  3. It rises, because greenhouse gases produce extra ultraviolet radiation
  4. It falls, because the extra gases reflect more sunlight back to space
Correct answer: A
More greenhouse gas absorbs more outgoing infrared and re-emits part of it back to the surface. Less energy escapes to space, so the surface warms until a new, higher equilibrium temperature is reached.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C — Kenyan Emissions Context: Lewis Glacier lost more than 85% of its ice area since 1934
Q4. The Lewis Glacier on Mount Kenya covered about 1.35 km² in 1934 and less than 0.20 km² today. Taking the area today as 0.20 km², what percentage of its ice area has it lost?
  1. 85.2%
  2. 1.15%
  3. 14.8%
  4. 675%
Correct answer: A
Area lost = 1.35 − 0.20 = 1.15 km². Percentage lost = 1.15 ÷ 1.35 × 100 ≈ 85.2%, which matches 'more than 85%'. The 14.8% option is the share that remains.
Use after: Explain phase — Addressing the misconception that 'the ozone hole is causing warming' (ozone blocks UV, greenhouse gases trap IR)
Q5. A student writes: 'The hole in the ozone layer is what is causing global warming.' Which statement correctly separates the ozone layer from greenhouse gases?
  1. Ozone traps infrared radiation, and CO₂ blocks ultraviolet radiation
  2. Greenhouse gases block UV from the Sun, which is why Earth warms
  3. Ozone and greenhouse gases do the same job, so a hole in the ozone layer lets heat escape from Earth
  4. The ozone layer blocks ultraviolet (UV) radiation, while greenhouse gases such as CO₂ trap infrared (IR) radiation, so they are two different mechanisms
Correct answer: D
Ozone absorbs UV from the Sun. Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit IR from Earth's surface, which amplifies warming. The two mechanisms should not be confused.
Use after: Explain phase — Causal chain scaffold and the exam-style question: 'Explain, using physics, why burning charcoal in Nairobi contributes to the melting of glaciers on Mount Kenya'
Q6. Which sequence correctly explains how burning charcoal in Nairobi contributes to the melting of the Lewis Glacier on Mount Kenya?
  1. Charcoal burning releases CO₂ → CO₂ reflects visible light from the Sun back to space → Earth warms → ice melts
  2. Charcoal burning releases CO₂ → CO₂ blocks ultraviolet radiation from the Sun → Earth's energy balance is unchanged → the glacier melts because the mountain is closer to the Sun
  3. Charcoal burning releases CO₂ → more CO₂ absorbs and re-emits infrared radiation from Earth's surface → less energy escapes to space, so Earth gains net energy → global temperature rises → ice melts faster than it builds up
  4. Charcoal burning releases CO₂ → CO₂ lets more infrared escape to space → temperature rises → ice melts
Correct answer: C
The full chain is: human emission of a greenhouse gas, more IR absorbed and re-emitted, a net energy gain, a higher global temperature, and then faster glacier melt. The other options give the wrong radiation type or a wrong energy-balance effect.
Use after: Model Building phase — Adding the 'Human Amplification Layer' to the cumulative atmospheric energy model
Q7. A student adds a 'Human Amplification Layer' to an energy model by thickening the greenhouse gas layer. Which new arrow should they draw to show the effect of the extra gas?
  1. An arrow showing more shortwave radiation coming from the Sun
  2. An arrow showing more infrared radiation going straight out to space
  3. An arrow showing more infrared radiation re-emitted back toward Earth's surface, giving a net energy gain at the surface
  4. An arrow showing more ultraviolet radiation absorbed by the ozone layer
Correct answer: C
A thicker greenhouse gas layer absorbs more outgoing IR and re-emits more of it back toward the surface. This causes a net energy gain and a rising surface temperature. The arrow must point back toward Earth, not away from it.
