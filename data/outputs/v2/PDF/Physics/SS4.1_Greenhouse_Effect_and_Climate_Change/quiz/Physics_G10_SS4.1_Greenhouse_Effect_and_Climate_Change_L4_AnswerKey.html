Q1. An Embu farmer says the long rains (March–May) now arrive weeks later than in her grandparents' time. Using what you know about the greenhouse effect, which statement best links the enhanced greenhouse effect to this change?
  1. Thinning of the ozone layer lets in more sunlight, which directly delays the rain clouds.
  2. More greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit more infrared radiation, raising surface temperatures, which changes evaporation and the circulation that brings the rains.
  3. Greenhouse gases remove water vapour from the air, so rain clouds can no longer form.
  4. Earth has moved closer to the Sun, so Embu receives more heat and the rains come later.
Correct answer: B
Enhanced back-radiation raises surface temperature, which changes evaporation and atmospheric circulation and so the timing of rainfall. Ozone depletion and Earth's distance from the Sun are common misconceptions and do not explain this.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: NASA GISS global temperature anomaly graph (find the year the anomaly first exceeded 0.5 °C)
Q2. A NASA graph shows global temperature anomaly from 1880 to the present. An anomaly of +0.5 °C for a certain year means that the global average surface temperature in that year was:
  1. 0.5 °C below the long-term average.
  2. exactly 0.5 °C in total.
  3. 0.5 °C higher than the previous year.
  4. 0.5 °C above the long-term average.
Correct answer: D
An anomaly is the difference from a long-term average. A positive value means the year was warmer than that average by that amount.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: NASA GISS graph, 'What pattern do you see from 1950 onward? Mark the steepest rise'
Q3. From 1950 onward, the NASA global temperature anomaly graph shows which overall pattern?
  1. Anomalies stay about the same, with no clear change.
  2. Anomalies rise overall, with the most recent decades warmer than earlier ones.
  3. Anomalies fall steadily as the atmosphere cools.
  4. Anomalies rise and fall in a regular cycle with no overall trend.
Correct answer: B
The NASA record shows a clear overall upward trend in global average temperature since 1950, with recent decades the warmest. Year-to-year ups and downs do not change this trend.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Lake Victoria supporting phenomenon (Kisumu flooding, 2019–20)
Q4. Lake Victoria rose about 1.2 m above its long-term average in 2019–20 and flooded homes in Kisumu. Scientists link this to very heavy rains driven mainly by a strong Indian Ocean Dipole. Which statement about global warming and this event is the most scientifically careful?
  1. The flooding shows that Lake Victoria will keep rising every year because of global warming.
  2. Because the flooding was linked to a natural pattern, global warming has nothing to do with extreme rainfall.
  3. Global warming alone caused the flooding, so no other natural pattern was involved.
  4. A warmer climate makes extreme rainfall more likely, so the event is one piece of the climate-change story but not proof on its own.
Correct answer: D
The lesson states that the main driver was the Indian Ocean Dipole, and that studies suggest a warmer climate makes such extreme rain more likely. A single event is not proof by itself.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: three-box cause-chain card (greenhouse gases to a Kenyan community affected)
Q5. Which cause chain correctly connects more greenhouse gases to flooding of homes near Lake Victoria, with no key step missing?
  1. More greenhouse gases → the Sun gets hotter → lake water expands and floods homes.
  2. More greenhouse gases → more carbon dioxide dissolves in the lake → lake floods.
  3. More greenhouse gases trap more infrared radiation → surface temperature rises → more evaporation and moisture in the air → heavier rain more likely → lake level rises and homes flood.
  4. More greenhouse gases → ozone layer thins → more sunlight reaches Earth → lake floods.
Correct answer: C
The complete chain includes the rise in surface temperature and its effect on evaporation and rainfall. Other options skip steps or use ozone and Sun misconceptions.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: whole-class discussion after the effects web and gallery walk ('How can warming cause both flooding and drought?')
Q6. Warming can cause flooding in some parts of Kenya and drought in others at the same time. Which explanation is best?
  1. Flooding and drought cannot both be linked to warming, so one must be caused by something else.
  2. Warming intensifies the water cycle, with more evaporation and heavier but more uneven rainfall, so some areas flood while others dry out.
  3. Warming stops evaporation in dry areas, so drought and floods have unrelated causes.
  4. Warming creates extra water on Earth, which falls only in flood areas.
Correct answer: B
Warming increases evaporation and makes rainfall more intense but unevenly distributed, so one change in the water cycle can produce both floods and drought.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: answering a Lesson 1 question using scientific vocabulary
Q7. A student writes a Driving Question Board answer to 'Why is Mount Kenya's Lewis Glacier shrinking?' Which answer uses correct scientific reasoning?
  1. The ozone hole is letting extra ultraviolet light melt the ice directly.
  2. The glacier is shrinking because Mount Kenya is getting closer to the Sun.
  3. Mount Kenya's volcano is becoming active and its heat is melting the glacier.
  4. Enhanced greenhouse gases increase back-radiation of infrared energy, raising surface temperature, so ice melts faster than snow builds up.
Correct answer: D
Higher concentrations of greenhouse gases increase back-radiation and raise air and surface temperatures, so melting exceeds snow accumulation. The mountain has not moved closer to the Sun, ozone is a separate issue, and volcanic heat is not the cause.
