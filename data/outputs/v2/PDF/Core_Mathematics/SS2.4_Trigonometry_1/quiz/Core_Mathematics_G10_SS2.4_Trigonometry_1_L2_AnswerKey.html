Q1. A student stands 40 m from the base of a mast and measures the angle from the ground up to the top as 50°. The mast's height is the side opposite the 50° angle, and the 40 m is the side adjacent to it. Which ratio links the angle to the height?
  1. sin 50° = opposite ÷ hypotenuse
  2. cos 50° = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse
  3. tan 50° = opposite ÷ adjacent
  4. tan 50° = adjacent ÷ opposite
Correct answer: C
The height (opposite) and the 40 m distance (adjacent) are both involved and the hypotenuse is not, so the tangent ratio, opposite ÷ adjacent, is the one that links them.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: pairs label O, A, H for each acute angle in their two triangles, noticing that labels change when the reference angle switches
Q2. In a right-angled triangle PQR, the right angle is at Q and PR is the longest side. Looking from angle P, a learner labels side QR as 'opposite'. She then switches to look from angle R. What is side QR called now?
  1. Still opposite, because the side does not move
  2. Adjacent, because it now lies next to the reference angle R
  3. It has no label, because it is only labelled from angle P
  4. Hypotenuse, because it is a side of the triangle
Correct answer: B
Opposite and adjacent depend on the reference angle. From angle R, side QR touches the angle and is not the hypotenuse, so it is adjacent.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: learners open the sine table and compare table values with their computed ratios
Q3. Two learners draw a right-angled triangle with an angle of about 35°. They measure the opposite side as 5.8 cm and the hypotenuse as 10.0 cm, so their sin 35° is 0.58. The mathematical table gives sin 35° = 0.574. What best explains the small difference?
  1. Small errors in drawing and measuring the sides; the table value is the reliable one
  2. The table is wrong because it does not match their triangle
  3. Their triangle is too small, so the sine ratio changes with size
  4. They should have used adjacent ÷ hypotenuse for sine
Correct answer: A
Sine depends only on the angle, not the triangle's size. The small gap comes from measurement error with ruler and protractor, while the table value is pre-computed and accurate.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: SOH-CAH-TOA definitions and choosing the correct ratio
Q4. A 10 m ladder leans against a wall and makes an angle of 60° with the ground. The ladder is the hypotenuse, and the height it reaches up the wall is the side opposite the 60° angle. Which ratio should be used to find that height?
  1. tan 60° = adjacent ÷ opposite
  2. tan 60° = opposite ÷ adjacent
  3. sin 60° = opposite ÷ hypotenuse
  4. cos 60° = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse
Correct answer: C
The known side is the hypotenuse and the wanted side is the opposite, so SOH (sine = opposite ÷ hypotenuse) is the ratio to use. Tangent would need the adjacent side, which is not given.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: guided mast example, height = 40 × tan 50°
Q5. A student stands 40 m from the base of a mast and measures the angle of elevation to the top as 50°. From tables, tan 50° ≈ 1.192. What is the height of the mast, to 1 decimal place?
  1. 25.7 m
  2. 30.6 m
  3. 33.6 m
  4. 47.7 m
Correct answer: D
tan 50° = height ÷ 40, so height = 40 × tan 50° = 40 × 1.192 ≈ 47.7 m. Dividing gives 33.6 m, and using sin or cos gives the other wrong values.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: independent practice (a), the Kisumu tree problem
Q6. A surveyor in Kisumu stands 25 m from the base of a tree and measures the angle of elevation to the top as 38°. Using tan 38° ≈ 0.781, what is the height of the tree, to 1 decimal place?
  1. 15.4 m
  2. 40.6 m
  3. 32.0 m
  4. 19.5 m
Correct answer: D
tan 38° = height ÷ 25, so height = 25 × 0.781 ≈ 19.5 m. Dividing 25 by 0.781 gives 32.0 m, which is a common error.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: independent practice (b), back-calculating θ from sin θ = 0.643
Q7. You know that sin θ = 0.643 for an acute angle θ and want to find θ using mathematical tables. What should you do?
  1. Divide 0.643 by 90 to get the angle
  2. Find 0.643 in the sine table and read the angle that goes with it
  3. Find 0.643 in the cosine table and read the angle that goes with it
  4. Multiply 0.643 by 100 to get the angle in degrees
Correct answer: B
Tables list each angle with its sine value, so you can work backwards by finding the given ratio in the sine column and reading the angle. Here it gives about 40°.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding sin θ, cos θ and tan θ formulas to the Lesson 1 triangle model
Q8. You are adding ratio formulas to your right-angled triangle model, which has sides labelled opposite (O), adjacent (A) and hypotenuse (H) relative to angle θ. Which formula belongs next to the adjacent side and the hypotenuse?
  1. tan θ = A ÷ H
  2. cos θ = O ÷ H
  3. cos θ = A ÷ H
  4. sin θ = A ÷ H
Correct answer: C
Cosine is adjacent ÷ hypotenuse (CAH), so cos θ = A ÷ H goes with the adjacent side and the hypotenuse.
