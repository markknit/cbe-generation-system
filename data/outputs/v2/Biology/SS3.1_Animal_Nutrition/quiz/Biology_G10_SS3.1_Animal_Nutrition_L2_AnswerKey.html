Q1. A farmer in Kakamega finds maize leaves with ragged, irregular holes and torn edges. What physical property must the feeding tool of the insect that caused this damage have?
  1. It must be a long hollow tube that pierces the leaf surface
  2. It must be soft and thin so it can suck sap from the leaf
  3. It must be hard and toothed so it can cut and grind solid leaf tissue
  4. It must be a smooth flap that only holds the leaf in place
Correct answer: C
Ragged, torn edges show that solid tissue was cut and ground by a hard, toothed tool. A soft or hollow tube would leave the leaf intact, not torn.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: close-read annotation of the Grasshopper Mouthpart Diagram handout
Q2. A grasshopper has five main mouthpart components. Which structure is the most important chewing tool, being hardened and toothed and moving sideways to cut, crush and grind plant tissue?
  1. Labrum
  2. Hypopharynx
  3. Mandibles
  4. Labium
Correct answer: C
The mandibles are hard, toothed and move sideways to cut and grind food. The labrum only holds food in place, even though it is at the front.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: probing the misconception that the labrum is the main cutting structure
Q3. A learner says, "The labrum is at the front of the grasshopper's mouth, so it must do the cutting." Which reply is correct?
  1. Wrong. The labrum is a flap-like upper lip that holds food in place, while the mandibles do the cutting
  2. Correct. The structure at the front always does the cutting
  3. Wrong. The labrum is the part that mixes saliva with food
  4. Correct. The labrum is hard and toothed like the mandibles
Correct answer: A
Position does not show function. The labrum is a soft flap that holds food, and the hardened toothed mandibles cut and grind. The hypopharynx is the part that helps mix saliva with food.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: structured text read aloud about the five mouthpart components
Q4. Which pair of structures correctly matches a grasshopper mouthpart with its function?
  1. Hypopharynx: cuts food sideways; labrum: moves food towards the mandibles
  2. Labium: forms a lower lip that stops food falling out; maxillae: move food towards the mandibles
  3. Maxillae: grind food; labium: mixes saliva with food
  4. Labium: cuts leaf tissue; maxillae: hold food in place as an upper lip
Correct answer: B
The labium is the lower lip that prevents food falling out, and the maxillae handle food and move it towards the mandibles. The other options mix up the functions.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: termite mouthpart comparison with the grasshopper
Q5. Both grasshoppers and termites are crop pests in Kenya. Which statement best explains why both can damage plants in similar ways?
  1. Both have a hollow tube for sucking sap from stems
  2. Both swallow plant material whole without any mouthparts
  3. Both have hard, toothed chewing mouthparts that can cut and grind solid plant material
  4. Both have soft mouthparts that dissolve plant tissue before eating it
Correct answer: C
Grasshoppers and termites both have chewing mouthparts with hard, toothed mandibles adapted to solid plant material. This shows a structural pattern across species.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: sentence-stem explanation ("This type of breakdown is called...")
Q6. Grasshoppers break maize leaves into small pieces using their mandibles, without changing the leaf chemically. What is this type of breakdown called?
  1. Chemical digestion
  2. Egestion
  3. Mechanical digestion
  4. Absorption
Correct answer: C
Physical tearing, cutting and grinding by mouthparts is mechanical digestion. Chemical digestion needs enzymes to change the food chemically.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: consolidation question about termites and fence posts
Q7. A farmer in Kakamega notices that termites are destroying the wooden posts of a fence. Using what you know about chewing mouthparts, how are the termites able to do this?
  1. They use hard, toothed mandibles to scrape and cut tiny pieces of wood, which is solid plant material
  2. They use the soft labrum to tear the wood apart
  3. They dissolve the wood with saliva before touching it
  4. They pierce the wood with a hollow needle and suck out liquid
Correct answer: A
Termites have chewing mouthparts, and hard toothed mandibles can cut off tiny pieces of wood, which is solid plant material. The soft labrum only holds food.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: seeding question "could a mosquito feed with mandibles shaped like these?"
Q8. Could an insect with mandibles like a grasshopper's (hard, toothed blades for cutting and grinding) drink blood from your skin as a mosquito does? Choose the best reason.
  1. No, because such mandibles are built to cut and grind solid food, not to carry liquid
  2. No, because mandibles are only found in grasshoppers
  3. Yes, because all insects use the same mouthparts for every kind of food
  4. Yes, because toothed mandibles are the best tool for sucking liquids
Correct answer: A
Hard, toothed mandibles suit solid food. Drinking blood needs a different structure, which Lesson 3 will explore. Mosquitoes do have mandibles, but they are thin and needle-like.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: oral feedback on the statement "the grasshopper chews with its teeth"
Q9. A learner writes in her notebook, "The grasshopper chews with its teeth, like we do." What is the best correction?
  1. Grasshoppers chew only with the labium
  2. Grasshoppers have teeth in their stomachs only
  3. Grasshoppers have no mouthparts that can cut anything
  4. The toothed edge is part of the mandible, a jointed mouthpart, not a set of teeth like ours
Correct answer: D
The toothed edge belongs to the mandible, a jointed mouthpart. Grasshoppers do not have teeth like humans.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: Cumulative Model inset showing mandibles and MECHANICAL DIGESTION
Q10. In your cumulative model, you draw the mandibles with arrows showing sideways movement and an arrow to the ragged maize leaf. Which annotation best completes the entry?
  1. The leaf is dissolved by the mandibles into a liquid
  2. Solid plant tissue is broken into smaller pieces by physical force before swallowing
  3. The mandibles pump blood from the plant into the grasshopper
  4. The mandibles break food down using enzymes into simple nutrients
Correct answer: B
Mandibles break solid plant tissue into smaller pieces by physical force (mechanical digestion), the first step in obtaining nutrients.
