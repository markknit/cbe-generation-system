Q1. A flamingo at Lake Nakuru has a bent beak with comb-like plates along its inner edges. Based on this beak, what does it most likely eat?
  1. Dead animals that it tears with a hooked beak
  2. Large fish that it spears and tears apart
  3. Nectar from tubular flowers
  4. Microscopic algae and tiny animals strained from the water
Correct answer: D
A bent beak with comb-like plates (lamellae) is built for filtering. At Lake Nakuru flamingos feed mainly on microscopic algae, cyanobacteria and small animals in the water.
Use after: Observe phase — Clip 1: flamingos filter-feeding at Lake Nakuru (freeze-frame on the beak interior)
Q2. A flamingo feeds with its beak upside down in the water while its tongue pumps water through the beak. What is the main job of the comb-like lamellae along the beak edges?
  1. To suck up nectar from the water
  2. To store water for the flamingo to drink later
  3. To cut the food into small pieces
  4. To trap tiny food particles while the water flows out
Correct answer: D
The lamellae work like a sieve. Water is pumped out through the gaps and the tiny food particles are left trapped inside the beak.
Use after: Observe phase — Clip 2: sunbird probing a flower for nectar, and the sunbird row of the Observation Chart
Q3. A Variable Sunbird has a long, curved beak and a tubular tongue. What does it use this beak to do?
  1. Tear flesh from a dead animal
  2. Reach into flowers to probe for nectar
  3. Crush hard seeds and nuts
  4. Filter algae from alkaline lake water
Correct answer: B
The long, curved beak fits into the flower tube and the tubular tongue draws up nectar. This is probing, not filtering, tearing or crushing.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning writing on why a flamingo cannot eat nectar
Q4. Why can a flamingo not feed on nectar from a narrow, tubular flower like the red-hot poker plant?
  1. Its beak is hooked for tearing flesh, which damages flowers
  2. Nectar is poisonous to all water birds
  3. Its beak is built to filter small particles from water, not to reach into a narrow flower tube
  4. Flamingos are too heavy to stand near flowers
Correct answer: C
The flamingo's bent, lamellae-lined beak is a structural adaptation for filtering water. It does not have the long, curved shape and tubular tongue needed to reach nectar, so structure limits the diet.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) writing and group sharing
Q5. A learner writes a CER response about the flamingo. Which sentence is the REASONING part (the science that connects the evidence to the claim)?
  1. The flamingo's beak is adapted for filtering algae.
  2. In the video, the flamingo holds its beak upside down in the water.
  3. The comb-like plates work like a sieve: water passes out but food particles are trapped.
  4. Flamingos gather in large groups at Lake Nakuru.
Correct answer: C
Reasoning explains how the structure does the job (the sieve mechanism). The first option is a claim, and the last two are observations (evidence), not a mechanism.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: annotated diagram of the flamingo beak and the teacher follow-up prompt on 'Could a fish pass between the lamellae?'
Q6. A learner says, 'Flamingos at Lake Nakuru catch large fish by filtering them from the water.' Which response best corrects this idea?
  1. Wrong: the gaps between lamellae are tiny, so only very small food such as algae and small crustaceans is trapped.
  2. Correct: the lamellae are sharp and cut fish into small pieces.
  3. Correct: the tongue spears fish before they reach the lamellae.
  4. Wrong: flamingos eat only flowers and nectar.
Correct answer: A
The lamellae form a fine sieve, so only microscopic algae, cyanobacteria and small animals small enough to be trapped are caught. Large fish are not filter-feeding food.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative class model update: the 'pattern I see across ALL the feeding structures' sentence
Q7. Learners have studied the grasshopper's mandibles, the aphid's and mosquito's stylets, the beaks of the fish eagle, vulture and hornbill, the flamingo's filter beak and the sunbird's probing beak. Which pattern sentence best describes ALL these feeding structures?
  1. Animals with larger mouthparts eat more food.
  2. All feeding structures are used for sucking liquid food.
  3. All animals use beaks to get their food.
  4. Each feeding structure is shaped to suit the food the animal eats and how it gets it.
Correct answer: D
The model shows that structure matches function and diet across insects and birds. This is a generalisation, while the other options are wrong or apply to only some of the animals.
