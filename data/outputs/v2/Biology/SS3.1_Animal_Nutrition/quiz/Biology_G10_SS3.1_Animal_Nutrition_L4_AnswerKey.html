Q1. An African fish eagle at Lake Naivasha has a beak that is sharply hooked, with a pointed tip and a strong base. Which feeding action is this beak best suited for?
  1. Piercing skin to suck blood
  2. Chewing leaves into small pieces using side-to-side jaws
  3. Gripping a slippery fish and tearing its flesh into pieces
  4. Crushing hard fruits and seeds into a pulp
Correct answer: C
A hooked beak grips slippery prey and a sharp pointed tip tears flesh. Crushing is for blunt beaks, and piercing-sucking and chewing are insect mouthpart actions.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: resource sheet with four labelled beak diagrams and the observation table (hornbill row)
Q2. An African grey hornbill in Arabuko-Sokoke Forest has a large beak with a casque, a curved but blunter tip, and a wide gape. Which food and feeding action fit these features best?
  1. Picking up and tossing fruits and insects into its wide mouth
  2. Sucking plant sap through a thin tube
  3. Gripping live tilapia with a sharp hooked tip
  4. Tearing flesh from a carcass with sharp cutting edges
Correct answer: A
The blunter tip and wide gape suit picking up and tossing fruit and insects. The beak is not sharply hooked, so it is not suited to tearing flesh.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: shared class table, discussion of fish eagle and vulture beak differences
Q3. The fish eagle and the white-backed vulture both have hooked beaks. A learner says, "All hooked beaks are identical and do the same job." Which statement best shows why this is a misconception?
  1. The fish eagle's beak is hooked but the vulture's beak is straight and general-purpose
  2. The vulture's beak is designed for crushing hard fruits, while the fish eagle's is designed for tearing
  3. The two beaks differ only in colour, not in structure or function
  4. The vulture has a sharp-edged hooked bill and a long bare neck for reaching into carcasses, while the fish eagle has a pointed tip and strong base for gripping live slippery fish
Correct answer: D
Both beaks are hooked, but the vulture's cutting edges and long bare neck suit feeding on carcasses, while the eagle's pointed tip and strong base suit gripping live fish. The pied crow, not the vulture, has the straight general-purpose beak.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: beak-swap question (fish eagle given a hornbill beak and a hornbill given a fish eagle beak)
Q4. Imagine a hornbill was given a fish eagle's beak. What would most likely happen when it tried to feed on fruit?
  1. It would feed easily, because every beak can handle any food
  2. It would handle fruit easily, because a sharp hook is best for picking up fruit
  3. It would grow a new beak shape within a few days to suit the fruit
  4. It would struggle to pick up and toss fruit, because the eagle beak is adapted for gripping and tearing flesh, not for handling fruit
Correct answer: D
A beak is an adaptation matched to a particular food and feeding action. A hooked, tearing beak is a poor tool for picking up and tossing fruit, just as a grasshopper's mouthparts are wrong for drinking blood.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER statements and the meaning of the word adaptation
Q5. A learner writes: "The fish eagle changed its beak shape so that it could eat fish." Which response corrects this idea best?
  1. The learner is correct, because an animal's body changes to suit the food it eats
  2. Beaks are inherited and shaped over many generations, so a chick hatches with the beak it will use as an adult
  3. Beaks change shape each time a bird eats a different food
  4. A bird chooses its beak shape when it is about to hatch from the egg
Correct answer: B
An adaptation is a structure shaped over many generations and inherited. An individual bird does not change its beak to suit what it eats.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: extending the cumulative feeding-structures comparison table with CRUSHING AND TEARING BEAKS rows
Q6. A learner completes a table row for the fish eagle. Which pair of entries correctly separates the Food Accessed column from the Feeding Action column?
  1. Food accessed: hooked tip; Feeding action: tilapia
  2. Food accessed: gripping and tearing; Feeding action: tilapia
  3. Food accessed: crushing fruit; Feeding action: fish
  4. Food accessed: tilapia (fish); Feeding action: gripping and tearing flesh
Correct answer: D
Food accessed is what the animal eats, here tilapia or fish. Feeding action is the physical thing the beak does to get it, here gripping and tearing.
Use after: End of lesson — Model: comparison sentence linking the fish eagle beak and the mosquito stylet
Q7. Which statement correctly compares a fish eagle's beak with a mosquito's stylets?
  1. Both crush hard food between blunt surfaces
  2. The eagle's hooked beak grips and tears solid flesh, while the mosquito's stylets pierce skin to suck liquid blood
  3. Both pierce skin and suck liquid food
  4. The eagle's beak sucks sap from plants, while the mosquito's stylets tear flesh
Correct answer: B
In both animals the structure fits the food. The eagle's hooked, pointed beak tears solid flesh, while the mosquito's thin stylets pierce skin to draw up liquid blood.
