Q1. Wanjiru's blood glucose rose from 4.5 to 8.0 mmol/L after she ate ugali, which is mostly starch. Starch is a very large molecule. What must happen to the starch before it can raise her blood glucose?
  1. It must be broken down by enzymes into small soluble sugars
  2. It must be heated to 100 degrees C so that it dissolves
  3. It must be made wet by saliva so that it can slide into the blood
  4. It must pass into the blood as starch and then turn into glucose
Correct answer: A
Starch is large and insoluble, so digestive enzymes must break it down into small soluble sugars such as glucose before it can enter the blood. The distractors are the misconceptions that saliva only makes food wet and that starch turns directly into blood.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: saliva and starch investigation, tube B control
Q2. In the saliva and starch investigation, tube A has starch and saliva, and tube B has starch and boiled saliva (or water). Both tubes are kept at about 37 degrees C. Why is tube B included?
  1. To show that the colour changes in tube A are caused by something active in saliva, and not by warmth or time alone
  2. To produce more reducing sugar for the Benedict's test
  3. To show that boiled saliva is a stronger enzyme than fresh saliva
  4. To make sure the starch solution is heated to the correct temperature
Correct answer: A
The control changes only one thing, which is active versus boiled saliva. If tube B stays blue-black while tube A changes, the change must come from the active enzyme in saliva.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: iodine test at 0, 5 and 10 minutes
Q3. A drop from tube A (starch plus saliva at 37 degrees C) is tested with iodine. At 0 minutes it is blue-black. At 10 minutes it is yellow-brown. What does the change in colour show?
  1. The saliva has been used up completely
  2. Glucose has been formed, because iodine tests for glucose
  3. The starch has been broken down and is no longer present
  4. More starch has been made in the tube
Correct answer: C
Iodine gives a blue-black colour only when starch is present. A change to yellow-brown means the starch has gone. Iodine does not test for sugar, and the enzyme is not used up.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Benedict's test at 10 minutes
Q4. At 10 minutes, Benedict's solution is added to a sample from tube A (starch plus saliva) and heated in a hot water bath. It changes from blue to orange. What does this result show?
  1. Protein is present
  2. A reducing sugar is present
  3. Starch is still present
  4. The enzyme has been denatured
Correct answer: B
Benedict's solution turns green, yellow or orange when heated with a reducing sugar. This shows that saliva has digested starch into sugar. Starch alone gives no change with Benedict's.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: claim-evidence-reasoning for how saliva changed the starch, and the boiled saliva control
Q5. Starch is mixed with saliva that has been boiled first and kept at 37 degrees C. After 10 minutes the iodine test is still blue-black. What is the best explanation?
  1. Boiling denatured the enzyme in the saliva, so it could not digest the starch
  2. Boiling turned the starch into protein
  3. Boiling used up all the starch before the test
  4. Boiling made the saliva too wet to work
Correct answer: A
Salivary amylase is a protein enzyme. Boiling changes its shape (denatures it), so it can no longer work. The starch therefore stays unchanged and the iodine stays blue-black.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: why the water bath is at about 37 degrees C and why the enzyme did not work at 0 degrees C
Q6. Starch and fresh saliva are placed in a tube in ice at 0 degrees C. After 10 minutes the iodine test is still blue-black. What does this show about the enzyme?
  1. It is inactive at this low temperature
  2. It has been destroyed (denatured) by the cold
  3. It has been used up by the starch
  4. It works fastest at 0 degrees C
Correct answer: A
At 0 degrees C the enzyme is inactive, so the starch is not digested. Unlike boiling, cold does not denature it. Enzymes work best at about 37 degrees C in the body.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: reasoning that saliva contains amylase, a protein catalyst
Q7. Which statement correctly describes salivary amylase?
  1. It is a mineral salt that keeps the pH of the mouth steady
  2. It is a carbohydrate that is used up as it breaks down starch
  3. It is a protein that acts as a biological catalyst, speeding up the breakdown of starch
  4. It is a lipid that dissolves the starch
Correct answer: C
Enzymes are proteins that act as biological catalysts. A catalyst speeds up a reaction and is not used up. Amylase breaks starch down to maltose, which is then broken down to glucose.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: roles of water and mineral salts
Q8. In the digestion of starch by saliva, what is the main role of water?
  1. It is the medium in which the starch and enzyme react and which carries the digested sugars
  2. It is the enzyme that breaks down the starch
  3. It is the substance that turns blue-black with iodine
  4. It is the food chemical that gives the body energy
Correct answer: A
Water is an inorganic compound that acts as the medium in which reactions take place. The starch solution and the enzyme react in it, and it carries the digested sugars.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the class flow-diagram (food chemicals, digestion by enzymes, glucose in blood)
Q9. A class revises its flow-diagram model to explain Wanjiru's rising blood glucose after eating ugali. Which sequence fits the evidence from the saliva and starch investigation?
  1. Digestion by enzymes, then food chemicals (starch), then glucose in the blood
  2. Food chemicals (starch), then digestion by enzymes to sugars, then glucose in the blood
  3. Food chemicals (starch), then glucose in the blood, then digestion by enzymes
  4. Glucose in the blood, then digestion by enzymes, then food chemicals (starch)
Correct answer: B
The investigation shows that starch is digested by an enzyme into sugars. The sugars can then become glucose in the blood. Digestion therefore comes between the food chemicals and the glucose.
