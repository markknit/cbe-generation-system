Use after: Predict phase — Predict: Wanjiru's graph and predicting which foods contain starch and sugar
Q1. Before the tests, a learner predicts that only sweet-tasting foods contain carbohydrates. Which fact from the lesson shows that this idea is wrong?
  1. Ugali is not sweet but is rich in carbohydrate, mostly starch from maize
  2. Carbohydrates are made only of carbon and hydrogen
  3. Sukuma wiki is the food with the most carbohydrate
  4. Carbohydrates are found only in foods that turn Benedict's solution brick-red
Correct answer: A
Ugali contains about 70 g of carbohydrate, mostly starch, yet it does not taste sweet. So carbohydrates are not only sweet foods.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Test 1, iodine test for starch
Q2. A learner adds 2-3 drops of iodine solution to a small amount of maize flour mixed with water. What colour shows that starch is present?
  1. Blue-black
  2. Brick-red
  3. Colourless
  4. Green
Correct answer: A
Iodine turns blue-black when starch is present. Brick-red is a Benedict's result, not an iodine result.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Test 2, Benedict's test for reducing sugars
Q3. A learner heats 2 cm3 of glucose solution with 2 cm3 of Benedict's solution in a hot water bath for 3-5 minutes. The solution turns brick-red. What does this show?
  1. A large amount of reducing sugar is present
  2. No reducing sugar is present
  3. Protein is present
  4. Starch is present
Correct answer: A
Benedict's solution changes from blue through green, yellow and orange to brick-red as the amount of reducing sugar increases. Brick-red means a lot of reducing sugar.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Test 2, Benedict's test, safe handling of hot water bath and reagents
Q4. Which of the following is the safe way to carry out Benedict's test in the lesson?
  1. Wear goggles and heat the test tubes in a hot water bath
  2. Taste a little of each food solution to see if it is sweet
  3. Heat the test tubes in a flame, then add iodine
  4. Heat the test tubes directly in a flame without goggles
Correct answer: A
The teacher's safety rules were: goggles on, and heat only in the water bath, never in the flame. Tasting chemicals or food in the laboratory is unsafe.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Step 1, ugali is blue-black with iodine but blue with Benedict's
Q5. Ugali gives a blue-black colour with iodine but stays blue with Benedict's solution. What does this tell us about the carbohydrate in ugali before digestion?
  1. It contains a lot of reducing sugar
  2. It is glucose, which is a reducing sugar
  3. It is starch, which is not a reducing sugar
  4. It contains no carbohydrate
Correct answer: C
Blue-black with iodine shows starch. No change with Benedict's shows there is no reducing sugar, so the ugali has starch but not free glucose.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Step 2, paper-strip or bead model of starch and glucose
Q6. In the bead-chain model of starch, what does each bead stand for?
  1. One drop of Benedict's solution
  2. One whole starch molecule
  3. One glucose unit
  4. One molecule of iodine
Correct answer: C
Starch is a large carbohydrate made of many glucose units (monosaccharides) joined together. Each bead is one glucose unit and the whole chain is the starch.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Step 3, claim-evidence-reasoning (CER) statement
Q7. Wanjiru's blood glucose rose from 4.5 mmol/L to 8.0 mmol/L in the 60 minutes after eating ugali, sukuma wiki and beans. Ugali has no reducing sugar when tested. Which statement is the best reasoning to link ugali to this rise?
  1. Ugali already contains a lot of glucose, so it goes straight into the blood
  2. Ugali has no carbohydrate, so it cannot affect blood glucose
  3. Starch turns into iodine in the stomach, which raises blood glucose
  4. Starch is made of glucose units, so it must be broken down into glucose before it can enter the blood
Correct answer: D
The evidence shows starch is present but no reducing sugar. Since starch is made of glucose units, it must be broken down to glucose to enter the blood. The statement that ugali already contains glucose is the misconception the Benedict's result challenges.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Step 3, using the numbers from Wanjiru's graph
Q8. Wanjiru's blood glucose was 4.5 mmol/L before lunch and 8.0 mmol/L at its peak. By how many mmol/L did it rise?
  1. 8.0
  2. 3.5
  3. 4.5
  4. 12.5
Correct answer: B
Rise = peak minus starting value = 8.0 - 4.5 = 3.5 mmol/L.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: sorting sticky notes into answered, partly answered and still to investigate
Q9. After the tests, a learner writes this question on the Driving Question Board: "How does starch become glucose?" What should the class do with this question in this lesson?
  1. Answer it by saying that glucose is made of starch
  2. Keep it as a question still to investigate in a later lesson
  3. Remove it because it is not about food
  4. Move it to the 'Answered' column because the iodine test explains it
Correct answer: B
Today's tests show that ugali contains starch and that starch is made of glucose units, but they do not show how starch is broken down. The question stays unanswered and is linked to a later lesson.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the class flow-diagram model, arrow with question mark between food chemicals and glucose in blood
Q10. Which statement about the flow-diagram model (Food chemicals, then ?, then glucose in blood, then ?, then back to normal) is best supported by today's evidence?
  1. Starch from ugali must be broken down into glucose between food chemicals and glucose in the blood
  2. Ugali puts glucose directly into the blood with no change
  3. Glucose changes into starch between food chemicals and glucose in the blood
  4. Iodine turns starch into glucose before it enters the blood
Correct answer: A
The gap in the model between food chemicals and blood glucose is where starch must be broken into glucose. Today's evidence shows starch is made of glucose units and is not itself a reducing sugar.
