Use after: Predict phase — Predict: 'Where does the glucose go after 60 minutes?' prediction and pair-share
Q1. Wanjiru's blood glucose peaks at 8.0 mmol/L, 60 minutes after eating ugali, sukuma wiki and beans. It then falls back to 4.5 mmol/L by 180 minutes. Which statement best explains where the glucose goes after the peak?
  1. It is destroyed in the blood and disappears.
  2. It is taken up by body cells, the liver and muscles, and used for energy or stored.
  3. It is mostly lost in sweat and urine.
  4. It turns back into starch in the small intestine.
Correct answer: B
After absorption, glucose is taken up by cells and used in respiration, and the excess is stored as glycogen in the liver and muscles. It is not simply lost in urine or sweat, and it does not disappear.
Use after: Observe phase — Part 1: Data analysis, calculating the fall in each interval from the glucose table
Q2. Wanjiru's blood glucose was 8.0 mmol/L at 60 minutes and 6.5 mmol/L at 90 minutes. By how much did it fall between 60 and 90 minutes (in mmol/L)?
  1. 1.3
  2. 1.5
  3. 3.5
  4. 2.0
Correct answer: B
Fall = 8.0 - 6.5 = 1.5 mmol/L. The value 1.3 is the fall from 90 to 120 minutes, and 3.5 is the total fall.
Use after: Observe phase — Part 1: Data analysis, comparing the rate of rise with the rate of fall
Q3. Wanjiru's blood glucose rose by 3.5 mmol/L in the first 60 minutes. It then fell by 3.5 mmol/L between 60 and 180 minutes. How many times longer did the fall take than the rise?
  1. 3 times longer
  2. 2 times longer
  3. 4 times longer
  4. The same time
Correct answer: B
The rise took 60 minutes and the fall took 180 - 60 = 120 minutes. 120 / 60 = 2, so the fall is slower and takes twice as long.
Use after: Observe phase — Part 2: Role-play 'Journey of the glucose', step 3 and 4 (pancreas releases insulin)
Q4. In the 'Journey of the glucose' role-play, the pancreas holds up insulin cards when the blood glucose is high (at the 60-minute peak) and not at 0 minutes. What does insulin do?
  1. It is a food that gives the body energy.
  2. It carries glucose out of the body in urine.
  3. It causes cells, the liver and muscles to take up glucose from the blood.
  4. It digests starch into glucose in the small intestine.
Correct answer: C
The pancreas releases insulin when blood glucose is high. Insulin causes cells, the liver and muscles to take up glucose, so the blood level falls. Insulin is a hormone, not a food, and it does not digest starch.
Use after: Explain phase — Part 2 role-play step 5 and the Final Explanation: reasoning on respiration
Q5. Inside body cells, glucose is used in respiration to release energy. Which equation correctly shows this?
  1. Glucose + water gives starch + oxygen + energy
  2. Glucose + carbon dioxide gives oxygen + water + energy
  3. Glucose + oxygen gives carbon dioxide + water + energy
  4. Glucose + oxygen gives glycogen + water + energy
Correct answer: C
In respiration, glucose reacts with oxygen to produce carbon dioxide and water, and energy (ATP) is released. This is why cells need oxygen and why we breathe harder after running.
Use after: Explain phase — Part 2 role-play step 6 and Final Explanation (CER): glycogen storage
Q6. After a meal, extra glucose that the body does not need straight away is changed and stored. Where is it stored and in what form?
  1. As glucose in the kidneys
  2. As starch in the blood
  3. As insulin in the pancreas
  4. As glycogen in the liver and muscles
Correct answer: D
Excess glucose is stored as glycogen in the liver and muscles. This helps to bring the blood glucose back down to normal.
Use after: Explain phase — Final Explanation (CER): roles of proteins and lipids and why carbohydrates cause the spike
Q7. Wanjiru's meal had ugali (starch), beans (protein) and cooking oil (lipid). Proteins and lipids are also digested. Which statement correctly explains why carbohydrates are the main cause of the spike in blood glucose?
  1. Proteins and lipids are turned into glucose faster than starch is.
  2. Carbohydrates are the only chemicals the body can absorb.
  3. Proteins and lipids are not digested at all.
  4. Starch is digested into glucose, which is absorbed into the blood, while proteins and lipids are used mainly for growth, repair and energy storage.
Correct answer: D
Digestion of starch in ugali produces glucose, which enters the blood and raises its concentration. Proteins and lipids are also digested but are used mainly for growth, repair and energy storage.
Use after: Model Building phase — Part 1: Building the final flow-diagram model on chart paper
Q8. Which order correctly shows the stages in the final flow-diagram model that explains Wanjiru's graph?
  1. Food chemicals, digestion by enzymes, glucose in blood, cells (respiration) and storage, back to normal
  2. Food chemicals, glucose in blood, digestion by enzymes, cells and storage, back to normal
  3. Digestion by enzymes, food chemicals, glucose in blood, back to normal, cells and storage
  4. Food chemicals, digestion by enzymes, cells and storage, glucose in blood, back to normal
Correct answer: A
Food chemicals are digested by enzymes into glucose, which is absorbed into the blood. Cells then use it in respiration or it is stored, and the level returns to normal (4.5 mmol/L).
