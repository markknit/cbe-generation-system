Use after: Predict phase — Predict: the 'sweating leaf bag' observation and Think–Pair–Share prediction
Q1. Before school, a clear polythene bag was tied tightly around a leafy branch of a Ficus tree. By mid-morning, water droplets were seen on the inside of the bag. Where did this water mainly come from?
  1. Water that dripped from the soil up the outside of the branch
  2. Water vapour lost by the leaves, which condensed on the inside of the bag
  3. Water that came through tiny holes in the polythene bag
  4. Rain that condensed on the outside of the bag and leaked in
Correct answer: B
The leaves lose water vapour (transpiration), and it cools and condenses on the inside of the bag. The bag was sealed and kept off the soil, so rain or leaking cannot explain the water.
Use after: Observe phase — Observation A: dye experiment results (celery stalks from Lesson 5)
Q2. A celery stalk was left standing in red food dye for several hours. When a cross-section was cut, only small dots in the stalk were stained red. What does this show?
  1. The dye moved through the outer skin of the stalk, so the skin carries water
  2. Phloem carries water up the stem, so phloem is stained
  3. The dye moved through xylem tissue, so xylem carries water up the stem
  4. The dye moved through all the cells equally, so all cells carry water up the stem
Correct answer: C
Only the xylem was stained, so the dye travelled in the xylem. This confirms that xylem carries water from the roots up the stem to the leaves.
Use after: Observe phase — Observation B: stomatal impression practical (upper vs lower surface counts)
Q3. A learner makes stomatal impressions with clear nail varnish from the upper and lower surfaces of a sukuma wiki leaf and counts the stomata under a microscope. Which result is normally expected?
  1. More stomata on the lower surface than on the upper surface
  2. More stomata on the upper surface than on the lower surface
  3. Exactly the same number of stomata on both surfaces
  4. No stomata on either surface, because stomata are found only in the roots
Correct answer: A
Most leaves such as sukuma wiki have more stomata on the lower surface, which is usually shaded and loses less water. If the counts are equal or reversed, the learner may have applied varnish to the wrong side.
Use after: Observe phase — Observation C: leaf cross-section and waxy cuticle of the upper surface
Q4. The upper surface of a sukuma wiki leaf feels slightly waxy and shiny. What is the main job of this waxy cuticle?
  1. To let water vapour escape quickly from the leaf
  2. To absorb water from the air into the leaf
  3. To reduce water loss from the leaf surface
  4. To carry water from the roots into the leaf
Correct answer: C
The waxy cuticle is waterproof and reduces water loss through the epidermis. Most water vapour therefore leaves through the stomata, not through the cuticle.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Kibwezi group explanation (2:00 pm, 38°C, strong wind, very low humidity)
Q5. At 2:00 pm in Kibwezi it is 38°C, windy and very dry. A farmer watered her maize in the morning, yet the maize is wilting. Which explanation is correct?
  1. The soil has no water left, so the roots have nothing to absorb
  2. The wind blows water into the leaves, which makes them heavy and droop
  3. Water is lost through the stomata faster than the roots and xylem can replace it
  4. The plants are thirsty because the xylem has stopped carrying water
Correct answer: C
High temperature, strong wind and low humidity increase the rate of transpiration. When water is lost through the stomata faster than it is taken up, the leaves lose turgor and the plant wilts, even if the soil still has water.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: factors affecting transpiration table (sheltered sukuma wiki under mango tree vs open maize field)
Q6. A sukuma wiki plot shaded by a large mango tree barely wilts, while maize in the open field wilts badly. Which is the best explanation?
  1. The mango tree gives its water to the sukuma wiki through the roots
  2. Sukuma wiki has no stomata, so it cannot lose water
  3. Shade makes the air drier, so the sukuma wiki loses less water
  4. Shade lowers light intensity and temperature, so the sukuma wiki loses water more slowly
Correct answer: D
Shade reduces light and temperature around the leaves, which lowers the rate of transpiration. Sukuma wiki does have stomata, and drier air would increase water loss, not reduce it.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: board timeline Soil → Root → Stem → Leaf → ATMOSPHERE
Q7. Which order correctly shows the journey of water from the soil to the atmosphere through a maize plant?
  1. Soil → root hair → phloem → leaf mesophyll → cuticle → atmosphere
  2. Soil → root hair → xylem → leaf mesophyll → stomata → atmosphere
  3. Soil → leaf mesophyll → xylem → root hair → stomata → atmosphere
  4. Soil → stem xylem → root hair → stomata → leaf mesophyll → atmosphere
Correct answer: B
Water enters the root hairs by osmosis, travels up the xylem to the leaf, moves into the mesophyll cells and leaves as water vapour through the stomata into the atmosphere.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the 'water vapour exits as transpiration' arrow to the cumulative transport model
Q8. A learner adds an arrow to her plant model to show transpiration. Which arrow and label is correct?
  1. From the waxy cuticle outward, labelled 'most water vapour exits'
  2. From the xylem to the roots, labelled 'water vapour exits'
  3. From the soil into the root hairs, labelled 'transpiration'
  4. From the stomata outward, labelled 'water vapour exits'
Correct answer: D
Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from a plant, mainly through the stomata of the leaves. The arrow must therefore leave through the stomata, not the cuticle, and not at the roots.
