Use after: Predict phase — Predict: prompt card story, writing two things you think you already know and one question
Q1. A farmer in Kibwezi waters her maize early in the morning. By midday the leaves are drooping. Amina writes on her prompt card: "Plants only wilt when the soil is dry." Which fact from the story is hard to fit with Amina's idea?
  1. Maize near the dry seasonal riverbed wilts sooner
  2. The maize wilted at midday even though the soil was watered that morning
  3. Some plants stand upright again by evening
  4. The sukuma wiki under the mango tree looks healthy all day
Correct answer: B
Amina's idea says wilting happens only when the soil is dry, but the maize wilted although the farmer had watered that morning. This is the tension the lesson wants students to notice. It is not yet explained.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: See-Think-Wonder observation sheet, column "What I See" for photograph A
Q2. Students look at a photograph of maize in a dry Kibwezi field at midday. They must write only what they can SEE, not an explanation. Which sentence is an observation?
  1. The maize is wilting because its roots cannot take in water
  2. The maize leaves are curling inward and the soil around the plants looks damp
  3. The maize will die because the farmer did not water enough
  4. The maize is wilting because the midday sun has dried the plant out
Correct answer: B
Curled leaves and damp-looking soil can be seen directly in the photograph. The other choices are explanations or predictions, which belong in the "What It Makes Me Think" column.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: photograph B, maize near the dry seasonal riverbed compared with maize on the lower, wetter slope
Q3. In the Kibwezi scenario, a farmer compares maize growing near a dry seasonal riverbed with maize on a lower, wetter slope. Which observation matches what was shown?
  1. Both groups of maize wilted at the same time
  2. Neither group of maize wilted, but the sukuma wiki did
  3. Maize on the lower, wetter slope wilted sooner than maize near the riverbed
  4. Maize near the dry riverbed wilted sooner and more severely than maize on the lower, wetter slope
Correct answer: D
Photograph B showed maize near the dry seasonal riverbed wilting more severely than maize on the lower, wetter slope. This is an observation only and does not yet explain why.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: group diagrams, gallery walk and individual first-draft explanation "I think the maize wilts because..."
Q4. At this point in the unit, a student is asked: "Why might a maize plant wilt even when the soil around it has water?" Which response best fits where the class is after the Kibwezi photographs?
  1. It is because maize plants cannot take in any water from the soil
  2. It has not been explained yet; we have first ideas and will investigate and test them in the coming lessons
  3. It is because the soil water is used up by midday, so the farmer must water again
  4. It is because the farmer did not water enough, so the question is already answered
Correct answer: B
This is the anchor lesson, so the phenomenon is left unexplained. Students record first ideas in their diagrams and notebooks and revisit them as the unit develops. The photographs show damp soil, so the other choices are not supported.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: sticky notes posted in the columns What We Think We Know and What We Wonder
Q5. The class is filling the Driving Question Board with two columns: "What We Think We Know" and "What We Wonder". Which sticky note belongs in the "What We Wonder" column?
  1. Sukuma wiki under a mango tree stays healthy
  2. How does water get to the very top of a tall tree?
  3. Plants need water to stay alive
  4. Maize leaves droop in the afternoon in Kibwezi
Correct answer: B
A genuine question about something we want to find out belongs under "What We Wonder". The other three are statements the students think they already know or have seen.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: starting the cumulative whole-plant model in the science notebook, marking unsure parts with a question mark
Q6. Students draw a first model of a maize plant (roots, stem, leaves). They put a question mark next to any part they are unsure about. Why?
  1. Because only students who already know everything are allowed to label parts
  2. To show honestly what they are unsure about, so the model can be improved as they investigate in later lessons
  3. So the teacher can mark the unsure parts wrong and take away marks
  4. So they can rub those parts out and leave them blank for ever
Correct answer: B
Question marks make uncertainty visible and treat it as the starting point of investigation. The baseline model is not graded and will be updated at the end of every lesson.
