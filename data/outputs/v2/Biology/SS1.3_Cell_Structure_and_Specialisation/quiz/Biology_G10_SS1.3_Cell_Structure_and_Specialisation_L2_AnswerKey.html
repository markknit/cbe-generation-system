Use after: Observe phase — Part A: The Ugali Kitchen Analogy
Q1. In the ugali kitchen analogy, the kitchen door is compared to the cell membrane. Which statement shows where this analogy does NOT match a real cell?
  1. A kitchen door is found at the edge of the kitchen, but the cell membrane is found at the edge of the cell.
  2. A kitchen door lets anything through when it is open, but the cell membrane chooses what passes in and out all the time.
  3. A kitchen door can be opened and closed, but the cell membrane can also let some things pass.
  4. A kitchen door separates the inside from the outside, but the cell membrane does not separate anything.
Correct answer: B
A kitchen door is open or shut and lets anything through when open, while the cell membrane is selective all the time. This is one of the places where the analogy breaks down.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: Prokaryote vs. Eukaryote Sort
Q2. Which feature belongs to a prokaryotic cell such as E. coli (a bacterium)?
  1. It has a nucleus surrounded by a membrane.
  2. It has mitochondria.
  3. Its DNA floats freely in the cell and is not inside a membrane-bound nucleus.
  4. It is found only in plants.
Correct answer: C
Prokaryotes have no membrane-bound nucleus, so their DNA floats freely in the cell. Mitochondria and a membrane-bound nucleus are features of eukaryotic cells.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Guided Research & Diagram Study (Cell Theory Evidence Table)
Q3. Which of these is one of the three tenets (main ideas) of cell theory?
  1. All living things are made of one or more cells.
  2. All cells in one organism look the same.
  3. New cells can form from non-living matter.
  4. Every cell has a nucleus.
Correct answer: A
Cell theory states that all living things are made of one or more cells, that the cell is the basic unit of structure and function, and that all cells come from pre-existing cells. Bacteria have no nucleus, and cells arise only from other cells.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: mini-lecture correcting card-sort errors and colour-coding the ARES diagram cards
Q4. A student says, "Ribosomes are found only in eukaryotic cells, so bacteria cannot make proteins." Which response is correct?
  1. The student is wrong because ribosomes are found only in plant cells and bacteria.
  2. The student is correct because ribosomes are found only in animal cells.
  3. The student is correct because bacteria have no organelles at all.
  4. The student is wrong because ribosomes are present in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, so bacteria can make proteins.
Correct answer: D
Ribosomes are present in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes, so even the simplest bacteria can make proteins. This was the most commonly misplaced card in the sort.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER paragraph on how Amina's body produced new skin cells
Q5. A learner cuts a finger and new skin grows in from the edges of the cut over a few days. Which explanation uses cell theory correctly?
  1. The new skin cells came from existing skin cells at the edge of the cut, because all cells come from pre-existing cells.
  2. The new skin cells appeared from non-living material in the scab, because cells can form on their own.
  3. The new skin cells came from platelets, because platelets are the basic unit of all skin.
  4. The new skin cells formed from the blood clot, because the clot turns into skin.
Correct answer: A
Virchow's tenet says all cells come from pre-existing cells. So new skin cells must have been made when existing skin cells divided.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: Version 1 of the Cumulative Cell Model (sketch and labels)
Q6. Otieno draws a model of an animal cell for his Version 1 notebook page and labels a cell wall on it. What should his teacher tell him?
  1. Remove it, because no cells have a cell wall.
  2. Keep it, because only animal cells have a cell wall.
  3. Remove it, because animal cells do not have a cell wall, although plant cells and bacteria do.
  4. Keep it, because all cells have a cell wall.
Correct answer: C
Animal cells lack a cell wall. Plant cells (cellulose) and bacteria (peptidoglycan) have one, so the idea that only plants have cell walls is also a misconception.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: answering 'What do ALL cells have in common?'
Q7. Bacteria have no nucleus, yet they are still cells. Which of these do ALL cells, including bacteria, have?
  1. Mitochondria
  2. DNA (genetic material) inside a cell membrane
  3. A nucleus with a membrane around it
  4. Chloroplasts
Correct answer: B
Every cell has a cell membrane and DNA. A membrane-bound nucleus, mitochondria and chloroplasts are not found in all cells, because prokaryotes lack them.
