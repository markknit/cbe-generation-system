Use after: Predict phase — Predict: teacher narrates Amina's cut and learners write in their science notebooks
Q1. Amina cuts her finger while slicing onions in the kitchen in Eastleigh. She presses the cut with her thumb. After about a minute, what does she notice?
  1. The bleeding is slowing and a dark, sticky material is starting to form
  2. A scab has formed and is beginning to fall off
  3. The cut has closed and there is no sign of any injury
  4. The bleeding has completely stopped and new pink skin has already grown
Correct answer: A
In the narrated scenario, after about a minute the bleeding slows and a dark, sticky material begins to form. The scab and new skin only appear over the following days.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: four-image photo sequence of the healing finger and Think-Pair-Share
Q2. A four-image sequence shows Amina's cut finger healing. Which order shows what learners observed, from first to last?
  1. Scab on healing skin, fresh cut with blood, new pink skin, dark clot forming
  2. Fresh cut with blood, dark clot forming, scab on healing skin after two days, new pink skin with the scab gone
  3. Fresh cut with blood, new pink skin, dark clot forming, scab on healing skin
  4. Dark clot forming, fresh cut with blood, new pink skin, scab on healing skin
Correct answer: B
The sequence runs: (1) fresh cut with blood, (2) dark clot forming, (3) scab after two days, (4) new pink skin with the scab gone.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: whole-class discussion on 'one thing or many things' doing the healing job
Q3. A learner says, "The blood goes hard, white cells come, and skin grows back." Which statement best describes what the healing finger shows about the body's response to injury?
  1. Each job happens one at a time, and the next job cannot start until the first has finished completely
  2. Only one single process happens, and it does every job in the wound
  3. The body only stops the bleeding and does nothing else until the skin is healed
  4. The body does several different things at the same time, such as stopping bleeding, fighting germs and rebuilding skin
Correct answer: D
The lesson shows that healing is not one process but many going on together, such as stopping bleeding, fighting infection and rebuilding skin. Learners begin to see that different responses may involve different types of cells.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher's bridging statement and 'Does that surprise you? Why?' follow-up
Q4. At the end of the discussion, the teacher says that dozens of different tiny structures work together in a healing wound, and that each looks different from the others. A learner asks, "So why do they all look so different from each other?" What is the best response at this point in the unit?
  1. They look different because each one eats different food
  2. They look different because each one has different DNA from the others
  3. This question has not been answered yet; it is the big question we will investigate in the coming lessons
  4. They look different because each one is a different age
Correct answer: C
This is the launch lesson and the phenomenon is deliberately left unexplained. The 'why' is the question the class will investigate across the unit, so learners should not settle on a ready-made answer.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: yellow 'What do we notice?' and blue 'What do we wonder?' sticky notes
Q5. On the Driving Question Board, learners write one 'What do we notice?' note and one 'What do we wonder?' note. Which of these is a good 'What do we wonder?' note?
  1. The finger looks almost the same as before after a few days
  2. How does the body know exactly what shape of skin to rebuild?
  3. A dark clot formed on the cut
  4. The bleeding slowed down after about a minute
Correct answer: B
A 'wonder' note is a genuine question. The other three are statements of things that can be seen, so they belong in the 'notice' column.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: teacher reads the driving question aloud from the top of the poster
Q6. Which question is the driving question introduced from the top of the Driving Question Board for this unit on cells?
  1. How do plants make their own food using sunlight?
  2. How many cells are there in a human finger?
  3. Why does the human body feel pain when it is cut?
  4. Why do cells in our body look so different from each other, and how does the shape of a cell determine the job it can do?
Correct answer: D
The driving question links the puzzle of the healing cut to cell shape and job. It will be answered piece by piece across the unit.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: Version 0 'Draw what you think a cell looks like'
Q7. In Version 0 of the cell model, learners draw what they think a cell looks like, without any diagrams or notes. Why do they draw it this way?
  1. To record what they think now, so they can compare it with their final model in Lesson 12
  2. To copy the correct cell diagram so that they remember it for the exam
  3. To test who can draw the most accurate cell in the class
  4. To mark the drawings as right or wrong before the unit begins
Correct answer: A
Version 0 is a baseline of learners' current ideas. It is not a test, and it will be compared with the final model at the end of the unit to show how their thinking has changed.
