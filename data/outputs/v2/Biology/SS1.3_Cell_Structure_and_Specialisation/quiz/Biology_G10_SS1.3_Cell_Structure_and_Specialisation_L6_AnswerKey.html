Q1. A Grade 10 learner looks at onion epidermal cells under a compound light microscope at high power (×400). Which of these can the learner see as a clearly separate structure?
  1. The cell membrane as a separate layer inside the cell wall
  2. The cell wall around each cell
  3. Chloroplasts packed in the cytoplasm
  4. Ribosomes inside the cytoplasm
Correct answer: B
The cell wall is thick enough to see as the outer boundary of each cell. The cell membrane is too thin to see separately from the wall with a light microscope, and onion skin has no chloroplasts.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: teacher demonstration of slide preparation
Q2. During the teacher demonstration, the coverslip is lowered onto the onion skin at an angle, like closing a book slowly. Why is this done?
  1. To stretch the onion skin so that it becomes thinner
  2. To avoid trapping air bubbles over the tissue
  3. To make the iodine stain spread faster
  4. To kill the cells so they stop moving
Correct answer: B
Lowering the coverslip slowly at an angle pushes air out and so avoids air bubbles, which would spoil the view of the cells.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: teacher demonstration, Step 5 (placing the slide and focusing)
Q3. A learner has just put a prepared onion slide on the microscope stage. Which is the best way to begin focusing?
  1. Remove the coverslip so that the light can reach the cells
  2. Start at low power and use the coarse focus wheel first, then fine focus
  3. Start at high power (×400) because it shows the cells biggest
  4. Use the fine focus only, at any power
Correct answer: B
Low power gives a wider field of view, so the cells are easier to find. Focus with the coarse wheel first, then the fine focus, before moving to a higher power.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: teacher demonstration, Step 3 (adding the iodine stain)
Q4. One drop of iodine solution is added to the onion epidermis on the slide. What is the main reason for adding it?
  1. It makes the cells grow larger so they are easier to see
  2. It adds chloroplasts to the cells
  3. It keeps the onion skin fresh for several days
  4. It makes the nucleus and other structures easier to see
Correct answer: D
Iodine stains parts of the cell, such as the nucleus, so that they show up clearly under the microscope. It does not make cells grow or add structures.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: cell size estimate (field diameter ÷ number of cells)
Q5. A learner measures the diameter of the field of view at low power as 4 mm. Along the long side of the cells, 16 onion cells fit across the field. What is the estimated length of one cell?
  1. 0.25 micrometres
  2. 25 micrometres
  3. 64 mm
  4. 250 micrometres
Correct answer: D
Length of one cell = field diameter ÷ number of cells = 4 mm ÷ 16 = 0.25 mm. Since 1 mm = 1000 micrometres, this is 250 micrometres.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-Evidence-Reasoning: 'Why are there no chloroplasts in onion epidermal cells?'
Q6. Learners looked at onion epidermal cells under the microscope and found no chloroplasts. Which explanation is best supported by this evidence and by where onions grow?
  1. Onion cells are animal cells, and animal cells never have chloroplasts
  2. The onion bulb grows underground where there is no light, so its cells do not need chloroplasts to capture light
  3. The iodine stain destroyed the chloroplasts
  4. The microscope was not strong enough to show chloroplasts
Correct answer: B
Chloroplasts capture light for photosynthesis. The onion bulb is an underground storage organ that gets no light, so its epidermal cells do not make chloroplasts.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class discussion linking the absence of chloroplasts to cell specialisation
Q7. Leaf cells of a plant have chloroplasts, but onion epidermal cells from the same kind of plant do not. What does this show about cells?
  1. The onion is not really a plant
  2. All plant cells must have the same structures and the same job
  3. Onion cells have lost all their structures
  4. Cells in different parts of one organism can be different because each is specialised for a different job
Correct answer: D
Cells in different parts of the same organism have different structures that suit their jobs. This is cell specialisation: leaf cells carry out photosynthesis, while onion epidermal cells are specialised for protection and storage.
Use after: Model Building phase — Revising the Version 3 plant cell model, revision prompt 3 (chloroplast annotation)
Q8. A learner is updating a plant cell model after looking at onion skin cells. Which annotation about chloroplasts is correct?
  1. Chloroplasts are found only in cells exposed to light, such as leaf cells, and not in onion epidermis or human cells
  2. Chloroplasts are found in every plant cell, including onion epidermis
  3. Chloroplasts are found in all living cells, including human cells
  4. Chloroplasts are found only in cells that grow in the dark
Correct answer: A
Chloroplasts are present in cells exposed to light that photosynthesise, such as leaf cells. They are absent from onion epidermal cells, which grow underground, and from animal cells.
