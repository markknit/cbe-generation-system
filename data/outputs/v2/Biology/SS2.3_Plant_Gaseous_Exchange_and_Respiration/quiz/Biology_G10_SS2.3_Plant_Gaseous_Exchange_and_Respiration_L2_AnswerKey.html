Q1. Mwalimu Njoroge's class submerged fresh sukuma wiki leaves in warm water and saw bubbles streaming from specific spots on the leaf surface. Which structure are these spots?
  1. Stomata, small pores in the leaf epidermis
  2. Pneumatophores, upward-growing roots
  3. The waxy cuticle, which covers the whole leaf
  4. Lenticels, raised spots on the leaf
Correct answer: A
Gas leaves a leaf through stomata, which are small pores mostly in the lower epidermis. Lenticels are on woody stems, pneumatophores are roots, and the cuticle is a continuous waxy layer that limits gas exchange.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Part A: READ-MARK-NOTE guided reading on sites of gaseous exchange
Q2. Which pair correctly matches a plant structure with where it is found?
  1. Pneumatophores: the stomatal pores of a leaf
  2. Lenticels: the lower epidermis of a leaf
  3. Stomata: the bark of woody stems
  4. Lenticels: woody stems, such as the bark of a mango tree
Correct answer: D
Lenticels are small raised pores in the bark of woody stems. Stomata are in leaf epidermis, and pneumatophores are the breathing roots of mangroves.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Part B: animation paused at pneumatophores (mangroves at Mida Creek)
Q3. Mangrove trees at Mida Creek, Kilifi, grow roots that stick upward out of the muddy water. Why is this an advantage for the tree?
  1. The roots can take in air above the water line, because the mud has very little oxygen
  2. The roots can stop water from entering the tree
  3. The roots can store carbon dioxide for the leaves
  4. The roots can make food by photosynthesis in the mud
Correct answer: A
Pneumatophores rise above the water and mud so that gases can enter and leave the roots. Waterlogged mud holds very little air.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Part B: animation paused at stomata, watching the guard cells open and close
Q4. What do guard cells do in a leaf?
  1. They open and close the stomata to control gas movement
  2. They store air in the spongy mesophyll
  3. They make the waxy cuticle on the leaf surface
  4. They carry water from the roots to the leaf
Correct answer: A
Guard cells surround each stomatal pore and change shape to open or close it, controlling gas exchange and water loss.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: group labelling of the leaf cross-section with label cards and one-sentence functional descriptions
Q5. Learners labelling a leaf cross-section placed 'stomata' on the lower epidermis, with a guard cell on each side of the pore. Which description of the stomata is the best?
  1. Air spaces between the palisade cells
  2. Solid cells that stop all gases entering the leaf
  3. Openings controlled by guard cells that let CO₂ in and O₂ out
  4. Waxy layers that stop water entering the leaf
Correct answer: C
Stomata are pores whose opening is controlled by guard cells. They allow gases to move in and out, so they are not just 'holes' and not a barrier.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Connect-Back question 3 on the waxy cuticle
Q6. Why is the waxy cuticle an advantage for a sukuma wiki plant growing under the hot Nairobi sun?
  1. It reduces water loss from the leaf surface
  2. It opens the stomata wider in hot weather
  3. It lets more carbon dioxide pass through the leaf surface
  4. It absorbs water from the air into the leaf
Correct answer: A
The cuticle is waxy and waterproof, so it limits water loss, which is vital in warm climates. It also limits gas exchange through the leaf surface, so gases mainly use the stomata.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: group 'We now know...' statements and checking for misconceptions
Q7. After today's lesson, a student claims: 'Plants only breathe through their roots.' Which statement best corrects this?
  1. Plants exchange gases only through the waxy cuticle
  2. Plants exchange gases only through stomata on the stem
  3. Plants do not exchange gases with their environment
  4. Plants exchange gases through several sites: stomata in leaves, lenticels on woody stems and pneumatophores in some roots
Correct answer: D
Plants exchange gases through several structures: stomata (leaves), lenticels (woody stems) and pneumatophores (mangrove roots). Roots are not the only site.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: building the 3D leaf cross-section model with cardboard, cellophane and clay
Q8. A group is building a leaf cross-section model. The pores they punched should be on the lower epidermis, each with a clay guard cell on either side. Where should they also show space for gases to move after entering through the pores?
  1. Air spaces in the spongy mesophyll
  2. Inside the waxy cuticle
  3. Outside the leaf, below the lower epidermis
  4. Inside the guard cells only
Correct answer: A
After gases enter through stomata they move through the air spaces in the spongy mesophyll to reach the cells. A complete model must show these spaces.
