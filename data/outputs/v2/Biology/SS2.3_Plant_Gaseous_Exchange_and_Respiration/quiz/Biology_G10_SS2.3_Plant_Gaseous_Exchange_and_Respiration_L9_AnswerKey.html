Q1. A mandazi vendor in Nairobi leaves her dough in a covered bowl. After some time the dough rises and is full of tiny gas bubbles. What is the best explanation for the gas in the dough?
  1. The flour itself slowly releases oxygen gas into the dough.
  2. Yeast cells in the dough break down sugar without much oxygen and release carbon dioxide.
  3. The dough is breathing in oxygen from the air in exactly the same way a kale leaf does.
  4. Warm air from the kitchen is pushed into the dough as it rests.
Correct answer: B
Yeast in the dough respires anaerobically (fermentation), breaking down sugar and releasing CO₂, which makes the dough rise. The dough does not simply breathe like a leaf, and flour does not release oxygen.
Use after: Observe phase — Gallery Walk, Station A: Yeast Fermentation (balloon over the bottle neck)
Q2. A bottle contains sugar solution and baker's yeast, with a balloon stretched over the neck. After some time the balloon inflates. Which gas has mainly inflated the balloon, and which process made it?
  1. Hydrogen, made when sugar dissolves in water
  2. Oxygen, made by photosynthesis in the yeast
  3. Water vapour, made by aerobic respiration in the sugar
  4. Carbon dioxide, made by anaerobic respiration (fermentation) in the yeast
Correct answer: D
Yeast ferments sugar without oxygen to make ethanol and carbon dioxide. The CO₂ gas is what inflates the balloon.
Use after: Observe phase — Gallery Walk, Station B: Biogas Simulation (sealed bottle of kitchen scraps with tube to inverted water-filled bottle)
Q3. At Station B, vegetable scraps such as sukuma wiki offcuts and ugali remnants are placed in a sealed plastic bottle, and gas is collected from it. Why does the bottle need to be sealed for the biogas simulation?
  1. Sealing stops the vegetable scraps from ever producing gas on their own.
  2. Sealing makes the microbes use more oxygen, so more gas is produced.
  3. Sealing is only needed to keep flies away; it does not change the process.
  4. Sealing keeps oxygen out, so microbes break down the waste by anaerobic respiration and release gas.
Correct answer: D
Biogas is produced by microbes respiring anaerobically. A sealed bottle keeps oxygen out, which is the condition needed for this process, as in farm biogas digesters.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class explanation using the 'Two Pathways' diagram
Q4. Which statement correctly describes anaerobic respiration in yeast?
  1. Glucose is broken down to lactic acid and carbon dioxide, with a lot of energy released.
  2. Glucose is broken down to carbon dioxide and water, with a lot of energy released.
  3. Glucose is broken down using oxygen to make ethanol and water.
  4. Glucose is broken down without oxygen to ethanol and carbon dioxide, with a little energy released.
Correct answer: D
In yeast, anaerobic respiration (fermentation) gives ethanol and CO₂ plus a small amount of ATP. Lactic acid is made in muscle cells and some bacteria, and CO₂ + water + lots of ATP describes aerobic respiration.
Use after: Explain phase — Connection Sentence: 'Anaerobic respiration is similar to what we observed in sukuma wiki leaves because ___, but it is different because ___'
Q5. A student writes this Connection Sentence: 'Anaerobic respiration is similar to aerobic respiration in plants because both ______, but different because ______.' Which pair of phrases completes it correctly?
  1. both happen only in plant leaves / anaerobic respiration happens only in animals
  2. both always produce carbon dioxide and ethanol / anaerobic respiration makes water
  3. both start with glucose and release energy / anaerobic respiration does not use oxygen and releases less energy
  4. both need oxygen to work / anaerobic respiration makes more ATP
Correct answer: C
Both pathways break down glucose to release energy. Anaerobic respiration happens without oxygen and releases less ATP, with different end-products such as ethanol or lactic acid.
Use after: Model Building phase — Project Planning Template: independent and dependent variables
Q6. A group plans to test how the temperature of the sugar solution affects fermentation. They set up three bottles, each with sugar, yeast and water, at 20 °C, 30 °C and 40 °C. Each bottle has a balloon on top, and they measure the balloon diameter after 20 minutes. What is the dependent variable?
  1. The type of bottle used
  2. The temperature of the sugar solution
  3. The balloon diameter after 20 minutes
  4. The mass of yeast added to each bottle
Correct answer: C
The dependent variable is what is measured as the result of the change. Here the balloon diameter shows how much CO₂ was produced. Temperature is the independent variable, the thing changed.
Use after: Model Building phase — Project Planning Template: at least 3 controlled variables
Q7. In the same investigation (sugar, yeast and water in bottles at 20 °C, 30 °C and 40 °C, each with a balloon), which of these must be kept the same in all three bottles as a controlled variable?
  1. The temperature of the solution
  2. The amount of carbon dioxide produced
  3. The mass of yeast and the volume of water
  4. The balloon diameter after 20 minutes
Correct answer: C
Controlled variables are kept constant so that only temperature changes. Mass of yeast and volume of water must be equal. Temperature is the variable being changed, and balloon size and CO₂ are the results being measured.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: connecting the anaerobic investigation back to the sukuma wiki bubbles from Lesson 1
Q8. Kale leaf cells normally respire using oxygen. Suppose a leaf's cells were kept without any oxygen. Based on this lesson, what would you expect?
  1. The cells could only release a little energy from glucose, and their end-products would be different.
  2. The cells would stop needing glucose because respiration is no longer needed.
  3. The cells would respire faster and release more energy than before.
  4. The cells would make oxygen from the glucose to continue aerobic respiration.
Correct answer: A
Without oxygen, glucose can only be broken down by anaerobic respiration. This releases much less energy and gives different end-products, such as ethanol and CO₂ in yeast.
