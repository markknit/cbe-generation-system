Q1. A learner draws arrows to show how photosynthesis and cellular respiration depend on each other in a sukuma wiki plant. Which arrow is correct?
  1. CO₂ released by respiration is used as a raw material for photosynthesis
  2. Photosynthesis uses up O₂ and releases CO₂ for respiration
  3. Glucose moves out of the leaf through the stomata to be used in respiration
  4. Respiration produces O₂ which photosynthesis then uses to make ATP
Correct answer: A
Respiration releases CO₂, which can be used in photosynthesis. Glucose is not released through stomata. Photosynthesis releases O₂ and respiration uses it, not the other way round.
Use after: Observe phase — Evidence Card Sorting: placing cards in the OVERLAP zone and justifying with molecular flow
Q2. During evidence card sorting, a group places the card 'C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP' in the OVERLAP zone. Which reason best explains why this card connects the processes?
  1. ATP is the gas that moves in and out of the stomata
  2. Glucose is made in respiration and then used up by photosynthesis
  3. The CO₂ and O₂ shown here are exchanged with photosynthesis, which releases the O₂ and uses the CO₂
  4. Respiration only happens when photosynthesis is also happening
Correct answer: C
Respiration uses O₂ (made by photosynthesis) and releases CO₂ (used by photosynthesis), so the same molecules link the two processes. Glucose is a product of photosynthesis and a reactant in respiration. ATP is not a gas.
Use after: Explain phase — Colour-Coded Systems Diagram: annotation of what happens at night versus during the day
Q3. At night, a sukuma wiki plant in the school garden has its stomata closed and there is no light. Which statement about this plant is correct?
  1. Respiration stops because the plant does not need energy at night
  2. Photosynthesis continues using the O₂ made by respiration
  3. Photosynthesis stops but respiration continues, and less CO₂ enters because the stomata are closed
  4. Both photosynthesis and respiration continue, but at a slower rate
Correct answer: C
Photosynthesis needs light, so it stops at night. Respiration happens all the time in living cells, and closed stomata reduce the entry of CO₂.
Use after: Explain phase — Colour-Coded Systems Diagram: chloroplast and mitochondrion drawn inside a cell
Q4. In a sukuma wiki leaf cell, where do photosynthesis and aerobic respiration take place?
  1. Both processes in the stomata
  2. Both processes in the chloroplast
  3. Photosynthesis in the chloroplast and respiration in the mitochondrion
  4. Photosynthesis in the mitochondrion and respiration in the chloroplast
Correct answer: C
The chloroplast is the site of photosynthesis and the mitochondrion is the site of aerobic respiration. Stomata are pores for gas movement and are not sites of either process.
Use after: DQB phase — Dual Sticky-Note DQB Protocol: green 'Now I can answer…' notes
Q5. A learner writes a green sticky note answering the question 'Why is gaseous exchange important to plants?' Which note gives the best answer?
  1. Plants must breathe in and out with lungs like animals do
  2. Gaseous exchange is only needed in the day because plants respire only in the light
  3. CO₂ must get in for photosynthesis and O₂ must get in for respiration, through stomata, lenticels and the cuticle
  4. Gaseous exchange lets glucose escape from the leaf so it does not build up
Correct answer: C
Plants need CO₂ for photosynthesis and O₂ for respiration, and gas enters through structures such as stomata and lenticels. Plants do not have lungs, glucose is not lost through stomata, and respiration goes on day and night.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Narrative: Sentence 3 explaining why warm water produced more bubbles and what was not tested
Q6. Mwalimu Njoroge's class saw more bubbles from sukuma wiki leaves in warm water than in cold water. The gas in the bubbles was never tested. Which statement is the most scientifically honest conclusion?
  1. The bubbles are definitely oxygen from photosynthesis, so warm water must make photosynthesis stop
  2. Warm water has no effect, because bubbles come only from the stomata
  3. Warmth may speed up enzyme-controlled reactions and make gas in the leaf expand and dissolve less, but the gas in the bubbles was not tested
  4. The bubbles are CO₂ because plants only respire and do not photosynthesise
Correct answer: C
Higher temperature can raise reaction rates and make gas expand and dissolve less, giving more bubbles. The identity of the gas was not tested, so it cannot be claimed as certain.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: linking gaseous exchange structures (stomata, lenticels, cuticle) to the whole system
Q7. A mango tree has woody stems with no stomata. Which structure allows the living cells in the stem to take in O₂ for respiration and release CO₂?
  1. Chloroplasts
  2. Root hairs only
  3. Guard cells on the leaf surface
  4. Lenticels
Correct answer: D
Lenticels are small openings in woody stems that let O₂ in and CO₂ out for respiration. Guard cells control stomata on leaves, and chloroplasts are the site of photosynthesis.
