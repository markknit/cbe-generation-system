Q1. Before looking closely at three plants, a student predicts: "The cactus from Turkana has no stomata, because it lives in a very dry place." Which statement best corrects this idea?
  1. Cacti have more stomata than any other plant, all on the upper surface of the leaf
  2. Cacti have stomata, but they are sunken, which helps reduce water loss while still allowing gas exchange
  3. Cacti have no stomata, so they take in gases directly through the cuticle
  4. Cacti have stomata, but they stay permanently open to take in as much air as possible
Correct answer: B
Xerophytes such as cacti and acacia do have stomata, but these are sunken in pits (usually on the stem in cacti). This reduces water loss while gas exchange still happens.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class CER discussion: claim that sunken stomata in acacia reduce water loss without preventing gas exchange
Q2. An acacia tree in the Rift Valley has stomata sunken in small pits below the leaf surface. Why does this help the tree survive in hot, dry conditions?
  1. The pit blocks all gas movement so that no water can escape
  2. The pit lets more sunlight reach the stoma so that it opens wider
  3. The pit traps moist air around the stoma, which slows the evaporation of water vapour from the leaf
  4. The pit lets the leaf absorb water directly from the dry air
Correct answer: C
Moist air trapped in the pit reduces the water vapour gradient above the stoma, so less water evaporates while gases can still diffuse.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: hydrophyte (water hyacinth) observation
Q3. Water hyacinth floats on Lake Victoria near Kisumu. Where would you expect to find most of its stomata, and why?
  1. On both surfaces equally, because the plant needs water from both sides
  2. On the lower surface, because the lower surface touches the water
  3. On the upper surface, because the upper surface faces the air
  4. Only on the roots, because the roots are under water
Correct answer: C
The upper (adaxial) surface of a floating leaf faces the air, so stomata are found there. Stomata on the lower surface would be blocked by water.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class discussion: aerenchyma in the water hyacinth at the Lake Victoria station
Q4. The submerged roots and stems of a water hyacinth in a waterlogged habitat contain large air spaces called aerenchyma. What is their main job in gaseous exchange?
  1. They stop carbon dioxide from leaving the plant
  2. They make the cuticle thicker to keep water out
  3. They store water for the plant to use during droughts
  4. They give internal channels so that oxygen can diffuse to submerged tissues
Correct answer: D
Aerenchyma are air-filled channels. Oxygen from the air diffuses along them to tissues under water, where little oxygen is available.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: three annotated panels (Turkana xerophyte / Lake Victoria hydrophyte / Nairobi mesophyte)
Q5. A student says: "Aerenchyma is a feature of xerophytes because they need to store air in the desert." Which plant type is aerenchyma actually an adaptation of?
  1. All plants have it equally
  2. Hydrophytes, such as water hyacinth
  3. Mesophytes, such as sukuma wiki
  4. Xerophytes, such as the cactus
Correct answer: B
Aerenchyma is an adaptation of hydrophytes living in waterlogged conditions. Labelling it as a xerophyte feature is a common model error.
Use after: Observe phase — Station C: mesophyte (sukuma wiki) cuticle compared with the cactus station
Q6. Compare the cuticle of a cactus from Turkana with the cuticle of sukuma wiki grown in a Nairobi school garden. Which statement is correct?
  1. The cactus has a thin cuticle, because it needs to absorb water through its surface
  2. The cactus has a thick cuticle, which reduces water loss in a hot, dry environment
  3. Sukuma wiki has a thicker cuticle, because Nairobi has more rainfall
  4. Both have equally thin cuticles because both have stomata
Correct answer: B
Xerophytes have a thick cuticle that cuts water loss through the surface. Mesophytes like sukuma wiki have a moderate cuticle suited to moderate conditions.
Use after: Observe phase — Station C: mesophyte (sukuma wiki) observation
Q7. Sukuma wiki is a mesophyte growing where rainfall and temperature are moderate. Which description of its leaf best fits this environment?
  1. No stomata and gas exchange through lenticels only
  2. Stomata mainly on the lower surface, a moderate cuticle and a broad leaf
  3. Stomata buried deep in pits and a very thick cuticle
  4. Large air spaces throughout the leaf and stomata only on the upper surface
Correct answer: B
Mesophytes show a typical arrangement: stomata mostly on the lower surface, a moderate cuticle and a broad leaf. They have no extreme adaptations for dryness or waterlogging.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: three-way comparison of xerophytes, hydrophytes and mesophytes
Q8. Which pairing of plant, environment and adaptation for gaseous exchange is correct?
  1. Acacia, Rift Valley, sunken stomata and reduced leaf area
  2. Sukuma wiki, Nairobi garden, aerenchyma throughout the stem
  3. Water hyacinth, Lake Victoria, thick cuticle and sunken stomata
  4. Cactus, Turkana, floating leaves with stomata on the upper surface
Correct answer: A
Acacia is a xerophyte of dry areas such as the Rift Valley, and shows sunken stomata and reduced leaf area to cut water loss. The other pairings give hydrophyte or mesophyte features to the wrong plant.
