Use after: Predict phase — Predict: half-sheet prompt asking each group to write its strongest CER claim
Q1. A group claims: "Warmer temperature increased the rate of respiration in yeast." Which piece of evidence best supports this claim?
  1. Yeast is a fungus that is used to make mandazi and uji.
  2. Our group worked well together during the experiment.
  3. Yeast at 35 °C made 24 bubbles per minute, but yeast at 15 °C made only 6 bubbles per minute.
  4. The yeast mixture smelled stronger than the other groups' mixtures.
Correct answer: C
Good evidence for a CER claim is measured data that compares the conditions. The two bubble rates at different temperatures directly show how temperature changed the rate. The other choices are background facts or opinions, not data.
Use after: Observe phase — Poster carousel: groups present their investigations and report anomalous results honestly
Q2. One group's results show that at 25 °C the yeast made fewer bubbles than at 15 °C, which they did not expect. What should the group do when presenting?
  1. Report the result honestly and suggest reasons for it, such as a leaking tube or different amounts of yeast.
  2. Leave out that result so the claim looks stronger.
  3. Say that the experiment failed and that the whole investigation is useless.
  4. Change the number so that it fits the expected pattern.
Correct answer: A
Scientists report anomalous data honestly and try to explain it by thinking about variables that may have affected the result. Hiding or changing data is dishonest, and an unexpected result is still useful evidence.
Use after: Observe phase — Poster carousel: yeast fermentation group presents its sealed-tube limewater result
Q3. In a yeast fermentation experiment, a group sealed the tube so that no air could enter. After 8 minutes the limewater connected to the tube turned cloudy. What does this show?
  1. Water vapour was the only gas released.
  2. Oxygen was produced by the yeast.
  3. The yeast produced carbon dioxide even without oxygen (anaerobic respiration).
  4. The yeast made its own food by photosynthesis.
Correct answer: C
Limewater turns cloudy when carbon dioxide is present. Because the tube was sealed from air, the CO2 came from anaerobic respiration (fermentation).
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class argumentation discussion: the teacher introduces the term 'scientific consensus'
Q4. Three groups used different investigations (yeast fermentation, biogas simulation and uji fermentation). All three found that CO2 was produced when oxygen was limited. Why does this increase confidence in the conclusion?
  1. Because the teacher agrees with the conclusion.
  2. Because several independent investigations reaching the same finding builds scientific consensus.
  3. Because the groups all used the same apparatus and the same materials.
  4. Because three groups is a bigger number than one, whatever the evidence.
Correct answer: B
When multiple independent investigations converge on the same finding, confidence in the conclusion increases. This is called scientific consensus.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class argumentation discussion, question 3: arguing back that plants do not really breathe
Q5. A student says: "Plants do not really breathe. They only make food by photosynthesis." Which reply is best supported by scientific evidence?
  1. Plants respire only at night, so they do nothing with gases during the day.
  2. Plants do not need oxygen because they make their own food.
  3. Photosynthesis and respiration are the same process, so plants do both together.
  4. All living plant cells respire to release energy. This releases CO2, and it leaves the plant through stomata and lenticels.
Correct answer: D
Respiration happens in all living cells all the time, including plant cells, and it releases CO2. Photosynthesis is a different process. Plants exchange gases with the air through stomata and lenticels.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB update: thumbs vote on the Lesson 1 question about why warm water increased bubble production
Q6. Yeast was placed in warm water (35 °C) and in cold water (10 °C). The warm mixture produced many more CO2 bubbles. Which explanation is best?
  1. Warm water makes the yeast produce gas through photosynthesis.
  2. Warm water contains more oxygen than cold water, so the yeast makes food.
  3. The enzymes that control respiration work faster at a warmer temperature, so more CO2 is released.
  4. Cold water destroys all the enzymes in the yeast permanently.
Correct answer: C
Respiration is controlled by enzymes. Within a moderate range, higher temperature makes them work faster, so respiration rate and CO2 release increase.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: extension prompt about closed stomata at midday during drought
Q7. On a hot, dry day at midday, the stomata of a leaf close. Mesophyll cells inside the leaf are still respiring. What happens to the CO2 they produce?
  1. It leaves quickly through the waxy cuticle.
  2. It turns into oxygen straight away, so none builds up.
  3. It is not produced, because respiration stops when stomata close.
  4. It cannot easily leave the leaf, so it builds up inside.
Correct answer: D
Stomata are the main exit for CO2 in a leaf. The waxy cuticle blocks gas movement, and cells keep respiring, so CO2 accumulates inside the leaf until the stomata open again.
