Q1. A group dissects a grasshopper to find the dorsal vessel, which acts as its simple pump. Where do they find it?
  1. Running along the belly, close to the legs
  2. Coiled inside each leg
  3. Running along the back of the body, under the upper surface
  4. Inside the head only, behind the eyes
Correct answer: C
The dorsal vessel is a tube-like pump that runs along the back (dorsal side) of the grasshopper. Learners find it by cutting along the dorsal midline.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A — Dissection or Diagram: Task 4, note the colour of haemolymph (pale yellow-green, not red)
Q2. A student cuts open a grasshopper and sees that its haemolymph is pale yellow-green, not red. What does this colour show?
  1. The grasshopper has been dead so long that its blood has lost its colour
  2. The haemolymph carries oxygen using a red pigment that has faded
  3. The haemolymph carries more oxygen than human blood does
  4. The haemolymph has no haemoglobin, so it does not carry oxygen to the cells
Correct answer: D
Red colour in human blood comes from haemoglobin, which carries oxygen. Pale haemolymph has no haemoglobin, so it does not transport oxygen in insects.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A — Dissection or Diagram: Task 3, observe that there are NO distinct blood vessels branching to organs
Q3. A group looks for blood vessels branching from the dorsal vessel of a grasshopper to its legs and organs, and finds none. What is the best explanation?
  1. The haemolymph flows out into an open body cavity (haemocoel) and bathes the organs directly
  2. The vessels are too small to see, but the grasshopper has a closed system like Kipchoge's
  3. The haemolymph stays inside the dorsal vessel and never reaches the organs
  4. The vessels were destroyed when the grasshopper was killed
Correct answer: A
The grasshopper has an open circulatory system. There are no vessels branching to organs, and haemolymph fills the haemocoel and bathes the organs directly.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A — Dissection or Diagram: Task 2, look for the ostia with a hand lens or use the labelled diagram
Q4. The dorsal vessel of a grasshopper has tiny paired slits along it called ostia. What is their function?
  1. They let oxygen from the air enter the dorsal vessel
  2. They let waste leave the body
  3. They are entry points that let haemolymph from the haemocoel flow into the dorsal vessel
  4. They let the dorsal vessel attach to the muscles of the legs
Correct answer: C
Ostia are the entry points for haemolymph. Haemolymph in the haemocoel enters the dorsal vessel through them, and closed systems have no equivalent.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class CER discussion: key conceptual target (2) — insects do NOT use haemolymph for oxygen transport
Q5. In insects such as the grasshopper, haemolymph does not carry oxygen. How do the body cells get their oxygen?
  1. Oxygen dissolves in haemolymph and is pumped to cells by the dorsal vessel
  2. Oxygen is absorbed through the mouth and carried in the food
  3. Oxygen enters through the ostia and is carried by haemoglobin
  4. The tracheal system delivers oxygen directly to the cells
Correct answer: D
Insects use a tracheal system for gas exchange, which handles oxygen directly. Haemolymph transports other substances, not oxygen.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class CER discussion: 'Could the grasshopper's circulatory system support Eliud Kipchoge running a marathon?'
Q6. Which statement best explains why a grasshopper's circulatory system could NOT support a body running a marathon like Eliud Kipchoge?
  1. Its open, low-pressure system cannot direct large amounts of oxygen-rich blood to working muscles
  2. Its haemolymph is too red and thick to flow quickly
  3. Its dorsal vessel has too many chambers, which slows the flow
  4. It has no dorsal vessel, so nothing pumps the haemolymph
Correct answer: A
In an open system haemolymph bathes organs directly at low pressure, with no capillaries, and it does not carry oxygen. It cannot deliver oxygen-rich blood to specific muscles during intense exercise, as Kipchoge's closed system does.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative model update: grasshopper panel, annotation (1) for the dorsal vessel and the comparison arrow to the human system
Q7. A learner adds the grasshopper's dorsal vessel to their cumulative model and compares it to the human heart. Which annotation is correct?
  1. A large muscular organ in the head that pumps blood to the legs
  2. A simple tube-like pump divided into segments, not four chambers
  3. A heart with no chambers that pumps red blood through closed vessels
  4. A four-chambered heart, the same as a human heart
Correct answer: B
The dorsal vessel is a simple tube-like pump divided into segments. It is not a four-chambered heart and does not pump red blood through closed vessels.
