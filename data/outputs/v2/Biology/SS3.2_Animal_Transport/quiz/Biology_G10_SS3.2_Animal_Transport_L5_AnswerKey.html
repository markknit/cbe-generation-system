Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Heart Comparison Resource Card (Frog three-chambered heart)
Q1. How many chambers does the heart of a frog have, and how are they arranged?
  1. Two atria and one ventricle
  2. One atrium and one ventricle
  3. One atrium and two ventricles
  4. Two atria and two ventricles
Correct answer: A
The amphibian heart is three-chambered: a right atrium, a left atrium and a single ventricle. Two atria and two ventricles describes the four-chambered mammalian heart.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Blood Flow Tracing Activity (frog diagram)
Q2. In a frog's heart, oxygenated blood from the left atrium and deoxygenated blood from the right atrium both empty into one ventricle. What happens to the blood there?
  1. All the blood becomes fully oxygenated before leaving the heart
  2. All the blood becomes deoxygenated before leaving the heart
  3. The two kinds of blood mix, so blood sent to the body is only partly oxygenated
  4. The two kinds of blood stay completely separate, so the body receives fully oxygenated blood
Correct answer: C
There is only one ventricle, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix (the purple arrows). The body therefore receives partly oxygenated blood.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Observation Table, 'Does blood mix?' column
Q3. In the blood-flow diagrams, red arrows show oxygenated blood and blue arrows show deoxygenated blood. A purple arrow inside a ventricle shows that the blood there is...
  1. fully oxygenated blood
  2. blood that has stopped moving
  3. fully deoxygenated blood
  4. a mixture of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
Correct answer: D
Purple is made by combining red and blue, so it shows mixed blood. This is how the lesson makes blood mixing visible.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Observation Table, 'Does blood mix?' column
Q4. Which row correctly shows whether blood mixes in each heart?
  1. Frog: No. Nile monitor lizard: Partial.
  2. Frog: Yes. Nile monitor lizard: No.
  3. Frog: Yes. Nile monitor lizard: Partial.
  4. Frog: Partial. Nile monitor lizard: Yes.
Correct answer: C
The frog has a single undivided ventricle, so blood mixes. The lizard has a partly divided ventricle, which reduces mixing but does not remove it.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: Data Table Analysis (bar graph, circle the lowest score)
Q5. A table gives illustrative oxygen-delivery scores out of 100: tilapia 80, frog 65, monitor lizard 75, human 98. Which animal has the lowest score, and what is the best explanation?
  1. The monitor lizard, because its ventricle is partly divided
  2. The human, because a four-chambered heart mixes blood the most
  3. The tilapia, because fish have no heart
  4. The frog, because its single ventricle mixes oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood
Correct answer: D
The frog scores 65, the lowest. Its single ventricle mixes the two blood streams. The scores are estimates, not measured values.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Structured Notes with Gaps (reptile partial septum)
Q6. What does the partial septum in the ventricle of a reptile such as the Nile monitor lizard do?
  1. It stops mixing of blood completely
  2. It reduces mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood but does not stop it completely
  3. It increases mixing of blood compared with a frog
  4. It stores oxygen for the heart
Correct answer: B
The partial division keeps the two blood streams partly apart, so reptiles mix less than frogs, but mixing still happens. The circulation is incomplete.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Trade-Off Discussion ('A frog does not need a four-chambered heart because...')
Q7. Why can a Rift Valley frog survive with a three-chambered heart that mixes blood?
  1. Its blood is completely oxygenated even though it mixes
  2. It is endothermic, so it needs very little oxygen
  3. It never needs oxygen because it has moist skin
  4. It is ectothermic, so it uses little energy to make body heat and has a low oxygen demand
Correct answer: D
Ectotherms do not use energy to keep their bodies warm, so their oxygen demand is low. Partly oxygenated blood is enough for them.
Use after: Explain phase — Part C: Application Question (Rift Valley frog and the 42 km marathon)
Q8. Which statement best explains why a Rift Valley frog could not run a 42 km marathon, even if its legs were strong enough?
  1. Its blood contains no oxygen at all
  2. Its heart has four chambers, which slows the blood down
  3. Its heart pumps oxygenated blood and deoxygenated blood completely separately, which wastes energy
  4. Mixed blood in its single ventricle gives muscles too little oxygen for the high oxygen demand of long, fast running
Correct answer: D
Because the ventricle mixes the blood, the muscles receive partly oxygenated blood. That cannot supply the high oxygen demand of marathon running.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Explanation Sentence (revising the Animal Transport System Model)
Q9. Complete the model sentence: 'As the heart becomes more chambered and blood mixing decreases, the efficiency of oxygen delivery to tissues ___.'
  1. decreases, because the blood no longer carries oxygen
  2. decreases, because more oxygen-rich blood reaches the tissues
  3. increases, because more oxygen-rich blood reaches the tissues
  4. stays the same, because every heart delivers the same oxygen
Correct answer: C
Less mixing means the blood sent to the body is more highly oxygenated, so oxygen delivery becomes more efficient.
Use after: End of lesson — Driving Question Board update: 'Could Kipchoge survive with a frog's heart?'
Q10. Which statement best sums up the lesson's idea that 'simpler' does not mean 'inferior' for a frog's heart?
  1. Frogs are not able to survive in the wild with such a heart
  2. A three-chambered heart works better than a four-chambered heart in every animal
  3. Frogs have not evolved yet, so their hearts are unfinished
  4. Each heart design is a trade-off that suits the animal's environment and energy needs
Correct answer: D
The three-chambered heart meets a frog's low energy demand, while a high-energy animal like Kipchoge needs a more efficient four-chambered heart. Each is a trade-off suited to the animal.
