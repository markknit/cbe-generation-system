Q1. Which statement correctly describes the circulatory system of a grasshopper?
  1. Blood flows inside closed vessels and carries oxygen to every cell, as in a mammal
  2. It has an open system: a tube-like pump moves haemolymph, which bathes the organs directly
  3. It has a four-chambered heart that separates oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
  4. Haemolymph carries oxygen on haemoglobin to the muscles, just like human blood
Correct answer: B
A grasshopper has an open circulatory system with a tube-like heart. Haemolymph is pumped out and surrounds the organs directly, and oxygen reaches cells through tracheal tubes.
Use after: Observe phase — Gallery walk: 'Two Stars and a Wondering' sticky-note protocol
Q2. During a gallery walk, a learner asks: 'Where in the mammalian heart are oxygenated and deoxygenated blood kept apart?' Which answer is correct?
  1. They are kept apart by haemolymph surrounding the heart
  2. They mix in the ventricles and are separated later in the lungs
  3. A wall (septum) divides the left side of the heart from the right side, so the two kinds of blood do not mix
  4. They are separated only by the valves in the veins
Correct answer: C
The mammalian heart has separate left and right sides divided by a septum. This lets double circulation keep oxygenated and deoxygenated blood from mixing.
Use after: Explain phase — Group presentations: directly answering the driving question 'Kipchoge can sustain world-record speeds and recover fast BECAUSE...'
Q3. Eliud Kipchoge sustains world-record speeds and recovers quickly after a race. Which explanation best uses the idea of double circulation?
  1. Oxygenated blood is kept separate from deoxygenated blood and pumped under pressure to his muscles, so oxygen delivery is efficient
  2. His heart is so large that it does not need to beat faster during a race
  3. His blood does not need oxygen when he runs fast
  4. His blood bathes his muscles directly, as haemolymph does in a grasshopper
Correct answer: A
Double circulation separates oxygenated from deoxygenated blood, so the blood sent to the muscles carries a lot of oxygen. This supports sustained aerobic activity and fast recovery.
Use after: Explain phase — Final Written Explanation (CER): citing evidence from the pulse-rate experiment
Q4. A learner's pulse rate was 72 beats per minute at rest and 138 beats per minute after star jumps. By how many beats per minute did the pulse rate increase?
  1. 66
  2. 138
  3. 33
  4. 210
Correct answer: A
Increase = pulse after exercise minus resting pulse = 138 - 72 = 66 beats per minute. The heart beats faster to meet the higher oxygen demand of the muscles.
Use after: Explain phase — Final Explanation: evidence from the SA:V cube calculations
Q5. In the surface-area-to-volume (SA:V) investigation with cubes, a cube with sides of 2 cm has an SA:V ratio of 3. A larger cube has sides of 4 cm (surface area 96 cm², volume 64 cm³). What is the SA:V ratio of the 4 cm cube, and what does this suggest about large animals?
  1. 24; larger bodies lose oxygen faster, so they do not need a transport system
  2. 1.5; larger bodies have less surface area for their volume, so they need a transport system
  3. 3; the ratio stays the same, so size does not affect transport needs
  4. 6; larger bodies have more surface area for their volume, so diffusion alone is enough
Correct answer: B
SA:V = 96 ÷ 64 = 1.5, which is lower than 3. As bodies get larger, surface area compared with volume falls, so diffusion alone cannot supply every cell and a transport system is needed.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB audit: thumbs vote and one-sentence evidence citation for ANSWERED questions
Q6. During the DQB audit, the class decides that the question 'Why does the heart beat faster during exercise?' is ANSWERED. Which piece of evidence best supports this?
  1. A pulse rose from 72 to 138 beats per minute after star jumps, showing the heart pumps faster when muscles need more oxygen
  2. The heart is about the size of a fist
  3. A grasshopper has a tube-like heart
  4. Eliud Kipchoge is a famous marathon runner
Correct answer: A
Measured pulse-rate data before and after exercise is direct evidence linking a faster heart rate to greater oxygen demand. The other statements are true or unrelated, but they do not answer the question.
Use after: Model Building phase — Organism voice role-play and the final reading of the driving question
Q7. Imagine Eliud Kipchoge had the circulatory system of a grasshopper. What would most likely happen during a marathon?
  1. He would run faster, because haemolymph reaches the muscles directly
  2. Nothing would change, because all animals have the same transport needs
  3. His muscles would be better supplied, because an open system holds more blood
  4. He could not sustain the speed, because an open system cannot deliver oxygen to his muscles fast enough for such high demand
Correct answer: D
In an open system the fluid just bathes the tissues and is not pumped quickly through closed vessels. This cannot meet the high oxygen demand of sustained marathon running.
Use after: Model Building phase — Class Consensus Model: TILAPIA (Lake Victoria) column, single/double circulation arrows
Q8. In the tilapia column of the class consensus model, how many times does blood pass through the heart in one complete circuit of the body?
  1. Twice, because fish have double circulation
  2. Not at all, because fish blood is not pumped by a heart
  3. Once, because fish have single circulation
  4. Four times, because fish have four heart chambers
Correct answer: C
A tilapia has a closed system with single circulation, so blood passes through the heart once in each complete circuit. Mammals such as Kipchoge have double circulation, with blood passing through the heart twice.
