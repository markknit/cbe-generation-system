Q1. In a blood typing simulation, vinegar stands in for Anti-A serum and dilute iodine stands in for Anti-B serum. Vinegar fizzes only with the A marker (baking soda) and iodine turns blue-black only with the B marker (starch). A patient sample fizzes when vinegar is added. When iodine is added, it shows only the pale yellow-brown tint of the iodine itself. What is the blood group of this sample?
  1. Group AB
  2. Group A
  3. Group O
  4. Group B
Correct answer: B
The fizz with Anti-A shows the A marker is present. The pale yellow-brown tint is not a blue-black reaction, so there is no B marker. A marker only means group A.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Blood Typing Simulation (teacher question 'What does NO reaction tell you?')
Q2. In the same simulation, a patient sample shows no fizz with 'Anti-A serum' (vinegar) and no blue-black colour with 'Anti-B serum' (iodine). What does this tell you about the sample?
  1. It carries both the A and B markers, so it is group AB
  2. It carries only the B marker, so it is group B
  3. It carries neither the A nor the B marker, so it is group O
  4. The sample is spoiled and the test must be thrown away
Correct answer: C
A reaction shows only when the serum matches a marker in the sample. No reaction with either serum means neither A nor B is present, which is group O. No reaction is a valid result.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Kenyan Blood Group Distribution Data (bar chart from the KNBTS data card)
Q3. KNBTS data estimate the blood groups of Kenyans as: group O about 46%, group A about 27%, group B about 20% and group AB about 7%. What percentage of Kenyans have a blood group that is NOT group O?
  1. 73%
  2. 47%
  3. 46%
  4. 54%
Correct answer: D
Add the other three groups: 27 + 20 + 7 = 54%. This is the same as 100 - 46 = 54%.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: Rhesus Factor Mini-Reading (Rh+ and Rh− designations)
Q4. An Rh-negative patient needs a blood transfusion. Why should the patient be given Rh-negative blood and not Rh-positive blood?
  1. Rh-negative people always have anti-D antibodies from birth, so no other blood can be used
  2. Rh-negative blood carries more oxygen than Rh-positive blood
  3. Exposure to Rh-positive red cells can make the patient's immune system produce anti-D antibodies that attack Rh-positive red cells
  4. Rh-positive blood has no antigens, so it cannot be used by anyone
Correct answer: C
Rh-positive red cells carry the Rh (D) antigen and Rh-negative cells do not. An Rh-negative person has no anti-D until exposed to Rh-positive blood. After that, the immune system can make anti-D that attacks Rh-positive red cells.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Vocabulary Anchor (Frayer Model for 'agglutination')
Q5. In real blood, what is agglutination?
  1. The fizzing of blood when it meets an acid
  2. The clumping together of red blood cells when antibodies meet the matching antigens
  3. The breakdown of white blood cells in the lymph
  4. The clotting of blood to seal a wound
Correct answer: B
Agglutination is the clumping of red blood cells when an antibody meets its matching antigen. The simulation only models this match with fizzing or a colour change and does not show clumping.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Transfusion Compatibility Matrix (rule card: 'A recipient has Anti-B antibodies and will reject B or AB donor blood')
Q6. A patient with blood group A has red cells with A antigens and Anti-B antibodies in the plasma. Which donor blood (ABO groups only) would this patient reject?
  1. Group AB blood
  2. Group O blood
  3. Group A blood
  4. None; a group A patient can safely receive any ABO group
Correct answer: A
Group AB red cells carry the B antigen, which the patient's Anti-B antibodies attack. Group O has no antigens and group A has only A antigens, so both are safe.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Transfusion Compatibility Matrix (rule card: 'O donor has no antigens — universal donor')
Q7. Why is group O blood called the 'universal donor' for the ABO system?
  1. Its plasma has no antibodies, so it can receive blood from anyone
  2. It is the most common group in Kenya, so it is always available
  3. Its red blood cells carry no A or B antigens, so the recipient's Anti-A or Anti-B antibodies have nothing to attack
  4. It carries both A and B antigens, so it matches every recipient
Correct answer: C
Group O red cells have no A or B antigens, so the recipient's antibodies find no match. Being common is a separate fact. Having no antibodies in the plasma describes group AB, not group O.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: comparison note 'Does a grasshopper have blood groups? Why or why not?'
Q8. A learner asks: 'Does a grasshopper have blood groups like A, B, AB and O?' Which is the best scientific answer?
  1. No, because a grasshopper's blood is too cold for antigens to form
  2. No, because a grasshopper has haemolymph, not true blood with red blood cells, so there is no ABO system
  3. Yes, because all animals with a heart have the ABO system
  4. Yes, but grasshopper blood groups are not tested because the insects are too small
Correct answer: B
Insects such as grasshoppers have haemolymph and no red blood cells. The ABO antigens are carried on red blood cells, so no ABO system exists in a grasshopper.
