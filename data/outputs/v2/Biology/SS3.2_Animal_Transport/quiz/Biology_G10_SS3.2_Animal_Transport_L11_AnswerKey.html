Q1. An Evidence Card says: "Frog ventricle is not fully divided, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix." What does this tell us about the African bullfrog's heart?
  1. Some oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mixes in the single ventricle, so separation is incomplete
  2. Blood passes through the heart only once in each complete circuit
  3. Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are kept completely separate
  4. The frog has no blood vessels, so blood mixes freely in the body cavity
Correct answer: A
An undivided ventricle lets oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix, so separation is incomplete. This is the amphibian pattern in the comparative table.
Use after: Observe phase — Evidence Cards: teacher asks which card explains why a grasshopper can survive without a closed system
Q2. An Evidence Card says: "Grasshopper haemolymph bathes organs directly and does not carry oxygen." Which description of the grasshopper's transport system is correct?
  1. It is a double circulation with a four-chambered heart
  2. It is a single closed circulation in which haemolymph carries oxygen to the muscles
  3. It is a closed system with capillaries linking arteries and veins
  4. It is an open system in which haemolymph flows through the body cavity around the organs
Correct answer: D
Haemolymph is pumped into the body cavity and bathes the organs directly, which is an open system. It does not need to carry oxygen, and an insect's low metabolic demand lets it survive without a closed system.
Use after: Explain phase — Comparative Table: completing the fish row (type of circulation)
Q3. An Evidence Card says: "Tilapia blood passes through the heart once per complete circuit." Which type of circulation does the Nile tilapia have?
  1. Single closed circulation
  2. Double circulation with mixing in the ventricle
  3. Double circulation with complete separation of blood
  4. Open circulation
Correct answer: A
Blood that passes through the heart once per circuit means single circulation. Fish blood stays inside vessels, so it is closed. Mistaking fish circulation for double is a common misconception.
Use after: Explain phase — Comparative Table: completing the amphibian and reptile rows (separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood)
Q4. Compare a reptile heart, which has a partial septum in the ventricle, with a frog heart, which has an undivided ventricle. Which statement is correct?
  1. Reptiles have completely separate blood, while frogs have some mixing
  2. Reptiles and frogs have exactly the same amount of mixing
  3. Reptiles have more mixing than frogs because of the partial septum
  4. Reptiles have less mixing than frogs, but the separation is still not complete
Correct answer: D
A partial septum reduces the mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood compared with an undivided ventricle. It does not remove the mixing completely, so full separation appears only in mammals.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class discussion: three patterns (open to closed, single to double, incomplete to complete separation)
Q5. Which description fits the circulatory system of an impala, a mammal?
  1. Open circulation in which blood bathes the organs directly
  2. Double circulation with a single ventricle in which the two kinds of blood mix
  3. Single closed circulation with a two-chambered heart
  4. Double circulation with a four-chambered heart and complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
Correct answer: D
Mammals have a four-chambered heart and double circulation. Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are fully separated, which is the third trend drawn out in the whole-class discussion.
Use after: DQB phase — Whole-class discussion: the limits of the trend lines ("comparisons, not a ladder")
Q6. A learner says: "A grasshopper is just a failed mammal, and insects are the ancestors of fish." Which response is the best correction?
  1. The learner is right about insects, but wrong about the grasshopper
  2. The learner is right, because the trends show a ladder from insect to mammal
  3. The learner is wrong, because closed circulation has evolved only once, in mammals
  4. The learner is wrong, because the trends are comparisons, not a ladder. Each system fits its animal's size and energy needs
Correct answer: D
The patterns compare systems and do not show a line of descent. Each system suits its own animal's size and energy demand, and closed circulation evolved more than once, for example in birds and crocodiles as well as mammals.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model update: annotation box "My Explanation of the Phenomenon" (Kipchoge vs a grasshopper's dorsal vessel)
Q7. Eliud Kipchoge's heart recovers quickly after a race. Which feature of the mammalian heart best explains how it supports such high energy demand?
  1. Blood passes through the heart only once per circuit, which saves time
  2. Full separation of blood means oxygenated blood reaches the muscles at maximum oxygen concentration without dilution
  3. The heart has a single ventricle, so blood mixes and reaches the muscles faster
  4. Haemolymph bathes his muscles directly, so oxygen reaches them quickly
Correct answer: B
In double circulation with a four-chambered heart, oxygenated blood is not diluted by deoxygenated blood. Muscles therefore receive blood with the maximum oxygen concentration, which supports a high metabolic rate.
