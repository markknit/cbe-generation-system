Q1. Blood is pumped through capillaries under pressure. A small amount of plasma leaks out of the blood capillaries into the spaces between body cells. What is this leaked fluid called when it is still in the spaces between the cells?
  1. Lymph
  2. Tissue fluid
  3. Haemolymph
  4. Serum from the kidneys
Correct answer: B
Plasma that leaks from blood capillaries into the spaces between cells is called tissue fluid. It is only called lymph after it has entered the lymph capillaries.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity C: Connecting to the phenomenon (why Kipchoge's legs did not swell during the Berlin Marathon)
Q2. Kipchoge's leg muscles contract with every stride during a marathon. How does this help to drain extra tissue fluid from his legs and prevent oedema?
  1. The contracting muscles stop the blood capillaries from leaking any plasma at all
  2. The contracting muscles squeeze the lymph vessels, and the valves make the lymph move one way towards the chest
  3. The contracting muscles turn tissue fluid into blood plasma inside the lymph nodes
  4. The contracting muscles make the lymph vessels push lymph back into the blood capillaries of the legs
Correct answer: B
Lymph vessels have valves like veins. Squeezing by contracting muscles pushes lymph along, and the valves stop backflow, so lymph moves towards the chest and excess fluid is drained.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity B: Tracing the lymphatic pathway (lymph nodes in armpits, groin and neck)
Q3. A learner has tonsillitis and feels swollen, tender lumps under the jaw. What is the main job of the lymph nodes in these lumps?
  1. They filter pathogens from the lymph and make white blood cells that fight infection
  2. They carry lymph directly from the heart to the leg muscles
  3. They store blood to be used during exercise
  4. They pump lymph around the body like a heart
Correct answer: A
Lymph nodes filter pathogens from lymph and contain white blood cells. They swell when fighting an infection such as tonsillitis.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Oedema case study (Mama Zawadi from Kilifi)
Q4. Mama Zawadi, a fish trader from Kilifi, has lymphatic filariasis. Parasitic worms block her lymph vessels, and her lower legs swell badly. Which explanation for the swelling is correct?
  1. Tissue fluid still leaks from her blood capillaries but cannot drain away through the blocked lymph vessels, so it builds up in the tissues
  2. Her heart is pumping blood backwards into her legs
  3. Her lymph nodes have turned into blood capillaries and make too much plasma
  4. Her blood capillaries have stopped leaking plasma, so the legs fill with air
Correct answer: A
Blood capillaries keep leaking plasma to form tissue fluid. If the lymph vessels are blocked, the fluid cannot be returned to the blood, so it collects in the tissues. This swelling is called oedema.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Compare with simpler animals (does the grasshopper need a lymphatic system?)
Q5. A grasshopper has an open circulatory system, and it does not have a lymphatic system. Which statement best explains why it does not need one?
  1. Its tracheae carry tissue fluid back to the heart
  2. Its body is too small to lose any fluid
  3. Its heart is stronger than a mammal's heart, so no fluid ever leaks
  4. Its haemolymph bathes the organs directly and is not separated from the tissues by capillary walls, so there is no leaked tissue fluid to drain
Correct answer: D
In an open system the haemolymph is already in direct contact with the tissues. There are no capillaries leaking plasma into a separate tissue fluid compartment, so no lymphatic drainage is needed.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: Lesson 8 evidence card and 'What We Now Know' sticky notes
Q6. Which statement best summarises what the lymphatic system does for the body, including for an athlete like Eliud Kipchoge?
  1. It carries oxygen to the muscles in place of the blood
  2. It replaces the heart when the heart is tired after a race
  3. It drains excess tissue fluid and returns it to the blood, which prevents oedema
  4. It pumps blood faster so that muscles get more oxygen
Correct answer: C
The lymphatic system returns excess tissue fluid (as lymph) to the blood. Without this, fluid would build up in the tissues and cause oedema.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative transport model: adding the lymphatic system in green (lymph capillaries to subclavian veins)
Q7. Which list shows the correct route of fluid that leaves the blood capillaries and is returned to the blood by the lymphatic system?
  1. Lymph capillaries, subclavian veins, lymph nodes, thoracic duct
  2. Lymph capillaries, lymph vessels (passing through lymph nodes), thoracic duct or right lymphatic duct, subclavian veins
  3. Subclavian veins, lymph ducts, lymph vessels, lymph capillaries
  4. Blood capillaries, lymph ducts, lymph nodes, tissue spaces
Correct answer: B
Tissue fluid enters blind-ended lymph capillaries and becomes lymph. It flows through lymph vessels and nodes, then into the thoracic duct or right lymphatic duct, which empty into the subclavian veins.
