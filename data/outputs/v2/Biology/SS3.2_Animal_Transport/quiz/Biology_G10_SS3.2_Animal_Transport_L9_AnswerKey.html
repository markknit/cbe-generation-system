Use after: Predict phase — Prediction Wall: CLOTTING predictions (Kipchoge's scratched leg)
Q1. A learner predicts that blood clotting happens inside the heart. Where does blood clotting actually take place?
  1. At the damaged blood vessel wall, at the site of the wound
  2. In the lymph nodes, where white cells are stored
  3. In the stomach, where blood is digested
  4. Inside the heart chambers, where blood is pumped
Correct answer: A
Clotting is triggered when a blood vessel wall is damaged, so platelets and clotting factors act at the wound site, not inside the heart.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: The Clotting Cascade Model (yarn and beads Observation Guide)
Q2. Which list shows the correct order of events when a blood vessel is cut?
  1. Thrombin becomes prothrombin, fibrin becomes fibrinogen, platelets aggregate, clot forms
  2. Clot forms, platelets aggregate, fibrinogen becomes fibrin, prothrombin becomes thrombin
  3. Fibrin mesh forms, platelets aggregate, prothrombin becomes thrombin, clot forms
  4. Platelets aggregate at the wound, prothrombin becomes thrombin, thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin, clot forms
Correct answer: D
Platelets gather at the wound and clotting factors are activated. Prothrombin is converted to thrombin, thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin, and the fibrin mesh traps red blood cells to form the clot.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity B: Immunity Comparison Data Cards (Card 1, non-specific response)
Q3. A thorn pierces Amina's finger. Within minutes the area turns red, swells and feels warm, and phagocytes engulf bacteria. Which statement correctly describes this response?
  1. It is a non-specific response because lymphocytes make memory cells
  2. It is a specific response because antibodies are made within minutes
  3. It is a non-specific response because phagocytes act quickly on many kinds of germs and no memory is formed
  4. It is a specific response because it targets only one type of bacteria
Correct answer: C
Phagocytes respond quickly to many types of germs without targeting one pathogen and do not form memory. Antibodies, lymphocytes and memory cells belong to specific immunity.
Use after: Explain phase — Part 2: Immunity Explanation Card (Amina's thorn and bacteria)
Q4. Three weeks after a thorn infection, the same bacteria enter Amina's blood and are cleared in 24 hours, much faster than the first time. What best explains this faster response?
  1. Phagocytes become larger after the first infection
  2. Her skin becomes thicker so bacteria cannot enter
  3. Memory cells from B-lymphocytes quickly make antibodies against those bacteria (specific immunity)
  4. Platelets remember the bacteria and destroy them
Correct answer: C
Specific immunity forms memory cells. On the second exposure, B-lymphocytes quickly produce antibodies against the same bacteria, so the infection clears faster.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity C: Malaria Case Study (Achieng's data table)
Q5. Achieng, age 12, has malaria. Plasmodium falciparum is destroying her red blood cells and her haemoglobin has dropped from 12 g/dL to 7 g/dL. Her heart beats faster than normal even at rest. Why?
  1. Malaria makes her blood clot too quickly in the heart
  2. Her heart is pumping faster to compensate for fewer red blood cells carrying oxygen
  3. Her heart is fighting the parasite directly with phagocytes
  4. Her heart has grown stronger like an athlete's heart
Correct answer: B
Fewer red blood cells and less haemoglobin means less oxygen is carried per beat. The heart compensates by pumping faster to deliver more oxygen to the tissues.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity C: Malaria Case Study (data table: resting heart rate)
Q6. The normal resting heart rate is 75 beats per minute. Achieng, who has malaria, has a resting heart rate of 98 beats per minute. By how many beats per minute is her resting heart rate above normal?
  1. 173
  2. 33
  3. 23
  4. 13
Correct answer: C
98 − 75 = 23 beats per minute higher than normal.
Use after: Explain phase — Part 3: Connecting to the Phenomenon (grasshopper pivot)
Q7. Which statement about a grasshopper's haemolymph is correct?
  1. It has no cells that defend the insect against microbes
  2. It clots using a fibrin mesh just like human blood
  3. It has haemocytes that engulf microbes and help seal wounds, but no fibrin clotting, no antibodies and no immune memory
  4. It has lymphocytes that make antibodies and form immune memory
Correct answer: C
Insect haemocytes engulf microbes and help seal wounds in a simpler way. Insects have no fibrin clotting, lymphocytes, antibodies or immune memory.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative Transport System Model: colour-coded legend (TRANSPORT, DEFENCE, REPAIR)
Q8. In a model of the three roles of blood, TRANSPORT is red, DEFENCE is blue and REPAIR is yellow. Which blood part belongs in the yellow REPAIR group?
  1. Lymphocytes, which make antibodies
  2. Red blood cells, which carry oxygen
  3. Platelets, which start the clotting cascade at a wound
  4. Phagocytes, which engulf bacteria
Correct answer: C
Platelets and the clotting cascade seal wounds, which is the repair role. Red blood cells are transport, and phagocytes and lymphocytes are defence.
