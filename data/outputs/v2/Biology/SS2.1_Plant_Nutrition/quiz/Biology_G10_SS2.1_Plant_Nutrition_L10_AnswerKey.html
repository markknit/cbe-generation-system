Q1. A kale (sukuma wiki) plant in a Nairobi kitchen garden wilts at midday even though the soil is moist, and it recovers the next morning. Which explanation is best?
  1. The soil has run out of water, so the plant dies a little each day.
  2. The roots stop working permanently in hot weather, so the plant cannot take in any water.
  3. The leaves wilt because the plant has used up all its minerals by midday.
  4. In strong midday sun the leaves lose water vapour through the stomata faster than the xylem can supply it, so cells lose turgor pressure and the leaf droops.
Correct answer: D
Strong light and heat increase transpiration through open stomata, so water loss exceeds uptake and turgor falls. At night transpiration slows, water supply catches up and turgor returns, so the plant recovers. Wet soil does not prevent this.

Q2. A leaf has green and white patches (a variegated leaf). After it has been in sunlight, it is boiled in water, soaked in alcohol and tested with iodine solution. Only the green patches turn blue-black. What does this show?
  1. Chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis, so starch is made only where the leaf is green.
  2. Starch is made in the roots and then moves only into the green parts of the leaf.
  3. Iodine solution only reacts with the colour green.
  4. White patches take in more water than green patches, so starch washes away from them.
Correct answer: A
Blue-black shows starch, which is stored glucose from photosynthesis. The white patches have no chlorophyll, so they cannot photosynthesise and make no starch.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 4: two-stage flow diagram of photosynthesis with key molecules labelled
Q3. Photosynthesis happens in two stages. What do the light-dependent reactions supply to the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle) so that carbon dioxide can be turned into glucose?
  1. Glucose and oxygen
  2. Carbon dioxide and water
  3. ATP and NADPH
  4. Chlorophyll and starch
Correct answer: C
The light-dependent reactions use light energy to make ATP and NADPH (and release oxygen). The light-independent reactions use these to fix carbon dioxide into glucose.
Use after: Explain phase — Building the annotated model: explaining the shade pallor and slow growth
Q4. A kale plant moved under a mango tree grows slowly. Which explanation of the slow growth is correct?
  1. Low light intensity slows the light-dependent reactions, so less ATP and NADPH are made, less glucose is produced and growth is slow.
  2. Shade removes all carbon dioxide from the air around the plant.
  3. In shade the plant makes too much glucose and cannot use it all.
  4. Shade stops the roots from absorbing water, so the plant cannot make food.
Correct answer: A
Light energy drives the light-dependent reactions. With less light there is less ATP and NADPH, so the light-independent reactions make less glucose and the plant has less food for growth.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 5: graph of photosynthesis rate against light intensity
Q5. In an experiment, a plant's rate of photosynthesis rises as light intensity increases, then levels off even when the light is made brighter. What is the best explanation?
  1. The plant no longer needs light for photosynthesis.
  2. Bright light destroys all the chlorophyll at once.
  3. Another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, has become the limiting factor.
  4. The plant has stopped making glucose and has started making only oxygen.
Correct answer: C
When light is no longer the factor in shortest supply, the rate is held back by another limiting factor such as CO2 concentration or temperature. More light then makes no further difference.
Use after: Explain phase — Building the annotated model: section on at least two minerals and their deficiency
Q6. Kale grown in soil that lacks nitrogen has pale yellowish-green leaves and grows slowly. Which explanation is best?
  1. Nitrogen shortage makes the stomata close, which causes wilting rather than pale leaves.
  2. Nitrogen is needed to build chlorophyll and other molecules, so less chlorophyll is made, less light is absorbed, photosynthesis slows and growth is poor.
  3. Nitrogen is the main energy source that the plant burns directly, so without it the plant has no energy.
  4. Nitrogen is a raw material that is combined with water to make glucose.
Correct answer: B
Nitrogen is a macronutrient used to build chlorophyll and proteins. Without it the leaves are pale, less light is trapped, photosynthesis is slower and growth is poor. Glucose is made from carbon dioxide and water, not nitrogen.
Use after: End of lesson — Part 3 of the written task: The Kenyan Context (plant nutrition and food security)
Q7. A smallholder farmer in Kiambu County finds that her kale leaves are pale and the plants grow slowly. They are grown in deep shade. Which action applies plant nutrition science best?
  1. Water the plants only at midday and keep them in deep shade, since shade stops all problems.
  2. Spray the leaves with iodine solution to turn them green.
  3. Move the plants to a spot with more sunlight and add manure or fertiliser that supplies nitrogen, so more chlorophyll and glucose are made.
  4. Cover the plants with dark cloth to reduce photosynthesis and save glucose.
Correct answer: C
More light raises the rate of the light-dependent reactions, and nitrogen helps build chlorophyll. Together they increase photosynthesis and growth, which improves yield and food security. Deep shade fixes wilting but causes pallor and slow growth.
