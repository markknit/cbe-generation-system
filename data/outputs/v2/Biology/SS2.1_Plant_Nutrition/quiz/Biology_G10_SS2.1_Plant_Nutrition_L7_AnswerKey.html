Q1. A student sketches a graph with light intensity on the x-axis and rate of photosynthesis on the y-axis. Which sketch best matches what scientists find when light intensity is increased steadily, with other conditions kept suitable?
  1. The rate rises in a straight line forever as light increases
  2. The rate rises, then drops to zero at high light intensity
  3. The rate rises steeply at first, then levels off at a maximum
  4. The rate stays flat at every light intensity
Correct answer: C
At low light intensity, light limits the rate, so the rate rises quickly. At higher intensity another factor such as CO₂ or temperature limits it, so the curve levels off at a maximum.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: data collection - probing question 'Why are we using sodium bicarbonate in the water?'
Q2. In an investigation, an aquatic plant (Elodea) is placed in a beaker of water with sodium bicarbonate dissolved in it. Why is sodium bicarbonate added to the water?
  1. To warm the water so the plant grows faster
  2. To give the plant oxygen to breathe
  3. To supply CO₂ so that CO₂ is not the limiting factor and light can be tested on its own
  4. To make the oxygen bubbles bigger and easier to count
Correct answer: C
Sodium bicarbonate releases CO₂ into the water. This keeps CO₂ plentiful so any change in the bubble rate is due to light intensity alone.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: data collection - Option 2 dataset card (relative intensity = 100 x (10/distance)²)
Q3. A class uses the formula: relative light intensity = 100 × (10 ÷ distance in cm)². What is the relative light intensity when the lamp is 20 cm from the plant?
  1. 25
  2. 50
  3. 400
  4. 200
Correct answer: A
Relative intensity = 100 × (10 ÷ 20)² = 100 × 0.25 = 25. Doubling the distance from 10 cm to 20 cm cuts the intensity to one quarter, not one half.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: graph plotting - smooth best-fit curve, not dot-to-dot
Q4. A learner plots her photosynthesis data (rate in bubbles per minute against light intensity). Which way of joining the points is correct for this graph?
  1. Draw a straight horizontal line through the first point
  2. Join only the highest and lowest points with one straight line
  3. Join every point with a ruler in a zigzag, dot to dot
  4. Draw a smooth best-fit curve through the middle of the points to show the trend
Correct answer: D
In biology we draw the trend with a smooth best-fit curve through the middle of the points. We do not join every dot.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher introduces the term LIMITING FACTOR after the three graph-analysis questions
Q5. Which statement correctly describes a limiting factor in photosynthesis?
  1. The factor that stops photosynthesis completely when it changes
  2. The gas that is released as a product of photosynthesis
  3. The factor present at the lowest or most restrictive level, which controls the overall rate
  4. The factor present at the highest level, which makes the rate as fast as possible
Correct answer: C
A limiting factor is the factor in shortest supply, or most restrictive, that controls the overall rate. For example, if rain is enough but there is no fertiliser, fertiliser limits tea growth.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: graph detective question Q3 - why the curve flattens at high light intensity
Q6. In a photosynthesis investigation, the lamp was moved closer so that relative light intensity increased from 100 to 400 units. The rate only rose from 34 to 36 bubbles per minute. What is the best explanation?
  1. The plant has stopped photosynthesising because the light is too bright
  2. Light is no longer the limiting factor, so another factor such as CO₂ or temperature is limiting the rate
  3. The extra light has no effect on photosynthesis at any intensity
  4. The learners must have counted the bubbles wrongly
Correct answer: B
Quadrupling the light gave almost no extra photosynthesis, so the curve has levelled off. Something else, such as CO₂ concentration or temperature, is now the limiting factor.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning frame - citing specific data points from the table
Q7. In an investigation, a lamp 40 cm from a plant gave 8 bubbles per minute. At 20 cm the plant gave 26 bubbles per minute. How many more bubbles per minute were produced at 20 cm than at 40 cm?
  1. 18
  2. 8
  3. 34
  4. 26
Correct answer: A
The difference is 26 − 8 = 18 bubbles per minute. More bubbles mean more oxygen released and a faster rate of photosynthesis with the brighter light.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: phenomenon reconnection - using a graph region to explain the pale, slow-growing sukuma wiki
Q8. Sukuma wiki plants moved under a mango tree grew slowly and turned pale. Using what a rate-against-intensity graph shows, which is the best explanation?
  1. In shade the light intensity is so high that the rate of photosynthesis drops
  2. In shade the plant has reached the flat part of the graph, where light has no effect
  3. In shade the plant gets too much CO₂, which slows photosynthesis
  4. In shade the light intensity is low, so light is the limiting factor and the rate of photosynthesis is low
Correct answer: D
Shade is the low-intensity part of the graph, where the rate is low and light limits it. Less glucose is made, so growth is slow and chlorophyll is poorly made, which gives pale leaves.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: whole-class close - 'What process involving water is still completely missing?'
Q9. Sukuma wiki wilts at midday even though the soil is moist. A photosynthesis model shows light intensity, CO₂, water as an input, and glucose and O₂ as outputs. Which process is still missing that would help explain the wilting?
  1. Respiration releasing energy from glucose at night
  2. Production of more chlorophyll in strong light
  3. Uptake of sodium bicarbonate through the roots
  4. Loss of water vapour from the leaves (transpiration) when the sun is strong
Correct answer: D
Photosynthesis data cannot explain wilting. Water leaves the leaf as vapour through open stomata in strong sunlight, which is transpiration. This is the missing link the class will return to later.
