Use after: Predict phase — Predict: photographs of the sukuma wiki and the narrative about Amina, followed by silent individual writing
Q1. Amina moved her sukuma wiki plants from the sunny garden bed to the shade of a mango tree. What did she observe over the next few weeks?
  1. The leaves turned dark green and the plants grew faster than before
  2. The wilting continued and the leaves stayed dark green
  3. The wilting stopped, but the leaves became pale yellowish-green and growth was slow
  4. The plants died within a few days
Correct answer: C
In the narrative, shade stopped the wilting but the leaves gradually turned pale yellowish-green and the plant grew slowly. The question checks what was observed and does not ask for an explanation.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: prompt 2 'Why do you think it recovers by the next morning?'
Q2. Amina's sukuma wiki leaves were wilted at midday, but by the next morning they were upright again. What does this observation tell us?
  1. The plants died and new plants grew overnight
  2. The leaves can only recover if the plant is moved into shade
  3. The soil became dry overnight and the plants then recovered
  4. The wilting was reversible, so it was not permanent damage to the plant
Correct answer: D
Recovery by the next morning shows that the change was reversible. This is an observation-based inference, and it does not say why the wilting happens.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: maize and mushroom photographs, pair discussion on how each gets its food
Q3. A mushroom grows on a log and a maize plant grows in an open Rift Valley farm. Which statement best describes the main difference in how they get their food?
  1. Only the mushroom needs energy to stay alive
  2. The mushroom makes its own food using sunlight, while the maize feeds on dead logs
  3. The maize makes its own food, while the mushroom takes ready-made food from dead matter
  4. Both get their food in exactly the same way, from the soil
Correct answer: C
The class collected the idea that maize makes its own food, while the mushroom depends on other organic matter such as the dead log. Both organisms need energy.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: two-column comparison of autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition
Q4. Which list contains ONLY heterotrophic organisms (organisms that cannot make their own food and must take in ready-made organic food)?
  1. Sukuma wiki, algae, cow
  2. Goat, maize, mushroom
  3. Maize, bread mould, sukuma wiki
  4. Goat, mushroom, bread mould
Correct answer: D
Animals such as goats and fungi such as mushrooms and bread mould are heterotrophs. Maize, sukuma wiki and algae are autotrophs that make their own food.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: extra minute on parasitic, saprophytic, symbiotic and insectivorous feeding
Q5. Striga (witchweed) attaches to maize and sorghum plants in western Kenya and takes food from them. Which special way of feeding is this?
  1. Insectivorous feeding
  2. Saprophytic feeding
  3. Parasitic feeding
  4. Symbiotic feeding
Correct answer: C
Striga feeds on living host plants, which is parasitic feeding. Saprophytes feed on dead matter, insectivorous plants trap insects, and symbiotic partners (such as legumes and bacteria) both benefit.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: grouping learners' sticky-note questions into clusters under the driving question
Q6. The class has grouped its questions on the Driving Question Board into four clusters: water and wilting, light and colour, food-making, and what plants take from soil and air. Which question about Amina's sukuma wiki best fits the cluster 'light and colour'?
  1. Why do the leaves droop at midday when the soil is wet?
  2. Why do the leaves turn pale yellowish-green when the plant is kept in the shade?
  3. How do plants make their own food?
  4. What does a plant take in from the soil and the air?
Correct answer: B
The question about leaves turning pale in shade is about light and leaf colour. The other questions belong to the food-making, water and wilting, and soil and air clusters.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: drawing 'My Starting Model - Lesson 1' showing what goes into, happens inside and comes out of the plant
Q7. At the end of Lesson 1, learners draw 'My Starting Model' of Amina's sukuma wiki plant. What is the purpose of this model?
  1. To give the final correct answer to the driving question
  2. To show only the parts of their thinking that they are sure are correct
  3. To show their current thinking, which they will add to and revise after each lesson
  4. To copy a correct textbook diagram of the plant
Correct answer: C
The starting model records each learner's current ideas before instruction, and it is expected to be incomplete. Learners revise it after each lesson and compare it with their final model in Lesson 10.
