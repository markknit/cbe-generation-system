Q1. A leaf has a white region with no chlorophyll, a green region that was in light, and a green region that was covered with black paper for 48 hours. Before the starch test, which region would you predict to contain starch?
  1. The green region that was in the light and the green region that was covered
  2. Only the green region that was in the light
  3. All three regions, because the whole leaf is alive
  4. Only the white region
Correct answer: B
Starch is made by photosynthesis, which needs both chlorophyll and light. Only the green, illuminated region has both.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: justifying the Zone 3 (green covered region) prediction using the word equation
Q2. A learner predicts that the green region of a leaf kept in the dark for 48 hours will still make starch 'because it is still green'. What is wrong with this reasoning?
  1. Leaves in the dark make more starch than leaves in the light
  2. Chlorophyll is only needed to take in water, not for making food
  3. Green leaves never make starch
  4. Chlorophyll can only work when light is also present, so being green is not enough
Correct answer: D
Having chlorophyll is not the same as chlorophyll working. Photosynthesis needs light energy, so the covered green region cannot make starch.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher demonstration of Step 1 (leaf in boiling water)
Q3. In the starch test, why is the leaf first placed in boiling water for about 2 minutes?
  1. To kill the cells and stop enzyme activity
  2. To turn the iodine solution blue-black
  3. To remove the green colour from the leaf
  4. To make the leaf produce more starch
Correct answer: A
Boiling water kills the leaf cells and stops enzyme activity, so the starch present is fixed. The ethanol step later removes the colour.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher demonstration of Step 2 (hot ethanol water bath, ethanol turns green)
Q4. During the starch test, a leaf is placed in a tube of ethanol in a hot water bath. The leaf turns pale and the ethanol turns green. What has happened, and why is the tube kept in a hot water bath and not over a flame?
  1. Iodine has been removed from the leaf; a flame would make the leaf produce starch
  2. Chlorophyll has dissolved into the ethanol; ethanol is flammable and must not be heated directly
  3. Starch has dissolved into the ethanol; the flame would make the ethanol turn green too fast
  4. Water has left the leaf; the ethanol would freeze over a flame
Correct answer: B
Hot ethanol dissolves chlorophyll out of the leaf so the iodine colour can be seen clearly. Ethanol catches fire easily, so it is heated only in a hot water bath.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Steps 4-5, flooding the leaf with iodine and recording colour of each zone
Q5. After the starch test, iodine solution is added to a decolourised leaf. One region turns blue-black. What does this show?
  1. Light did not reach that region
  2. The region has no starch
  3. Chlorophyll is still present in that region
  4. Starch is present in that region
Correct answer: D
Iodine solution turns blue-black when starch is present. It stays brown or orange-brown when there is no starch.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: working through the white zone in the Zone / Colour after iodine / Conclusion table
Q6. After the iodine test, the white region (no chlorophyll) of a variegated leaf stayed brown, while the green region that had been in light turned blue-black. What is the correct conclusion?
  1. The white region made starch but stored it elsewhere
  2. The white region turned brown because it had too much starch
  3. Chlorophyll is needed for the leaf to make starch
  4. Light is not needed for starch production
Correct answer: C
Both regions had light, but only the one with chlorophyll made starch. So chlorophyll is required for photosynthesis.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: working through the covered green zone in the results table
Q7. A green region of a leaf was covered with black paper for 48 hours. After the starch test it stayed brown. The region still had chlorophyll. What does this result show?
  1. Chlorophyll is not needed for starch production
  2. The covered region lost its chlorophyll, so no starch formed
  3. Light is needed for starch production, even when chlorophyll is present
  4. Black paper adds iodine to the leaf
Correct answer: C
The covered region had chlorophyll but no light and made no starch. This shows light is also necessary.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: writing one sentence connecting the practical result to the shaded sukuma wiki
Q8. Sukuma wiki moved under a mango tree grew slowly and became pale. Which explanation best uses the starch test evidence?
  1. Shade gives the plant too much water, so it cannot make starch
  2. Plants in shade make food only from the soil
  3. Shade removes all chlorophyll at once, so the plant makes extra starch
  4. Less light means less photosynthesis, so less starch and glucose are made for growth
Correct answer: D
The practical showed that without light, starch is not made even when chlorophyll is present. Less light means less food, so slower growth.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: annotating the leaf cross-section diagram with a label for each zone
Q9. A learner wants to write a result for the white region of a leaf. Which statement is the best evidence-based conclusion?
  1. The region had starch but it was hidden by the white colour
  2. The region stayed brown, so it had no starch because it had no chlorophyll to carry out photosynthesis
  3. The region stayed brown because iodine does not work on white leaves
  4. The region turned blue-black because it had no starch
Correct answer: B
A good conclusion states the observation (brown), the inference (no starch) and the reason (no chlorophyll, so no photosynthesis). Blue-black means starch is present, not absent.
Use after: End of lesson — Model: annotated leaf cross-section diagram, Zone 3 (covered green) exit check
Q10. On your annotated leaf diagram, you label a green region that was covered with black paper and gave no starch. What should the label say about why starch is absent?
  1. It had no chlorophyll and no light
  2. It had light but no chlorophyll
  3. It had chlorophyll but received no light
  4. It had no carbon dioxide because it was covered
Correct answer: C
The covered zone was green, so chlorophyll was present. The missing factor was light, which is why no starch formed.
