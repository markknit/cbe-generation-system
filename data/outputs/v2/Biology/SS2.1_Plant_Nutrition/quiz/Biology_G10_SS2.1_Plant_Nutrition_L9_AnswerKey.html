Q1. In a maize field in Nakuru, the older lower leaves are turning uniformly yellow while the younger upper leaves stay green. Which mineral is most likely deficient?
  1. Phosphorus
  2. Iron
  3. Zinc
  4. Nitrogen
Correct answer: D
Nitrogen is mobile in the plant and is moved from old leaves to young ones, so yellowing (chlorosis) shows first on the older leaves. Iron deficiency shows on young leaves, zinc causes white striping, and phosphorus causes purple-tinged leaves.
Use after: Observe phase — CER worksheet: photograph E, iron-deficient spinach, and the magnesium vs iron interveinal chlorosis discussion
Q2. Spinach grown on an urban farm in Nairobi shows yellowing between the veins, but only on the youngest leaves. The older leaves are still green. Which mineral is most likely deficient?
  1. Magnesium
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Iron
  4. Phosphorus
Correct answer: C
Iron is immobile in the plant, so a shortage appears first on the youngest leaves. Magnesium and nitrogen deficiencies show first on older leaves.
Use after: Observe phase — CER worksheet: photograph D, magnesium-deficient tea from Kericho, and the old vs young leaves teacher prompt
Q3. Tea plants in Kericho and spinach in Nairobi both show yellowing between the veins (interveinal chlorosis). In the tea plants it appears on the older leaves first. Why does magnesium deficiency show on older leaves first?
  1. Magnesium is immobile, so it stays in the young leaves
  2. Older leaves are closer to the roots, so they run out of magnesium first
  3. Magnesium is mobile, so the plant moves it from older leaves to young growing leaves
  4. Older leaves receive more sunlight, so they use up magnesium faster
Correct answer: C
Magnesium is mobile in the phloem. When there is too little, the plant transports it from older leaves to young leaves, so the older leaves show the symptoms first.
Use after: Explain phase — Class discussion: how root hair cells absorb mineral ions by active transport
Q4. How do mineral ions such as nitrates and magnesium ions from the soil enter a sukuma wiki plant?
  1. They enter through the stem bark and are carried down to the roots
  2. Root hair cells absorb them by osmosis, in the same way as water
  3. Root hair cells absorb them by active transport, which uses energy from respiration
  4. The leaves absorb them through the stomata
Correct answer: C
Mineral ions are dissolved in soil water and are taken up by root hair cells by active transport. This needs energy from respiration, and it is different from osmosis, which only moves water.
Use after: Explain phase — 'So What?' paragraph and teacher check for the misconception that plants 'eat minerals'
Q5. A learner writes: 'A sukuma wiki plant eats minerals from the soil as its food.' Which statement corrects this idea?
  1. Minerals give the plant energy directly, so the plant does not need light
  2. Minerals are needed to build things like chlorophyll and proteins, but the plant's food is glucose made by photosynthesis
  3. Minerals are the main food of the plant, and glucose is only a waste product
  4. Minerals are the plant's food, but only when the plant is growing under shade
Correct answer: B
Minerals are raw materials for making chlorophyll, proteins and other structures. They are not food. The plant's food is glucose, which it makes in photosynthesis.
Use after: Explain phase — 'So What?' paragraph: which cause fits the plant under the mango tree best
Q6. A sukuma wiki plant growing under a mango tree has pale yellowish-green leaves and grows slowly. Which explanation fits best?
  1. The mango tree only causes the problem by blocking carbon dioxide, so minerals are not involved
  2. The shade gives less light for photosynthesis and chlorophyll making, and the mango tree's roots may also take nitrogen from the soil, adding a second problem
  3. The leaves are pale only because the soil under the mango tree has too much water
  4. The shade gives the plant too many minerals, which turns the leaves pale
Correct answer: B
Low light is the main cause of the paleness and slow growth. Competition from the mango roots for nitrogen and other minerals can add a mineral deficiency, which also reduces chlorophyll.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative model revision: annotation arrows from mineral ions to the plant structures they support
Q7. Which pairing of a mineral and the plant part or substance it helps to build or support is correct?
  1. Calcium – the cell wall
  2. Phosphorus – stomatal guard cells
  3. Magnesium – the cell wall
  4. Potassium – chlorophyll
Correct answer: A
Calcium supports the cell wall. Nitrogen and magnesium are needed for chlorophyll, potassium supports guard cells, and phosphorus supports DNA and cell membranes.
