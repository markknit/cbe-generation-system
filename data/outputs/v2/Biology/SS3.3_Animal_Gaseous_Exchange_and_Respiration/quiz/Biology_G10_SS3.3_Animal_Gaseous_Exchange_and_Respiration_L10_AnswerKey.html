Use after: Predict phase — Scenario A: Achieng, ugali and githeri before the 400 m race
Q1. Achieng ate a large plate of ugali and githeri before a race, so her muscles have plenty of glucose. In a yeast experiment, the rate of CO₂ production rose as glucose concentration increased, then levelled off (plateau) at high glucose concentrations. What does the plateau show?
  1. The yeast cells stopped respiring because the glucose was used up
  2. Adding more glucose always makes respiration faster
  3. Glucose is no longer needed for respiration at high concentrations
  4. Enzymes are working at their maximum rate, so extra glucose cannot speed up respiration
Correct answer: D
At the plateau the enzyme active sites are saturated, so more substrate cannot increase the rate. The common misconception is that more glucose always means faster energy release.
Use after: Predict phase — Scenario B: Kamau, maize farmer at 2,400 m altitude
Q2. Kamau farms at 2,400 m in the Rift Valley, where the air has 21% oxygen but lower air pressure. A farmer in Mombasa does the same task at sea level. Which prediction is best?
  1. Kamau's cells get less oxygen per breath, so his maximum aerobic respiration rate is lower
  2. Kamau's aerobic respiration rate is higher because the air is thinner
  3. Altitude has no effect on respiration because only glucose matters
  4. Kamau's cells get the same oxygen per breath because the air is still 21% oxygen
Correct answer: A
Lower air pressure means each breath delivers fewer oxygen molecules even though the percentage is the same, so less oxygen reaches the cells for aerobic respiration.
Use after: Observe phase — Graph 2: VO₂ max of Kenyan long-distance runners at different altitudes (DESCRIBE prompt)
Q3. Kenyan long-distance runners were tested at Nairobi (1,795 m), Eldoret (2,085 m) and a camp near Mt. Kenya (3,000 m). Their maximum aerobic respiration rate (VO₂ max) decreased as altitude increased. Which statement correctly DESCRIBES the pattern without explaining it?
  1. Respiration rate falls because each breath delivers fewer oxygen molecules
  2. Runners at high altitude have damaged lungs, so their respiration rate falls
  3. Maximum aerobic respiration rate falls as altitude rises
  4. The runners at 3,000 m used more glucose, so the rate fell
Correct answer: C
A description states only the pattern in the data (rate decreases as altitude increases). The other options give explanations or unsupported claims, which belong to the explain phase.
Use after: Observe phase — Graph 1: Substrate concentration vs. rate of CO₂ production in yeast (IDENTIFY prompt)
Q4. In a yeast experiment, glucose concentrations of 0%, 2%, 5%, 10% and 20% were used and CO₂ production was measured. Which variable is the factor affecting respiration, and which is the measure of respiration rate?
  1. Factor: CO₂ production; measure: glucose concentration
  2. Factor: glucose concentration; measure: CO₂ production
  3. Factor: CO₂ production; measure: time of day
  4. Factor: yeast; measure: glucose concentration
Correct answer: B
Glucose concentration (substrate) is the variable changed, so it is on the x-axis. CO₂ production is the response that shows the rate of respiration. Mixing these up is a common graph-reading error.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: CER discussion on oxygen availability (Graph 2)
Q5. Why does lower oxygen availability reduce the rate of aerobic respiration?
  1. Oxygen is the substrate that is broken down to release energy
  2. Oxygen makes enzymes of glycolysis denature
  3. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain, so less oxygen slows ATP production
  4. Oxygen is only needed to remove lactic acid after exercise
Correct answer: C
Without enough O₂ the electron transport chain slows and less ATP is made, so cells rely more on anaerobic respiration. Oxygen is not the substrate; glucose is.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: CER discussion on oxygen availability, linking the burning sensation to the step-up data
Q6. After 2 minutes of step-ups, a learner breathes hard but still feels a burning sensation in the leg muscles. What is the best explanation?
  1. The muscles have run out of glucose completely
  2. Breathing hard damages the lungs and causes burning in the legs
  3. Too much oxygen has reached the muscles and burned them
  4. Oxygen delivery to the muscle cells is not fast enough, so some anaerobic respiration produces lactic acid
Correct answer: D
When oxygen supply to muscle cells cannot keep up with demand, cells use anaerobic respiration and lactic acid builds up, causing the burning feeling.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: CER discussion on surface area of respiratory membrane (Fick's Law)
Q7. A disease destroys alveoli and reduces the respiratory surface area of a person's lungs from 70 m² to 30 m². According to the idea that diffusion rate is proportional to surface area, what happens?
  1. More oxygen diffuses into the blood per breath
  2. Carbon dioxide is no longer produced by the cells
  3. Less oxygen diffuses into the blood, so the maximum aerobic respiration rate falls
  4. Oxygen diffusion stays the same because breathing rate does not change
Correct answer: C
A smaller respiratory surface allows less oxygen to diffuse per unit time, which lowers the maximum rate of aerobic respiration, as in emphysema.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: concept map, HEAT REGULATION node with Kenyan example
Q8. A tea picker in Kericho works in the cold highlands and keeps her body temperature steady. Which life process that depends on energy from cellular respiration does this show?
  1. Active transport
  2. Heat regulation
  3. Reproduction
  4. Growth
Correct answer: B
Energy released by respiration produces heat that helps maintain body temperature in cold conditions.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: concept map, ACTIVE TRANSPORT node with Kenyan example
Q9. Which example correctly shows ACTIVE TRANSPORT using energy from respiration?
  1. Absorption of glucose from digested githeri into the cells lining the small intestine against a concentration gradient
  2. A runner's leg muscles contracting during a marathon
  3. Water vapour leaving the body when breathing out
  4. Oxygen diffusing from the alveoli into the blood
Correct answer: A
Active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient and needs ATP from respiration. Oxygen diffusion is passive; muscle contraction is movement.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative model revision: annotation 2 (O₂ in blood to electron transport chain)
Q10. On a cumulative respiratory model, a learner adds the note: 'Rate decreases if O₂ falls, such as at high altitude, so less ATP is made, anaerobic respiration occurs and lactic acid causes burning.' Where should the arrow for this note point?
  1. The alveolar membrane
  2. The glucose label in the blood
  3. The windpipe
  4. The electron transport chain section of the mitochondrion
Correct answer: D
Oxygen is used at the electron transport chain in the mitochondrion, so the oxygen-availability annotation belongs there. Surface area is annotated on the alveolar membrane.
