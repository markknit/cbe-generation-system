Use after: Observe phase — Step 1 (Word Equation): glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy (ATP)
Q1. Which word equation correctly shows aerobic respiration in a muscle cell?
  1. ATP + oxygen → glucose + carbon dioxide + water
  2. glucose + carbon dioxide → oxygen + water + ATP
  3. glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + ATP
  4. oxygen + water → glucose + carbon dioxide + ATP
Correct answer: C
Glucose and oxygen are the reactants. Carbon dioxide, water and ATP (energy) are the products.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 2 (Balanced Chemical Equation): pair atom-counting of C, H and O
Q2. Mwangi's muscles use the balanced equation C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP. How many hydrogen atoms are in 6H₂O on the right-hand side?
  1. 6
  2. 24
  3. 12
  4. 18
Correct answer: C
Each H₂O has 2 hydrogen atoms, so 6 × 2 = 12 hydrogen atoms. This matches the 12 hydrogen atoms in C₆H₁₂O₆, so the equation is balanced for hydrogen.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 3 (Mitochondrion Annotation): arrows for reactants entering and products leaving
Q3. A learner annotates a diagram of a mitochondrion. Which set of arrows is correct for aerobic respiration?
  1. Glucose and oxygen enter; carbon dioxide, water and ATP leave or are made
  2. ATP and oxygen enter; glucose and carbon dioxide leave
  3. Only oxygen enters; only ATP leaves
  4. Carbon dioxide and water enter; glucose and ATP leave
Correct answer: A
The reactants (glucose-derived fuel and oxygen) go in. The products (CO₂, H₂O and ATP) come out. ATP is made, not taken in.
Use after: Observe phase — Naming the three stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain
Q4. Aerobic respiration has three stages. Which stage takes place in the cytoplasm, outside the mitochondrion, and gives only 2 ATP per glucose?
  1. The Krebs cycle
  2. The electron transport chain
  3. Glycolysis
  4. Breathing in
Correct answer: C
Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm, where glucose is split into pyruvate with a small yield of 2 ATP. The Krebs cycle and electron transport chain happen in the mitochondrion, where most ATP is made.
Use after: Explain phase — Teacher addresses the misconception 'Oxygen IS the energy' after the CER exercise
Q5. A student says, "Oxygen is the energy that makes my muscles work." Which statement is the best correction?
  1. Oxygen is stored in muscles and used up first before any food is used.
  2. Glucose is the fuel, and oxygen allows it to be fully broken down to release its energy as ATP.
  3. Oxygen is turned into glucose, and glucose is the energy.
  4. Oxygen is the fuel, and glucose only helps to carry it to the cells.
Correct answer: B
Glucose holds the chemical energy. Oxygen is the enabler that lets glucose be fully broken down so the energy is released as ATP.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim–Evidence–Reasoning exercise about Achieng's breathing and pulse rates
Q6. During 2 minutes of step-ups, Achieng's breathing rate rose from 16 to 34 breaths per minute and her pulse rose from 74 to 128 beats per minute. Which explanation best uses aerobic respiration?
  1. Her lungs needed to get bigger, so her heart beat faster to push them open.
  2. Oxygen is the energy her muscles use directly, so she had to breathe more to store energy.
  3. Her body made less carbon dioxide during exercise, so she breathed faster to get more of it.
  4. Her muscle cells needed more ATP, and making ATP uses oxygen, so faster breathing and heartbeat bring oxygen in and carry carbon dioxide away.
Correct answer: D
More muscle contraction needs more ATP. Aerobic respiration uses glucose and oxygen to make ATP and produces CO₂. Faster breathing and pulse deliver oxygen and remove CO₂.
Use after: DQB phase — New red DQB card: 'What happens when the oxygen supply cannot keep up with the ATP demand?'
Q7. Achieng felt a burning sensation in her legs even though she was breathing hard. The aerobic respiration equation assumes something that may not always be true during hard exercise. What is it?
  1. That muscle cells do not need ATP
  2. That glucose is made inside the mitochondrion
  3. That enough oxygen is always available to the muscle cells
  4. That carbon dioxide is never produced
Correct answer: C
The equation for aerobic respiration only works when oxygen is available. The burning raises the new question of what happens when oxygen cannot keep up with ATP demand.
Use after: Model Building phase — Adding the cellular layer to the cumulative model: CO₂ leaving the mitochondrion → blood → alveolus → exhaled
Q8. In a model of a muscle cell, carbon dioxide leaves the mitochondrion. Which path should the model show for this carbon dioxide?
  1. It moves into the blood, then to the alveoli, and is exhaled.
  2. It is turned back into glucose inside the muscle cell.
  3. It stays in the mitochondrion until the muscle stops working.
  4. It is used to make ATP, which leaves the cell.
Correct answer: A
CO₂ made in respiration diffuses from the cell into the blood. The blood carries it to the lungs, where it passes into the alveoli and is exhaled. This links to the rise in CO₂ during exercise.
