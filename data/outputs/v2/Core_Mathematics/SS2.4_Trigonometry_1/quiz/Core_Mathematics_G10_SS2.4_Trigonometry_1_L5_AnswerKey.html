Q1. In Construction A, a group draws an isosceles right-angled triangle with both legs (AB and AC) 6 cm long. What is the exact length of the hypotenuse BC?
  1. 6√3 cm (about 10.39 cm)
  2. 12 cm
  3. √6 cm (about 2.45 cm)
  4. 6√2 cm (about 8.49 cm)
Correct answer: D
By Pythagoras, BC² = 6² + 6² = 72, so BC = √72 = 6√2 ≈ 8.49 cm. Adding the legs (12 cm) is the common error.
Use after: Observe phase — Construction B: equilateral triangle of side 8 cm bisected into a 30-60-90 triangle, measuring PM
Q2. In Construction B, a group draws an equilateral triangle PQR with side 8 cm and bisects it by drawing PM perpendicular to QR, where M is the midpoint of QR. What is the exact length of PM?
  1. 4√3 cm (about 6.93 cm)
  2. 12 cm
  3. 4√2 cm (about 5.66 cm)
  4. 4 cm
Correct answer: A
QM = 4 cm and QP = 8 cm, so PM² = 8² − 4² = 48 and PM = √48 = 4√3 ≈ 6.93 cm. This is longer than QM but shorter than QP.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Exact derivation for the 45° triangle (legs = 1 unit, hypotenuse = √2)
Q3. An isosceles right-angled triangle has both legs 1 unit long and a hypotenuse of √2 units. What is the exact value of sin 45°?
  1. √2
  2. 1
  3. 1/√2
  4. 1/2
Correct answer: C
sin 45° = opposite/hypotenuse = 1/√2. Choosing √2 comes from inverting the ratio, and 1 is the value of tan 45°, not sin 45°.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Exact derivation for the 30-60-90 triangle (short leg = 1, long leg = √3, hypotenuse = 2)
Q4. A 30-60-90 triangle has sides 1 (opposite 30°), √3 (opposite 60°) and 2 (the hypotenuse). What is the exact value of tan 30°?
  1. √3/2
  2. 1/2
  3. 1/√3
  4. √3
Correct answer: C
tan 30° = opposite/adjacent = 1/√3. Using √3 mixes up the sides, which gives tan 60°. √3/2 is sin 60°, and 1/2 is sin 30°.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Mast application (angle of elevation 60°, distance 40 m)
Q5. A surveyor in Nakuru stands 40 m from the base of a Kenya Power mast and measures the angle of elevation to the top as exactly 60°. Using tan 60° = √3, what is the height of the mast?
  1. 40√3 m (about 69.3 m)
  2. 80 m
  3. 40 m
  4. 40/√3 m (about 23.1 m)
Correct answer: A
height = 40 × tan 60° = 40 × √3 = 40√3 ≈ 69.3 m. Dividing by √3 comes from wrongly using tan 60° = 1/√3.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: looking back at the trig-table columns to complete the values for 0° and 90°
Q6. Why is tan 90° described as undefined?
  1. tan 90° = 1, because the opposite side and the hypotenuse become equal
  2. tan 90° = 0, because the adjacent side becomes 0
  3. The adjacent side approaches 0, and division by zero is not possible
  4. The opposite side becomes 0, so there is nothing to divide
Correct answer: C
tan θ = opposite/adjacent. As the angle approaches 90°, the adjacent side approaches 0, and dividing by 0 has no answer, so tan 90° is undefined.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB phase: blue sticky note new question 'What if the angle is 50° — can we get an exact answer, or must we use tables?'
Q7. After today's lesson, a group asks: 'Can we find the exact height of the Nakuru mast if the angle of elevation is 50°?' Which answer is correct?
  1. No, a mast can only be measured when the angle is 30°, 45° or 60°
  2. Yes, use the 60° triangle, because 50° is close to 60°, and the answer will be exact
  3. The two special triangles give exact values only for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, so for 50° we use tables or a calculator
  4. Yes, use the 45° triangle, because 50° is close to 45°, and the answer will be exact
Correct answer: C
Exact surd values come only from the special triangles. For a non-special angle such as 50°, we use tables or a calculator, which gives an approximate value.
