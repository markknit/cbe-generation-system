Q1. Which list puts these materials in order from the LOWEST resistivity to the HIGHEST resistivity?
  1. Copper, silicon, germanium, glass
  2. Glass, silicon, germanium, copper
  3. Copper, germanium, silicon, glass
  4. Germanium, copper, silicon, glass
Correct answer: C
Copper is about 1.7 x 10^-8 ohm metre, germanium about 0.46, silicon about 2.3 x 10^3 and glass 10^10 or more ohm metre. The same order matches the energy gaps: copper 0 eV, germanium 0.67 eV, silicon 1.1 eV, glass above 5 eV.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: temperature dataset for pure silicon
Q2. A pure silicon sample has a resistivity of about 2300 ohm metre at 300 K, about 600 ohm metre at 320 K and about 180 ohm metre at 340 K. What do these data show?
  1. Silicon conducts less as it gets warmer
  2. Silicon only conducts once it is above 340 K
  3. Silicon's conduction does not change with temperature
  4. Silicon conducts better as it gets warmer
Correct answer: D
Resistivity falls as temperature rises, so conductivity (1/resistivity) rises. Warmer pure silicon conducts better.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: copper dataset and conductivity-against-temperature graphs
Q3. A copper wire has a resistivity of about 1.7 x 10^-8 ohm metre at 300 K, about 1.8 x 10^-8 at 320 K and about 2.0 x 10^-8 at 340 K. How does copper's behaviour with temperature compare with that of pure silicon?
  1. Both copper and silicon become more resistive when warmed
  2. Copper's resistance rises slightly when warmed, but silicon's resistivity falls steeply
  3. Copper's resistance falls steeply when warmed, just like silicon's
  4. Neither copper nor silicon changes when warmed
Correct answer: B
Copper's resistivity increases slightly with temperature, while silicon's decreases a lot. Metals and pure semiconductors respond in opposite ways.
Use after: Explain phase — Energy band picture: explaining copper, glass, silicon and germanium
Q4. Silicon has an energy gap of 1.1 eV and glass has an energy gap above about 5 eV. Why does pure silicon conduct a little at room temperature while glass conducts almost nothing?
  1. Silicon has no energy gap, so its electrons are all free
  2. Glass has no electrons in its atoms
  3. Silicon's gap is small enough for a few electrons to gain the energy to jump it, but glass's gap is too large
  4. Silicon atoms hold more electrons than glass atoms, so they conduct
Correct answer: C
At room temperature a few electrons in silicon gain enough thermal energy to cross the small gap and become free. In glass the gap is so large that almost no electrons cross. Glass still has electrons, and silicon still has a gap.
Use after: Explain phase — Energy band picture: free electrons, holes and electron-hole pairs
Q5. In pure silicon, an electron gains enough energy to jump across the energy gap and becomes a free electron. What is left behind in the valence band?
  1. A hole, so an electron-hole pair forms and the silicon stays electrically neutral
  2. An extra positive particle that is added to the silicon from the air
  3. Nothing, because the free electron comes from outside the silicon
  4. A hole, which makes the silicon piece positively charged overall
Correct answer: A
The electron leaves an empty place called a hole, and the free electron and hole together form an electron-hole pair. No charge is added or removed, so the silicon stays neutral overall.
Use after: Explain phase — Think-pair-share: why germanium conducts more than silicon at the same temperature
Q6. At the same temperature, germanium (energy gap 0.67 eV) has lower resistivity than silicon (energy gap 1.1 eV). What is the correct reason?
  1. Germanium has a larger gap, so its electrons are pushed across it more easily
  2. Germanium has a smaller gap, so more electrons cross it and more electron-hole pairs form
  3. Germanium is a metal, so its resistance rises with temperature
  4. Germanium has no gap, so it behaves like copper
Correct answer: B
A smaller gap means more electrons gain enough energy to cross it at the same temperature. That gives more electron-hole pairs and lower resistivity.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: arrow labelled 'warmer, more pairs, higher conductivity' and exit prompt
Q7. Which sentence best explains why warming increases the conduction of pure silicon?
  1. Warming removes the energy gap completely
  2. Warming adds extra electrons to the silicon from the surroundings
  3. Warming gives more electrons enough energy to cross the gap, forming more electron-hole pairs
  4. Warming makes silicon behave like a metal, so its resistance rises
Correct answer: C
Higher temperature gives more electrons enough energy to cross the gap. More electron-hole pairs means more charge carriers and higher conductivity. The gap stays the same, and the silicon stays neutral.
