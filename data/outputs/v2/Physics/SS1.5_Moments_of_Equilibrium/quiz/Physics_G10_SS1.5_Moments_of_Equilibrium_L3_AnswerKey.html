Q1. A learner is asked to mark the 'centre of gravity' of a flat piece of cardboard cut in an irregular shape. Which statement correctly describes the centre of gravity?
  1. The point through which the total weight of the body appears to act
  2. It is always at the exact middle of the outline, whatever the shape
  3. The point farthest from the ground when the shape lies flat
  4. The lowest edge of the shape, because weight pulls downward
Correct answer: A
Centre of gravity is defined as the point through which the whole weight of a body appears to act. For an irregular shape it depends on how the mass is spread, so it is not always at the geometric middle.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: Plumb-Line Centre of Gravity Hunt
Q2. A group hangs an irregular cardboard shape from three different pin-holes in turn. Each time they hang a plumb line (string with a nut) from the same pin and draw the vertical line it makes. How do they find the centre of gravity of the shape?
  1. It is the point where the nut touches the cardboard
  2. It is the middle of the longest of the three lines only
  3. It is the hole that is nearest to the edge of the cardboard
  4. It is the point where the three plumb lines cross
Correct answer: D
When the shape hangs freely, the plumb line shows the vertical direction through the centre of gravity. The centre of gravity lies on each line, so it is where the three lines meet.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity B: Tipping Towers (wide-base Tower A and narrow-base Tower B with 500 g mass)
Q3. Tower A is made of 4 light blocks side by side, 1 block high (wide base). Tower B is made of 4 light blocks stacked 1 block wide and 4 blocks tall (narrow base). A 500 g mass is slid from the centre of the top outwards towards the edge of each tower. Which tower topples first, and why?
  1. Tower A, because a wider base always makes a tower fall more easily
  2. Tower B, because its centre of gravity is higher and its base is narrower, so the vertical line through it leaves the base sooner
  3. Tower A, because it has more blocks touching the table
  4. Both topple at the same moment, because the 500 g mass has the same weight on each
Correct answer: B
A tall narrow tower has a higher centre of gravity and a smaller base, so only a small overhang of the mass moves the vertical line through the centre of gravity outside the base. The same weight is on both towers, but the geometry is different.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: co-constructing 'A body topples when the vertical line through its centre of gravity passes ____ the base of support'
Q4. Complete the sentence: A body topples when the vertical line through its centre of gravity passes ______ its base of support.
  1. just above the edge of but still within
  2. exactly through the middle of
  3. outside
  4. inside
Correct answer: C
Toppling happens once the vertical line from the centre of gravity falls outside the base of support. While the line stays inside the base, the body returns upright.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: three canonical diagrams of equilibrium (sphere on a flat surface)
Q5. A solid sphere (like a ball) rests on a flat table. If it is pushed slightly, it rolls to a new position and stays there without returning or toppling. Its centre of gravity is always directly above its contact point with the table. Which state of equilibrium is this?
  1. Stable equilibrium
  2. Neutral equilibrium
  3. Unstable equilibrium
  4. It is not in equilibrium at all
Correct answer: B
In neutral equilibrium, a small push moves the body to a new position where it is again in balance, because the centre of gravity stays directly above the contact point. It neither returns (stable) nor topples (unstable).
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: annotating the Jiko stool diagram with the jerrycan at the corner (CG, base of support, vertical CG line)
Q6. A three-legged Jiko stool stands steady with a sufuria of ugali near its centre. A full 20-litre jerrycan is then placed on one corner of the seat, overhanging past the leg, and the stool is nudged and topples. Which explanation is best?
  1. The jerrycan makes the stool lighter, so it is easier to push over
  2. The jerrycan lowers the centre of gravity so much that the stool cannot stay upright
  3. The weight of the stool stops acting once a jerrycan is placed on it
  4. The combined centre of gravity of stool and jerrycan moves so that its vertical line falls outside the triangle formed by the legs
Correct answer: D
The stool and jerrycan act as one system, and the combined centre of gravity shifts towards the overhanging corner. When its vertical line falls outside the base of support formed by the legs, the stool topples.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: Teacher question 1 about the Kericho craftsman's taller stool with narrower legs
Q7. A craftsman in Kericho makes a stool taller but with narrower legs, so the base is smaller. What happens to the stability of the stool, and why?
  1. It becomes less stable, because the centre of gravity is higher and the base is smaller, so a small tilt puts the vertical line outside the base
  2. It becomes more stable, because a taller stool has more weight
  3. It becomes more stable, because a smaller base means less area to tip over
  4. Its stability does not change, because the weight of the stool is the same
Correct answer: A
A higher centre of gravity and a narrower base mean that only a small tilt moves the vertical line through the centre of gravity outside the base. This makes the stool less stable.
