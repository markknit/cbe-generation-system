Q1. A bao plank is 2 m long and balanced on a log at its midpoint. Child A (mass 40 kg) sits 30 cm from the centre. Child B (mass 20 kg) sits on the opposite side. How far from the centre must Child B sit for the plank to balance?
  1. 90 cm
  2. 15 cm
  3. 60 cm
  4. 30 cm
Correct answer: C
At balance, 40 kg × 30 cm = 1200 kg·cm, so 20 kg × d = 1200 kg·cm and d = 60 cm. The lighter child must sit farther from the pivot.
Use after: Explain phase — Learning outcome (a): defining the moment of a force and its SI unit, when learners state the Principle of Moments
Q2. Which statement correctly defines the moment of a force and gives its SI unit?
  1. Force × time taken; measured in newton-seconds (N·s)
  2. Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot; measured in newton-metres (N·m)
  3. Mass × distance from the pivot; measured in kilograms (kg)
  4. Force ÷ distance from the pivot; measured in newtons per metre (N/m)
Correct answer: B
The moment of a force is the force multiplied by its perpendicular distance from the pivot, so its SI unit is the newton-metre (N·m).
Use after: Observe phase — Metre-rule balance experiment: Data Checkpoint on comparing the two moment columns
Q3. A metre rule is balanced on a pencil pivot at its 50 cm mark. A 100 g bag of beans hangs 20 cm to the left of the pivot. A 50 g bag of beans hangs 30 cm to the right of the pivot. What happens to the rule?
  1. It stays balanced because 100 g and 50 g are in the ratio 2 : 1
  2. It tips to the right because the right bag is farther from the pivot
  3. It tips to the left because the left moment (2000 g·cm) is greater than the right moment (1500 g·cm)
  4. It stays balanced because the masses are both small
Correct answer: C
Left moment = 100 g × 20 cm = 2000 g·cm. Right moment = 50 g × 30 cm = 1500 g·cm. The larger moment is on the left, so the rule tips left.
Use after: Observe phase — Metre-rule balance experiment: computing moment = mass × distance for each trial
Q4. In the metre-rule experiment, a group hangs an 80 g bag of beans 25 cm from the pivot. What is the moment of this bag about the pivot?
  1. 2000 g·cm
  2. 105 g·cm
  3. 3.2 g·cm
  4. 200 g·cm
Correct answer: A
Moment = mass × distance = 80 g × 25 cm = 2000 g·cm. The unit is g·cm because mass is in grams and distance in centimetres.
Use after: Explain phase — Pooling class data and stating the Principle of Moments
Q5. Which statement correctly gives the Principle of Moments for a body in equilibrium?
  1. The sum of all the forces on the body is always zero, whatever the distances from the pivot
  2. The sum of clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of anti-clockwise moments about the same point
  3. The mass on the left of the pivot equals the mass on the right of the pivot
  4. The distance of the load on the left from the pivot equals the distance of the load on the right
Correct answer: B
Balance depends on both force and distance. At equilibrium, the total clockwise moment equals the total anti-clockwise moment about the same point. Equal masses or equal distances alone are not required.
Use after: Explain phase — Individual problem 2: sufuria of ugali on the market weighing plank
Q6. A market weighing plank is balanced on a pivot. A sufuria of ugali of mass 3 kg is placed 0.4 m from the pivot on one side. What mass of maize grain placed 0.6 m from the pivot on the other side will balance it?
  1. 4.5 kg
  2. 3 kg
  3. 1.2 kg
  4. 2 kg
Correct answer: D
At balance, 3 kg × 0.4 m = m × 0.6 m. So 1.2 = 0.6 m and m = 2 kg.
Use after: Explain phase — Individual problem 3: the Jiko stool, calculating the toppling moment of the jerrycan about the tipping edge
Q7. A narrow three-legged Jiko stool has a 20 kg jerrycan on it. Its weight is 200 N and its centre is 0.10 m beyond the tipping edge. Taking the tipping edge as the pivot, what is the toppling moment of the jerrycan?
  1. 20 N·m
  2. 2 N·m
  3. 7.5 N·m
  4. 200 N·m
Correct answer: A
Toppling moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot = 200 N × 0.10 m = 20 N·m.
Use after: Explain phase — Individual problem 3: repeating the Jiko stool calculation for the wider four-legged stool
Q8. A wider four-legged stool has a mass of 8 kg (weight 80 N). Its centre of gravity is 0.30 m inside its tipping edge. A 20 kg jerrycan (weight 200 N) sits with its centre 0.10 m beyond the tipping edge. What happens when the stool is nudged?
  1. It topples, because any load placed beyond the tipping edge always causes toppling
  2. It rocks and returns upright, because the restoring moment (80 N × 0.30 m = 24 N·m) is greater than the toppling moment (200 N × 0.10 m = 20 N·m)
  3. It topples, because the jerrycan's weight (200 N) is greater than the stool's weight (80 N)
  4. It stays perfectly balanced, because the restoring moment equals the toppling moment
Correct answer: B
The restoring moment of the stool is 80 × 0.30 = 24 N·m. The toppling moment of the jerrycan is 200 × 0.10 = 20 N·m. Since 24 N·m is greater than 20 N·m, the stool rocks back. Comparing the weights alone is not enough, because the distances matter too.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: adding the 'balance condition' annotation to the cumulative Jiko stool diagram
Q9. When groups add a 'balance condition' annotation to their Jiko stool model, which statement correctly describes when the stool is stable about its tipping edge?
  1. When the toppling moment of the load beyond the tipping edge is greater than the restoring moment of the stool's weight
  2. When the restoring moment from the stool's weight is greater than the toppling moment from the load beyond the tipping edge
  3. When the jerrycan is heavier than the stool
  4. When the stool has more than three legs, whatever the moments
Correct answer: B
The stool stays stable when its restoring moment about the tipping edge is greater than the toppling moment of the load. Toppling happens when the toppling moment becomes larger. The number of legs or the weight of the load alone does not decide this.
