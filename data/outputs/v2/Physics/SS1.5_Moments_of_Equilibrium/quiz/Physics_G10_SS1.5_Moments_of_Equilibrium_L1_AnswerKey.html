Q1. In a class demonstration, the same full 20-litre jerrycan was placed on one corner of a narrow three-legged Jiko stool, with part of it overhanging beyond the leg, and the stool was given a gentle nudge. The jerrycan was then placed at the same corner with the same small overhang on a wider, lower four-legged stool, and that stool was nudged too. What was observed?
  1. Both stools toppled over.
  2. Neither stool moved at all.
  3. The four-legged stool toppled, but the three-legged stool stayed upright.
  4. The three-legged stool toppled, but the four-legged stool rocked and returned upright.
Correct answer: D
Demos 2 and 3 showed the same jerrycan in the same place toppling the narrow stool while the wide four-legged stool only rocked and self-righted. This is an observation only, not an explanation.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Beam-Balance Turning Activity (50 g at 5 cm left, 50 g at 10 cm right)
Q2. A 30 cm ruler is balanced on a pencil at its centre. A 50 g mass is placed 5 cm from the pivot on the left side. A second 50 g mass is placed 10 cm from the pivot on the right side. What happens?
  1. The ruler stays balanced because both masses are 50 g.
  2. The ruler does not move because the pivot is at the centre.
  3. The left side goes down because it is closer to the pivot.
  4. The right side goes down because the same mass is further from the pivot.
Correct answer: D
Both masses are equal, but the one at 10 cm gives a bigger turning effect than the one at 5 cm. So the right side goes down.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Beam-Balance Turning Activity (finding where a 50 g mass balances 100 g at 6 cm)
Q3. On a ruler balanced at its centre on a pencil, a 100 g mass is placed 6 cm to the left of the pivot. A single 50 g mass is placed on the right side to balance it. How far from the pivot should the 50 g mass be?
  1. 12 cm
  2. 6 cm
  3. 24 cm
  4. 3 cm
Correct answer: A
Balance needs equal mass × distance on each side: 100 g × 6 cm = 600 g·cm. So 50 g × d = 600 g·cm, giving d = 12 cm.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Concept Introduction via Guided Notes (defining MOMENT OF A FORCE)
Q4. Which statement correctly defines the moment of a force?
  1. The weight of the object only, measured in newtons
  2. The turning effect of a force about a pivot, equal to the force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the pivot
  3. The force divided by its distance from the pivot
  4. The distance from the pivot to the object only, measured in metres
Correct answer: B
A moment is the turning effect of a force about a pivot: M = F × d. It depends on both the force and the perpendicular distance, not on weight alone or distance alone.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Worked Example with Kenyan Context (sufuria of ugali on a Jiko stool)
Q5. A sufuria containing 4 kg of ugali has a weight of 40 N. It is placed 0.3 m from one leg of a Jiko stool, and that leg acts as the pivot. What is the moment of the sufuria's weight about that leg?
  1. 13.3 N·m
  2. 12 N·m
  3. 120 N·m
  4. 1.2 N·m
Correct answer: B
M = F × d = 40 N × 0.3 m = 12 N·m.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Concept Introduction via Guided Notes (labelling pivot, force and perpendicular distance)
Q6. A student pushes on the handle of a kiosk door to open it. The door turns on its hinges. Which part is the pivot for this turning effect?
  1. The student's hand
  2. The handle
  3. The hinges
  4. The middle of the door
Correct answer: C
The door turns about its hinges, so the hinges are the pivot. The push is the force, and the perpendicular distance is measured from the hinges to the line of action of the push.
Use after: Model Building phase — Initial Model Construction (labelled force arrow F = 200 N and calculated moment)
Q7. In a sketch-model, a full 20-litre jerrycan has a mass of 20 kg, so its weight is 200 N (taking 10 N per kg). Its weight acts 0.1 m from the pivot leg of a stool. What is the moment of the jerrycan's weight about that leg?
  1. 200.1 N·m
  2. 20 N·m
  3. 2000 N·m
  4. 2 N·m
Correct answer: B
M = F × d = 200 N × 0.1 m = 20 N·m. The common error is to multiply the mass (20 kg) by the distance instead of the weight in newtons.
Use after: End of lesson — Driving Question Board (clustering the 'I wonder...' questions) and the closing remarks of the Model phase
Q8. At the end of Lesson 1, the class has named the turning effect (moment) of the jerrycan about a leg. Which statement best describes where the class has got to on the stool puzzle?
  1. The four-legged stool did not topple only because its wood is stronger.
  2. We can describe moments, but we cannot yet fully explain why the four-legged stool did not topple, so this stays on the Driving Question Board.
  3. It is settled that heavier loads always topple stools, so nothing is left to find out.
  4. The class has fully explained why every stool topples or stays upright.
Correct answer: B
Lesson 1 introduces moments and opens the Driving Question Board. The full explanation of why the two stools behave differently is not yet reached and is left for later lessons.
