Q1. Two identical tall cardboard boxes stand on a table. Each has the same lump of clay stuck inside. In Box A the clay is near the top. In Box B the clay is on the inside base. The table is tilted slowly. Which box will topple first, and why?
  1. Neither box topples, because the clay holds the box in place
  2. Box A, because its centre of gravity is higher, so the vertical line through it leaves the base after only a small tilt
  3. Both topple at the same time, because the boxes and clay have the same mass
  4. Box B, because the clay at the bottom makes it heavier at the base and easier to push over
Correct answer: B
Clay near the top raises the centre of gravity, so the box needs only a small tilt before the vertical line through the centre of gravity falls outside the base. Mass is equal in both boxes, so it is the position of the mass that matters.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation A: Centre of Gravity Height (clay at top vs clay at base, critical tipping angle table)
Q2. In Investigation A, a student tilts a tall box until it just topples. With two clay lumps taped at the top, the critical tipping angle is 12°. After moving both lumps to the inside base, the critical tipping angle is 31°. What does this result show?
  1. The clay lumps became heavier when moved to the base
  2. Lowering the centre of gravity means the box must tilt further before it topples, so it is more stable
  3. The box became wider at the base, so it needs less turning effect to topple
  4. A bigger angle means the box toppled more easily
Correct answer: B
The same box and the same clay gave a larger critical angle when the clay was low. The lower centre of gravity has to be tilted further before the vertical line through it leaves the base, so the box is more stable.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation B: Base of Support Width (flaps folded in, two flaps out, all four flaps out)
Q3. In Investigation B, a pair folds out the cardboard flaps of a box to widen its base from 10 cm to 20 cm to 30 cm, and records the critical tipping angle each time. To make this a fair test of base width, what must they do?
  1. Change the base width and the height of the box together to save time
  2. Use the same box with the same clay, tilt it the same way, and change only the width of the base
  3. Use a different box each time so the results are more varied
  4. Add more clay each time the base is widened
Correct answer: B
A fair test changes one variable at a time. Only the base width may change, and the box, its mass, the position of its centre of gravity and the tilting method stay the same.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher diagram of the tall box with the vertical line through the centre of gravity at the critical tipping moment
Q4. Which statement correctly gives the condition for an object to topple?
  1. It topples when its centre of gravity is at the lowest point of the object
  2. It topples when the vertical line through its centre of gravity falls outside its base of support
  3. It topples whenever it is tilted by any angle at all
  4. It topples when its weight is greater than the force pushing it
Correct answer: B
While the vertical line through the centre of gravity is inside the base, the weight turns the object back upright. Once the line is outside the base, the weight produces a toppling moment and the object falls over.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher redirect of 'heavier base makes it stable' – is it the mass or where the mass is placed?
Q5. Two tall boxes have exactly the same total mass. In Box X the heavy mass is kept at the bottom. In Box Y the same heavy mass is kept near the top. A student says, "Box X is more stable because it is heavier." What is the best correction?
  1. Box Y is more stable because mass near the top balances the box
  2. Both boxes are equally stable because they have the same mass
  3. Box X is more stable because its extra weight pushes down harder on the table
  4. Box X is more stable because the heavy mass is low, which keeps its centre of gravity low; the total mass is the same in both
Correct answer: D
The masses are equal, so Box X is not heavier. The stability difference comes from where the mass is placed: a low centre of gravity means the box must tilt further before the vertical line leaves the base.
Use after: Observe phase — Case study: 'A Tower-Crane Overturning: A Composite Case Study', guided question 1 (using the word moment)
Q6. A tower crane on a building site lifts a heavy concrete block and swings it far beyond the crane's rated radius. The crane overturns. Which explanation uses the idea of moments correctly?
  1. The crane was too wide at the base, so it could not stay upright
  2. The turning effect (weight × distance from the tipping edge) of the load became greater than the opposing moment of the crane's own weight and counterweight
  3. The block became lighter as it moved outwards, so the crane lost balance
  4. The block's weight stopped acting once the crane swung it outside the base
Correct answer: B
Moment = force × perpendicular distance. A larger radius gives the load a larger moment about the tipping edge. When it exceeds the opposing moment from the crane's weight and counterweight, the crane overturns.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: engineering example inset (double-decker bus) labelled with the two stability strategies
Q7. Double-decker buses in Nairobi have the heavy engine, gearbox and fuel tank placed low in the chassis, and the wheels are set wide apart. How do these two design choices make the bus less likely to topple on a bend?
  1. The low heavy parts make the bus heavier, and a heavier bus cannot tilt
  2. The wide wheels and low parts lower the bus's speed, so it cannot tilt
  3. They move the centre of gravity to the top, which balances the bus
  4. They keep the centre of gravity low and the base wide, so the vertical line through it stays inside the base even when the bus leans
Correct answer: D
A low centre of gravity and a wide wheel track both mean the bus must lean much further before the vertical line through its centre of gravity leaves the base. Those are the two strategies found in the investigations.
Use after: End of lesson — Model: Stool-vs-Jerrycan scenario update (why the narrow stool topples but the wide stool does not)
Q8. A three-legged Jiko stool topples when a full 20-litre jerrycan is placed on one corner so that it overhangs the seat edge. A wider, lower four-legged stool with the same jerrycan in the same place only rocks and then returns upright. Which is the best explanation?
  1. The four-legged stool has more legs, and the number of legs alone decides stability
  2. The four-legged stool is stronger, so the jerrycan weighs less on it
  3. The jerrycan's weight acts only on the three-legged stool
  4. The wide, low stool has a bigger base and a lower centre of gravity, so the vertical line through its centre of gravity stays inside the base
Correct answer: D
The jerrycan weighs the same in both cases. The wide, low stool keeps the vertical line through the combined centre of gravity inside its base, so the weight turns it back upright. On the narrow stool the line moves outside the base and it topples.
