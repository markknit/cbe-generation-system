Q1. During a class discussion, a student says: "Only factories add greenhouse gases to the air." Which reply is best?
  1. That is correct, because factory chimneys are the only place where smoke is made.
  2. That is incomplete: farming, burning charcoal and clearing forests also add greenhouse gases in Kenya.
  3. That is incomplete: only cooking fires add greenhouse gases, and factories do not.
  4. That is correct, because cattle and forests only take gases in and never release them.
Correct answer: B
Many Kenyan greenhouse gases come from everyday activities such as burning charcoal, keeping livestock and clearing forests, not only from factories.
Use after: Observe phase — Observation table using the Kenya sectoral emissions bar graph and global pie chart data sheet
Q2. A bar graph shows Kenya's greenhouse gas emissions by sector: agriculture, energy, land use, waste and industrial processes. Which two sectors together account for more than 75 percent of Kenya's national emissions?
  1. Energy and waste
  2. Waste and industrial processes
  3. Industrial processes and energy
  4. Agriculture and land use
Correct answer: D
The Kenya data shows that agriculture and land-use change together make up more than 75 percent of national emissions. Industrial emissions are visually dramatic but much smaller.
Use after: Observe phase — Group observation table: livestock (CH4) and the agriculture bar
Q3. Cattle farming is part of the agriculture sector in Kenya's emissions data. Which greenhouse gas do cattle release, and how?
  1. Oxygen, released when cattle digest grass
  2. Carbon monoxide, released through cattle sweat
  3. Methane, made by microbes in the stomach and released mainly by belching
  4. Methane, released only when cattle dung is burned
Correct answer: C
Microbes in a cow's stomach make methane (CH₄) during digestion, and it is released mainly by belching. This is called enteric fermentation.
Use after: Explain phase — Evidence claim: In Kenya, [source] releases [gas] because [mechanism] (charcoal burning example)
Q4. A family in Nairobi cooks ugali on a charcoal jiko. Which gas does burning charcoal release, and why?
  1. Carbon dioxide, because carbon in the charcoal combines with oxygen when it burns
  2. Carbon dioxide, because charcoal absorbs heat from the Sun and gives it out as gas
  3. Oxygen, because burning produces oxygen from the wood
  4. Methane, because charcoal contains stored methane gas
Correct answer: A
Charcoal is mostly carbon. When it burns, the carbon combines with oxygen from the air and forms carbon dioxide (CO₂).
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class share-out and T-chart: Mau Forest deforestation, gas and mechanism
Q5. Large areas of the Mau Forest are cleared. Why does this increase carbon dioxide in the atmosphere?
  1. The carbon stored in the wood is released as CO₂ when it burns or rots, and fewer trees remain to absorb CO₂
  2. Trees never take in carbon dioxide, so removing them changes nothing
  3. Cut trees release methane, which turns into carbon dioxide in the air
  4. Cleared land moves closer to the Sun, so more carbon dioxide forms
Correct answer: A
Trees store carbon taken from CO₂ over many years. Clearing them releases this carbon as CO₂ when the wood burns or rots, and the loss of trees means less CO₂ is absorbed.
Use after: Explain phase — Third sentence of the evidence claim: connecting to the Lesson 1 physics and the PhET simulation
Q6. Using the physics of the greenhouse effect, why does adding more CO₂ to the atmosphere increase warming?
  1. More CO₂ molecules reflect all sunlight back into space, which warms the atmosphere
  2. More CO₂ molecules make the Earth's surface give out less infrared radiation
  3. More CO₂ molecules absorb more of the infrared radiation re-emitted by Earth's surface, trapping more heat
  4. More CO₂ molecules move the Earth closer to the Sun
Correct answer: C
CO₂ absorbs and re-emits infrared radiation from Earth's surface. More CO₂ means more infrared is trapped, which strengthens the greenhouse effect.
Use after: Model Building phase — Revise the cumulative atmospheric model: add the sources layer with three Kenyan sources and their gases
Q7. A student adds a sources layer to their atmospheric model. Which set of labels is correct for Kenyan sources?
  1. Charcoal burning gives CO₂; livestock give CH₄; Mau Forest clearing gives CO₂
  2. Charcoal burning gives CO₂; livestock give CO₂ only; Mau Forest clearing removes all greenhouse gases
  3. Charcoal burning gives CH₄; livestock give CO₂; Mau deforestation gives oxygen
  4. Charcoal burning gives CH₄; livestock give CH₄; Mau Forest clearing gives CH₄
Correct answer: A
Burning charcoal and clearing forests release CO₂, while livestock release CH₄ made by microbes in their stomachs.
