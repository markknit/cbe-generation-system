Use after: Predict phase — Predict: bob held at a small height, 'Where is it fastest?'
Q1. A 0.5 kg bob on a string is pulled up to one side and released so that it swings like a pendulum. Where is the bob moving fastest?
  1. It moves at the same speed everywhere
  2. At the lowest point of the swing
  3. At the highest point of the swing, where it starts
  4. Half-way up either side of the swing
Correct answer: B
The bob loses height and so loses potential energy as it swings down. That energy becomes kinetic energy, so the speed is greatest at the lowest point. At the highest point the bob stops for an instant.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Part A: pendulum, PE at top = mgh
Q2. A bob of mass 0.5 kg is raised 0.2 m above its lowest point. Using PE = mgh with g = 10 N/kg, what is its potential energy at the top?
  1. 0.1 J
  2. 10 J
  3. 0.98 J
  4. 1 J
Correct answer: D
PE = mgh = 0.5 x 10 x 0.2 = 1 J. Leaving out g gives 0.1 J. Using g = 9.8 gives 0.98 J, but this lesson uses g = 10 N/kg.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Part A: pendulum, KE at bottom = ½mv²
Q3. The 0.5 kg bob passes the lowest point of its swing at a speed of 2 m/s. Using KE = ½mv², what is its kinetic energy there?
  1. 1 J
  2. 2 J
  3. 0.5 J
  4. 4 J
Correct answer: A
KE = ½mv² = ½ x 0.5 x 2² = ½ x 0.5 x 4 = 1 J. The common error is forgetting to square v, which gives 0.5 J.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Part B: roller coaster, KE at the bottom with v = 20 m/s
Q4. A roller coaster car of mass 1000 kg reaches the bottom of a hill at a speed of 20 m/s. Ignoring friction, what is its kinetic energy at the bottom?
  1. 200 000 J
  2. 400 000 J
  3. 10 000 J
  4. 20 000 J
Correct answer: A
KE = ½mv² = ½ x 1000 x 20² = ½ x 1000 x 400 = 200 000 J. Not squaring v gives 10 000 J. Forgetting the ½ gives 400 000 J.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, Part A: PE and KE graph for the free-fall of the bucket
Q5. A 20 kg bucket falls down a well from 8 m above the water surface. Ignoring friction, its PE at 8 m is 1600 J and its KE is 0 J. When it is 2 m above the water its PE is 400 J. What is its KE at this point?
  1. 800 J
  2. 1200 J
  3. 400 J
  4. 1600 J
Correct answer: B
The PE lost changes into KE. The bucket has lost 1600 - 400 = 1200 J of PE, so its KE is 1200 J. This matches the KE column of the table.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, Part C: PE gain of the bucket by hand or with the pulley system
Q6. Mama Kavata lifts a 20 kg bucket (weight 200 N) by hand from the water surface to 8 m above it. Mwende lifts the same bucket the same 8 m using a pulley system. How much PE does the bucket gain in each case?
  1. 1600 J by hand and less than 1600 J with the pulley system
  2. 1600 J in both cases
  3. 1600 J by hand and more than 1600 J with the pulley system
  4. 800 J in both cases
Correct answer: B
The PE gain depends only on weight and height gained: 200 x 8 = 1600 J (or 20 x 10 x 8). The pulley does not appear in PE = mgh, so the gain is the same either way.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: sorting questions into 'answered today', 'partly answered' and 'still to investigate'
Q7. After this lesson, which question about the bucket in the Kitui well is still NOT answered?
  1. How does PE change into KE when the bucket falls?
  2. Which formula gives the PE of the bucket at a given height?
  3. How much PE does the bucket gain when lifted 8 m?
  4. Why does the pulley system feel easier to use when the bucket gains the same energy?
Correct answer: D
The lesson answered how much PE the bucket gains (1600 J), how energy changes form (PE to KE), and which formula to use (PE = mgh). It did not explain why the pulley needs less effort, so that question stays on the DQB.
