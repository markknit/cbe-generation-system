Q1. At a well in Kitui, Mama Kavata lifts a 20 kg bucket of water (weight 200 N) straight up 8 m by hand at a steady speed. How much work does she do on the bucket?
  1. 208 J
  2. 160 J
  3. 1600 J
  4. 25 J
Correct answer: C
Work = force x distance = 200 N x 8 m = 1600 J. The other values come from multiplying the mass by the distance, adding the numbers, or dividing them.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: bar-chart sketch of 'energy given by the person' and 'energy gained by the bucket' for each method
Q2. A 20 kg bucket is raised 8 m up a well, once by hand and once using a pulley system that needs a smaller pulling force. Which statement about the energy gained by the bucket is correct?
  1. It is zero in both cases, because the bucket moves at a steady speed.
  2. It is more with the pulley, because the pulley adds extra energy.
  3. It is the same both ways, because the bucket rises the same height.
  4. It is less with the pulley, because the pulling force is smaller.
Correct answer: C
The bucket's energy gain depends on its weight and the height it rises (the gain in PE), not on the method used. Less effort does not mean less energy gained, and a pulley does not create energy.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: model lift with spring balance, string and two pulleys
Q3. In a model lift, a learner pulls a string over a two-pulley arrangement with a steady effort of 12 N. The string moves 0.8 m while the 2 kg mass rises 0.4 m. How much work does the learner do on the string?
  1. 8.0 J
  2. 12.8 J
  3. 9.6 J
  4. 4.8 J
Correct answer: C
Work input = effort x distance the string moves = 12 N x 0.8 m = 9.6 J. This is more than the 8 J (20 N x 0.4 m) needed to lift the mass directly, because of friction.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Kitui data (hand lift 1600 J, pulley system 2000 J, difference 400 J)
Q4. With the pulley system at the Kitui well, Mwende pulls the rope with a steady force of 125 N through 16 m. The 20 kg bucket gains 1600 J of potential energy. How much energy is transferred as heat by friction in the pulleys and rope?
  1. 0 J
  2. 400 J
  3. 1600 J
  4. 2000 J
Correct answer: B
Work input = 125 N x 16 m = 2000 J. Energy transferred as heat = 2000 J - 1600 J = 400 J.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-Evidence-Reasoning: why the pulley system feels easier even though the bucket gains the same energy
Q5. Why does the pulley system feel easier for Mwende than lifting the bucket by hand?
  1. It makes the bucket lighter, so its weight is smaller.
  2. It reduces the work needed to lift the bucket, so the bucket gains less energy.
  3. It gives the bucket extra energy that comes from the pulleys.
  4. It needs a smaller force, but the rope is pulled over a longer distance.
Correct answer: D
The pulley system trades force for distance: 125 N over 16 m instead of 200 N over 8 m. The bucket still gains 1600 J, and its weight is unchanged.
Use after: Explain phase — Sankey-style energy-flow diagram: where is the other 400 J?
Q6. Mwende does 2000 J of work with the pulley system, but the bucket gains only 1600 J of potential energy. What happens to the other 400 J?
  1. It is destroyed by the pulleys.
  2. It is transferred to the surroundings as heat (and a little sound) by friction.
  3. It is stored in the bucket as extra kinetic energy.
  4. It is stored in the bucket as extra potential energy.
Correct answer: B
Energy is conserved. Friction in the pulleys and rope transfers the 400 J to the surroundings as heat and a little sound. The bucket moves at a steady speed, so its KE does not change.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: note that 400 J is 20% of the input
Q7. In the Kitui pulley system, 400 J of the 2000 J of work input is transferred as heat. What percentage of the work input is this?
  1. 25%
  2. 4%
  3. 20%
  4. 80%
Correct answer: C
Percentage = 400 J / 2000 J x 100 = 20%. A learner who divides by 1600 J instead of the input gets 25%.
