Q1. A group wants to measure the potential difference (p.d.) across one bulb in a working circuit. How should the voltmeter be connected?
  1. In the loop next to the bulb, in series with it
  2. Across the bulb, in parallel with it
  3. Between the battery and the switch, in series
  4. Directly across the battery only, never across a bulb
Correct answer: B
A voltmeter is connected in parallel, like a bridge across the component being measured. An ammeter is the instrument that goes in series in the loop.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe phase, measurement (2): series circuit of three bulbs, voltmeter across each bulb in turn
Q2. Three identical bulbs are connected in series to a 9 V battery pack. A voltmeter is connected across ONE of the bulbs. What does it read?
  1. 0.10 V
  2. 9.0 V
  3. 3.0 V
  4. 1.5 V
Correct answer: C
The three identical bulbs share the 9 V equally, so each has 9 ÷ 3 = 3.0 V. The three readings add up to the battery e.m.f.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe phase, measurement (3): parallel circuit of three bulbs, voltmeter across each bulb
Q3. Three identical bulbs are connected in parallel to a 9 V battery pack. A voltmeter is connected across ONE bulb. What does it read, and how bright are the bulbs compared with the same bulbs in series?
  1. 3.0 V, brighter than in series
  2. 9.0 V, brighter than in series
  3. 3.0 V, the same brightness as in series
  4. 9.0 V, dimmer than in series
Correct answer: B
Each parallel bulb is connected across the full battery, so each has 9.0 V. This is why the parallel bulbs glow brightly, while the series bulbs with 3.0 V each are dim.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: porter analogy and 'What does 1 V mean?' (1 volt is one ___ per ___)
Q4. A 9 V battery is described as having an e.m.f. of 9 V. Which statement correctly uses the idea 1 V = 1 J/C?
  1. The battery pushes a current of 9 amperes round the circuit
  2. The battery stores 9 joules of energy in total
  3. The battery supplies 9 coulombs of charge every second
  4. The battery supplies 9 joules of energy to every coulomb of charge that passes through it
Correct answer: D
One volt is one joule per coulomb, so 9 V means 9 J of energy supplied to each coulomb. Coulombs per second would be current, and the stored total energy depends on the battery's capacity.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: CER question 'What is the difference between e.m.f. and p.d.?'
Q5. Which pair of statements correctly describes the difference between e.m.f. and potential difference (p.d.)?
  1. e.m.f. is the energy per coulomb supplied by the source; p.d. is the energy per coulomb transferred in a component
  2. e.m.f. is the current in the source; p.d. is the current in a component
  3. e.m.f. is the energy per coulomb transferred in a component; p.d. is the energy per coulomb supplied by the source
  4. e.m.f. is measured in volts; p.d. is measured in amperes
Correct answer: A
e.m.f. belongs to the source, which supplies energy to each coulomb. p.d. belongs to a component, where energy is transferred as light and heat. Both are measured in volts.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: pair work calculating the energy given up by charge passing through a 3.0 V bulb and a 9.0 V bulb
Q6. A charge of 4 C passes through a bulb that has a p.d. of 3.0 V across it. How much energy is transferred by the bulb as light and heat? (Use energy = charge × p.d.)
  1. 12 J
  2. 0.75 J
  3. 7 J
  4. 1.33 J
Correct answer: A
Energy = charge × p.d. = 4 C × 3.0 V = 12 J. Adding the numbers (7) or dividing them gives wrong answers.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building phase: caption on why the series bulb is dim, using energy per coulomb
Q7. In a series circuit of three identical bulbs on a 9 V battery, the current is 0.10 A at every point in the loop, and each bulb has 3.0 V across it. Which statement best explains why each series bulb is dim?
  1. Each coulomb of charge gives up only 3 J to each bulb, and the charge itself is not used up
  2. The charge is used up as it passes through each bulb, so less is left for the next bulb
  3. The voltage is used up by the first bulb, so the other bulbs get less current
  4. Each bulb receives the full 9 V but only a third of the battery's current
Correct answer: A
The current is the same everywhere, so charge is not used up. Each coulomb carries 9 J and gives up 3 J at each of the three bulbs, so each bulb has only 3.0 V across it and glows dimly.
