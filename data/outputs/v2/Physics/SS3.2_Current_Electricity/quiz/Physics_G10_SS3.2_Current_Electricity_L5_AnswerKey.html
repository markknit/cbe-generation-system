Q1. A group wants to measure the current through a resistor and the p.d. across it. How should they connect the two meters?
  1. Ammeter in parallel with the resistor, voltmeter in series with it
  2. Both meters in series with the resistor
  3. Ammeter in series with the resistor, voltmeter in parallel across the resistor
  4. Both meters in parallel across the resistor
Correct answer: C
All the current must pass through the ammeter, so it goes in series. The voltmeter measures the p.d. between two points, so it goes in parallel across the resistor.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: adding the V/I column to the data table
Q2. A group measures a 30 Ω resistor and finds: 1.5 V gives 0.05 A, 3.0 V gives 0.10 A, 4.5 V gives 0.15 A. They add a column V/I and get 30 Ω every time. What does this show?
  1. The resistor uses up more current as the p.d. rises
  2. The resistance stays constant, so the current is directly proportional to the p.d.
  3. The resistance falls as the current rises
  4. The resistance of the resistor rises as the p.d. rises
Correct answer: B
V/I is the resistance. It stays at 30 Ω, so doubling the p.d. doubles the current: the current is directly proportional to the p.d.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: plotting V against I and drawing a line of best fit
Q3. A graph is plotted for a fixed 30 Ω resistor at constant temperature, with V on the y-axis and I on the x-axis. What does the graph look like?
  1. A straight line passing through the origin
  2. A curve that gets steeper as the current rises
  3. A straight line that does not pass through the origin
  4. A horizontal straight line
Correct answer: A
For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, V is directly proportional to I. The graph is therefore a straight line through the origin.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: calculating the gradient of the V-I graph using two points far apart
Q4. On a V-I graph for a resistor (V on the y-axis, I on the x-axis), two points on the line of best fit are (0.10 A, 3.0 V) and (0.25 A, 7.5 V). What is the gradient, and what does it represent?
  1. 0.033 Ω, the resistance of the resistor
  2. 30 A, the current in the resistor
  3. 4.5 V, the p.d. across the resistor
  4. 30 Ω, the resistance of the resistor
Correct answer: D
Gradient = (7.5 - 3.0)/(0.25 - 0.10) = 4.5/0.15 = 30. Since V is on the y-axis and I is on the x-axis, the gradient is V/I, which is the resistance in ohms.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'If I doubled the resistance to 60 Ω and kept 9 V, what would the current be?'
Q5. A 30 Ω resistor passes 0.30 A when connected to a 9 V supply. The 30 Ω resistor is replaced by a 60 Ω resistor and the supply stays at 9 V. What is the current now?
  1. 0.60 A
  2. 0.15 A
  3. 540 A
  4. 0.30 A, because the supply is still 9 V
Correct answer: B
Using I = V/R = 9/60 = 0.15 A. Doubling the resistance for the same p.d. halves the current.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: series loop problem (9 V supply, 0.10 A, find total resistance)
Q6. Three bulbs are connected in series to a 9 V battery. The measured current in the loop is 0.10 A. Using R = V/I, what is the total resistance of the loop?
  1. 0.011 Ω
  2. 90 Ω
  3. 0.9 Ω
  4. 30 Ω
Correct answer: B
R = V/I = 9/0.10 = 90 Ω. The 30 Ω is the resistance of one bulb only, not the whole loop.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: KICD extension, classifying ohmic and non-ohmic conductors using the resistor graph and the bulb reading
Q7. A filament bulb gives a V/I value that gets larger as it heats up and glows brighter. How should this bulb be described, and why?
  1. Non-ohmic, because it uses up current as it glows
  2. Non-ohmic, because its V/I does not stay constant as the filament heats up
  3. Ohmic, because it obeys V = IR at every temperature
  4. Ohmic, because its resistance rises with temperature
Correct answer: B
An ohmic conductor has a constant V/I and a straight V-I line through the origin at constant temperature. A filament's V/I changes as it heats up, so it is non-ohmic.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: KICD extension, reading a resistor's colour bands (orange, black, black = 30 Ω)
Q8. A resistor has colour bands brown, black, red. Using the code black 0, brown 1, red 2, the first two bands are digits and the third band is the multiplier. What is its resistance?
  1. 102 Ω
  2. 1000 Ω
  3. 12 Ω
  4. 10 Ω
Correct answer: B
Brown = 1 and black = 0 give 10. Red as multiplier means x 100, so 10 x 100 = 1000 Ω (1 kΩ).
