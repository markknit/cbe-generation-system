Use after: Observe phase — Station A (Length): nichrome wire tested at 25 cm, 50 cm, 75 cm and 100 cm
Q1. In a length test, a piece of thin nichrome wire 25 cm long has a resistance of 9 Ω. The same wire (same thickness, same temperature) is now used at a length of 100 cm. What is its resistance?
  1. 18 Ω
  2. 9 Ω
  3. 13 Ω
  4. 36 Ω
Correct answer: D
Resistance is proportional to length. 100 cm is 4 times 25 cm, so R = 4 × 9 = 36 Ω.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B (Thickness): one strand versus two strands twisted together to double the area
Q2. At Station B, a single strand of nichrome wire has a resistance of 20 Ω. Two identical strands of the same length are twisted together to double the cross-sectional area. What is the resistance of the twisted pair (same length, same temperature)?
  1. 10 Ω
  2. 5 Ω
  3. 40 Ω
  4. 20 Ω
Correct answer: A
Doubling the area halves the resistance, so R = 20 ÷ 2 = 10 Ω. A thicker wire has lower resistance.
Use after: Observe phase — Station D (Temperature): the 9 V, 2.7 W demo bulb on one cell and then three cells
Q3. A bulb filament is connected to one 1.5 V cell. It is dim and cool, and R = V/I gives 15 Ω. It is then connected to three cells (4.5 V). It is brighter and hotter, and R = V/I gives 30 Ω. What best explains the higher resistance?
  1. Resistance depends only on the current, so more current always means more resistance.
  2. The filament is hotter, and the resistance of a metal increases when its temperature rises.
  3. The voltmeter reads incorrectly at higher voltages.
  4. The filament became a different material when it got brighter.
Correct answer: B
The material, length and thickness are unchanged. Only the temperature changed, and for metals resistance increases as temperature increases.
Use after: Observe phase — Demonstration: copper connecting wire next to the 30 Ω bulb filament
Q4. The same current flows through a short copper connecting wire and through a bulb filament of 30 Ω. The filament glows but the copper wire stays cool. Why?
  1. The copper wire is thicker, so it resists the current more and stays cool.
  2. The filament has a much higher resistance, so more energy is transferred as heat in it.
  3. Current is used up in the filament but not in the copper wire.
  4. Copper is an insulator, so no current flows through it.
Correct answer: B
For the same current, a component with a much higher resistance transfers much more energy as heat. The filament has a high resistance (30 Ω) and copper has a very low resistance.
Use after: Explain phase — CER explanation: 'Why does a long thin extension cable heat up?'
Q5. A long thin extension cable becomes warm when a heater is used on it, but a short thick cable carrying the same current stays cool. Which is the best explanation?
  1. The resistance of the cable depends only on the current, not on its length or thickness.
  2. Thick wire resists more because it is bigger, so the thick cable should heat up more.
  3. The long thin cable has a higher resistance, so more energy is transferred as heat for the same current.
  4. The long thin cable has a lower resistance, so more current flows and it heats up.
Correct answer: C
Resistance increases with length and decreases as thickness (area) increases. A long thin cable therefore has a high resistance and transfers more energy as heat for the same current.
Use after: Explain phase — KICD extension: R = ρL/A worked with the nichrome example, finding R for a 2.0 m length
Q6. Nichrome has resistivity ρ = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m. A nichrome wire has cross-sectional area A = 3.1 × 10⁻⁸ m². Using R = ρL/A, what is the resistance of a 2.0 m length of this wire?
  1. 35 Ω
  2. 71 Ω
  3. 7.1 × 10⁻⁵ Ω
  4. 18 Ω
Correct answer: B
R = ρL/A = (1.1 × 10⁻⁶ × 2.0) ÷ (3.1 × 10⁻⁸) = about 71 Ω. This is double the value for 1.0 m (about 35 Ω).
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision: add an arrow showing that a larger resistance means a smaller current for the same p.d. (R = V/I)
Q7. A 9 V battery is connected across a 30 Ω bulb filament. It is then connected across a longer, thinner wire of resistance 90 Ω. Using R = V/I, what is the current in the 90 Ω wire?
  1. 0.1 A
  2. 10 A
  3. 810 A
  4. 0.3 A
Correct answer: A
I = V/R = 9 ÷ 90 = 0.1 A. This is smaller than the 0.3 A in the 30 Ω filament, so a larger resistance gives a smaller current for the same p.d.
