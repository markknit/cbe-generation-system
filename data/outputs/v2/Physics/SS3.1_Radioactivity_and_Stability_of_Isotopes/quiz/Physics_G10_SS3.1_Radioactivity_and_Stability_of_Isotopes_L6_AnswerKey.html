Q1. In the PhET simulation, a single neutron is fired at a uranium-235 nucleus and the nucleus undergoes fission. Which list correctly describes what is produced?
  1. Only energy, because the uranium nucleus disappears completely
  2. Two smaller nuclei only, because the neutrons are absorbed and never released
  3. Two smaller nuclei (fission fragments), two or three neutrons and energy
  4. One larger nucleus and a flash of light, with no other particles
Correct answer: C
Fission splits a heavy nucleus into two smaller fragments and releases 2–3 neutrons plus energy. These neutrons are what make a chain reaction possible.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A, Step 2: enable 'Chain Reaction' mode and fire one neutron
Q2. A single neutron hits a uranium-235 nucleus in a block of uranium-235. Soon many nuclei are splitting. What best explains why the reaction keeps going?
  1. Each fission adds new uranium to the block, so there is always more fuel
  2. The first neutron keeps bouncing between nuclei and splits each one in turn
  3. The energy released changes the uranium into a different element, which then splits
  4. The neutrons released by each fission can strike other uranium-235 nuclei and split them
Correct answer: D
Each fission releases 2–3 neutrons, and each of these can cause another fission. This cascade is called a chain reaction.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A, Step 3: 'Nuclear Reactor' tab, adding control rods
Q3. Control rods are lowered into a nuclear reactor core. What do they do, and why is this important in a power plant?
  1. They absorb neutrons, which slows the chain reaction so that it stays controlled
  2. They cool the steam so that the turbine can turn faster
  3. They turn the uranium into a stable element so that it stops being radioactive
  4. They add extra neutrons so that more energy is produced
Correct answer: A
Control rods absorb neutrons, so fewer fissions are triggered. This lets operators keep the chain reaction steady in a reactor, unlike in a weapon where it is left to run away.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Mass Defect and E = mc² (worked example)
Q4. Cooking ugali is a chemical reaction, and the total mass at the end is the same as at the start. In a nuclear reaction such as fission, what is different?
  1. The products have slightly more mass than the starting nucleus because energy is added to them
  2. The products have slightly less mass than the starting nucleus, and the missing mass has become energy
  3. The missing mass escapes as a gas that is too light to measure
  4. The mass is the same, but the energy comes from the neutrons escaping as steam
Correct answer: B
In nuclear reactions the products weigh slightly less than the starting nucleus. The 'missing' mass (mass defect) is converted to energy by E = mc², and it is not material that escapes.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Mass Defect and E = mc² (worked example: 1 gram becomes 9 × 10¹³ J)
Q5. Using E = mc² with c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, how much energy is released when 2 grams (0.002 kg) of mass is completely converted to energy?
  1. 1.8 × 10¹⁴ J
  2. 1.8 × 10¹⁷ J
  3. 6 × 10⁵ J
  4. 9 × 10¹³ J
Correct answer: A
E = mc² = 0.002 × (3 × 10⁸)² = 0.002 × 9 × 10¹⁶ = 1.8 × 10¹⁴ J. The mass must be in kilograms and c must be squared.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Fission vs. Fusion Comparison Table
Q6. Which statement correctly compares nuclear fission and nuclear fusion?
  1. Fission joins light nuclei such as hydrogen isotopes, and fusion splits heavy nuclei such as uranium-235
  2. Fission splits a heavy nucleus such as uranium-235, while fusion joins light nuclei and releases more energy per kilogram of fuel
  3. Fission releases more energy per kilogram of fuel than fusion, because it splits the nucleus
  4. Both processes split heavy nuclei, but fusion needs no heat or pressure
Correct answer: B
Fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus. Fusion is the joining of light nuclei, needs extreme heat and pressure, and releases even more energy per kilogram of fuel.

Q8. Kenya's Nuclear Power and Energy Agency (NuPEA, formerly KNEB) is in the news. What is its role in Kenya's energy planning?
  1. It mines uranium in Kwale County for export
  2. It controls the water released from the Turkwel hydroelectric dam
  3. It runs the Olkaria geothermal power stations and sells electricity to households
  4. It leads Kenya's planning for nuclear energy, including a proposed 1,000 MW nuclear power plant
Correct answer: D
NuPEA is the agency that plans Kenya's nuclear power programme, including the proposed 1,000 MW plant. Olkaria (geothermal) and Turkwel (hydro) are separate existing energy sources.
