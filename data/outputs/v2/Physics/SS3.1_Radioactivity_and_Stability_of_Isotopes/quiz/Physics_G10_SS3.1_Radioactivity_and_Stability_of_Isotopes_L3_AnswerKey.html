Q1. In a Geiger-Müller (GM) counter simulation, there is no radioactive source anywhere near the tube, yet the counter still clicks about 20 times in 30 seconds. What is the best explanation?
  1. The clicks are caused by light entering the tube
  2. The GM tube produces its own radiation when it is switched on
  3. The counter is faulty and should be replaced
  4. The tube detects background radiation from natural sources such as cosmic rays, rocks and soil
Correct answer: D
Radiation is always present at a low level from cosmic rays, radon, rocks, building materials and food, so a GM tube clicks even with no source present. This is called background radiation.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Virtual GM Counter, (iii) Shielding test with paper, aluminium and lead
Q2. A GM counter gives a high count rate near a radioactive source. When a single sheet of paper is placed between the source and the tube, the count rate drops to the background level. Which type of radiation does the source emit?
  1. Beta particles only
  2. Gamma rays only
  3. Alpha particles
  4. Gamma rays and beta particles
Correct answer: C
Alpha particles are heavy and strongly ionising, so they have a very short range and are stopped by paper. Beta particles need aluminium to be reduced, and gamma rays pass through paper.

Q3. Which sequence correctly explains how a GM tube produces a 'click' when radiation enters it?
  1. Radiation heats the argon gas, the gas expands, and the expansion is counted as a click
  2. Radiation enters the mica window and ionises argon atoms, the freed electrons accelerate to the central electrode and cause an avalanche, and the resulting electrical pulse is counted
  3. Radiation makes the argon gas glow, and a light sensor in the counter detects the glow
  4. Radiation melts the mica window, which completes the circuit and gives a click
Correct answer: B
Ionising radiation frees electrons from argon atoms. The high voltage accelerates them towards the central electrode, producing an avalanche of electrons that gives one pulse, and the counter registers one click.

Q4. In a cloud chamber, an alpha particle leaves a thick, straight track. Which explanation fits best?
  1. Alpha particles are very light, so nothing bends their path
  2. Alpha particles are heavy and cause a lot of ionisation along their path, so the track is thick and they are not easily deflected
  3. Alpha particles are electrically neutral, so they leave a wide trail
  4. Alpha particles are a type of light, so they travel in straight lines
Correct answer: B
Alpha particles are heavy and strongly ionising, so they leave a dense, thick trail of ionisation and travel in a fairly straight line. Light beta particles are deflected more easily and leave thinner, curved tracks.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Leaf electroscope demonstration and the detection summary table
Q5. A positively charged leaf electroscope has its leaf standing out. When a radioactive source is brought near the cap, the leaf slowly falls. Why?
  1. The radiation adds more charge of the same sign to the leaf
  2. The radiation ionises the air, and the ions carry the charge away from the electroscope
  3. The radiation pushes the leaf down with a force like wind
  4. The radiation heats the metal leaf and makes it limp
Correct answer: B
Radiation ionises the air around the cap. The ions carry the charge away, so the electroscope discharges and the leaf falls. This is another effect of ionisation.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Background Radiation Explanation, 'Where is the radiation coming from if there is no source?'
Q6. A GM counter in a Nairobi classroom picks up some counts even with no source present. Which of these is a natural source of that background radiation?
  1. Sound waves from the street outside
  2. The heat from learners' bodies
  3. Cosmic rays from space and radon gas from the soil
  4. Light from the classroom bulbs
Correct answer: C
Background radiation comes from cosmic rays, radon gas, radioactive isotopes in rocks such as granite and building materials, and trace isotopes such as potassium-40 in food. Light, sound and body heat are not ionising radiation from unstable nuclei.

Q7. A pitchblende rock emits alpha, beta and gamma radiation. A GM tube is placed a few centimetres away in air. Which statement correctly describes what is detected?
  1. Gamma rays are stopped by the air gap, while alpha rays pass through easily
  2. Alpha passes through the rock and air most easily, so it is detected most
  3. Gamma rays pass through most barriers and are detected, while alpha is stopped by the rock itself and the air gap
  4. All three types reach the tube equally, because the tube has a window
Correct answer: C
Gamma rays are the most penetrating, beta is partly absorbed, and alpha is stopped by the rock and a few centimetres of air. Reversing alpha and gamma penetration is a common misconception.
