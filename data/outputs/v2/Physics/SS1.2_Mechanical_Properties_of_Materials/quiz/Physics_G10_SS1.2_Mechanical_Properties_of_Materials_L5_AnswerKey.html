Q1. Two rubber strips are both 20 cm long and look the same width. One is new and the other has been left in the sun and stretched many times. A learner says, 'They look the same, so they will both snap at the same load.' What is the best way to decide which strip will snap first?
  1. Measure stress and strain for each strip and compare the stress at which each one fractures
  2. Choose the strip that looks newer, because appearance shows strength
  3. Pick the strip that stretches more, because stretching always means it is stronger
  4. Assume both are equal because they have the same length and width
Correct answer: A
Appearance does not show a material's internal properties. Measuring stress and strain gives numbers (such as fracture stress) that allow a prediction before the test.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 1 and Step 2 of the Stress-Strain Investigation: calculating cross-sectional area, force F = mg and stress F/A
Q2. In the Stress-Strain Investigation, a rubber strip has a width of 10 mm and a thickness of 2 mm. A mass of 120 g hangs from it. Take g = 10 N/kg. What is the stress in the strip?
  1. 600 000 Pa
  2. 60 000 Pa
  3. 6 000 Pa
  4. 0.06 Pa
Correct answer: B
Area = 0.010 m × 0.002 m = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ m². Force = 0.12 kg × 10 N/kg = 1.2 N. Stress = F/A = 1.2 / 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ = 60 000 Pa.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 2 of the Stress-Strain Investigation: calculating extension ΔL and strain (ΔL/L₀) for each load
Q3. A rubber strip has an original length of 20 cm. After a load is added its length is 23 cm. What is the strain?
  1. 6.7
  2. 0.13
  3. 0.15
  4. 3 cm
Correct answer: C
Extension = 23 − 20 = 3 cm. Strain = ΔL/L₀ = 3/20 = 0.15. Strain is a ratio of two lengths, so it has no units.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class debrief: groups calculate E for Strip A and Strip B from the linear portion of their graphs
Q4. From a stress-strain graph, a learner finds that in the linear region a material has a stress of 2.0 × 10⁶ Pa when the strain is 0.25. What is the Young's Modulus of the material?
  1. 2.25 × 10⁶ Pa
  2. 5.0 × 10⁵ Pa
  3. 8.0 × 10⁶ Pa
  4. 1.25 × 10⁻⁷ Pa
Correct answer: C
E = stress ÷ strain = 2.0 × 10⁶ / 0.25 = 8.0 × 10⁶ Pa. E is the gradient of the linear region of the graph.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class debrief: misconception check 'a thicker rope has a higher E' (thick and thin rope, same load)
Q5. Two ropes are made of the same fibre. One is thick and one is thin. Both carry the same load. Which statement is correct?
  1. The thick rope has the larger stress and the larger E
  2. The thin rope has the larger stress, and both ropes have the same E
  3. The thin rope has the larger E, because it stretches more
  4. The thick rope has the larger E, because it has more material
Correct answer: B
Stress = F/A, so the thin rope (smaller area) has the larger stress. E is a property of the material, so it is the same for both ropes because the fibre is the same.
Use after: Explain phase — Whole-class debrief: CER discussion linking E-values and fracture stress to the Kericho bridge rope
Q6. Strip B has a lower Young's Modulus than Strip A. It also has a lower fracture stress, and it snaps first under the same load. Which statement best explains why Strip B snaps first?
  1. Its lower Young's Modulus means it is stronger, so it snaps first
  2. Its lower Young's Modulus means it is harder, so it snaps first
  3. Its fracture stress is lower, so it breaks at a smaller stress than Strip A
  4. Its strain has units, so it breaks sooner
Correct answer: C
Fracture stress tells how much stress a material can take before it breaks. Young's Modulus only measures stiffness (how much stress is needed for a given strain). Stiffness and strength are separate properties.
Use after: Explain phase — Hardness scratch test: 'who scratches whom' table and ranking from softest to hardest
Q7. In a scratch test, a nail scratches aluminium and chalk. Aluminium scratches chalk but cannot scratch the nail. Chalk cannot scratch either of the others. Which list ranks the samples from softest to hardest?
  1. Chalk, aluminium, nail
  2. Nail, aluminium, chalk
  3. Aluminium, chalk, nail
  4. Chalk, nail, aluminium
Correct answer: A
A sample that scratches another is harder than it. Chalk is scratched by both others, so it is softest. Aluminium is scratched only by the nail, so it is in the middle. The nail is scratched by none, so it is hardest.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: adding the quantitative layer, the stress on the rope from the 80 kg tea-leaf sack
Q8. An 80 kg sack of tea leaves hangs from one rope with a diameter of 10 mm. Take g = 10 N/kg and use A = πr². Which value is the stress in the rope?
  1. 1.0 × 10⁷ Pa
  2. 8.0 × 10⁶ Pa
  3. 2.5 × 10⁶ Pa
  4. 1.0 × 10⁶ Pa
Correct answer: A
F = 80 × 10 = 800 N. Radius = 5 mm = 0.005 m, so A = π × 0.005² ≈ 7.9 × 10⁻⁵ m². Stress = 800 / 7.9 × 10⁻⁵ ≈ 1.0 × 10⁷ Pa.
