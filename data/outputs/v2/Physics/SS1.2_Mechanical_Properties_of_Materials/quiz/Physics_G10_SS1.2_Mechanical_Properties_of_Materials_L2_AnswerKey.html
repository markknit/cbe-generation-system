Q1. A group hangs a rubber band from a clamp. Its original length is 20 cm. With a 300 g load added, its length is 26.5 cm. What is the extension of the rubber band?
  1. 46.5 cm
  2. 6.5 cm
  3. 5.5 cm
  4. 26.5 cm
Correct answer: B
Extension = length under load − original length = 26.5 − 20 = 6.5 cm. Choosing 26.5 cm confuses extension with total length, and 46.5 cm adds the two lengths instead of subtracting.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: structured stretching investigation, 'Length After Load Removed' and 'Returned to Original? (Y/N)' columns
Q2. A 20 cm piece of sisal twine is loaded with 300 g and stretches. After the mass is removed, the twine measures 22 cm. Which statement is correct?
  1. The twine is still elastic, because it stretched when the load was added.
  2. The twine returned to its original length, so it is elastic.
  3. The measurement must be wrong, because materials always return to 20 cm.
  4. The twine has been permanently deformed, so the load was beyond its elastic limit.
Correct answer: D
A material that does not return to its original length after the load is removed has been permanently deformed. This means its elastic limit was passed. Stretching alone does not show elastic behaviour.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: key observation prompt, 'At what load did the material STOP returning to its original length? Mark this row with a ★'
Q3. A group tests a 20 cm copper wire. After 100 g and after 200 g it returned to 20 cm once the mass was removed. After 300 g it stayed at 20.8 cm, and after 400 g and 500 g it stayed longer still. At which load should the group mark the row with a ★ (the load where the wire STOPPED returning to its original length)?
  1. 500 g
  2. 200 g
  3. 300 g
  4. 400 g
Correct answer: C
The ★ marks the first load at which the material did not return to its original length. The wire was still returning after 200 g and first failed to return at 300 g, so the elastic limit lies between 200 g and 300 g.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Step 3, formalising the definitions of ELASTICITY and PLASTIC BEHAVIOUR on the board
Q4. A learner says: 'A material is elastic if it stretches a lot.' A thin copper wire stretched a little and stayed longer after the load was removed. Which is the best correction of the learner's idea?
  1. Elasticity means the material does not stretch at all when a force is applied.
  2. Elasticity is the ability to return to the original shape and size after the deforming force is removed, however much the material stretched.
  3. Elasticity means the material breaks easily when stretched.
  4. Elasticity means the material stays permanently stretched after the force is removed.
Correct answer: B
Elasticity is about returning to the original shape and size, not about how far a material stretches. Copper wire stretched but did not return, so it is not behaving elastically.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Step 2, individual CER statement in the science journal
Q5. In a CER (Claim–Evidence–Reasoning) statement about sisal twine, which sentence is EVIDENCE?
  1. The sisal twine is plastic.
  2. Plastic materials are permanently deformed after a force is removed.
  3. When 300 g was added, the extension was 2.0 cm, and after the load was removed the twine did not return to 20 cm.
  4. This means the twine passed its elastic limit, so it cannot return to its original shape.
Correct answer: C
Evidence is what was measured or observed: the load, the extension and whether the twine returned. 'The twine is plastic' is the claim, and the other two sentences are reasoning or a definition.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: Panel B, plastic material (copper wire under load, then released)
Q6. A thin copper wire is bent by hand and stays bent when released. Which particle-level description best explains this?
  1. The particles are destroyed when the wire is bent.
  2. The particles return to their original positions, but the bonds disappear.
  3. The particles move apart and then spring back to their original positions.
  4. The particles shift to new positions permanently and are held there by new bonds.
Correct answer: D
In plastic deformation, particles move past one another to new positions and are held there by new bonds, so the change stays after the force is removed. Particles that spring back would mean elastic behaviour.
Use after: End of lesson — Model Building: connecting sentence 'The old sisal rope behaved like Panel ___ because…' and the Explain-phase question about the OLD Kericho rope
Q7. Two ropes on a footbridge are made of sisal. Under the same load, the old rope stretches and then snaps without returning to its original length. Which statement best describes the old rope's behaviour?
  1. It behaved elastically, because it is made of a natural fibre.
  2. It behaved plastically, because plastic materials always stretch less than elastic ones.
  3. It behaved plastically, because it was permanently deformed beyond its elastic limit before it snapped.
  4. It behaved elastically, because it stretched a lot.
Correct answer: C
The old rope did not return to its original length, which is permanent (plastic) deformation beyond the elastic limit. Stretching a lot does not make a material elastic, and the fibre type does not decide this.
