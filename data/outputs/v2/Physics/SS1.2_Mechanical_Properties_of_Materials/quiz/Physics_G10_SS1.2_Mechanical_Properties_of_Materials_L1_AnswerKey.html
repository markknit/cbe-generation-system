Q1. In the Kericho story, an 80 kg sack of tea leaves is placed on a footbridge held by two sisal ropes, Rope A and Rope B. Which statement correctly describes what was observed?
  1. Rope A stretched and returned to its original length when the load was removed, while Rope B stretched and then snapped.
  2. Rope A snapped straight away, while Rope B stretched and returned to its original length.
  3. Both ropes stretched and returned to their original length, but Rope B was slower.
  4. Neither rope changed length, but Rope B snapped when the sack was added.
Correct answer: A
The story shows Rope A stretching and springing back, and Rope B stretching permanently and then snapping. The question checks only what was observed.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: sticky-note predictions and teacher clustering of ideas on the board
Q2. After the sticky-note predictions are put on the board, the teacher does not say which ones are right. What is the best way to think about these predictions at this stage?
  1. They are first ideas to be tested with evidence later, so some may need to change.
  2. They are final answers, because every learner wrote one.
  3. They are correct only if most of the class wrote the same idea.
  4. They are wrong, because the teacher did not confirm them.
Correct answer: A
Predictions are first guesses made before evidence is collected. The class holds all ideas and tests them as the unit goes on.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: hands-on exploration with rubber band, sisal twine and copper wire, two-column table
Q3. A group hangs masses from a 15 cm piece of copper wire, then removes all the masses. The wire stays bent and does not return to its original shape. Using the activity card, how should the group classify the copper wire?
  1. Plastic, because it did not return to its original shape when the load was removed
  2. Elastic, because the wire did not break
  3. Fractured, because any change in shape counts as breaking
  4. Elastic, because it changed shape when the masses were added
Correct answer: A
The test for elastic behaviour is whether the material returns to its original shape after unloading. The wire did not, so it is plastic. Bending under load alone does not make a material elastic.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: recording 'What happened when unloaded' in the two-column table
Q4. A group records the length of a piece of sisal twine in its two-column table. Resting length: 15.0 cm. Length while holding 200 g: 16.5 cm. Length after all masses are removed: 16.2 cm. By how much is the twine permanently longer than its resting length?
  1. 1.2 cm
  2. 0.3 cm
  3. 1.5 cm
  4. 16.2 cm
Correct answer: A
Permanent change is the length after unloading minus the resting length: 16.2 − 15.0 = 1.2 cm. This twine did not return to its original length, so it shows plastic behaviour.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: classifying each material as elastic, plastic or fractured
Q5. During the activity, one material stretches as masses are added and then snaps into two pieces before 200 g is reached. Which category on the activity card (elastic, plastic or fractured) fits it best?
  1. Elastic, because it stretched at first
  2. Fractured, because it broke under the load
  3. Plastic, because it is still in one piece but permanently longer
  4. Unchanged, because it did not return to its original length
Correct answer: B
A material that breaks apart under the load is classified as fractured. It cannot be called elastic because it did not return to its original length.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: posting yellow and pink sticky-note questions about why the ropes behaved differently
Q6. A learner says: 'Rope B snapped because it was older, so we already know the reason.' Which response is best at this point in the unit?
  1. The reason has not been explained yet. Age is one idea to test with evidence in later lessons.
  2. The learner is right, because Rope B was thinner than Rope A.
  3. The learner is right, because older things are always weaker.
  4. The learner is wrong, because age never affects a rope.
Correct answer: A
The lesson leaves the puzzle open. Age was only one of the learners' ideas, and the class will test such ideas in later lessons rather than accept them now.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: mini-explanation introducing the terms elastic behaviour and plastic deformation
Q7. Which statement correctly describes elastic behaviour of a material?
  1. The material breaks when a force is applied.
  2. The material stretches a very large amount.
  3. The material returns to its original shape and size when the force is removed.
  4. The material bends and stays bent after the force is removed.
Correct answer: C
Elastic behaviour means the material returns to its original shape and size after the force is removed. Stretchiness alone is not enough.
