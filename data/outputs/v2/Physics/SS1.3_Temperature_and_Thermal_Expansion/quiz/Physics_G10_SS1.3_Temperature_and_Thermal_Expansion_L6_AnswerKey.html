Q1. A Grade 10 learner fills a glass bottle to the brim with water, seals it with a metal cap and leaves it in a freezer overnight. The bottle cracks. Which explanation is correct?
  1. The water molecules arranged into an open hexagonal lattice with more spacing, so the water gained about 9% in volume and pushed outwards on the glass.
  2. The cold air in the freezer pushed inwards on the glass until it cracked.
  3. The water molecules became larger when they were cooled, so the water took up more space.
  4. The glass shrank in the cold more than the water did, so the water was squeezed out.
Correct answer: A
Hydrogen bonding locks water molecules into a hexagonal lattice with greater spacing, so freezing increases the volume by about 9%. This pushes outwards on the glass. The molecules do not get bigger, and cold air does not push the glass in.
Use after: Observe phase — Gallery walk, Station 1: Temperature Scales
Q2. A home freezer is set at -18 °C. Using T(K) = T(°C) + 273, what is this temperature on the Kelvin scale?
  1. -255 K
  2. -18 K
  3. 255 K
  4. 291 K
Correct answer: C
T(K) = -18 + 273 = 255 K. Kelvin values are never negative here, and adding 18 instead of subtracting gives the wrong answer of 291 K.
Use after: Observe phase — Gallery walk, Station 3: Linear Expansion of Solids (steel bridge girder)
Q3. A steel bridge girder is 25 m long. Its temperature rises by 45 °C. The expansion coefficient of steel is 11 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹. Using ΔL = αL₀ΔT, by how much does the girder lengthen?
  1. 12.4 mm
  2. 124 mm
  3. 1.24 mm
  4. 275 mm
Correct answer: A
ΔL = 11 × 10⁻⁶ × 25 × 45 = 0.012375 m, which is about 12.4 mm. The other values come from wrong powers of ten or from leaving out 10⁻⁶ and ΔT.
Use after: Observe phase — Gallery walk, Station 4: Volume Expansion (500 ml becomes about 545 ml)
Q4. Water increases in volume by about 9% when it freezes at 0 °C. A bottle is completely full with 500 ml of water. By about how much does the volume increase when all the water freezes?
  1. 9 ml
  2. 450 ml
  3. 4.5 ml
  4. 45 ml
Correct answer: D
9% of 500 ml = (9 ÷ 100) × 500 = 45 ml, so the ice takes up about 545 ml.
Use after: Observe phase — Gallery walk, Station 5: Anomalous Expansion of Water (density vs. temperature graph)
Q5. A beaker of water at 10 °C is cooled slowly down to 0 °C. How does the density of the water change?
  1. It increases until 4 °C and then decreases as it cools towards 0 °C.
  2. It increases all the way from 10 °C to 0 °C, like most liquids.
  3. It decreases all the way from 10 °C to 0 °C.
  4. It stays the same, because water does not change volume when cooled.
Correct answer: A
Water has its maximum density at 4 °C. Below 4 °C it expands as it cools, which is why water is anomalous. Most liquids keep getting denser as they cool.

Q6. A bottle completely full of water and tightly sealed cracks in a freezer. Why does the expanding water crack the glass instead of simply pushing the cap off or fitting inside?
  1. Ice is lighter than water, so it floats up and hits the cap.
  2. Glass always becomes brittle at 0 °C and breaks by itself, with or without water.
  3. The ice pulls the glass walls inwards until they snap.
  4. The rigid, sealed, full bottle gives the expanding ice no room to grow, so it presses very hard outwards on the glass walls.
Correct answer: D
Water expands by about 9% on freezing. A rigid, sealed and completely full bottle cannot make room for this, so a large outward pressure acts on the glass and can exceed its tensile strength.
Use after: Model Building phase — Final revision of the cumulative particle model (ice at 0 °C, hexagonal lattice with greater intermolecular spacing)
Q7. Why do the molecules in ice have greater spacing than the molecules in liquid water?
  1. Ice molecules move faster than liquid molecules, so they spread apart.
  2. Hydrogen bonding holds the molecules in an open hexagonal lattice with greater spacing.
  3. Air bubbles trapped between the molecules push them apart.
  4. Freezing makes each water molecule bigger.
Correct answer: B
Hydrogen bonds fix each water molecule in a hexagonal lattice with large gaps, so ice is less dense than liquid water. Molecules in ice vibrate less, not more, and the molecules do not grow.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board resolution, yellow cluster: questions about the formula and the numbers
Q8. A learner asks: "Why can we not use ΔV = γV₀ΔT to work out the 9% volume increase when water freezes at 0 °C?" What is the best answer?
  1. The formula only works for temperatures above 100 °C.
  2. The formula gives the wrong answer because water does not expand when it freezes.
  3. The formula only works for gases and never for liquids.
  4. Freezing is a phase change with its own volume increase of about 9%, while the formula describes expansion within one phase.
Correct answer: D
ΔV = γV₀ΔT describes ordinary heating or cooling within a single phase, such as the aluminium sufuria. The 9% increase on freezing is a phase-change effect caused by the open ice lattice.

Q9. Farmers in the Nyandarua highlands cover their water pipes with insulation in January. What is the main scientific reason?
  1. Insulation makes the pipe metal expand so that it fits the water better.
  2. Insulation turns the water into steam, which cannot damage the pipe.
  3. Cold makes water contract, and the shrinking water pulls the pipe walls inwards.
  4. Insulation stops the water in the pipes from freezing, because ice takes up about 9% more volume and can burst the pipe.
Correct answer: D
If water freezes inside a pipe it expands by about 9%, just like in the bottle, and the pressure can burst the pipe. Insulation helps keep the water above freezing.
