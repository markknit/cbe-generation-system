Use after: Predict phase — Predict: how might an engineer use the expansion formula to prevent damage to a long steel structure?
Q1. An engineer is designing a long steel bridge in Kisumu, where the weather is hot for much of the year. What should the engineer build into the design so the beams do not push against their supports and crack?
  1. Small gaps or expansion joints at the ends of the beams
  2. Beams made thicker so they cannot expand
  3. Beams fixed tightly against the supports with no space
  4. Steel painted a dark colour so it absorbs less heat
Correct answer: A
Steel lengthens when it warms, so the engineer leaves space (expansion joints) for the beam to expand. Fixing the beam tightly causes large forces, like the ice cracking the bottle.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Card B - Bimetallic Strip Thermostat in an Electric Iron
Q2. A bimetallic strip in an electric iron has brass bonded to invar steel. Brass expands more than invar for the same temperature rise. What happens to the strip when it is heated?
  1. It becomes shorter on both sides equally
  2. It bends towards the brass side because brass becomes shorter
  3. It bends towards the invar side because the brass lengthens more
  4. It stays straight because the two metals cancel each other out
Correct answer: C
The brass lengthens more than the invar, so the brass side is on the outside of the curve and the strip bends towards the invar side. This bending breaks the circuit and switches the iron off.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Card C - Fitting a Metal Tyre onto a Wooden Cart Wheel in Rural Kenya
Q3. A blacksmith heats a slightly undersized iron tyre before fitting it onto a wooden cart wheel, then cools it with water. Why does this make the tyre grip the wheel tightly?
  1. Heating makes the tyre expand so it slips over the wheel, and cooling makes it contract and squeeze the wheel
  2. Water makes the wood swell, and this has nothing to do with the tyre
  3. Heating makes the tyre shrink so it fits, and cooling makes it expand to grip the wheel
  4. Heating melts the tyre so it takes the shape of the wheel
Correct answer: A
The hot tyre has a larger diameter so it slips on. As it cools it contracts back towards its original size and grips the wheel tightly.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Card D - Riveting Corrugated Metal Roofing Sheets
Q4. Roofers in Kenya leave small gaps between neighbouring corrugated metal roofing sheets and use dome-headed rivets that allow slight movement. What is the engineering reason for this?
  1. To let rainwater drain between the sheets
  2. To give the sheets room to expand in the afternoon heat so they do not buckle
  3. To stop the sheets from expanding at all
  4. To make the roof lighter
Correct answer: B
The sheets expand when heated by the sun. The gaps and movable rivets accommodate this expansion and prevent buckling. The engineer is accommodating expansion, not stopping it.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: whole-class worked example of the 10 m steel bridge beam in Kisumu
Q5. A steel bridge beam is 10 m long at 20 °C. The temperature in Kisumu rises by 15 °C. The linear expansivity of steel is 11 × 10⁻⁶ per °C. Using ΔL = αL₀ΔT, what is the total increase in length of the beam?
  1. 0.165 mm
  2. 16.5 mm
  3. 1.65 mm
  4. 165 mm
Correct answer: C
ΔL = αL₀ΔT = (11 × 10⁻⁶) × 10 × 15 = 1.65 × 10⁻³ m = 1.65 mm.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: independent parallel problem on the Kenya Power copper overhead cable
Q6. A Kenya Power copper cable is 200 m long at 15 °C. On a hot afternoon in Nairobi the temperature rises to 40 °C. The linear expansivity of copper is 17 × 10⁻⁶ per °C. By how much does the cable increase in length?
  1. 3.4 cm
  2. 136 cm
  3. 13.6 cm
  4. 8.5 cm
Correct answer: D
The temperature rise is ΔT = 40 − 15 = 25 °C, not 40 °C. ΔL = αL₀ΔT = 17 × 10⁻⁶ × 200 × 25 = 0.085 m = 8.5 cm.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: independent copper cable problem - why engineers design the cable to sag
Q7. Why do engineers let overhead power cables sag between pylons instead of pulling them tight?
  1. The cable changes length with temperature, so the sag gives room for it to lengthen and shorten without being damaged
  2. Sagging cables are cheaper because they use less metal
  3. A tight cable would be too heavy for the pylons
  4. Sagging cables carry more electric current
Correct answer: A
The metal expands in heat and contracts in cold. A cable pulled taut would be stretched or put under great force when it contracts. Sag allows for these length changes safely.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: Lesson 5 panel - Engineering Response to Expansion (bridge beam cold and hot)
Q8. In a particle model of a steel beam heated on a hot day, which drawing best explains why the beam gets longer?
  1. The particles themselves become bigger
  2. Particles stay bonded but vibrate with bigger amplitude, so their average spacing increases slightly
  3. Particles stop moving and spread out evenly
  4. Particles break away from each other and fly far apart
Correct answer: B
On heating, particles gain energy and vibrate more strongly about their fixed positions. Their average separation increases slightly, and this adds up to a small increase in the beam's length. The particles do not fly apart.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: Engineering Solutions column - 'Engineers use the expansion formula to ___'
Q9. Which statement best links the cracked frozen bottle to the expansion gaps in a bridge?
  1. Expansion gaps are needed only because steel expands more than water
  2. Expansion only causes damage in liquids, never in solid steel
  3. Expansion that has nowhere to go produces a very large force, so engineers leave gaps to allow for it
  4. Bridges and bottles are both damaged only when they get cold
Correct answer: C
When expansion is contained, as with ice in the bottle, it creates huge forces. Engineers use ΔL = αL₀ΔT to leave gaps that give the expansion room and avoid damage.
