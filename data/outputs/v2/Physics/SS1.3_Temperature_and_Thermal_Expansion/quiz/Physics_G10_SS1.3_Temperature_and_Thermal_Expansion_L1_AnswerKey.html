Use after: Predict phase — Predict card, stem (b): 'When things get cold, I think they generally… because…'
Q1. A learner fills a 500 ml glass bottle to the brim with water, seals it with a metal cap and leaves it in a freezer overnight. In the morning the glass is cracked. Why did this surprise the class?
  1. Water cannot freeze when it is inside a sealed container
  2. Freezers are not cold enough to freeze water
  3. Glass is known to be the strongest material and should never crack
  4. Everyday experience tells us that things usually shrink when they get cold, yet the water seemed to push the glass outwards
Correct answer: D
The puzzle is that the common idea 'things shrink when cooled' does not match the cracked glass. The other choices are not what the lesson identifies as the source of surprise.
Use after: Observe phase — Observation Record: describe the crack pattern and the ice plug
Q2. Learners studied a glass bottle that was filled to the brim with water, sealed with a metal cap and frozen overnight. Which of these is an OBSERVATION (something you can see) rather than an explanation?
  1. The glass shrank in the cold and squeezed the water until it cracked
  2. The water heated up just before it froze and burst the bottle
  3. A column of ice stuck out from the bottle where the cap had been
  4. The metal cap rusted and weakened the glass
Correct answer: C
The protruding ice plug is direct evidence that can be seen on the specimen. The other choices are untested claims about why the bottle cracked.
Use after: Observe phase — Kenyan Context Bridge: highland nights at or below 0°C
Q3. The teacher said that in some places water left in a pipe, bucket or clay pot can freeze overnight and split its container. Which situation in Kenya is MOST likely to cause this?
  1. A rainy evening at 20°C in Kisumu
  2. A hot afternoon at 32°C on the coast at Mombasa
  3. A warm night at 28°C in Garissa
  4. A night at 0°C or below in the highlands near the Aberdares or Mount Kenya
Correct answer: D
The Kenyan context bridge says that water freezes and splits containers where night temperatures fall to 0°C or below, such as the Aberdares, Mount Kenya slopes and Nyandarua.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict card, stem (c): plastic bottle instead of glass
Q4. A learner predicts that a plastic bottle filled to the brim with water, sealed and frozen overnight would give a different result from a glass bottle. To test this prediction fairly, which set of conditions should be kept the same for both bottles?
  1. Same volume of water filled to the brim, same type of cap, and same time in the same freezer
  2. One bottle in the freezer and the other left on the bench
  3. Different amounts of water, because plastic is a different material
  4. Only the colour of the bottles
Correct answer: A
A fair test changes only the bottle material. Water volume, filling, cap and freezing conditions must stay the same.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: sticky-note questions about the cracked bottle
Q5. After seeing the cracked bottle, which of these is the best INVESTIGABLE question, one the class could test with an experiment?
  1. Does a bottle that is only half-full of water crack less often than one filled to the brim?
  2. Should learners be allowed to freeze bottles at home?
  3. Who bought the bottle used in the demonstration?
  4. Is glass prettier than plastic?
Correct answer: A
Changing how full the bottle is and observing whether it cracks can be tested. The other choices are opinions or facts that cannot be investigated by experiment.
Use after: Explain phase — Connect to phenomenon: naming 'anomalous expansion of water' on the DQB anchor strip
Q6. At the end of this first lesson, the class has seen the cracked bottle and heard the term 'anomalous expansion of water'. What is the status of the explanation for why the bottle cracked?
  1. The behaviour has been named and observed, but the full explanation will be investigated in later lessons
  2. No one can ever explain it
  3. It has been fully explained by the teacher, so there is nothing more to investigate
  4. The cap was too tight, so that is the complete answer
Correct answer: A
This is an anchor lesson. The behaviour is named and the evidence is recorded, but the full explanation is deliberately left for the following lessons.
Use after: Model Building phase — Baseline Particle Model: two-panel sketch signed and dated
Q7. At the end of the lesson you draw a baseline model labelled 'MY MODEL — LESSON 1 (BEFORE INSTRUCTION)' showing what you think happens to the water in the sealed bottle overnight. What is the main purpose of this model?
  1. To get a mark for having the correct answer
  2. To replace the Predict card, which can then be thrown away
  3. To record your current ideas honestly, even if they are wrong, so you can compare and update them later in the unit
  4. To copy the teacher's correct drawing
Correct answer: C
The teacher said the model 'does not have to be correct — it has to be honest'. It will be revised through the unit and compared with the final model.
