Q1. Wanjiru can hear Mr. Mwangi calling from around the corner of the classroom block, but she cannot see him. Which explanation is best?
  1. Sound waves spread round edges and through gaps much more than light, because their wavelength is far larger
  2. Sound is louder than light, so it can get round corners
  3. Sound travels only in straight lines, but it bounces off the walls
  4. Light is too fast to bend round corners, but sound is slow enough to bend
Correct answer: A
Sound has a wavelength of about 1 m, comparable to doorways and building edges, so it diffracts strongly. Light has a wavelength of about 5.5 x 10^-7 m, far smaller than the gap, so it barely bends.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: calculating wavelengths using v = f x wavelength (340 Hz and 1000 Hz sounds)
Q2. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s. What is the wavelength of a sound of frequency 1000 Hz?
  1. 340 000 m
  2. 1.0 m
  3. 0.34 m
  4. 3.4 m
Correct answer: C
Use lambda = v / f = 340 / 1000 = 0.34 m.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Task A, changing the gap width in the PhET Wave Interference simulation and recording the amount of spreading
Q3. In a ripple tank, plane waves of wavelength 1.0 m (scaled) meet a barrier with a gap. Only the gap width is changed. Which gap width will give the strongest spreading of the waves on the far side?
  1. A gap of 1.0 m
  2. A gap of 20 m
  3. A gap of 5 m
  4. A gap of 100 m
Correct answer: A
Diffraction is greatest when the gap is comparable to the wavelength. A 1.0 m gap equals the 1.0 m wavelength, while the other gaps are much wider than the wavelength and give little spreading.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: data table comparing the 1.0 m gap with light of wavelength 5.5 x 10^-7 m
Q4. A doorway is 1.0 m wide. Green light has a wavelength of 5.5 x 10^-7 m. About how many light wavelengths fit across the doorway?
  1. About 550
  2. About 1 818 182 (1.8 million)
  3. About 0.00000055
  4. About 5.5
Correct answer: B
Number of wavelengths = gap / wavelength = 1.0 / 5.5 x 10^-7 = about 1.8 million. The gap is enormously larger than the wavelength, so light shows almost no visible bending.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: applying the idea to a new situation (340 Hz versus 1000 Hz sound heard around a corner) and the exit-ticket question
Q5. Sound of frequency 340 Hz and sound of frequency 1000 Hz (speed 340 m/s) pass through a doorway 1.0 m wide. Which statement is correct?
  1. The 1000 Hz sound spreads more, because a higher frequency means more energy
  2. The 340 Hz sound spreads more, because its wavelength of 1.0 m is comparable to the doorway width
  3. Both spread equally, because the gap is the same for both
  4. The 1000 Hz sound spreads more, because its wavelength of 0.34 m is smaller than the gap
Correct answer: B
The 340 Hz sound has lambda = 340/340 = 1.0 m, equal to the gap, so it spreads strongly. The 1000 Hz sound has lambda = 0.34 m, so the gap is about 3 wavelengths and the spreading is less. This is why low sounds such as matatu bass are heard round corners better.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher question 'Does diffraction change the speed or the pitch of the sound?'
Q6. A 340 Hz sound wave passes through a doorway and spreads out into the corridor beyond. What happens to the frequency and wavelength of the sound because of this diffraction?
  1. The frequency becomes lower and the pitch drops
  2. The wavelength becomes longer and the frequency stays the same
  3. The frequency and the wavelength both stay the same
  4. The frequency increases because the wave is squeezed through the gap
Correct answer: C
Diffraction changes the direction in which the wave travels and spreads, but it does not change the frequency or wavelength of the wave, so the pitch stays the same.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the class echo model to show the shout spreading across the open Nairobi grounds, and keeping this separate from diffraction
Q7. A learner shouts on a large open school field. The sound gets weaker as it travels away in all directions, so no echo is heard. What is the best explanation for the sound becoming weaker?
  1. The wavelength of the sound gets shorter as the wave travels
  2. Diffraction destroys part of the wave's energy at the edges
  3. The frequency of the sound drops as it moves across open ground
  4. The wave spreads out in all directions, so the same energy covers a bigger area; this is different from diffraction
Correct answer: D
As a shout spreads out in all directions its energy covers a larger area, so it gets weaker. This is ordinary spreading out, not diffraction, which is the bending of waves round edges and through gaps and does not change wavelength or frequency.
