Q1. In the formula d = vt/2 used to find the distance to a wall from an echo, why is the time t divided by 2?
  1. The ear can only detect half of the time the sound travels
  2. The speed of sound halves after it hits the wall
  3. Only half of the sound energy reaches the wall
  4. t is the time for the sound to go to the wall and come back, so the one-way distance uses half of it
Correct answer: D
The measured time t is for the round trip (to the wall and back). The distance to the wall is one way, so we use half the time, or half the total distance vt.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: deriving the minimum distance for a distinct echo (0.1 x 340 / 2 = 17 m)
Q2. The ear can separate two sounds only if they are at least 0.1 s apart. Taking the speed of sound in air as 340 m/s, what is the minimum distance to a reflecting wall for a distinct echo?
  1. 8.5 m
  2. 34 m
  3. 17 m
  4. 3.4 m
Correct answer: C
The round trip must take at least 0.1 s, so d = vt/2 = 340 x 0.1 / 2 = 17 m. Forgetting to halve gives 34 m.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: field measurement with d = vt/2 (and exit check on echo delay)
Q3. A learner at a school in Nakuru claps and hears the echo 0.40 s later. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. How far away is the reflecting wall?
  1. 34 m
  2. 136 m
  3. 68 m
  4. 272 m
Correct answer: C
d = vt/2 = 340 x 0.40 / 2 = 68 m. The value 136 m is the round-trip distance, which is wrong because the wall is only half of that distance away.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: timing 10 clap-echo intervals at the school's long stone wall
Q4. When measuring the echo distance with a stopwatch, a group times 10 clap-echo intervals and then divides by 10, instead of timing just one interval. Why do they do this?
  1. The speed of sound is only correct when 10 claps are used
  2. The reaction-time error of starting and stopping the watch is spread over 10 intervals, so each interval is more accurate
  3. The echo only becomes audible after 10 claps
  4. The stopwatch works only for times longer than 1 second
Correct answer: B
Reaction time (about 0.2 s) is a big fraction of one short interval (about 0.3 s). Spread over 10 intervals, it becomes a small error in each one after dividing by 10.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: comparing the surface table (cliff rock 90%, painted concrete 85%, wooden board 60%, curtains 15%, grass or foam 10%)
Q5. A thick curtain reflects only 15% of the sound that hits it, while painted concrete reflects 85%. Which statement is correct?
  1. The curtain absorbs most of the sound energy and sends back a weak reflection
  2. The curtain reflects more sound than concrete because it is thicker
  3. Both surfaces reflect the same amount, but concrete is louder because it is heavier
  4. The curtain reflects no sound at all, so it gives no echo
Correct answer: A
Soft surfaces like curtains absorb most of the sound energy (about 85%) but still reflect a small amount (15%). Hard smooth surfaces such as painted concrete reflect most of it.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-evidence-reasoning for each site: why is the echo absent at site D
Q6. On the open grounds of a Nairobi school covered with grass and bushes, the only large wall is 300 m away. A learner shouts and the calculated echo delay is 1.76 s, which is much longer than 0.1 s, yet no echo is heard. What is the best explanation?
  1. A delay longer than 0.1 s is too long for the ear to hear an echo
  2. Sound cannot travel as far as 300 m in air
  3. Grass and bushes absorb most of the sound and it spreads over a large area, so the returning sound is too faint to hear
  4. The sound wave is destroyed when it hits a wall
Correct answer: C
The delay is 2 x 300 / 340 = 1.76 s, so time is not the problem. The grass reflects only about 10% and absorbs the rest, and the sound spreads out, so very little energy returns.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-evidence-reasoning for each site: why the Kisumu hall is muddy (echo versus reverberation)
Q7. In a school hall in Kisumu with hard walls and a hard roof, a speaker 12 m from the far wall sounds loud, muddy and drawn out. What is the best explanation?
  1. Sound travels more slowly inside buildings than outside
  2. Sound reflects many times from the hard surfaces and the reflections overlap and merge, which is called reverberation
  3. The hall walls absorb all the sound, so it takes longer to fade
  4. The far wall is more than 17 m away, so the echo comes back too late to be heard
Correct answer: B
The 12 m distance gives a delay of only about 0.07 s, less than 0.1 s. Repeated reflections from hard surfaces overlap and merge with the original sound, prolonging it (reverberation).
Use after: Model Building phase — Revising the class echo model: test case with a curtained wall 40 m away
Q8. A learner stands 40 m from a wall completely covered with thick curtains and claps. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. Which prediction is best?
  1. A loud echo, because the delay is longer than 0.1 s
  2. No echo at all, because the wall is too close
  3. A separate but weak echo, because the delay is about 0.24 s but the curtains absorb most of the sound
  4. No sound returns, because curtains reflect 0%
Correct answer: C
The delay is 2 x 40 / 340 = 0.24 s, which is more than 0.1 s, and 40 m is more than 17 m, so the echo would be separate. The curtains reflect only about 15%, so the echo is weak.
