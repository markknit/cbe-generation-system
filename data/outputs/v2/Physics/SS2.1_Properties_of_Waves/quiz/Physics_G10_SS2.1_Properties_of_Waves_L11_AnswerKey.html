Q1. Wanjiru listens to an FM radio station broadcast from a studio in Nairobi while she is in Kericho. What carries the station's signal through the space between the transmitter and her radio?
  1. Ultrasound pulses sent from the studio to the radio
  2. Sound waves travelling through the air at about 340 m/s
  3. Echoes of the announcer's voice bouncing between hills
  4. Electromagnetic (radio) waves travelling at 3.0 x 10^8 m/s, which need no medium
Correct answer: D
Radio waves are electromagnetic waves. They travel at 3.0 x 10^8 m/s and need no medium, so the signal is not ordinary sound moving through air.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict Phase: how a doctor can use sound we cannot hear to find an organ boundary
Q2. A doctor uses ultrasound to scan a patient at a county hospital. What does the word 'ultrasound' mean?
  1. Sound that travels only through air and cannot enter the body
  2. A high-frequency radio wave that travels at 3.0 x 10^8 m/s
  3. Sound with a frequency above the range of human hearing, which is about 20 000 Hz
  4. Sound that is extremely loud but still within the range of human hearing
Correct answer: C
Ultrasound is sound whose frequency is above what humans can hear. It is not extra-loud sound and it is not an electromagnetic wave.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: FM radio, calculating the wavelength of the 91.0 MHz carrier
Q3. An FM station broadcasts at a carrier frequency of 91.0 MHz. Radio waves travel at 3.0 x 10^8 m/s. What is the wavelength of the carrier wave? (1 MHz = 1 000 000 Hz)
  1. 0.33 m
  2. 33.0 m
  3. 3.30 m
  4. 3.7 x 10^-6 m
Correct answer: C
Wavelength = v / f = 3.0 x 10^8 / (91.0 x 10^6) = 3.30 m. The other values come from slipping the power of ten or inverting the formula.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: FM radio, looking at the graphs of a carrier wave and a voice signal
Q4. In FM (frequency modulation) radio, a voice is carried by the radio wave. What does the voice signal change in the carrier wave?
  1. The speed of the carrier, which becomes 340 m/s
  2. The height (amplitude) of the carrier, while its frequency stays the same
  3. The carrier frequency, which is squeezed and stretched, while the amplitude stays the same
  4. Nothing, because the radio wave carries the sound itself as air vibrations
Correct answer: C
In FM the voice signal makes the carrier frequency go up and down slightly, while the amplitude stays constant. The radio then changes these variations back into sound.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Ultrasound, using d = vt/2 for the 7.7 cm boundary
Q5. An ultrasound probe sends a pulse into soft tissue, where the speed of ultrasound is 1540 m/s. A tissue boundary is 7.7 cm (0.077 m) deep. After how long does the echo from the boundary return to the probe?
  1. 1000 microseconds
  2. 50 microseconds
  3. 453 microseconds
  4. 100 microseconds
Correct answer: D
The pulse travels to the boundary and back, so t = 2d / v = 2 x 0.077 / 1540 = 1.0 x 10^-4 s = 100 microseconds. Forgetting the factor 2 gives 50 microseconds, and using 340 m/s gives about 453 microseconds.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Phase: why a doctor cannot use the Hell's Gate shout to scan a body
Q6. A 3.5 MHz ultrasound probe in soft tissue has a wavelength of about 0.44 mm. Why can a doctor use this ultrasound, but not a loud shout like the one at Hell's Gate, to find small boundaries inside the body?
  1. Sound that we can hear cannot reflect from anything inside the body
  2. A shout is too loud and would break the tissue, while ultrasound is quiet
  3. Ultrasound travels faster than light, so it returns before the shout does
  4. The wavelength of audible sound is too long to show small detail, while the 0.44 mm wavelength of ultrasound suits small boundaries
Correct answer: D
Audible sound has wavelengths far too long to reveal small features. Ultrasound has a wavelength of only 0.44 mm, so it can show fine detail. Loudness and speed are not the reason.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Phase: adding the 'Technology' branch with arrows back to Hell's Gate, and the exit slip on similarity and difference
Q7. At Hell's Gate a shout reflects from a cliff 85 m away and returns after 0.50 s. An ultrasound pulse reflects from a tissue boundary 7.7 cm deep and returns after 100 microseconds. Which statement correctly compares the two echoes?
  1. Both echoes take the same time, because the speed of sound is the same in air and in tissue
  2. Both use reflection and the same relation d = vt/2, but the wave speed, distance and time are different
  3. Hell's Gate uses d = vt/2, but ultrasound uses d = vt because it is a different kind of wave
  4. Ultrasound does not reflect, so the timing method used at Hell's Gate cannot work for it
Correct answer: B
Both are reflections where the wave travels there and back, so d = vt/2 applies. Only the speed (about 340 m/s in air, 1540 m/s in tissue), the distance and the time change.
