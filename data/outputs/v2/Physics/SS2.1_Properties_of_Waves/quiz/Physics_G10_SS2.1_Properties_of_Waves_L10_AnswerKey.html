Q1. An ambulance siren always produces a steady 700 Hz note. As the ambulance passes a bus, the people in the bus hear the pitch drop. Which explanation is correct?
  1. The siren becomes louder as it comes closer, and loudness is the same as pitch
  2. The wind speeds up the sound as the ambulance goes past
  3. The siren changes to a lower note as the ambulance passes
  4. Because the ambulance is moving, the number of sound waves reaching the ear each second changes, so the pitch heard changes
Correct answer: D
The siren itself stays at 700 Hz. The motion of the source changes how often wavefronts reach the listener. Loudness and pitch are different things.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: learners study the ambulance data table and calculate the wavelength of the 700 Hz siren at rest
Q2. A parked ambulance sounds its siren at 700 Hz. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s. Using v = f x wavelength, what is the wavelength of the siren sound?
  1. 360 m
  2. 0.486 m
  3. 238 000 m
  4. 2.06 m
Correct answer: B
wavelength = v / f = 340 / 700 = 0.486 m. Dividing the other way round (700/340) gives 2.06 m, which is wrong.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: scale model walk with Amina as the siren, wavefront circles drawn with string and chalk or a compass
Q3. In a scale model of a moving siren, Amina walks steadily along a line at 0.5 m/s while a timekeeper claps once every second. Each clap's wavefront travels outwards at 1 m/s. Otieno stands ahead of Amina and Wanjiru stands behind her. The wavefronts are 0.5 m apart in front of Amina and 1.5 m apart behind her. Who receives wavefronts more often, and why?
  1. Otieno, because the wavefronts ahead of Amina are closer together, so more of them reach him each second
  2. Otieno, because Amina claps faster when she walks towards him
  3. Wanjiru, because the wavefronts behind Amina are further apart and carry more energy
  4. Both receive them equally often, because Amina claps once every second
Correct answer: A
Wavefronts are bunched up ahead of the moving source (0.5 m apart), so about 2 reach Otieno each second. Behind the source they are stretched out, so fewer reach Wanjiru. The clapping rate stays the same.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: learners use the data to work out the approaching frequency of 744 Hz
Q4. An ambulance siren emits 700 Hz. The ambulance moves towards you at 20 m/s and the speed of sound is 340 m/s. Using observed frequency = 700 x 340 / (340 - 20), what frequency do you hear?
  1. 720 Hz
  2. 744 Hz
  3. 700 Hz
  4. 661 Hz
Correct answer: B
f = 700 x 340 / (340 - 20) = 238 000 / 320 = about 744 Hz. This is higher than 700 Hz because the waves are bunched up in front of the approaching source.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER statement and discussion of why the bottom number is 340 plus 20 for receding
Q5. An ambulance with a 700 Hz siren drives away from you. A learner says: 'The sound must now travel more slowly, which is why the pitch is lower.' Which statement correctly describes what happens?
  1. The speed of sound drops below 340 m/s, so the frequency drops
  2. The speed of sound stays at 340 m/s, the waves behind the ambulance are stretched out, so the wavelength is longer and the frequency heard is lower
  3. The siren frequency drops because the engine is further away
  4. The speed of sound stays at 340 m/s, the waves are bunched up, so the frequency heard is lower
Correct answer: B
The speed of sound in air does not change when the source moves. Wavefronts behind a receding source are stretched out (longer wavelength), so fewer reach the listener each second, giving a lower frequency (661 Hz in the example).
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Mr. Mwangi introduces uses of the Doppler effect (radar speed gun and redshift)
Q6. A traffic officer on a Nairobi highway uses a radar speed gun. How does the gun find the speed of a moving matatu?
  1. It measures the colour of the matatu using light waves
  2. It sends radio waves at the matatu and measures how much the frequency of the reflected waves has shifted
  3. It measures only how long a sound takes to return, which does not depend on speed
  4. It measures how loud the matatu's engine sounds
Correct answer: B
The radar gun uses the Doppler effect: the reflected radio waves from a moving vehicle return with a shifted frequency, and the size of the shift gives the speed.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: learners link Doppler to echoes and discuss whether Doppler explains why the Kisumu hall is muddy
Q7. At the Kisumu school hall, a shout gives a muddy, drawn-out echo. A learner says the Doppler effect explains the muddy echo. Which reply is correct?
  1. Yes, because the hall makes the pitch of the shout higher
  2. No, the muddy echo comes from many overlapping reflections, while the Doppler effect is about motion changing the frequency
  3. Yes, because the sound is travelling at a changing speed in the hall
  4. No, because the Doppler effect only happens outdoors
Correct answer: B
A muddy echo is caused by overlapping reflections arriving close together. The Doppler effect needs relative motion between source and observer and changes pitch.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the Doppler panel and the note about a moving reflector or source to the class echo model
Q8. At Hell's Gate, the cliff does not move, so the echo of a shout has the same pitch as the shout. What would make the pitch of the returning echo change?
  1. The shouter and the cliff moving towards or away from each other
  2. The echo taking longer to return
  3. The shout being louder
  4. The cliff being taller
Correct answer: A
Relative motion between the sound source and the reflecting surface changes the observed frequency. Delay, height of the cliff and loudness do not change the pitch.
