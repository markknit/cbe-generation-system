Q1. On 11 May 2024, HF radio used for weather messages went silent and GPS positions jumped around. Which cause best explains this?
  1. The Sun's heat warmed Earth's surface and melted the radio signals
  2. Charged particles and magnetic energy from a coronal mass ejection disturbed Earth's magnetic field and upper atmosphere
  3. The Moon's gravity pulled radio waves away from the weather station
  4. Earth's magnetosphere disappeared and let the Sun's light through
Correct answer: B
The blackouts came from a solar eruption (CME) that sent charged particles and magnetic disturbance towards Earth, not from heat or the Moon. The magnetosphere was compressed, not removed.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: case study handout Panel B (Journey Through Space)
Q2. A coronal mass ejection travels at 800 km per second. The Sun is 150 million km from Earth. About how long does the cloud take to reach Earth?
  1. About 52 hours
  2. About 8 minutes
  3. About 5 hours
  4. About 187,500 hours
Correct answer: A
Time = distance ÷ speed = 150,000,000 ÷ 800 = 187,500 s. Dividing by 3600 gives about 52 hours, or roughly 2 days.

Q3. A solar storm sends out two things at the same moment: electromagnetic radiation (radio waves and X-rays) and a cloud of charged particles (a CME). Which statement is correct?
  1. The radiation arrives in about 8 minutes, and the particle cloud arrives about 2 days later
  2. Both arrive in about 8 minutes because they come from the same eruption
  3. Both arrive at Earth together after about 2 days
  4. The particle cloud arrives first because it is heavier
Correct answer: A
Electromagnetic radiation travels at the speed of light and takes about 8 minutes. The particle cloud travels at about 800 km/s and takes about 52 hours. They are two separate arrivals.

Q4. Earth's magnetosphere is hit by a strong CME during a geomagnetic storm. What does the magnetosphere do?
  1. It absorbs all the particles, so nothing reaches Earth's atmosphere
  2. It deflects most of the charged particles, although some spiral towards the poles and it is compressed on the sunward side
  3. It creates the charged particles that cause the storm
  4. It has no effect, so the particles pass straight through to the ground
Correct answer: B
The magnetosphere protects Earth by deflecting most solar particles. In a storm it is compressed and some particles spiral towards the poles, causing auroras and disruption.

Q5. Why can a total solar eclipse be seen only from a narrow strip of Earth's surface?
  1. The Sun is switched off everywhere except along that strip
  2. Earth's magnetosphere blocks the view everywhere else
  3. The Moon's dark central shadow (umbra) that reaches Earth is small, so only places inside it see the Sun fully covered
  4. Only that strip is facing the Moon's gravity
Correct answer: C
The Moon's umbra falls on only a small spot that sweeps across Earth as a narrow path. Places in the penumbra see only a partial eclipse.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: eclipse question 'If the Moon were twice as far away...' (30-second wait)
Q6. The Sun is about 400 times wider than the Moon and about 390 times farther away, so they look almost the same size in our sky. If the Moon were twice as far from Earth as it is now, what would happen?
  1. The Moon would look smaller than the Sun and could not fully cover it, so total solar eclipses would not happen
  2. The Moon would look larger and total eclipses would last longer
  3. Total eclipses would happen every month
  4. Nothing would change, because the Moon's size in the sky depends only on the Sun
Correct answer: A
A more distant Moon looks smaller in the sky. It could no longer cover the Sun's whole disc, so only ring-shaped or partial eclipses would be seen.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Tides versus space weather question (did the May 2024 storm change the tides at Mombasa?)
Q7. A strong geomagnetic storm happens in May 2024. Did it change the tides at Mombasa?
  1. Yes, because the Sun's charged particles pull the ocean upwards
  2. No, because tides come from the Moon's (and Sun's) gravity, while the storm comes from the Sun's charged particles and magnetic field
  3. Yes, because the storm heats the sea and makes the tides larger
  4. No, because tides are caused by Earth's magnetosphere
Correct answer: B
Tides are caused by gravity, mainly the Moon's. A geomagnetic storm involves charged particles and magnetic fields, which are a different cause and do not change the tide cycle.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Tides versus space weather (Sun's pull and spring tides)
Q8. When are the tides at Mombasa largest (spring tides)?
  1. When the Sun and Moon line up with Earth, at new moon and full moon
  2. Only during a solar eclipse
  3. During a geomagnetic storm
  4. When the Sun and Moon are at right angles to each other
Correct answer: A
When the Sun and Moon line up, the Sun's pull (about 46 percent of the Moon's) adds to the Moon's, so tides are largest at new and full moon.
