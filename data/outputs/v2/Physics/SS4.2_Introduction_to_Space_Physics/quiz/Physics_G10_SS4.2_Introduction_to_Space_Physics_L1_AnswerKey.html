Q1. A learner stands on the escarpment above Nakuru on a moonless night, switches off the torch and looks up. Which statement best describes how the sky looks to the unaided eye?
  1. The sky looks calm and unchanging, as if it has always been this way
  2. The Milky Way band changes shape every minute
  3. Stars can be seen exploding every few seconds
  4. The stars are visibly moving apart from each other as the learner watches
Correct answer: A
The anchoring phenomenon is that the night sky looks still, permanent and ancient to direct observation, even though scientists claim the universe had a beginning. Nothing visibly expands or explodes while we watch.
Use after: Observe phase — Kurzgesagt video 'The Big Bang: How Did Everything Begin?' and Khan Academy article 'The Big Bang'
Q2. According to the Big Bang Theory, approximately how long ago did the universe begin?
  1. About 13.8 million years ago
  2. About 13.8 billion years ago
  3. About 6 000 years ago
  4. About 4.6 billion years ago
Correct answer: B
The Big Bang Theory proposes that the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, extremely dense singularity.
Use after: Observe phase — Evidence Collection Table and discussion of 'Evidence We Found'
Q3. A class looks at the night sky above Nairobi and discusses what they know. Which statement is a scientific inference rather than a direct observation?
  1. The Moon looks large and bright
  2. The Milky Way appears as a band of light across the sky
  3. The universe began about 13.8 billion years ago
  4. Jupiter appears low in the east tonight
Correct answer: C
We can see Jupiter, the Moon and the Milky Way directly. Nobody can watch the universe begin, so its age is concluded from evidence and reasoning.
Use after: Explain phase — Teacher zooms out after the CER discussion: 'why does this sky look so still?'
Q4. A learner asks: 'If the Big Bang happened 13.8 billion years ago, why does the night sky above Nairobi look so calm and permanent tonight?' What is the best response at this point in the unit?
  1. The Big Bang happened far away from Earth, so we cannot see any effects
  2. The universe stopped expanding and became permanent after the Big Bang
  3. This is an open question that has not been explained yet, and the class will investigate it through the unit
  4. The stars all stopped moving soon after the Big Bang
Correct answer: C
In this lesson the question is deliberately left unanswered and is carried forward as the driving question of the unit. The other choices are guesses that the lesson has not supported.
Use after: Explain phase — CER written explanation: 'Make a claim about how the universe began'
Q5. In a Claim–Evidence–Reasoning (CER) explanation about how the universe began, which description fits 'evidence'?
  1. Specific information from a source, such as the video or article, that supports the claim
  2. Your opinion about which answer is best
  3. How much you like the theory
  4. A restatement of the question in your own words
Correct answer: A
Evidence is specific information, such as a fact or data from a source, that supports the claim. Opinions and vague restatements are not evidence.
Use after: DQB phase — Learners write questions on sticky notes and post them on the Driving Question Board
Q6. Which of these questions for the Driving Question Board is the most specific and scientifically testable?
  1. Why is everything so big?
  2. What is space?
  3. Is the sky beautiful?
  4. How do scientists measure the age of the universe?
Correct answer: D
A good DQB question is specific and can be investigated with evidence. 'How do scientists measure the age of the universe?' is focused. The others are vague or about opinion.
Use after: Model Building phase — MY UNIVERSE MODEL, Version 1: timeline diagram from the Big Bang to tonight's sky
Q7. A learner draws a timeline from the Big Bang to tonight's sky above Nairobi. Which event should be at the far left, as the earliest event?
  1. The Solar System forms
  2. Learners look at the night sky
  3. The Milky Way is seen from Earth tonight
  4. The Big Bang, about 13.8 billion years ago
Correct answer: D
The timeline starts at the Big Bang, about 13.8 billion years ago, and ends with tonight's sky. Every other event comes after it.
