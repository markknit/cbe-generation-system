Q1. A Grade 10 learner writes a short story of the universe. Which list puts the events in the correct order, from earliest to latest?
  1. Big Bang, then expansion and cooling, then first stars, then the Solar System, then Earth
  2. First stars, then the Big Bang, then Earth, then expansion and cooling, then the Solar System
  3. Big Bang, then the Solar System, then first stars, then expansion and cooling, then Earth
  4. Expansion and cooling, then Earth, then first stars, then the Big Bang, then the Solar System
Correct answer: A
The universe began in the Big Bang, then expanded and cooled. Stars formed next, and the Solar System and Earth formed later from material that earlier stars had made.
Use after: Observe phase — Part: synthesis template, Group A anchor lessons 1 & 2 (Big Bang and evolution of the universe)
Q2. Which statement best describes what scientists mean by the Big Bang?
  1. The universe has always existed and has never changed
  2. The universe began about 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding ever since
  3. A giant star exploded about 13.8 thousand years ago inside space that was already empty
  4. The Sun exploded and its pieces formed all the stars and planets
Correct answer: B
The Big Bang describes the start of the universe, including space and time, from a hot, dense state about 13.8 billion years ago. The universe has been expanding since then.
Use after: Observe phase — Part: synthesis template, Group C anchor lessons 5 & 6 (Kepler's Laws, gravitational forces)
Q3. Which force keeps the planets in orbit around the Sun?
  1. Friction between the planets and space
  2. The magnetic force of the Moon
  3. The gravitational pull of Earth on all the planets
  4. The gravitational pull of the Sun on each planet
Correct answer: D
The Sun's gravity pulls on each planet and keeps it moving in orbit. Kepler's Laws describe these orbits and gravity explains why they happen.
Use after: Observe phase — Part: synthesis template, Group D anchor lessons 7 & 8 (telescopes, history of space exploration) and gallery walk
Q4. Scientists cannot travel back in time or visit distant galaxies. Which observation is indirect evidence that the universe began in a hot, dense state?
  1. Venus looks bright in the evening sky
  2. Craters are visible on the surface of the Moon
  3. Faint microwave radiation (the cosmic microwave background) reaches Earth from every direction in the sky
  4. The Moon changes shape through the month
Correct answer: C
The cosmic microwave background is leftover radiation from the early hot universe. Telescopes detect it today, so it is indirect evidence for the Big Bang. The other observations do not show how the universe began.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 5-sentence 'Universe Narrative' paragraph (Big Bang to tonight's Nairobi sky)
Q5. How did the first stars form after the Big Bang?
  1. Gravity pulled clouds of hydrogen and helium gas together until they became hot and dense enough for nuclear fusion to start
  2. Rocks inside the first planets caught fire and began to burn
  3. Planets collided and joined together to make stars
  4. Stars were made complete in the Big Bang and have not changed since
Correct answer: A
Gravity pulled clouds of gas together, which heated them until nuclear fusion started and the first stars began to shine. Planets formed later, so they cannot have made the first stars.

Q6. A learner says: 'The night sky above Nairobi looks the same every night, so the universe cannot have changed or had a beginning.' Which response is best?
  1. Scientists have travelled back to the Big Bang to see it for themselves
  2. The changes happened over billions of years, which is far too slow to notice in one lifetime, and scientists use indirect evidence such as the cosmic microwave background and telescope observations to study them
  3. The learner is correct because stars never change
  4. The universe changed only in the last few years, so we cannot see it
Correct answer: B
The universe's history unfolds over billions of years, so the sky seems still to us. Scientists use indirect evidence gathered with telescopes to reconstruct that history.

Q7. A learner is labelling celestial bodies on a model of the Solar System. Which set of labels is correct?
  1. The Sun is a planet, Venus is a star and the Moon is a planet
  2. The Sun is a star, Venus is a planet and the Moon is a natural satellite of Earth
  3. The Sun is a planet, Venus is a natural satellite and the Moon is a star
  4. The Sun is a star, Venus is a star and the Moon is a planet
Correct answer: B
The Sun is a star that makes its own light. Venus is a planet that orbits the Sun. The Moon is a natural satellite that orbits Earth. Venus looks bright but only reflects sunlight.
