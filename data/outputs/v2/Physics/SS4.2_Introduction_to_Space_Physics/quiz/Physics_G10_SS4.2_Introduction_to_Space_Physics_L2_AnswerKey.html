Q1. Scientists say the universe began in an event called the Big Bang. About how long ago did this happen?
  1. About 380,000 years ago
  2. About 200 million years ago
  3. About 4.6 billion years ago
  4. About 13.8 billion years ago
Correct answer: D
The universe began about 13.8 billion years ago. 4.6 billion years is when the Solar System formed, 380,000 years is when the first atoms formed, and 200 million years is when the first stars ignited.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: Big Bang Timeline Construction (Cosmic Events Card Set)
Q2. Which list shows these events in the correct order, from earliest to latest?
  1. Quark-gluon plasma, then protons and neutrons form, then first atoms form, then first stars ignite
  2. First atoms form, then protons and neutrons form, then quark-gluon plasma, then first stars
  3. Protons and neutrons form, then the Solar System forms, then first stars ignite, then first atoms form
  4. Quark-gluon plasma, then first stars ignite, then first atoms form, then protons and neutrons form
Correct answer: A
The universe began as a hot quark-gluon plasma. Protons and neutrons formed next, then atoms about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, then the first stars at about 200 million years. Atoms cannot form before protons, and stars cannot form before atoms.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: Big Bang Timeline Construction (marking where each event falls on the 2-metre rope)
Q3. A 2-metre rope is used as a straight, linear timeline of 13.8 billion years, with the Big Bang at one end and tonight at the other end. The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago. How far from the Big Bang end of the rope should the Solar System card be placed?
  1. About 46 cm
  2. About 133 cm
  3. About 92 cm
  4. About 67 cm
Correct answer: B
The Solar System formed 13.8 - 4.6 = 9.2 billion years after the Big Bang. So the distance is 9.2 / 13.8 x 200 cm, which is about 133 cm. The 67 cm answer measures 4.6 billion years from the Big Bang end instead of from tonight.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A: Big Bang Timeline Construction (misconception check on placing first stars)
Q4. On a 2-metre rope representing 13.8 billion years, the first stars ignited about 200 million years after the Big Bang. How far from the Big Bang end of the rope do the first stars belong?
  1. 29 cm
  2. 100 cm
  3. 0.029 cm
  4. 2.9 cm
Correct answer: D
200 million is 0.2 billion. The fraction of the timeline is 0.2 / 13.8, and 0.2 / 13.8 x 200 cm is about 2.9 cm. This shows the first stars appeared very early on the timeline, but not within minutes of the Big Bang.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity B: NASA Galaxy Classification (identify the Milky Way)
Q5. Which type of galaxy is our home galaxy, the Milky Way?
  1. An elliptical galaxy
  2. An irregular galaxy
  3. A barred spiral galaxy
  4. A single very large star cluster with no arms
Correct answer: C
The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy: it has spiral arms and a bar-shaped centre. Our Solar System lies inside it.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher input gap (1) CMB, after the video 'Evolution of the Universe in 5 Minutes'
Q6. The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) is a faint glow of microwaves that reaches Earth almost equally from every direction. Why is it important evidence for the Big Bang?
  1. It is light from the first stars, which ignited a few minutes after the Big Bang
  2. It is old light released about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, which suggests the early universe was hot and dense
  3. It is sunlight reflected from the Moon and spread across the sky
  4. It is radio signals sent by human-made satellites orbiting Earth
Correct answer: B
The CMB is the oldest light we can see. It was released about 380,000 years after the Big Bang and has since been stretched into microwaves. This supports a hot, dense beginning. The first stars came much later.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher input gap (2) Expansion, balloon demonstration
Q7. Dots are drawn on a balloon and the balloon is inflated. Every dot moves away from every other dot. What does this model show about the Big Bang?
  1. The Big Bang happened only to the galaxies nearest to Earth
  2. The Big Bang was an expansion of space itself, and no galaxy is the centre of the expansion
  3. The Big Bang stopped expanding once the first galaxies formed
  4. The Big Bang was an explosion at one point in space, and galaxies were thrown outward from that centre
Correct answer: B
On the balloon every dot moves away from every other dot and no dot is the centre. In the same way, space itself expanded, and the universe is still expanding today. It was not an explosion inside space.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher input gap (3) Stars, and the Kericho farmer explanation paragraph
Q8. Carbon and oxygen are found in living things and on Earth. According to the evolution of the universe, where were these heavier elements made?
  1. They were made by the Moon and carried to Earth
  2. They were all made in the first few minutes after the Big Bang
  3. They were made inside stars and scattered into space when massive stars ended as supernovae
  4. They were made inside the Earth after the planet formed
Correct answer: C
The Big Bang mainly produced hydrogen and helium. Stars fuse elements, and massive stars scatter carbon, oxygen, iron and others in supernovae. The Sun and Earth later formed from a gas and dust cloud enriched by earlier stars.
Use after: End of lesson — Model Building: first model update and closing link to the anchoring phenomenon (night sky above Nairobi)
Q9. A learner says: 'The night sky above Nairobi looks the same every night, so the universe has always been like this.' Which reply best uses the evidence from the lesson?
  1. The sky only looks unchanged because a human lifetime is a tiny sliver of the 13.8 billion years over which the universe has evolved
  2. The sky looks unchanged because the universe is only a few thousand years old
  3. The sky looks unchanged because the Big Bang created all the stars, planets and galaxies at the same time
  4. The sky looks unchanged because the universe stopped expanding after the first galaxies formed
Correct answer: A
On the 2-metre rope timeline, human history is almost an invisible sliver of cosmic time. The sky seems still because we observe it for so short a time, even though the universe evolved over 13.8 billion years and is still expanding.
