Use after: Predict phase — Predict: Rift Valley night-sky photograph, writing a type-list and deciding when two objects belong to the same category
Q1. A student sorts the objects in a Rift Valley night-sky photograph into two groups: objects that MAKE their own light and objects that only REFLECT light. Which object belongs in the group that makes its own light?
  1. Jupiter, a planet
  2. Venus, a planet
  3. The Moon
  4. Sirius, a star
Correct answer: D
Stars such as Sirius produce their own light. Planets and moons only reflect light from a star, so Venus, Jupiter and the Moon belong in the reflecting group.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Stellarium Observation Sheet, logging Venus and classifying it using the data panel
Q2. In Stellarium, a learner sees that Venus is one of the brightest objects in the Nairobi evening sky and writes 'star' as its type. Which statement correctly explains why Venus is NOT a star?
  1. Venus is a star, but it is much closer than the other stars
  2. Venus is too small to be seen without a telescope
  3. Venus does not make its own light; it reflects light from the Sun, so it is a planet
  4. Venus is brighter than every star, so it must be something different
Correct answer: C
Brightness to the eye does not decide the type of object. Venus is a planet because it only reflects sunlight, while stars make their own light.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Stellarium Observation Sheet, 'How I can tell (evidence from Stellarium data panel)' column
Q3. A learner fills in the 'How I can tell' column of a Stellarium Observation Sheet for the Andromeda Galaxy. Which entry is the strongest scientific evidence for its type?
  1. 'The data panel gives its type as galaxy and its distance as millions of light-years.'
  2. 'It is in the sky near the Milky Way.'
  3. 'My friend said it is a galaxy.'
  4. 'It looks bright and fuzzy to me.'
Correct answer: A
Evidence should come from data such as type, distance and magnitude in the data panel. How bright an object looks to the eye does not show what type of object it is.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: 'Evolution of the Universe in 5 Minutes' clip paused at first stars, galaxies and solar system formation
Q4. Which list puts these events in the correct order of cosmic evolution, from earliest to latest?
  1. First stars form, then the Big Bang, then galaxies, then rocky bodies
  2. Big Bang, then first stars, then galaxies, then solar systems, then rocky bodies
  3. Big Bang, then solar systems, then first stars, then galaxies
  4. Solar systems form, then first stars form, then galaxies form, then the Big Bang
Correct answer: B
The sequence in the lesson is Big Bang, then first stars, then galaxies, then solar systems, then rocky bodies. Planets and rocky bodies could only form after earlier stars existed.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Classification Table, six rows on stars, planets, moons, comets, asteroids and galaxies with defining characteristics
Q5. The Milky Way band that students see on a clear night in the Rift Valley is part of which class of celestial body, and what is that class?
  1. A planet: a body that orbits a star and reflects its light
  2. A galaxy: a huge collection of billions of stars held together in space
  3. An asteroid: a small rocky body that orbits the Sun
  4. A comet: an icy body with a glowing tail
Correct answer: B
The Milky Way is our galaxy, an enormous system of billions of stars. Comets, planets and asteroids are much smaller objects.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Classification Table, 'Approximate Cosmic Age' column and checking that the age of the universe is not confused with the age of the solar system
Q6. The universe is about 13.8 billion years old. Our solar system formed about 9 billion years after the Big Bang. About how many billion years ago did our solar system form?
  1. 13.8 billion years ago
  2. 9 billion years ago
  3. 4.8 billion years ago
  4. 22.8 billion years ago
Correct answer: C
The solar system formed 9 billion years after the Big Bang, so the time since then is 13.8 - 9 = 4.8 billion years. The age of the universe is not the same as the age of the solar system.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: second sticky note with one new question raised by the classification activity
Q7. After the classification activity, a learner wants to post a question on the Driving Question Board. Which question is the best scientific question that the class could investigate?
  1. Why are stars more beautiful than planets?
  2. Why did God make the stars?
  3. What evidence shows how far away each star is from Earth?
  4. Is the night sky happy?
Correct answer: C
A testable question can be answered using evidence and measurement, such as distance data. The other questions are about purpose, opinion or feelings, which science cannot test.
