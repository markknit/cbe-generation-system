Q1. In the corridor model, each toilet paper square stands for 0.5 AU. One group's strip has a planet marked 8 squares from the Sun. How far is that planet from the Sun?
  1. 8 AU
  2. 16 AU
  3. 4 AU
  4. 2 AU
Correct answer: C
Each square is 0.5 AU, so 8 squares is 8 × 0.5 = 4 AU. Learners who answer 8 AU forget the scale, and those who answer 16 AU divide instead of multiplying.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: walking the corridor and recording 'I noticed that ...' observations at the Neptune end
Q2. In a scale model of the Solar System, Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars all lie within 1.52 AU of the Sun, while Neptune is 30.05 AU away. What does this show about the structure of the Solar System?
  1. The planets are spread out evenly, with about the same gap between each one
  2. The outer planets are crowded together while the inner planets are far apart
  3. The Solar System is mostly filled with planets and moons, with very little empty space
  4. The four inner planets are bunched close to the Sun and most of the Solar System is empty space
Correct answer: D
All four inner planets fit within about 1.5 AU, while Neptune is 30 AU out. So the planets are clustered near the Sun and huge gaps separate them from the outer planets, leaving mostly empty space.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Solar System Distance Data Card, where 1 AU is about 150 million km and Neptune is 30.05 AU
Q3. 1 AU is about 150 million km. Neptune is about 30 AU from the Sun. About how far is Neptune from the Sun in kilometres?
  1. 450 million km
  2. 30 million km
  3. 4 500 million km
  4. 180 million km
Correct answer: C
Distance = 30 × 150 million km = 4 500 million km (4.5 billion km). One AU is the Earth–Sun distance, so 30 AU is 30 times as far.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: class discussion 'what stops them from simply flying off in a straight line?' and force diagram of the Sun and Earth
Q4. The planets are tiny compared with the empty space around them, and nothing solid connects them to the Sun. What keeps a planet such as Jupiter orbiting the Sun instead of flying off into space?
  1. The Sun's gravitational pull toward the Sun, together with the planet's sideways motion, bends its path into an orbit
  2. The asteroid belt acts like a barrier that stops the planets from drifting away
  3. The planets are not moving sideways at all; they hang still because the Sun's gravity holds them in place
  4. The Sun's heat pushes the planets so they stay at a fixed distance
Correct answer: A
The Sun's large mass pulls each planet inward. The planet also moves sideways at high speed, so the two together give a curved orbit rather than a straight-line escape or a crash into the Sun.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: panga-on-a-rope analogy for orbital motion
Q5. A panga tied to a rope is swung in a circle, and the rope acts like the Sun's gravity on a planet. Suppose the Sun's gravity suddenly disappeared. What would Jupiter do?
  1. Stop moving and stay where it is
  2. Keep moving in the same circle because it is used to orbiting
  3. Move off in a straight line, like the panga when the rope breaks
  4. Spiral inward and crash into the Sun
Correct answer: C
The inward pull is what bends the planet's path. With no pull, the planet would keep its sideways motion and travel in a straight line, as the panga does when the rope breaks.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: written question (b) 'Why is Jupiter much farther from the Sun than Venus, yet both orbit the same Sun?'
Q6. Jupiter is about 5.2 AU from the Sun and Venus is about 0.72 AU away, yet both orbit the same Sun. Which statement explains this correctly?
  1. Jupiter stays in orbit only because it formed in the cold outer Solar System
  2. The Sun's gravity still reaches Jupiter but is weaker there, so Jupiter moves more slowly than Venus and takes much longer to go round
  3. The Sun's gravity does not reach Jupiter, so Jupiter is held in orbit by its own gravity
  4. The Sun's gravity is stronger at Jupiter than at Venus, so Jupiter must move faster
Correct answer: B
Gravity weakens with distance but still acts across the whole Solar System. Venus feels a stronger pull and moves faster, while Jupiter feels a weaker pull and moves slower. The Sun's pull keeps bending both paths.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding gravity arrows to the Solar System Structure panel of the cumulative model
Q7. A learner draws a Solar System model with arrows showing the gravitational force of the Sun on each planet. Which drawing is correct?
  1. Arrows pointing along the orbit, in the direction the planet is moving
  2. No arrows are needed, because there is no force acting on planets in empty space
  3. Arrows pointing from each planet toward the Sun, because gravity pulls the planets inward
  4. Arrows pointing away from the Sun, because the Sun pushes the planets outward
Correct answer: C
The Sun's gravity pulls each planet toward the Sun. The idea that the Sun pushes planets away is a common misconception to correct.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the asteroid belt to the Solar System Structure panel
Q8. The Solar System has an asteroid belt at roughly 2.2 to 3.2 AU from the Sun. Mars is at 1.52 AU, Jupiter at 5.20 AU and Saturn at 9.58 AU. Where should the asteroid belt be drawn in a model?
  1. Beyond Neptune
  2. Between Earth and Mars
  3. Between Mars and Jupiter
  4. Between Jupiter and Saturn
Correct answer: C
A distance of 2.2 to 3.2 AU is greater than Mars (1.52 AU) and less than Jupiter (5.20 AU), so the belt lies between Mars and Jupiter.
