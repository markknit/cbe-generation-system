Q1. A photograph shows a metallic sphere about the size of a basketball, covered in antenna rods. It was launched from Kazakhstan on 4 October 1957. What was this object?
  1. Voyager 1, the probe that took the first detailed images of the outer planets
  2. Vostok 1, the spacecraft that carried the first human into space
  3. Sputnik 1, the first human-made object to orbit Earth
  4. The Hubble Space Telescope, which images distant galaxies
Correct answer: C
Sputnik 1 (1957) was the Soviet satellite that became the first human-made object to orbit Earth. Vostok 1 (1961), Voyager (1977) and Hubble (1990) came later.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Mission Data Cards and observation table (timeline of missions)
Q2. Which list shows these four missions in the correct order, from earliest to latest?
  1. Sputnik 1, Vostok 1, Hubble Space Telescope, Apollo 11
  2. Sputnik 1, Apollo 11, Vostok 1, Hubble Space Telescope
  3. Sputnik 1, Vostok 1, Apollo 11, Hubble Space Telescope
  4. Vostok 1, Sputnik 1, Apollo 11, Hubble Space Telescope
Correct answer: C
Sputnik 1 was launched in 1957, Vostok 1 in 1961, Apollo 11 in 1969 and Hubble in 1990.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: 1-page briefing on the Kenya Space Agency (KSA)
Q3. The Kenya Space Agency (KSA) was founded in 2017 and is headquartered in Nairobi. In 2018 Kenya's first satellite, 1KUNS-PF, was launched. Where was it launched from?
  1. The International Space Station (ISS)
  2. A launch pad in Nairobi
  3. Kazakhstan, the same site as Sputnik 1
  4. The surface of the Moon
Correct answer: A
1KUNS-PF was launched from the International Space Station in 2018. It was not launched from Nairobi or from the Moon.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: briefing on Africa's role in the Square Kilometre Array (SKA)
Q4. SKA-Mid is being built in South Africa's Karoo, and its precursor MeerKAT already operates there. What kind of instrument is it, and what does it detect?
  1. A radio telescope array that detects radio waves from space
  2. A satellite that measures the speed of rockets
  3. An optical telescope in orbit that photographs visible light
  4. A rover that collects rock samples from Mars
Correct answer: A
The Square Kilometre Array is a large array of radio telescopes. It detects radio waves from space and does not collect samples or orbit Earth.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: anchor question 2 on JWST, light and distance
Q5. The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) detects light that left a galaxy 13.5 billion years ago. What does this tell us?
  1. Distance and time are unrelated, so the image shows the galaxy as it is now
  2. We see the galaxy as it looks today, because light from space arrives instantly
  3. The galaxy is only 13.5 years old
  4. We see the galaxy as it looked 13.5 billion years ago, because light takes time to travel across space
Correct answer: D
Light travels at a finite speed. Light from very distant objects shows them as they were long ago, so looking far away means looking back in time.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: second half of the driving question on gathering evidence about a universe we cannot touch
Q6. No human has touched a star or visited another galaxy. Which statement best explains how scientists have gathered evidence about the distant universe?
  1. Instruments such as telescopes and radio arrays detect light and radio waves that reach us from space
  2. Scientists only use naked-eye observations from dark places like the Rift Valley
  3. Astronauts have travelled to distant galaxies and brought back samples
  4. Scientists guessed what the universe looks like, because it cannot be studied
Correct answer: A
Evidence about the distant universe is gathered indirectly. Instruments such as Hubble, JWST and radio arrays detect light and radio waves that travel to us.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the 'Human Evidence Layer' timeline bar with arrows linking missions to evidence
Q7. A learner adds arrows to the 'Human Evidence Layer' of a universe model. Which mission and evidence pairing is correct?
  1. Voyager 1 & 2: lunar rock samples
  2. Apollo 11: lunar rock samples brought back to Earth
  3. Mars rovers: infrared images of the earliest galaxies
  4. Hubble Space Telescope: first human in space
Correct answer: B
Apollo 11 (1969) was the first Moon landing and returned lunar rock samples. Voyager imaged the outer planets, Vostok 1 carried the first human, and JWST took the infrared images of the earliest galaxies.
