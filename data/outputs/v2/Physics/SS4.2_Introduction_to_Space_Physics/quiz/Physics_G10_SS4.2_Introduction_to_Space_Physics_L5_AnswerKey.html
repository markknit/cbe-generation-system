Use after: Observe phase — Task A: Orbit Shape (PhET 'Gravity and Orbits', Sun and Planet mode)
Q1. In the PhET 'Gravity and Orbits' simulation, a planet is set moving around the Sun at a speed that is too high for a circular orbit. Which description of the path matches Kepler's First Law?
  1. An ellipse (oval) with the Sun at one focus, not at the centre
  2. A perfect circle with the Sun exactly at the centre
  3. A straight line that carries the planet away from the Sun
  4. An ellipse with the Sun at the exact centre of the oval
Correct answer: A
Kepler's First Law says planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus. The Sun is offset from the centre of the oval, so a circle or a centred ellipse is wrong.
Use after: Observe phase — Task B: Speed Variation (Kepler's Second Law) with Path and Velocity vectors
Q2. A planet moves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit. A learner shows the planet's velocity arrow at two points. Where is the planet moving fastest?
  1. At the same speed everywhere, because planets move at constant speed
  2. Nearest to the Sun, where the velocity arrow is longest
  3. Farthest from the Sun, because it has the most room to speed up
  4. Halfway between the nearest and farthest points only
Correct answer: B
The planet moves fastest at perihelion, nearest the Sun, and slowest at aphelion, farthest from the Sun. This is the pattern behind Kepler's Second Law.
Use after: Observe phase — Task C: Period vs Distance (Kepler's Third Law, Qualitative)
Q3. A data strip gives these orbital periods: Mercury 0.24 years (0.39 AU from the Sun), Earth 1.00 year (1.00 AU) and Jupiter 11.86 years (5.20 AU). Which pattern do the data show?
  1. The farther a planet is from the Sun, the shorter its orbital period
  2. Orbital period does not depend on distance, only on the size of the planet
  3. The farther a planet is from the Sun, the longer its orbital period
  4. All planets take about the same time to orbit the Sun
Correct answer: C
As distance increases from 0.39 to 1.00 to 5.20 AU, the period increases from 0.24 to 1.00 to 11.86 years. This is the qualitative pattern behind Kepler's Third Law.
Use after: Explain phase — Calculation table: T², r³ and T²/r³ for six planets
Q4. Mars has an orbital period T = 1.88 years. What is T² for Mars, in years², when completing the Kepler's Third Law calculation table?
  1. 1.37
  2. 3.76
  3. 5.64
  4. 3.53
Correct answer: D
T² means T multiplied by itself: 1.88 × 1.88 = 3.53 years². Squaring is not the same as doubling (3.76), tripling (5.64) or taking a square root (1.37).
Use after: Explain phase — Calculation table and T² vs r³ graph with the CER paragraph
Q5. A learner calculates T²/r³ for Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn, using T in years and r in AU. Every answer is close to 1.0. A graph of T² against r³ is also a straight line through the origin. What does this show?
  1. The planets all travel at the same speed in their orbits
  2. All six planets are the same distance from the Sun
  3. All six planets have the same orbital period
  4. T² is proportional to r³ for all these planets, which verifies Kepler's Third Law
Correct answer: D
A constant ratio T²/r³ and a straight-line graph through the origin both show that T² is proportional to r³. This is Kepler's Third Law. The planets have different distances and periods.
Use after: Explain phase — Formative assessment: common misconception about elliptical orbits and seasons
Q6. A learner says: 'Earth has seasons because its elliptical orbit makes it much closer to the Sun in summer.' Which response is correct?
  1. Correct, because Earth is closest to the Sun in June
  2. Correct, because Earth's orbit is a very long, thin ellipse
  3. Incorrect, because Earth's orbit is almost circular and the seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis
  4. Incorrect, because the Sun moves closer to Earth in summer
Correct answer: C
Earth's orbit is almost circular and Earth is closest to the Sun in early January. Seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis, not by distance from the Sun.
Use after: End of lesson — After the whole lesson: applying Kepler's Third Law (T² ∝ r³) to a new orbit
Q7. Using years for T and AU for r, T²/r³ = 1 for planets orbiting the Sun. A new object orbits the Sun with a semi-major axis r = 4 AU. What is its orbital period T?
  1. 4 years
  2. 64 years
  3. 16 years
  4. 8 years
Correct answer: D
T² = r³ = 4³ = 64, so T = √64 = 8 years. The answer 4 treats T as equal to r, 16 uses r², and 64 forgets to take the square root.
