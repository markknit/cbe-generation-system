Q1. A Grade 10 student writes on a prediction card: "Space science is done in other countries. Kenyans do not work in space science." Which statement best corrects this belief?
  1. Kenyans can only work in space science if they first study abroad, because no Kenyan university teaches related courses.
  2. Kenyan professionals work in space-related careers, for example at the Kenya Space Agency and the Kenya Meteorological Department.
  3. Kenyans work only in telecommunications, never in satellite engineering or astrophysics.
  4. Space science jobs in Kenya exist only in Nairobi and are not open to students from other counties.
Correct answer: B
Kenyan institutions such as the Kenya Space Agency, the Kenya Meteorological Department and universities such as the University of Nairobi and Strathmore University employ space professionals, and the career is open to any Kenyan student.
Use after: Observe phase — Career cluster folders: Cluster 2 – Satellite Engineering (Kenya Space Agency, Taifa-1)
Q2. Which of the following is Taifa-1, featured in the Satellite Engineering career cluster?
  1. A weather balloon launched by the Kenya Meteorological Department in 2023
  2. A telescope at the University of Nairobi used to study the Big Bang
  3. Kenya's first operational Earth-observation satellite, launched in 2023
  4. A geostationary communications satellite owned by Safaricom
Correct answer: C
Taifa-1 is Kenya's first operational Earth-observation satellite, built by the Kenya Space Agency team and launched in 2023.
Use after: Observe phase — Career cluster folders: evaluating sources for credibility while completing the Career Profile Poster
Q3. A group needs reliable information about the education pathway to become a satellite engineer in Kenya. Which source should they trust MOST?
  1. An unsigned social media post that says "anyone can do it without studying"
  2. A forwarded message with no author or date
  3. The Kenya Space Agency website and the course pages of a Kenyan university
  4. A friend's memory of something a relative once said
Correct answer: C
Credible sources come from named, relevant institutions such as the Kenya Space Agency and universities. Anonymous posts, hearsay and forwarded messages cannot be checked.
Use after: Explain phase — Structured whole-class PCI discussion on gender equity in space careers (UNESCO infographic)
Q4. Worldwide, about one in three researchers (about 33%) is a woman (UNESCO Institute for Statistics). Which conclusion is best supported by this lesson?
  1. Girls should avoid physics until more women work in space science.
  2. Space science is only suitable for students from certain counties, which explains the lower share.
  3. Women are less able than men at physics, which explains the lower share.
  4. The idea that physics is not for girls is a myth. Women already work in space science and engineering, including in Africa, and ability, not gender, decides a person's path.
Correct answer: D
The lower share of women does not show lower ability. The lesson concludes that the belief that space science is not for girls is a myth, and that many women already work in space science, including in Africa.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: three-colour sticky notes, blue 'connection' note
Q5. Which sticky note is the strongest "blue" connection note linking a career to evidence from an earlier lesson?
  1. "I want to be a scientist one day."
  2. "It connects to space."
  3. "Space careers are interesting and exciting."
  4. "Meteorologists use weather satellites in orbit, which links to our lesson on satellites, because the satellite images are what forecasters use."
Correct answer: D
A strong connection note names both a career and a specific concept from an earlier lesson. Here the meteorologist is linked to satellites in orbit sending images used for forecasts. The other notes are vague or give no link.
Use after: Model Building phase — Human Network overlay: adding career labels at the correct scale on the cumulative 'How We Know' model
Q6. On a diagram of the universe, students label where each career works. Which career belongs at the LARGEST scale, the whole observable universe (Big Bang and cosmic microwave background)?
  1. Meteorologist
  2. Cosmologist
  3. Telecommunications engineer working with geostationary satellites
  4. Satellite engineer building Earth-orbiting satellites
Correct answer: B
Cosmologists study the origin and evolution of the whole universe, including the Big Bang and the cosmic microwave background. The other careers work at Earth orbit or atmosphere scale.
Use after: Model Building phase — Human Network overlay: teacher check of career labels placed at scientifically appropriate scales
Q7. A student places "meteorologist at the Kenya Meteorological Department" at the galaxy scale on the model. Which correction is best?
  1. Move it to the scale of the Solar System, because forecasters measure the Sun's distance.
  2. Leave it at the galaxy scale, because satellites collect data from other galaxies.
  3. Move it to the scale of Earth's atmosphere, because meteorologists use data on weather and satellite images collected near Earth.
  4. Move it to the scale of the whole universe, because weather forecasts depend on the Big Bang.
Correct answer: C
Meteorologists study Earth's atmosphere using satellite data (for example from EUMETSAT). The data is collected near Earth, not at galaxy distances.
