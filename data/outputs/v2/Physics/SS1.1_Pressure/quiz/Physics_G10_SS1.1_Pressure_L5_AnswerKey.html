Q1. Three identical cans each have a hole 10 cm below the surface. They are filled to the same level with water, salt water and cooking oil. You want to predict which liquid pushes hardest on the side of its can at this depth. Which prediction best uses what you learned in this lesson?
  1. Salt water pushes hardest, because it is the densest liquid
  2. All three push the same, because the depth is the same
  3. Cooking oil pushes hardest, because it is thick and heavy-looking
  4. Water pushes hardest, because it is the most common liquid
Correct answer: A
At the same depth, pressure increases with density. Salt water (1,030 kg/m3) is denser than water (1,000 kg/m3) and oil (920 kg/m3), so it gives the largest pressure.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: wide basin and thin tall tube filled to the same depth
Q2. A wide basin and a thin tall tube both hold water to the same depth of 10 cm. The basin holds far more water. A learner says, 'The basin has more water, so the pressure at its bottom is greater.' What is the best response?
  1. Correct: more water always gives more pressure at the bottom
  2. Wrong: the thin tube gives greater pressure because the water is squeezed into a small space
  3. Wrong: the pressure at the bottom is the same in both, because it depends on depth and not on the amount of water
  4. Correct: a wider container spreads the pressure and makes it larger
Correct answer: C
At the same depth in the same liquid the pressure is the same, about 1,000 Pa, whatever the container shape or amount of water.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation A (density): bottles with a hole at 10 cm and the U-tube pressure gauge
Q3. Three bottles each have a hole at 10 cm depth and contain water, salt water and cooking oil. The three streams land at about the same distance. What does this tell you about using jets to compare liquids?
  1. The three liquids must have exactly the same pressure
  2. Density has no effect on the pressure in a liquid
  3. The jet from the oil must be broken
  4. Jets are not good for comparing the pressure of different liquids at the same depth, so a pressure gauge is needed
Correct answer: D
In the investigation the jets landed at about the same place, so they cannot show the effect of density. A U-tube pressure gauge showed the differences.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation A (density): table of pressures at 10 cm
Q4. A U-tube pressure gauge is lowered to 10 cm depth in water, in salt water and in cooking oil. In which order are the pressures, from largest to smallest?
  1. Salt water, water, cooking oil
  2. Water, cooking oil, salt water
  3. Cooking oil, water, salt water
  4. Water, salt water, cooking oil
Correct answer: A
The pressures at 10 cm are 1,030 Pa for salt water, 1,000 Pa for water and 920 Pa for cooking oil, matching their densities.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation A: teacher asks 'Keep the depth the same. Only the liquid changes. Why do we do that?'
Q5. In an experiment, learners compare water, salt water and cooking oil using a pressure gauge. They keep the gauge at the same depth of 10 cm in every liquid. Why do they keep the depth the same?
  1. So that only the liquid changes, making it a fair test of density
  2. Because depth has no effect on liquid pressure
  3. So that the liquids all give the same pressure
  4. So that less liquid is used
Correct answer: A
A fair test changes one variable (the liquid) and controls the others (depth), so any difference in pressure is due to density.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation C (direction): bottle with three holes at the same 10 cm depth
Q6. A bottle of water has three holes at the same depth of 10 cm. One faces sideways, one is angled and one faces down. What is observed?
  1. The sideways hole gives the strongest stream
  2. The angled hole gives the weakest pressure
  3. The streams or readings are the same size in every direction
  4. The downward hole gives the strongest stream because gravity pulls down
Correct answer: C
At the same depth, the pressure in a liquid acts equally in all directions, so the streams or readings are the same.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain question 1: which liquid gave the largest pressure at 10 cm and why
Q7. At 10 cm depth, the pressure in water is 1,000 Pa and in cooking oil it is 920 Pa. Why is the pressure in oil smaller?
  1. Oil has less depth than water
  2. Oil is less dense than water (920 kg/m3 compared with 1,000 kg/m3)
  3. Oil pushes only upwards
  4. Oil is in a different shaped container
Correct answer: B
Depth is the same, so the difference comes from density. Oil is less dense, so it gives a smaller pressure at the same depth.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: diver in Lake Victoria at Kisumu feels more pressure deeper down
Q8. A diver in Lake Victoria at Kisumu swims from 2 m depth down to 5 m depth. What happens to the water pressure on the diver?
  1. It increases, because pressure in a liquid increases with depth
  2. It stays the same, because the water is the same lake
  3. It decreases, because there is more water above
  4. It stays the same, because pressure acts equally in all directions
Correct answer: A
Liquid pressure increases with depth. Equal pressure in all directions applies only at the same depth, so it does not mean pressure stays constant as depth changes.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model building: list 'what matters' and 'what does not matter'
Q9. Which list names the factors that DO change the pressure at a point in a liquid, and the ones that do NOT?
  1. Change it: direction and amount of liquid. Do not change it: depth and density
  2. Change it: depth and density. Do not change it: container shape and amount of liquid
  3. Change it: container shape and depth. Do not change it: density and direction
  4. Change it: amount of liquid and density. Do not change it: depth and shape
Correct answer: B
The evidence showed pressure depends on depth and density. Container shape and amount of liquid made no difference at the same depth.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model building: arrows showing pressure at different depths and directions
Q10. In a model of a container of liquid, arrows show the pressure. Which drawing is correct?
  1. Arrows pointing only downwards, all of equal length
  2. Arrows longer sideways than downwards at the same depth
  3. Arrows pointing only downwards, longer near the surface
  4. Arrows of equal length in all directions at the same depth, and longer arrows lower down
Correct answer: D
Pressure acts equally in all directions at the same depth and increases with depth, so arrows are equal at one depth and longer lower down.
