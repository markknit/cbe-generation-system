Q1. Two water-filled syringes, one small and one large, are joined by a short tube with no air in it. A learner pushes the small plunger in by 4 cm and measures how far the large plunger moves out. Which result is correct?
  1. The large plunger does not move because water cannot be pushed
  2. The large plunger moves out more than 4 cm
  3. The large plunger moves out 4 cm, the same distance
  4. The large plunger moves out about 1 cm, less than the small plunger moved in
Correct answer: D
The same volume of water leaves the small syringe and enters the large one. The large piston has more area, so it moves a shorter distance. The small plunger moves farther.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 1: calculate the pressure on the small piston (P = F / A)
Q2. In a workshop hydraulic jack, a 50 N effort pushes on a small piston of area 5 cm² (0.0005 m²). What is the pressure on the small piston?
  1. 25,000 Pa
  2. 100,000 Pa (100 kPa)
  3. 250 Pa
  4. 10 Pa
Correct answer: B
P = F / A = 50 N / 0.0005 m² = 100,000 Pa, which is 100 kPa.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 2: state that the enclosed water transmits pressure equally (Pascal's principle)
Q3. In a hydraulic jack, the water is enclosed and the pressure on the small piston is 100 kPa. The large piston has 20 times the area of the small piston. What is the pressure on the large piston?
  1. 100 kPa, the same as on the small piston
  2. 2,000 kPa, because the area is 20 times bigger
  3. 0 kPa, because the large piston is not pushed directly
  4. 5 kPa, because the area is bigger
Correct answer: A
Pascal's principle: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions. The pressure is the same on both pistons, but the forces are different because the areas are different.
Use after: Explain phase — Steps 3-5: find the force on the large piston and show F1/A1 = F2/A2
Q4. A hydraulic jack has a small piston of area 5 cm² and a large piston of area 100 cm². A 50 N effort is applied to the small piston. Using F1/A1 = F2/A2, what load force acts on the large piston?
  1. 1,000 N
  2. 50 N
  3. 10 N
  4. 2,500 N
Correct answer: A
F2 = F1 × A2 / A1 = 50 N × 100 cm² / 5 cm² = 1,000 N. The pressure is 100 kPa on both pistons and the area is 20 times bigger, so the force is 20 times bigger.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 8: hydraulic brakes (master piston and wheel piston calculation)
Q5. In a matatu's hydraulic brakes, a driver's foot pushes the master piston (area 2 cm²) with a force of 300 N. The brake fluid transmits the pressure to a wheel piston of area 10 cm². What force acts on the wheel piston?
  1. 1,500 N
  2. 60 N
  3. 300 N
  4. 6,000 N
Correct answer: A
The pressure is the same in the fluid, so F2 = F1 × A2 / A1 = 300 N × 10 cm² / 2 cm² = 1,500 N. The wheel piston has 5 times the area, so it gets 5 times the force.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 8: why a mechanic bleeds air out of the brake lines
Q6. A mechanic bleeds air out of a matatu's brake lines. Why is this done?
  1. Air can be squeezed but liquid cannot, so air in the fluid gives a soft pedal and weaker braking
  2. Air changes the pressure in the fluid so that it is not transmitted equally in all directions at all
  3. Air is heavier than brake fluid and makes the wheels turn slowly
  4. Air makes the brake fluid freeze
Correct answer: A
Air is compressible, so part of the pedal push goes into squeezing the air bubbles instead of transmitting pressure through the fluid. This gives a soft pedal and weaker braking.
Use after: Explain phase — Step 9: tyre pressure and vehicle loading (W = P × A)
Q7. A matatu tyre carries a load of 10,000 N and is inflated to 250 kPa (250,000 Pa). Using W = P × A, what area of the tyre touches the road?
  1. 0.4 m²
  2. 0.04 m²
  3. 4 m²
  4. 0.025 m²
Correct answer: B
A = W / P = 10,000 N / 250,000 Pa = 0.04 m² (400 cm²). If the pressure is not raised for a heavier load, more rubber touches the road, so the tyre flexes and heats more and can burst.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: 'How does the jack help us think about the knives? What is the same? What is different?'
Q8. A learner compares the two situations in this unit. With the knives, the same 40 N force on different areas gave different pressures. In the hydraulic jack, which statement is correct?
  1. The pressure and the force are the same on both pistons
  2. The jack creates extra energy, so the load force is bigger
  3. The same pressure acts on both pistons, but the areas differ, so the forces are different
  4. The same force acts on both pistons, so the pressures are different
Correct answer: C
In the jack the pressure (100 kPa) is the same in the enclosed fluid and the area differs, so the force differs (50 N and 1,000 N). The knives are the opposite case: the same force on different areas gives different pressures. The jack does not create energy, because the small piston moves farther.
