Use after: Explain phase — Station 1: Lightning conductor (Kericho and Kisumu), and the CER explanation for the lightning conductor
Q1. A school in Kericho has a lightning conductor: a pointed metal rod on the roof joined by a thick copper strip to a metal plate buried in the ground. Which statement best explains how it protects the building?
  1. The pointed rod is magnetic, so it pulls lightning away from the roof and into the sky
  2. Charge crowds at the sharp point and leaks gradually into the air, and the copper strip lets electrons flow safely to or from the ground
  3. The copper strip is an insulator that stops electrons from reaching the building
  4. The rod stores all the charge from the storm so that the building stays neutral
Correct answer: B
Charge crowds at a sharp point, so charge leaks away through the air gradually. The thick copper strip and earth plate give electrons a safe path to or from the ground. It does not attract lightning by magnetism.
Use after: Explain phase — Station 2: Fuel tanker at a Nairobi petrol depot, and the CER explanation for the fuel tanker
Q2. A fuel tanker at a Nairobi petrol depot trails a metal chain on the ground. Fuel rubbing on the pipe wall can charge the tank. Why is the chain used?
  1. It cools the fuel so that less vapour is produced
  2. It stops the fuel from rubbing on the pipe wall, so no charge forms at all
  3. It lets electrons flow to the ground, so charge cannot build up on the tank and cause a spark near fuel vapour
  4. It adds extra positive charge to the tank so that the tank becomes neutral
Correct answer: C
The chain earths the tanker. Electrons flow to the ground, so friction charge cannot build up and no spark can ignite the fuel vapour. The chain does not stop the rubbing; it removes the charge that rubbing produces.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 4: Electrostatic precipitator at Athi River
Q3. In an electrostatic precipitator at a cement factory, smoke dust particles are given a negative charge as they rise up the chimney. The dust is then collected on metal plates. What charge must the plates carry to collect the dust?
  1. No charge at all, because dust falls onto plates by gravity only
  2. Positive, because unlike charges attract
  3. Negative, because like charges attract
  4. Either sign, because the sign of the plate does not affect the dust
Correct answer: B
Unlike charges attract. Negatively charged dust is pulled to positively charged plates. Plates with the same negative charge would repel the dust.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 3: Photocopier, dry demonstration with a rubbed polythene strip and paper pieces
Q4. A photocopier drum attracts toner powder that has the opposite charge to the drum. A learner says the same idea can be seen when a rubbed polythene strip is passed over small paper pieces. What do the paper pieces do?
  1. They jump up and stick to the strip, because the charged strip attracts the neutral paper
  2. They jump up only if the strip is wet
  3. They are not affected, because paper is an insulator and cannot be attracted
  4. They are pushed away, because the strip and the paper have the same charge
Correct answer: A
The charged strip makes the near side of each neutral paper piece oppositely charged (induction), so the attraction is stronger than the repulsion. The pieces jump and stick, as toner does on the drum. Keeping the strip dry matters, because moisture lets charge leak away.
Use after: Observe phase — Guided research worksheet: Card A, spray gun
Q5. In a spray gun used to paint a metal gate, all the paint droplets are given the same sign of charge. What is the effect of this?
  1. The droplets can only coat the side of the gate that faces the gun
  2. The droplets repel each other and spread into a fine, even mist
  3. The droplets attract each other and join into large drops
  4. The droplets become neutral as they leave the gun
Correct answer: B
Droplets with the same sign of charge repel one another, so they spread out into a fine mist. The gate is earthed or oppositely charged, so the droplets are attracted to it and coat it evenly.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: Kipchoge's question, how the jersey shock is like and unlike lightning
Q6. Kipchoge feels a small shock after removing a woollen jersey in Nairobi. He asks if it is like lightning. Which answer is correct?
  1. No, lightning is caused by electrons but the jersey shock is caused by positive charge moving
  2. Yes, and both involve the same amount of charge
  3. Yes, both are discharges of built-up charge, but lightning involves vastly more charge
  4. No, the jersey shock is caused by magnetism, but lightning is caused by charge
Correct answer: C
Both are the sudden discharge of charge built up by friction and transferred as electrons. The difference is the amount of charge: the jersey shock involves a tiny charge, like the balloon's about 1 nC, while lightning involves vastly more.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain phase: 'Amina, why did the balloon fail at your coastal home?' and the balloon humidity data
Q7. At Amina's home in Mombasa, a rubbed balloon sticks to the wall for under 30 seconds or not at all when the relative humidity is about 80%. In Nairobi, at about 35% relative humidity, it sticks for about 3 minutes. What is the best explanation?
  1. Water vapour in humid air lets the charge on the balloon escape quickly
  2. Friction cannot transfer electrons in warm places
  3. Mombasa walls are always made of conductors
  4. Balloons made in Mombasa carry less mass
Correct answer: A
At high humidity, water vapour in the air lets the charge leak away from the balloon quickly, so it loses its charge and falls. In dry air the charge stays much longer.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model phase: final particle-level model, part (4) wall and paper
Q8. A rubbed balloon sticks to a neutral wall. Which statement explains why the wall attracts the balloon?
  1. Neutral objects are repelled by charged objects, but the wall is held by static electricity
  2. The wall is positively charged to begin with, so it attracts the negative balloon
  3. The balloon is magnetic, and the wall contains iron
  4. The negative balloon pushes electrons in the wall away from the near surface, leaving it positive, so the attraction is stronger than the repulsion
Correct answer: D
The negative balloon makes electrons in the wall shift away from the near surface (induction). The near surface becomes positive and is attracted to the balloon. This attraction is stronger than the repulsion from the far, negative side.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model phase: final particle-level model, part (2) after rubbing, and the Final Explanation on conservation of charge
Q9. A balloon is rubbed on hair. Electrons move from the hair to the balloon, and the balloon gains a charge of 1 nC (negative). What is true of the hair?
  1. The hair has a larger positive charge than the balloon's, because positive charges move in the hair
  2. The hair has a positive charge of 1 nC, because charge is conserved
  3. The hair has no charge, because charge is not created
  4. The hair gains an extra negative charge of 1 nC
Correct answer: B
Charge is transferred, not created. Whatever electrons the balloon gains, the hair loses, so the hair ends up with an equal positive charge of 1 nC (a deficit of electrons). Total charge stays the same.
