Use after: Observe phase — Station 1, Hair: 20 strokes of the balloon on dry hair
Q1. A group rubs a dry balloon on one learner's hair 20 times and then slowly pulls it away. Which statement best describes what they observed?
  1. The hair rises and follows the balloon, even though nothing visible connects them.
  2. The hair is pushed away from the balloon.
  3. The hair stays attached to the balloon only because of glue-like stickiness.
  4. The hair stays flat unless the balloon touches it.
Correct answer: A
The observation was that hair strands rise and follow the balloon, up to about 5 cm away, without any visible link. This describes what was seen and does not explain it.
Use after: Observe phase — Control: an unrubbed balloon tried on hair, wall and paper
Q2. After testing a rubbed balloon on hair, the wall and paper, a group also tries an unrubbed balloon on the same things. Why do they do this?
  1. To find out whether the balloon is magnetic.
  2. To show that the effects are linked to rubbing, because the unrubbed balloon does nothing.
  3. To check which colour of balloon works best.
  4. To save the rubbed balloon so it lasts longer.
Correct answer: B
The unrubbed balloon is the control. It had no effect on hair, wall or paper, so the effects are linked to the rubbing.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 4, Two balloons: rubbed balloons hung from threads push apart
Q3. Two balloons are rubbed on hair and hung from separate threads. When they are brought near each other, they move apart. Which word do learners use to describe this pattern?
  1. Repel
  2. No effect
  3. Magnetise
  4. Attract
Correct answer: A
Objects that move apart are said to repel. Objects that come together attract. Learners are only describing the pattern here, not explaining it.
Use after: Observe phase — Mr Mwangi shows the humid-day (Mombasa) and dry-season (Nairobi) data
Q4. Amina says a rubbed balloon hardly sticks to the wall at her home in Mombasa on a humid day (about 80% humidity). In Nairobi's dry season (about 35% humidity) a balloon sticks for about 3 minutes. Which statement is supported by this information?
  1. The balloon effect is the same on humid and dry days.
  2. The balloon effect is stronger on humid days than on dry days.
  3. The balloon effect is weaker on humid days than on dry days.
  4. The balloon effect only works at the coast.
Correct answer: C
On the humid Mombasa day the balloon sticks for under 30 seconds or not at all. In dry Nairobi it sticks for about 3 minutes. So the effect is weaker when it is humid.
Use after: Explain phase — Comparing the balloon with a magnet: what is the same and what is different
Q5. Learners compare a rubbed balloon with a magnet. Which observation shows a difference between them?
  1. The rubbed balloon pulls things towards it from a short distance.
  2. The rubbed balloon pulls on hair without touching it.
  3. The rubbed balloon can pull on small pieces of paper, which are not metal.
  4. The rubbed balloon can pull on things across a small gap of air.
Correct answer: C
A magnet needs a magnetic material such as iron to pull on, but the balloon pulls on paper pieces. Pulling without touching is a similarity, not a difference.
Use after: DQB phase — Building the Driving Question Board: Kipchoge's question about the shock and lightning
Q6. Kipchoge asks, 'Is the shock I feel from my woollen jersey like lightning?' At this point in the unit, what is the best way for the class to treat his question?
  1. Say the shock is only in his imagination.
  2. Say the shock is caused by magnetism.
  3. Write it on the Driving Question Board as a question to investigate in later lessons, since it has not been explained yet.
  4. Answer that it is exactly the same as lightning, so the class can move on.
Correct answer: C
This is the opening lesson and the phenomenon is deliberately left unexplained. Good questions go on the DQB and are answered with evidence in later lessons.
Use after: Model Building phase — Initial Model drawing of balloon, hair, wall and paper piece
Q7. Learners draw an Initial Model of the balloon, hair, wall and paper. Which is the best thing to include in the model?
  1. Only the things they can see, with no arrows.
  2. Nothing until the teacher tells them the right answer.
  3. Only the correct textbook explanation, copied from a book.
  4. Arrows and labels for anything they think moves or acts, even if they cannot name it yet.
Correct answer: D
An initial model shows what learners currently think is happening, including invisible things. It is a starting point that will be revised in later lessons, so there are no wrong models today.
