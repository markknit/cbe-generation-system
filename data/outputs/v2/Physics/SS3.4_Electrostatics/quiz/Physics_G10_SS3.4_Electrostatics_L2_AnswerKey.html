Q1. Two identical polythene strips are each rubbed 20 times with wool. One hangs from a thread and the other is brought near it without touching. What happens?
  1. The hanging strip is pushed away (they repel)
  2. The strips attract because rubbed plastic becomes magnetic
  3. Nothing happens because the strips are identical
  4. The hanging strip is pulled towards the other strip (they attract)
Correct answer: A
Both strips are rubbed in the same way so they carry the same type of charge. Like charges repel, as the class table showed for two polythene strips.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: rods-and-strips investigation, step 3 (silk-rubbed perspex rod near hanging polythene strip)
Q2. A polythene strip rubbed with wool hangs from a thread. A perspex rod rubbed with silk is brought near it. The strip moves towards the rod. What does this result support?
  1. Neutral objects always repel charged ones
  2. Unlike charges attract
  3. Only perspex can attract objects
  4. Like charges attract
Correct answer: B
Polythene rubbed with wool/fur is negative and perspex rubbed with silk is positive. Their attraction shows that unlike charges attract.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: rods-and-strips investigation, step 4 (unrubbed control strips)
Q3. One unrubbed polythene strip hangs from a thread. A second unrubbed polythene strip is brought near it. Nothing happens. Why was this control trial included?
  1. It shows that the push or pull seen earlier needs the strips to be rubbed (charged)
  2. It shows that the strips only repel when they touch
  3. It shows that thread stops all forces
  4. It shows that polythene never carries charge
Correct answer: A
Unrubbed strips are not charged and show no effect. This shows the pushing and pulling in the other trials came from the charge produced by rubbing.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: 'Inside the Atom' card set and charge table
Q4. An atom is made of protons, neutrons and electrons. Which statement about the charge of a neutron is correct?
  1. A neutron has a charge of -1.6 x 10^-19 C
  2. A neutron has a very small positive charge
  3. A neutron has a charge of 0 C (no charge)
  4. A neutron has a charge of +1.6 x 10^-19 C
Correct answer: C
From the charge table, the proton is +1.6 x 10^-19 C, the electron is -1.6 x 10^-19 C and the neutron has no charge (0 C).
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: card sort building a neutral atom and a second atom with 3 protons and 2 electrons
Q5. An atom has 3 protons and 2 electrons. A proton has a charge of +1.6 x 10^-19 C and an electron has a charge of -1.6 x 10^-19 C. What is the total charge of the atom?
  1. -1.6 x 10^-19 C
  2. 0 C
  3. +4.8 x 10^-19 C
  4. +1.6 x 10^-19 C
Correct answer: D
Protons give 3 x (+1.6 x 10^-19) and electrons give 2 x (-1.6 x 10^-19). The total is (3 - 2) x 1.6 x 10^-19 = +1.6 x 10^-19 C, so the atom is not neutral.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-evidence-reasoning poster, question 3 (applying the rule to the two balloons)
Q6. In Lesson 1, two balloons were each rubbed on hair and hung on separate threads, and they pushed apart. What does this tell us about the charges on the two balloons?
  1. They carry opposite types of charge
  2. One is charged and the other is neutral
  3. Neither balloon is charged; the air pushes them apart
  4. They carry the same type of charge
Correct answer: D
Objects that repel carry the same type of charge, because like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Both balloons were rubbed in the same way.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-evidence-reasoning poster, question 2 (why a normal atom is neutral) and gallery walk discussion
Q7. An ordinary piece of rubber contains protons (positive) and electrons (negative). Why does it not push or pull on everything around it all the time?
  1. It contains only neutrons, which are uncharged
  2. Its protons and electrons are equal in number, so their charges cancel and it is neutral overall
  3. Its electrons are held in the nucleus where they cannot act
  4. It has no charged particles inside it
Correct answer: B
A normal atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons of equal size but opposite charge. The charges cancel, but the charged particles are still inside the atom.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model revision 2: zoomed-in atoms in the balloon rubber and hair strand
Q8. You draw a model of a neutral atom in the balloon rubber. Which drawing is correct?
  1. Protons and neutrons in the nucleus, with electrons around it and equal numbers of + and - charges
  2. Protons and electrons together in the nucleus, with neutrons around it
  3. Protons and neutrons in the nucleus, with twice as many electrons as protons around it
  4. Electrons in the nucleus and protons around it, with equal numbers of + and - charges
Correct answer: A
The nucleus holds protons (+) and neutrons (0) and the electrons (-) are outside it. A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons.
