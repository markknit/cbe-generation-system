Q1. A hollow metal can on an insulating stand is given a negative charge. A proof plane touched on the OUTSIDE of the can and then on the cap of a gold-leaf electroscope makes the leaves diverge clearly. Where do the extra electrons sit on the can?
  1. Only at the bottom of the can
  2. On the inner surface of the can
  3. Evenly spread through the inside of the metal
  4. On the outer surface of the can
Correct answer: D
Free electrons in a conductor repel each other and move as far apart as possible, so they collect on the outer surface. The clear leaf divergence from the outside is the evidence.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: one-sentence reason using free electrons and like charges repelling
Q2. Why do the extra electrons on a charged metal conductor move apart to the outside?
  1. The positive charges in the metal pull the electrons outwards
  2. Electrons are heavier than the metal atoms, so they sink to the surface
  3. The air outside the metal attracts the electrons
  4. Electrons repel each other (like charges repel) and are free to move in a conductor
Correct answer: D
Metals have free electrons. Like charges repel, so the electrons push each other as far apart as possible, which is the outer surface.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: proof plane on the inside of the can, then the electroscope
Q3. After the can is charged negatively, a proof plane touched on the INSIDE of the can is moved to a gold-leaf electroscope and the leaves hardly diverge. What is the correct conclusion?
  1. The electroscope is faulty because the can is charged
  2. There are no electrons inside the metal of the can
  3. There is little or no charge on the inside surface, but the can is still charged
  4. The can is not charged at all
Correct answer: C
The proof plane collects almost no charge from the inside because the extra charge sits on the outer surface. The can is still charged, and the electrons are on the outside.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: pear-shaped conductor, pointed end and flat end
Q4. A charged pear-shaped metal conductor is tested with a proof plane and an electroscope. The pointed end makes the leaves diverge strongly and the flat end makes them diverge only slightly. What does this show?
  1. Charge is more concentrated at the pointed end
  2. The flat end has more charge than the pointed end
  3. The charge is spread evenly over the whole conductor
  4. Charge is created at the pointed end
Correct answer: A
Greater leaf divergence means the proof plane collected more charge, so charge is concentrated at the point, where electrons crowd closer together.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A and Part B: earthing the electroscope between tests
Q5. Before each new reading with the same electroscope, the teacher touches the cap with a finger so the leaves fall together. Why is this done?
  1. To give the electroscope extra charge
  2. To earth the electroscope and remove the charge left from the previous test
  3. To cool the metal cap
  4. To make the leaves more sensitive by adding positive charge
Correct answer: B
Touching with a finger earths the electroscope, so the leaves fall together and each new reading starts with no charge.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: introducing point discharge
Q6. At a sharply pointed part of a charged conductor, the electric field is so strong that it ionises the air and charge leaks away. What is this process called?
  1. Electrostatic shielding
  2. Earthing by friction
  3. Point discharge
  4. Induction
Correct answer: C
Point discharge is the leaking of charge into the air from a point, where the field is strongest. It explains sparks and the action of lightning conductors.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: lightning conductor in Kericho (pointed rod, copper strip, earth plate)
Q7. A lightning conductor on a building in Kericho has a pointed metal rod on top, joined by thick copper strip to a metal plate buried in the ground. Why is the top of the rod pointed?
  1. Points create extra electrons to cancel the lightning
  2. Points attract more lightning than flat plates
  3. Charge leaks away gradually from a point (point discharge)
  4. Points stop the copper strip from rusting
Correct answer: C
A point has strong charge concentration, so charge leaks away into the air gradually. If lightning strikes, the rod and copper give a safe path to earth.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: comparing the lightning conductor with Kipchoge's question
Q8. Kipchoge asks if the shock from a woollen jersey on a dry Nairobi day is like lightning. Which answer is best?
  1. No, a jersey shock involves protons moving, while lightning involves electrons
  2. No, the shock is magnetic and lightning is electrical
  3. Yes, because both are caused by static electricity creating new charges
  4. Yes, both are discharges of charge, but lightning is much larger
Correct answer: D
Both are electrostatic discharges where built-up charge moves. Lightning is a much larger discharge. Charge is transferred, not created.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the particle-level diagram of the balloon, hair, wall and paper
Q9. On a student's particle-level model of a charged metal object touched by a balloon, which drawing is correct?
  1. Electrons spread on the outer surface, closer together at any pointed part
  2. Electrons spread evenly through the inside of the metal
  3. Electrons all collected at the flat part of the object
  4. Positive charges moving along the surface
Correct answer: A
On a conductor the extra electrons sit on the outer surface and crowd closer together at points. In solids only electrons move.
