Use after: Predict phase — Predict: Mr Mwangi holds a rubbed balloon 2 cm above torn paper pieces and learners predict why a neutral paper jumps
Q1. A balloon is rubbed on dry hair and becomes negative. It is held 2 cm above small pieces of neutral paper, and the pieces jump up to the balloon. Which explanation is correct?
  1. Electrons in the paper shift away from the balloon, leaving the near side of the paper positive, so it is attracted.
  2. The balloon gives electrons to the paper before touching it, so the paper becomes negative.
  3. The balloon is sticky, so it pulls the paper up.
  4. The paper was already positively charged before the balloon arrived.
Correct answer: A
The negative balloon repels electrons in the neutral paper, leaving the side nearest the balloon positive. The balloon attracts this nearer positive side, and no charge needs to be added to the paper.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 2: Induction on the electroscope (strip near the cap, then removed)
Q2. A neutral gold-leaf electroscope has its leaf hanging down. A rubbed polythene strip (negative) is brought near the cap without touching it, and the leaf diverges. When the strip is taken away, the leaf falls back down. What does this show?
  1. The strip lost all its charge when it was brought near the cap.
  2. The electroscope lost protons while the strip was near and got them back later.
  3. The electroscope gained electrons from the strip and then lost them to the air.
  4. No charge stayed on the electroscope. Its electrons only moved within the metal and then spread back.
Correct answer: D
The strip never touched the cap, so no charge was transferred. The electrons moved down to the leaf while the strip was near, then spread back when it was removed, and the total charge stayed zero.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 3: Charging by induction with earthing, Step 2 (touch the cap with a finger)
Q3. A negative polythene strip is held near the cap of a gold-leaf electroscope, and the leaf diverges. With the strip still near, a learner touches the cap with a finger and the leaf collapses. What has happened?
  1. Electrons have been repelled through the finger to the Earth, leaving the electroscope with a deficit of electrons.
  2. Protons have moved from the electroscope through the finger to the Earth.
  3. The finger has neutralised the charge on the polythene strip.
  4. Electrons have flowed from the Earth through the finger onto the electroscope, making it more negative.
Correct answer: A
The negative strip repels the free electrons away from the cap, and with the finger touching they escape to the Earth. Less negative charge on the leaf means the leaf collapses.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Task 2: Earthing and the opposite sign (why the order of finger off first matters)
Q4. In charging by induction, the finger must be removed from the cap BEFORE the negative strip is taken away. Why?
  1. If the strip were removed first, electrons would flow back from the Earth and the electroscope would end up neutral.
  2. The strip would lose its charge if it were taken away while the finger touched the cap.
  3. The finger would become charged and give the learner a shock.
  4. Removing the strip first would give the electroscope the same sign as the strip.
Correct answer: A
While the finger touches the cap, the electroscope is connected to the Earth. If the strip leaves first, electrons would flow back in from the Earth and cancel the deficit, so no charge would be left.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Task 2: Earthing and the opposite sign (sign of the electroscope after Step 4)
Q5. A neutral electroscope is charged by induction using a negative strip and earthing, following the steps: strip near, finger touches the cap, finger removed, strip removed. The leaf then stays diverged. What is the charge on the electroscope?
  1. Positive, the opposite sign to the strip, because it has a deficit of electrons.
  2. Negative, the same sign as the strip, because the strip transferred electrons to it.
  3. Neutral, because the strip never touched the cap.
  4. Negative, because electrons came from the Earth through the finger.
Correct answer: A
Electrons were pushed to the Earth while the strip was near. The electroscope is left with a deficit of electrons, so it is positive, the opposite of the strip.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Task 3: Contrast with contact (three-column comparison of contact, induction and separation)
Q6. Which statement correctly compares charging by contact and charging by induction with earthing?
  1. Both give the same sign, but induction needs earthing.
  2. Contact gives the same sign as the charged object, while induction with earthing gives the opposite sign.
  3. Both give the opposite sign, but contact needs the objects to touch.
  4. Contact gives the opposite sign and induction gives the same sign as the charged object.
Correct answer: B
In contact, electrons are shared with the touching object, so it takes the same sign. In induction, the charged object never touches and earthing removes or adds electrons, leaving the opposite sign.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain Task 4: Back to the phenomenon (wall and paper, attraction stronger than repulsion)
Q7. A negative balloon is brought near a neutral piece of paper. The near side of the paper becomes positive and the far side becomes negative. Why is the paper pulled towards the balloon and not pushed away?
  1. The far side of the paper has no charge, so only attraction acts.
  2. The positive charges on the paper are larger than the negative charges.
  3. The paper gains extra electrons from the balloon and becomes positive overall.
  4. The positive side is nearer to the balloon, so the attraction is stronger than the repulsion from the farther negative side.
Correct answer: D
The electric force is stronger when charges are closer. The nearer positive side is attracted more strongly than the farther negative side is repelled, so there is a net attraction. The total charge of the paper is still zero.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: note on the effect of humidity (balloon falls quickly in Mombasa)
Q8. On a dry day in Nairobi, a rubbed balloon sticks to a painted wall for several minutes. In Mombasa during the rainy season, the same balloon falls off the wall quickly. What is the best explanation?
  1. The balloon is heavier in humid air, so it falls.
  2. Rain makes the wall conduct electricity, so the wall repels the balloon.
  3. Water vapour in the air lets charge leak away from the balloon, so there is less charge to separate charge in the wall.
  4. The humid air adds protons to the balloon, making it neutral.
Correct answer: C
Moist air lets the balloon's charge leak away. With less charge on the balloon, it causes less charge separation in the wall, so the attraction is too weak to hold it.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: answering 'Why is paper attracted to the balloon if it is neutral?' and 'Where do the charges in the paper come from?'
Q9. A learner asks: 'Where do the charges in the neutral paper come from when the balloon makes it jump?' Which answer is correct?
  1. The charges were in the paper all along. Electrons only moved a little, so no charge was created.
  2. The air gives the paper new positive charges.
  3. The paper makes extra electrons when it is attracted.
  4. The balloon creates new charges in the paper as it comes near.
Correct answer: A
Paper is made of atoms with both positive and negative charges, so a neutral piece already has charge. The balloon only makes the electrons shift, and charge is conserved.
