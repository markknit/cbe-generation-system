Q1. A matatu starts at Kencom Bus Stop and follows the Tom Mboya Street arrow for 3 blocks to the right and 2 blocks up. Which column vector describes this journey (horizontal component on top, vertical component below)?
  1. −3 on top, 2 below
  2. 5 (the total number of blocks)
  3. 2 on top, 3 below
  4. 3 on top, 2 below
Correct answer: D
A column vector is written with the horizontal component on top and the vertical component below. Right 3 gives +3 and up 2 gives +2, so the vector is (3 over 2). A single number such as 5 is only a scalar and loses the direction.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: Tom Mboya Street vs Kenyatta Avenue prediction (will they end up at the same place?)
Q2. Two matatus start at the same stop. Matatu A moves by the column vector (3 over 2), meaning 3 blocks right and 2 blocks up. Matatu B moves by the column vector (−3 over 2), meaning 3 blocks left and 2 blocks up. Which statement is correct?
  1. They end at the same place because both go 2 blocks up.
  2. They end at different places because their horizontal movements are in opposite directions.
  3. They end at different places because Matatu B travels a longer distance.
  4. They end at the same place because both go 3 blocks sideways.
Correct answer: B
The upward parts are equal but one matatu goes right and the other goes left, so their vectors are different and they end at different places. Both travel the same distance, so distance alone does not decide the destination.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher translation of triangle KNB by t = (4 over 2)
Q3. Triangle KNB has vertices K(1,1), N(3,1) and B(2,3). It is translated by the translation vector t = (4 over 2), meaning 4 units right and 2 units up. What are the coordinates of B', the image of B?
  1. (6, 5)
  2. (2, 3)
  3. (4, 2)
  4. (−2, 1)
Correct answer: A
Every point moves by the same vector, so B' = (2 + 4, 3 + 2) = (6, 5). The point (4, 2) is the vector itself, not the image of B.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: image triangle K'N'B' compared with triangle KNB and the MJUA object–image pair
Q4. A triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm is translated by the vector (4 over 2). What are the side lengths of its image?
  1. 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm, because a translation changes only position
  2. 7 cm, 6 cm and 5 cm, because the vector is added to the sides
  3. 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm, because the translation doubles the size
  4. They cannot be known, because the size depends on the direction of the vector
Correct answer: A
A translation is an isometry: shape and size are preserved and only the position changes. The image is congruent to the object, so the sides stay 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Part B reverse-engineering the translation vector for triangle SUK
Q5. Triangle SUK has the vertex S(−4, 1). After a translation, the image of S is S'(1, 3). What is the translation vector (horizontal component on top, vertical below)?
  1. (5 over 2)
  2. (−3 over 4)
  3. (−5 over −2)
  4. (5 over 4)
Correct answer: A
The translation vector is the image position vector minus the object position vector: (1 − (−4), 3 − 1) = (5, 2). Subtracting in the wrong order gives (−5, −2), and adding the coordinates gives (−3, 4).
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Part A forward translation of quadrilateral GITI by p = (−3 over 4)
Q6. The quadrilateral GITI has the vertex T(2, 3). It is translated by the vector p = (−3 over 4), meaning 3 units left and 4 units up. What are the coordinates of T', the image of T?
  1. (−1, 7)
  2. (−3, 4)
  3. (−1, −1)
  4. (5, −1)
Correct answer: A
Add the vector components to the coordinates: (2 + (−3), 3 + 4) = (−1, 7). The pair (5, −1) comes from reversing both signs, and (−3, 4) is just the vector.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: green 'We now know…' note and class consensus statement about the two matatu drivers
Q7. Two matatu drivers both travel exactly 8 km from Kencom Bus Stop, but one reaches Westlands and the other reaches South B. Which explanation uses what we learned about vectors?
  1. Their journeys are described by different translation vectors because their directions are different, even though the distance is the same.
  2. Both followed the same vector but started from different bus stops.
  3. Distance alone fixes the destination, so the map must be wrong.
  4. One driver must have travelled further than 8 km.
Correct answer: A
A distance is a scalar and gives only the size. A vector gives size and direction, so two journeys of 8 km in different directions are different vectors and end at different places. A road arrow gives the direction only, not how far to go.
