Q1. A matatu leaves Kencom Bus Stop, goes to the University of Nairobi (leg 1), then turns and goes to GPO (leg 2). Which description matches the ONE arrow that would take a passenger directly from Kencom to GPO?
  1. An arrow that starts at Kencom and ends at the University of Nairobi
  2. An arrow that starts at the University of Nairobi and ends at GPO
  3. An arrow that starts at Kencom and continues in the same direction as leg 2
  4. An arrow that starts at Kencom and ends at GPO
Correct answer: D
The resultant is the single displacement from where the journey starts (Kencom) to where it finally ends (GPO). Simply continuing in the direction of leg 2 is the common misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Triangle Law (tip-to-tail) on the squared-paper grid
Q2. Vector a is the journey from Kencom to the University of Nairobi. Vector b is the journey from the University of Nairobi to GPO. Using the triangle law (tip-to-tail), how do you draw the resultant r = a + b?
  1. Draw r from the tail of a to the tip of b, with b starting at the tip of a
  2. Draw r from the tip of b back to the tail of a
  3. Draw r from the tail of a to the tail of b
  4. Draw r from the tip of a to the tip of b
Correct answer: A
In the triangle law, b is placed with its tail at the tip of a. The resultant runs from where the first vector starts (tail of a) to where the last vector ends (tip of b).
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: measuring the resultant length in grid squares (1 square = 100 m)
Q3. On a grid where 1 square = 100 m, a matatu makes two displacements: a = (5, 2) and b = (−2, 2), measured in squares. What is the length of the resultant displacement a + b?
  1. 500 m
  2. 400 m
  3. 820 m
  4. 700 m
Correct answer: A
a + b = (5 + (−2), 2 + 2) = (3, 4). Its length is √(3² + 4²) = 5 squares = 500 m. Adding the lengths of a and b separately (about 820 m) is scalar thinking.
Use after: Observe phase — Parallelogram law shown on the class poster/slide
Q4. In the parallelogram law of vector addition, how are the two vectors drawn and where is the resultant?
  1. The vectors are drawn in opposite directions, and the resultant is the shorter side
  2. Both vectors start at the same point, and the resultant is the diagonal that joins the tips of the two vectors
  3. The vectors are placed tip-to-tail, and the resultant is one side of the parallelogram
  4. Both vectors start at the same point, and the resultant is the diagonal of the parallelogram from that point
Correct answer: D
With both vectors drawn from the same starting point (tail-to-tail) and the parallelogram completed, the resultant is the diagonal that starts at that common point. It gives the same resultant as the triangle law.
Use after: Explain phase — Task 2: Apply to the phenomenon (Westlands and South B, both 8 km from Kencom)
Q5. Two matatu drivers each travel exactly 8 km from Kencom Bus Stop. One ends at Westlands and the other at South B. Which statement best explains this?
  1. The resultant is the sum of the distances, so both drivers should end at the same place
  2. One driver must have travelled more than 8 km, because the destinations are different
  3. The two journeys have the same distance but their displacement vectors have different directions, so their resultants are different
  4. Distance and displacement are the same thing, so the drivers must have taken different roads
Correct answer: C
Distance is a scalar and only tells how far. The resultant displacement has both magnitude and direction, so equal distances in different directions give different final positions.
Use after: Explain phase — Task 3: Practise column vector addition (Kisumu courier bike)
Q6. A courier bike in Kisumu makes two journeys, written as column vectors in km: leg 1 = (6, 3) and leg 2 = (−2, 5). What is the resultant vector?
  1. (4, 8)
  2. (8, −2)
  3. (8, 8)
  4. (−12, 15)
Correct answer: A
Add corresponding components: (6 + (−2), 3 + 5) = (4, 8). The negative component means 2 km in the opposite direction (left/west) and must be subtracted from 6.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model building: caption for the Kencom → UoN → GPO resultant
Q7. Which caption correctly describes the resultant vector of a two-leg matatu journey?
  1. The resultant gives the direct displacement from the start to the final destination, whatever path was taken
  2. The resultant is the total distance travelled along both legs of the route
  3. The resultant always points in the same direction as the last leg of the journey
  4. The resultant is the length of the first leg only
Correct answer: A
The resultant is the single displacement vector equivalent to the successive displacements. It joins the start of the first vector to the end of the last vector, and it is not the distance along the path.
