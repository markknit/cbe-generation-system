Q1. A fishing boat leaves Kisumu pier, travels 6 km due east and then 8 km due north to reach a fishing ground on Lake Victoria. What is the straight-line distance from the pier to the fishing ground?
  1. 48 km
  2. 2 km
  3. 14 km
  4. 10 km
Correct answer: D
The two legs form a right-angled triangle, so the straight-line distance is the hypotenuse: √(6² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 km.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Step 3 of the guided derivation, comparing predictions of 10 and 14 on the predictions board
Q2. A learner says, "The boat travelled 6 km east and 8 km north, so the straight-line distance from the pier is 6 + 8 = 14 km." What is wrong with this reasoning?
  1. The learner should have multiplied 6 × 8 instead.
  2. Nothing is wrong, because distances are always added together.
  3. 14 km is the distance along the two legs. The straight line is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, so it is shorter.
  4. The learner should have subtracted 8 − 6 = 2 km.
Correct answer: C
Adding 6 + 8 gives the length of the path along the two legs. The straight-line displacement is the hypotenuse, found with Pythagoras' theorem (10 km), which is shorter than 14 km.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: second fishing-boat problem with v = (−5; 12)
Q3. A fishing boat leaves Kisumu pier with displacement vector v = (−5; 12), in km. What is the straight-line distance |v| to the fishing ground?
  1. −13 km
  2. 7 km
  3. 13 km
  4. 17 km
Correct answer: C
|v| = √((−5)² + 12²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 km. Squaring removes the negative sign, and a distance cannot be negative.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: midpoint practice problem with negative coordinates, A = (−3; 5) and B = (7; −1)
Q4. Achieng runs a relay leg at Kasarani Stadium from A = (−3; 5) to B = (7; −1), with units in metres. Her coach stands at the halfway point (midpoint) of her leg. Where does the coach stand?
  1. (2; 2)
  2. (−2; 2)
  3. (4; 4)
  4. (5; −3)
Correct answer: A
M = ((−3 + 7)/2 ; (5 + (−1))/2) = (4/2 ; 4/2) = (2; 2). The midpoint has the mean of the x-coordinates and the mean of the y-coordinates.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: returning to the driving question map to compute the magnitude of the South B route
Q5. On a map of Nairobi, a matatu route from Kencom Bus Stop (the origin) has displacement vector v₂ = (5; −6), in km. What is its magnitude |v₂|, to 1 decimal place?
  1. 61 km
  2. 1 km
  3. 11 km
  4. 7.8 km
Correct answer: D
|v₂| = √(5² + (−6)²) = √(25 + 36) = √61 ≈ 7.8 km, which is close to the 8 km label on the map.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'Same magnitude. COMPLETELY different direction.' discussion on what the 8 km label does and does not tell us
Q6. Two matatus leave Kencom Bus Stop. One has displacement vector (−6; 5) km and the other has (5; −6) km. Each journey has a magnitude of about 7.8 km, yet the matatus end up at different places. What explains this?
  1. The vector with the negative x-component travels a negative distance.
  2. Magnitude gives only 'how far'. The two vectors have different directions, so they lead to different destinations.
  3. Two displacement vectors can only have the same magnitude if they end at the same place.
  4. The magnitudes must really be different, because different destinations need different distances.
Correct answer: B
Magnitude strips away direction and gives only the distance. Equal magnitudes with different directions give different endpoints, which is why the 8 km label alone cannot tell us the destination.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: marking and labelling the midpoint of each route's displacement vector on the matatu map
Q7. A matatu starts at Kencom Bus Stop, the origin (0; 0), and its displacement vector is (−6; 5), in km. What are the coordinates of the midpoint of this displacement, where the matatu is halfway through its journey?
  1. (−3; 2.5)
  2. (−12; 10)
  3. (−3; −2.5)
  4. (3; −2.5)
Correct answer: A
M = ((0 + (−6))/2 ; (0 + 5)/2) = (−3; 2.5). Each coordinate of the midpoint is the mean of the start and end coordinates.
