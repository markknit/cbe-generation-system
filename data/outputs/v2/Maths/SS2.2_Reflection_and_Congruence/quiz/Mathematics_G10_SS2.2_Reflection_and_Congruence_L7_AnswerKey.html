Q1. Two triangles are cut from different pieces of paper. Which piece of information about them would be enough to guarantee that they are exactly identical in shape and size?
  1. Two sides and the angle between them in one triangle equal two sides and the angle between them in the other
  2. One side and one angle of one triangle equal one side and one angle of the other
  3. Two sides of one triangle equal two sides of the other, with the equal angle not between them
  4. All three angles of one triangle equal the three angles of the other
Correct answer: A
Two sides and the included angle (SAS) fix a unique triangle. Equal angles alone give only the same shape, and one side with one angle, or an angle that is not between the two sides, does not fix the triangle.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Task C (second reference triangle NMK, NM = 6 cm, angle N = 40°, angle M = 60°)
Q2. Triangle NMK has NM = 6 cm, angle N = 40° and angle M = 60°. Wanjiru draws another triangle with a 6 cm side and angles of 40° and 60° at the two ends of that side. Which congruence test shows her triangle is congruent to triangle NMK?
  1. SAS (two sides and the included angle)
  2. ASA (two angles and the side between them)
  3. SSS (three sides)
  4. RHS (right angle, hypotenuse and a side)
Correct answer: B
Two angles and the side between them are given, which is the ASA test. No other sides are given, so SSS and SAS do not apply, and there is no right angle, so RHS does not apply.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Task D (right-angled reference triangle, hypotenuse 10 cm, one leg 6 cm)
Q3. A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 10 cm and one other side of 6 cm. Using the RHS idea, a pair builds a second triangle with the same hypotenuse and side. What is the length of the remaining side, which shows the two triangles are identical?
  1. 4 cm
  2. 11.7 cm
  3. 8 cm
  4. 16 cm
Correct answer: C
By Pythagoras, the other leg is sqrt(10² − 6²) = sqrt(64) = 8 cm. Any triangle built from the same hypotenuse and leg must have this same third side, so it is congruent.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: formative check on the 'three angles are enough (AAA)' misconception during the construction tasks
Q4. Kamau draws a triangle with angles 40°, 60° and 80° and a 6 cm base. Achieng draws a triangle with angles 40°, 60° and 80° and a 9 cm base. Kamau says the two triangles must be congruent because all their angles match. Which statement is correct?
  1. He is correct, because three equal angles always give congruent triangles
  2. He is wrong, because triangles with a 60° angle can never be congruent
  3. He is correct, because the angles add up to 180° in both triangles
  4. He is wrong, because the triangles have the same shape but different sizes, so equal angles alone do not give congruence
Correct answer: D
Equal angles (AAA) give triangles of the same shape but not necessarily the same size. The 6 cm and 9 cm bases show they are different sizes, so they are similar but not congruent.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: worked example, triangle PQR and its reflection P'Q'R' in the y-axis
Q5. Triangle PQR has P(1, 1), Q(5, 1) and R(5, 4). Using the distance formula or Pythagoras on the grid, what is the length of side PR?
  1. 25 units
  2. 7 units
  3. 3 units
  4. 5 units
Correct answer: D
PR = sqrt((5 − 1)² + (4 − 1)²) = sqrt(16 + 9) = sqrt(25) = 5 units.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: writing the congruence statement 'Triangle PQR ≅ Triangle P'Q'R' by SSS'
Q6. Triangle PQR has sides 4, 3 and 5 units. Its reflection in the y-axis, triangle P'Q'R', also has sides 4, 3 and 5 units. If you only know these side lengths, which congruence test proves the triangles are congruent?
  1. SSS
  2. SAS
  3. AAS
  4. RHS
Correct answer: A
All three pairs of corresponding sides are equal (4 = 4, 3 = 3, 5 = 5), and only the SSS test uses three sides. The information given contains no angles, so SAS, AAS and RHS cannot be used from it alone.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the congruence proof box and the 'Opposite (indirect) congruence' label to the cumulative model
Q7. A triangle and its mirror image are congruent by SSS. Why is this called opposite (indirect) congruence, like the lorry and its image in the matatu's rear-view mirror?
  1. The angles of the image are different from the angles of the object
  2. The image has the same sides and angles but is flipped, so it cannot be placed on the object without turning it over
  3. The image is smaller than the object because it appears behind the mirror
  4. The sides of the image are longer than the sides of the object
Correct answer: B
Reflection keeps all lengths and angles the same, so the image is congruent to the object. It reverses orientation, so the triangle must be flipped over to fit exactly on the object. This is opposite congruence.
