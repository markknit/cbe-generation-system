Use after: Predict phase — Predict: three individual predictions about the lorry's image in the mirror (Think-Pair-Share)
Q1. In the matatu mirror task, learners wrote three predictions about the lorry's image in the conductor's mirror. Which set shows the three things they were asked to think about?
  1. The number of passengers, the length of the highway, and the time of day
  2. How the image compares in size with the real lorry, where the right headlight appears and which way the image faces, and how far behind the mirror the image seems to be
  3. The speed of the lorry, the colour of the lorry, and the price of the mirror
  4. The number of lines of symmetry of the mirror, the weight of the lorry, and the colour of the matatu
Correct answer: B
The Predict task asked about size, orientation (headlight position and facing direction) and distance of the image behind the mirror. The other options bring in details that were not part of the predictions.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: folding the paper cut-out rectangle (non-square) and recording in the symmetry table
Q2. A paper rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide (it is not a square). How many lines of symmetry does it have?
  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 0
  4. 1
Correct answer: A
Folding a non-square rectangle in half lengthways or widthways makes the halves match, giving 2 lines. Folding along a diagonal does not make the halves match, so the diagonals are not lines of symmetry.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: pairs who find that 'F' has NO lines of symmetry and explain why it differs from 'A'
Q3. A pair of learners fold a paper cut-out of the capital letter F in every way they can and find that it has no lines of symmetry. Which statement gives the correct reason?
  1. F is made only of straight lines, and shapes with straight lines cannot have symmetry
  2. F has three arms, and a figure must have an even number of arms to have symmetry
  3. No fold line makes one half land exactly on top of the other half
  4. F is too small to fold properly
Correct answer: C
A line of symmetry needs the two halves to match perfectly when folded. For the letter F, no fold does this, whereas the letter A matches along one vertical fold. Straight lines do not prevent symmetry: a square has 4 lines of symmetry.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: building the definition of 'line of symmetry' from the class table (probe with the rectangle diagonal)
Q4. A learner draws a line from one corner of a non-square rectangle to the opposite corner. The line splits the rectangle into two triangles of equal size. Is this line a line of symmetry of the rectangle?
  1. No, because a rectangle has no lines of symmetry at all
  2. Yes, because any line that divides a shape into two equal parts is a line of symmetry
  3. No, because when the rectangle is folded along this line the two halves do not land exactly on each other
  4. Yes, because every line through the centre of a rectangle is a line of symmetry
Correct answer: C
A line of symmetry must divide the figure into two halves that are mirror images, so that folding makes them match exactly. The diagonal of a non-square rectangle gives two equal triangles, but they do not fold onto each other. The rectangle itself has 2 lines of symmetry.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: consolidation task on the Kenyan flag lines of symmetry
Q5. The Kenyan flag has three horizontal bands: black at the top, red in the middle and green at the bottom, with a Maasai shield and spears in the centre. A learner draws a horizontal line through the middle of the flag. Is it a line of symmetry?
  1. Yes, because the shield is in the centre of the flag
  2. Yes, because the three bands have the same width
  3. No, because the flag is a rectangle and rectangles have no lines of symmetry
  4. No, because the top half is black and the bottom half is green, so the two halves do not match
Correct answer: D
For a line of symmetry, the two halves must match as mirror images, including colour. The top half has a black band and the bottom half a green band, so they do not match.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: extension envelope (rhombus, regular hexagon and maize leaf with a small plane mirror)
Q6. A rhombus is a four-sided shape with all sides equal, and it is not a square. Which lines are its lines of symmetry?
  1. The lines joining the midpoints of opposite sides
  2. Each of its four sides
  3. The two diagonals
  4. The rhombus has no lines of symmetry
Correct answer: C
Folding a rhombus along either diagonal makes the two halves match, so it has 2 lines of symmetry. A common error is to choose the lines joining midpoints of opposite sides, which do not fold the halves onto each other.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: sticky-note questions and observations about the matatu mirror
Q7. At the end of Lesson 1, the class has found that a matatu mirror image is the same size as the real lorry but seems to face back at the driver. A learner writes this question on a sticky note for the Driving Question Board: 'If the image is a perfect copy, why does it look flipped?' What is the best description of this question at this point?
  1. It is answered by the fact that the lorry is moving
  2. It was fully answered by finding lines of symmetry in the paper cut-outs
  3. It is not yet explained, so it stays on the board for the class to investigate in later lessons
  4. It is not a mathematics question and should be removed
Correct answer: C
Lesson 1 only opens the puzzle. The class has found lines of symmetry but has not yet explained why a perfect copy can look flipped, so the question stays on the board.
