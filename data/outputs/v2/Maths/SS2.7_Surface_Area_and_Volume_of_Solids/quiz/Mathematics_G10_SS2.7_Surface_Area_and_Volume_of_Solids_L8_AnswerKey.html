Q1. Wanjiru made a closed cylindrical drum and a closed rectangular box for Mzee Kiplagat's posho mill, each from exactly 30,800 cm² of iron sheet. Which statement about their capacities is correct?
  1. The one with the greater surface area holds more grain.
  2. Their capacities cannot be compared until the sheet thickness is known.
  3. They hold the same amount of grain, because the same amount of sheet metal was used.
  4. They may hold different amounts of grain, because the shape also decides the volume.
Correct answer: D
Surface area measures the material and volume measures the space inside. Equal surface areas do not guarantee equal volumes, and here the drum holds 404,250 cm³ while the box holds 322,500 cm³.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: group project presentations (volume converted to litres)
Q2. A drum has a volume of 404,250 cm³ and a box has a volume of 322,500 cm³. Given that 1 litre = 1,000 cm³, how many more litres of maize does the drum hold than the box?
  1. 8.175 litres
  2. 726.75 litres
  3. 81,750 litres
  4. 81.75 litres
Correct answer: D
The difference is 404,250 − 322,500 = 81,750 cm³. Dividing by 1,000 gives 81.75 litres.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: peer critique, 'Did you use slant height l or vertical height h for the cone roof?'
Q3. A group designs a grain silo with a cone-shaped roof. The cone has base radius 7 m, vertical height 24 m and slant height 25 m. Using π = 22/7, what is the curved surface area of the roof?
  1. 154 m²
  2. 528 m²
  3. 550 m²
  4. 704 m²
Correct answer: C
Curved surface area = πrl, using the slant height l. So 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550 m². Using the vertical height 24 m gives the wrong answer of 528 m².
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Final Explanation using Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (reasoning about shape)
Q4. Both a closed cylinder (r = 35 cm, h = 105 cm) and a closed square-based box (50 cm × 50 cm × 129 cm) use 30,800 cm² of sheet, but the cylinder holds more. Which is the best reason?
  1. The cylinder is taller, and the tallest solid always holds the most.
  2. Volume is found by adding the face areas, and the cylinder's faces add up to more.
  3. The cylinder's circular cross-section encloses more area for its perimeter than a square one, so the same material encloses more space.
  4. The cylinder has more faces than the box, so it has more surface area.
Correct answer: C
For the same sheet, a circular cross-section encloses more area than a square one, which gives more space inside. Surface area and volume are different quantities, so equal sheet does not mean equal capacity.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: testing the Predict-phase sphere prediction (sphere made from 30,800 cm² of sheet)
Q5. Wanjiru bends 30,800 cm² of sheet into a sphere. Using π = 22/7, which equation gives its radius r?
  1. 4 × 22/7 × r² = 30,800
  2. 4/3 × 22/7 × r³ = 30,800
  3. 2 × 22/7 × r² = 30,800
  4. 22/7 × r² = 30,800
Correct answer: A
The sheet is the surface area, and the surface area of a sphere is 4πr². So 4 × 22/7 × r² = 30,800, which gives r² = 2,450 and r ≈ 49.5 cm. The 4/3πr³ option is the volume formula.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: final cumulative model, part (4) the principle that shape determines capacity
Q6. The sphere made from 30,800 cm² of sheet holds about 508 litres, the drum holds 404.25 litres and the box holds 322.5 litres. Which statement best sums up the shape principle?
  1. For a fixed amount of sheet, a sphere holds the least and a box holds the most.
  2. For a fixed amount of sheet, the shape with the most flat faces holds the most.
  3. For a fixed amount of sheet, every shape holds the same volume.
  4. For a fixed amount of sheet, the shape decides how much space can be enclosed, and a sphere encloses the most.
Correct answer: D
With the same 30,800 cm² of sheet, the sphere (about 508 L) holds more than the drum (404.25 L), which holds more than the box (322.5 L). So shape decides capacity, although cost, stacking and welding also matter in practice.
Use after: End of lesson — End: exit ticket on which measure Wanjiru would use to order sheet metal and which Mzee Kiplagat would use for sacks
Q7. Wanjiru is making a water tank for a Kajiado community. Which measure should she use to decide how much sheet metal to buy, and which should she use to decide how many litres of water it holds?
  1. Surface area for both, because the sheet is the outside of the tank
  2. Surface area for the sheet metal, and volume for the litres of water
  3. Volume for both, because the tank is a solid
  4. Volume for the sheet metal, and surface area for the litres of water
Correct answer: B
Surface area is the material covering the solid, so it tells how much sheet to buy. Volume is the space inside, so it tells how much water the tank holds.
