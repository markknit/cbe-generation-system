Q1. A paper cone is cut open along its slant edge, from the base circle up to the apex, and flattened on the table. What shape do you get, and what does its straight edge represent?
  1. A sector of a circle whose straight edge is the slant height l of the cone
  2. A triangle whose height is the vertical height h of the cone
  3. A rectangle whose length is the circumference of the base
  4. A sector of a circle whose straight edge is the base radius r of the cone
Correct answer: A
Unrolling the curved surface gives a sector of a circle. Its straight edge is the sloping side of the cone, so the sector's radius is the slant height l, not r or h.
Use after: Observe phase — Task A (cone net): comparing the sector's arc length with the string around the base circle
Q2. A paper cone has base radius 3.5 cm and slant height 12 cm. It is cut open along its slant edge and flattened into a sector. Using π = 22/7, how long is the curved arc of the sector?
  1. About 75.4 cm
  2. 11 cm
  3. 22 cm
  4. 44 cm
Correct answer: C
The arc of the sector is the same length as the string around the base of the cone, which is the circumference 2πr = 2 × 22/7 × 3.5 = 22 cm. 75.4 cm would be the whole circle of radius l, which is not the arc.
Use after: Explain phase — Task 1 (conical tank top): calculating the sheet needed for r = 1.4 m, l = 2 m
Q3. A conical top for a school water tank in Narok has base radius 1.4 m, vertical height about 1.43 m and slant height 2 m. The cone sits on top of the tank, so no base is needed. Using π = 22/7, how much sheet metal is needed for the conical top?
  1. 14.96 m²
  2. 6.29 m²
  3. 17.6 m²
  4. 8.8 m²
Correct answer: D
The sheet covers the curved surface, so curved SA = πrl = 22/7 × 1.4 × 2 = 8.8 m². Using the vertical height instead of the slant height gives 6.29 m², and adding the base gives 14.96 m².
Use after: Explain phase — Task 2 (total SA contrast): ice-cream cone curved SA versus total SA
Q4. An ice-cream cone has base radius 3.5 cm and slant height 12 cm. Using π = 22/7, what is its total surface area, including the circular base?
  1. 38.5 cm²
  2. 132 cm²
  3. 170.5 cm²
  4. 135.5 cm²
Correct answer: C
Total SA = πr(r + l) = 22/7 × 3.5 × (3.5 + 12) = 11 × 15.5 = 170.5 cm². This is the curved part, 132 cm², plus the base circle, 38.5 cm².
Use after: Explain phase — Task 3 (frustum formula): choosing the slant height for the Turkana frustum
Q5. A Turkana water tank is a frustum made by removing a small cone from the top of a big cone. The big cone has slant height 3.5 m and the small cone has slant height 1.75 m. The frustum has big radius R = 2.1 m and small radius r = 1.05 m. Which slant height l goes into the formula π(R + r)l?
  1. 3.5 m, the slant height of the big cone
  2. 1.4 m, the vertical height of the frustum
  3. 1.75 m, the length along the slanted side of the frustum itself
  4. 5.25 m, the two slant heights added together
Correct answer: C
The slant height l in the frustum formula is the length of the frustum's own sloping side, which is 3.5 − 1.75 = 1.75 m. The big cone's slant height and the vertical height are not the slanted side of the frustum.
Use after: Explain phase — Task 3 (frustum formula): Turkana frustum computed by subtraction and by π(R + r)l
Q6. A Turkana tank is a frustum made by cutting a small cone off a big cone. The big cone has base radius 2.1 m and slant height 3.5 m. The small cone that is removed has base radius 1.05 m and slant height 1.75 m. Using π = 22/7, what is the curved surface area of the frustum?
  1. 5.775 m²
  2. 28.875 m²
  3. 23.1 m²
  4. 17.325 m²
Correct answer: D
The frustum is the big cone minus the small cone: 22/7 × 2.1 × 3.5 = 23.1 m² and 22/7 × 1.05 × 1.75 = 5.775 m², so 23.1 − 5.775 = 17.325 m². The formula π(R + r)l = 22/7 × 3.15 × 1.75 gives the same value.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: sorting notes into 'surface area tools' and 'questions about capacity'
Q7. A class works out that a conical tank top needs 8.8 m² of sheet metal. What does this number tell us?
  1. How much the conical top will weigh once it is made
  2. How much sheet metal is needed to cover the curved surface, but nothing about how much the tank can hold
  3. The capacity of the tank in litres, because a larger area always means more storage
  4. The volume of grain or water the tank can hold
Correct answer: B
Surface area measures the material needed to cover a solid. It does not measure the space inside, so the tank's capacity needs volume, which is still an open question on the Driving Question Board.
