Q1. In Kisumu, a student measures the sun's angle with the ground as 53°. The mango tree's shadow on the ground is 9.0 m long. She wants the tree's height. The tree is vertical and the ground is horizontal. Which ratio links the two lengths she knows or wants (height and shadow) with the 53° angle?
  1. No ratio works unless she first measures the length of the sun's ray (the hypotenuse)
  2. sin 53°, because sine is the most useful ratio for every triangle
  3. cos 53°, because the shadow is on the ground
  4. tan 53°, because it links the opposite side (height) and the adjacent side (shadow)
Correct answer: D
The height is opposite the 53° angle and the shadow is adjacent to it. Tangent is Opposite/Adjacent, so it connects these two sides without needing the hypotenuse.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Algebraic Derivation of tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
Q2. In a right-angled triangle, sin θ = O/H and cos θ = A/H. What do we get when we simplify (O/H) ÷ (A/H) = (O/H) × (H/A)?
  1. O/A, which is tan θ
  2. O/H
  3. A/O
  4. H/A
Correct answer: A
The H in the numerator cancels with the H in the denominator, leaving O/A. This is Opposite/Adjacent, the definition of tan θ.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Algebraic Derivation, where tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent (TAO) is written
Q3. A right-angled triangle has sides of 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm (the hypotenuse). For the angle θ opposite the 6 cm side, what is tan θ?
  1. 1.33
  2. 0.75
  3. 0.6
  4. 0.8
Correct answer: B
tan θ = Opposite/Adjacent = 6/8 = 0.75. The value 0.6 is sin θ (6/10), 0.8 is cos θ (8/10), and 1.33 is the inverted ratio 8/6.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Numerical Verification table (40° row)
Q4. From mathematical tables, sin 40° = 0.6428 and cos 40° = 0.7660. Using tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ, what is tan 40° to 3 decimal places?
  1. 1.409
  2. 0.839
  3. 1.192
  4. 0.492
Correct answer: B
tan 40° = sin 40° ÷ cos 40° = 0.6428 ÷ 0.7660 ≈ 0.839. The value 0.492 comes from multiplying, 1.192 from dividing cos by sin, and 1.409 from adding.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Numerical Verification, comparing column 4 and column 5
Q5. A pair finds that for one angle, sin θ ÷ cos θ is 0.8391 and tan θ read from tables is 0.8390. What is the most sensible explanation of this tiny difference?
  1. They used the wrong triangle, because tan changes with the size of the triangle
  2. The identity only works for 45°
  3. Table values are rounded, so tiny differences are expected
  4. The identity tan θ = sin θ / cos θ does not work for that angle
Correct answer: C
Table values are rounded to a few decimal places, so tiny differences are normal. The identity itself is exact for every acute angle.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Sensemaking Discussion, Q1 (is tan unnecessary?)
Q6. A learner says, "We can already get tan θ from sin θ and cos θ, so tan is unnecessary." What is the best reply?
  1. Tan can only be used when the hypotenuse is known
  2. Tan is only used for angles bigger than 90°
  3. They are right: tan is redundant and is never needed
  4. Tan is a convenient shortcut when we know an angle and one side but not the hypotenuse
Correct answer: D
Tan is not independent of sin and cos, but it is convenient. It connects the opposite and adjacent sides directly, without needing the hypotenuse.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Sensemaking Discussion, Q2 (which side is 9.0 m: opposite or adjacent?)
Q7. The sun makes an angle of 53° with the ground. A vertical mango tree of height h casts a 9.0 m shadow along the ground, and the 53° angle is at the end of the shadow, between the ground and the sun's ray. Which statement is correct?
  1. The 9.0 m shadow is the side opposite the 53° angle, and the tree height h is the side adjacent to it
  2. Both the shadow and the tree are adjacent to the 53° angle
  3. The 9.0 m shadow is the side adjacent to the 53° angle, and the tree height h is the side opposite it
  4. The 9.0 m shadow is the hypotenuse
Correct answer: C
The shadow lies along the ground next to the 53° angle, so it is adjacent. The tree stands across from the angle, so it is opposite. The hypotenuse is the sun's ray.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Mango-Tree Calculation (h = 9.0 × tan 53°)
Q8. A mango tree has a shadow 9.0 m long when the sun makes an angle of 53° with the ground. Given tan 53° = 1.3270 and tan 53° = h ÷ 9.0, what is the height h of the tree?
  1. 5.42 m
  2. 11.943 m
  3. 7.19 m
  4. 6.78 m
Correct answer: B
From tan 53° = h/9.0, h = 9.0 × tan 53° = 9.0 × 1.3270 = 11.943 m. The value 6.78 m comes from the inverted ratio 9.0 ÷ 1.3270.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Mango-Tree Calculation, shadow-ratio cross-check
Q9. A student is 1.6 m tall and her shadow is 1.2 m long. At the same time, a mango tree has a shadow 9.0 m long. Using similar triangles, (tree height) ÷ 9.0 = 1.6 ÷ 1.2. What is the height of the tree?
  1. 14.4 m
  2. 12.0 m
  3. 9.4 m
  4. 6.75 m
Correct answer: B
h = 9.0 × (1.6 ÷ 1.2) = 9.0 × 1.333 = 12.0 m. This agrees with the tangent method (about 11.94 m) to within rounding.
Use after: Model Building phase — Trigonometry Model: adding the 'Ratio Web' annotation (tan θ = sin θ / cos θ)
Q10. On the 'Ratio Web' in the Trigonometry Model, tan θ is linked to sin θ and cos θ. For an angle of 53°, sin 53° ≈ 0.799 and cos 53° ≈ 0.602. Using the identity, what is tan 53° to 3 decimal places?
  1. 1.327
  2. 0.481
  3. 0.753
  4. 1.401
Correct answer: A
tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ = 0.799 ÷ 0.602 ≈ 1.327. Dividing cos by sin gives 0.753, which reverses the identity, and multiplying gives 0.481.
