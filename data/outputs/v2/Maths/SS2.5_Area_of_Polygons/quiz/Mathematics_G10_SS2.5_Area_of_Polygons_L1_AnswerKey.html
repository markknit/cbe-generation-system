Q1. On the scaled drawing of the Kericho plot, 1 cm stands for 5 m. One side of the plot measures 3.6 cm on the drawing. What is the real length of that side?
  1. 8.6 m
  2. 18 m
  3. 3.6 m
  4. 1.8 m
Correct answer: B
Each centimetre stands for 5 m, so 3.6 cm × 5 = 18 m. Adding 5 m instead of multiplying gives 8.6 m. Dividing by 2 gives 1.8 m, which is also wrong.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: pair-share of reasoning strategies (surrounding rectangle, grid squares, known shape inside)
Q2. A learner estimates the area of the irregular Kericho plot by drawing a rectangle that completely surrounds the plot and finding the area of that rectangle. Compared with the true area of the plot, what is this estimate likely to be?
  1. Too small, because the rectangle has only four sides
  2. Exactly right, because a rectangle is a standard shape
  3. Impossible to compare, because the plot has no area formula
  4. Too big, because the rectangle also covers some land outside the plot
Correct answer: D
A rectangle drawn around the whole plot includes corners of space outside the boundary, so its area is more than the area of the plot. This is why it only gives an estimate.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: group 'Observation Record' (formula audit and 'This plot is puzzling because ___')
Q3. The Kericho plot has seven sides of 18 m, 22 m, 15 m, 20 m, 25 m, 17 m and 19 m, and none of its angles is 90°. Which statement best describes the problem the village meeting faces?
  1. Land with seven sides cannot have an area
  2. The plot is too big to be measured in square metres
  3. The area formulas for a rectangle, a square or a triangle do not directly fit this one shape
  4. The families must first agree that all the sides are equal
Correct answer: C
The plot is not a single rectangle, square or triangle, so no one familiar formula gives its area directly. The area still exists. We just do not yet have a method for it.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: triangle area review with the right-angled card triangle (base 8 cm, height 6 cm)
Q4. A right-angled card triangle has a base of 8 cm and a perpendicular height of 6 cm. Using A = ½bh, what is its area?
  1. 48 cm²
  2. 24 cm²
  3. 14 cm²
  4. 28 cm²
Correct answer: B
A = ½ × 8 × 6 = 24 cm². Forgetting the ½ gives 48 cm². Adding the two lengths gives 14 cm².
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: acute triangle cut-out (base 10 cm, height 5 cm) and the altitude demonstration
Q5. An acute triangle has a base of 10 cm. Its perpendicular height from the base to the opposite vertex is 5 cm. Its slanting side is 6.4 cm long. Using A = ½bh, what is the area of the triangle?
  1. 25 cm²
  2. 64 cm²
  3. 50 cm²
  4. 32 cm²
Correct answer: A
The height must be the perpendicular distance, 5 cm. So A = ½ × 10 × 5 = 25 cm². Using the slant side gives 32 cm², which is the common error. 50 cm² leaves out the ½.
Use after: End of lesson — Model: first Unit Model Book entry and closing link to the driving question
Q6. At the end of this lesson, which statement about the total area of the Kericho plot is correct?
  1. The area can never be found because the plot is irregular
  2. Every learner's estimate is correct because the plot has no single formula
  3. The exact area is already known and the plot can now be divided equally
  4. We only have estimates so far, and the real area has not yet been calculated
Correct answer: D
The lesson gives estimates and reviews triangle area as a first tool. The plot itself is not measured and calculated until a later lesson, so the total area and the fair division are still open questions.
