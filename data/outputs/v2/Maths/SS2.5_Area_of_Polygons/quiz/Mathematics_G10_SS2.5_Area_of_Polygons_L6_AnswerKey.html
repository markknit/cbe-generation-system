Q1. A five-sided polygon (pentagon) is split into triangles by drawing diagonals from ONE corner to every corner that is not next to it. How many triangles are formed?
  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 2
  4. 5
Correct answer: A
A polygon with n sides gives n − 2 triangles from one vertex, so 5 − 2 = 3 triangles.
Use after: Observe phase — Step 2 – Measure: measuring base and perpendicular height with ruler and set square
Q2. A group is finding the area of a triangle on a scale drawing. The base is 6 cm. One student measures the slanted side from the top corner to the end of the base as 5 cm. Another student measures the straight distance from the top corner to the base, meeting it at 90°, as 4 cm. Which measurement should be used as the height in A = ½bh?
  1. Either one, because both reach the base
  2. The 4 cm distance that meets the base at 90°
  3. The average of the two, 4.5 cm
  4. The 5 cm slanted side, because it is the longest distance
Correct answer: B
The height in A = ½bh must be the perpendicular height, which meets the base at 90°. Using a slanted side overestimates the area.
Use after: Observe phase — Steps 3–4 – Calculate, convert cm² to m², sum and post the total on the Evidence Wall
Q3. A shamba drawing has scale 1 cm : 4 m. It is split into three triangles with areas on the drawing of 2.5 cm², 4.0 cm² and 3.0 cm². What is the total real area of the shamba?
  1. 76 m²
  2. 9.5 m²
  3. 152 m²
  4. 38 m²
Correct answer: C
Total on the drawing = 2.5 + 4.0 + 3.0 = 9.5 cm². Since 1 cm² represents 16 m² (4 × 4), the real area is 9.5 × 16 = 152 m².
Use after: Explain phase — Reconciliation: diagnosing Group 3's conversion error (multiplying by 4 instead of 16)
Q4. On a drawing with scale 1 cm : 4 m, a group converted an area from cm² to m² by multiplying by 4. Why is this wrong?
  1. Area is multiplied by the scale factor squared, 4 × 4 = 16, because both length and width are enlarged
  2. Area must be multiplied by 8, which is 4 × 2
  3. Multiplying by 4 is correct for lengths and so also for areas
  4. The scale factor should be added to the area, not multiplied
Correct answer: A
Both dimensions are enlarged 4 times, so area is enlarged 4² = 16 times. Multiplying by 4 only works for lengths.
Use after: Explain phase — Heron's formula segment: using s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2 and A = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]
Q5. A surveyor measures the three sides of a triangular plot as 13 m, 14 m and 15 m. She cannot measure its height. Using Heron's formula, what is the area of the plot?
  1. 42 m²
  2. 168 m²
  3. 21 m²
  4. 84 m²
Correct answer: D
s = (13 + 14 + 15) ÷ 2 = 21. A = √[21 × (21 − 13) × (21 − 14) × (21 − 15)] = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7056 = 84 m².
Use after: Explain phase — Heron's formula segment: 'What if a surveyor can measure only the three sides of a triangle?'
Q6. When is Heron's formula most useful for finding the area of a triangle?
  1. When only the three side lengths are known and the height cannot be measured
  2. When the triangle has a right angle
  3. When the triangle has two equal sides
  4. When the area is needed in square metres instead of square centimetres
Correct answer: A
Heron's formula needs only the three side lengths, so it is useful when the perpendicular height is not known or cannot be measured.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB: 'Does our method work for the Kericho plot even if its sides are all different lengths?'
Q7. A village near Kericho has a seven-sided plot whose sides all have different lengths and whose angles are all different. A student says, 'We cannot split it into triangles because it is not a regular polygon.' Which reply is correct?
  1. The student is right, because triangulation only works on polygons with equal sides
  2. The student is wrong, but only if the plot is first redrawn as a regular polygon
  3. The student is right, because triangulation only works on five-sided polygons
  4. The student is wrong, because any polygon can be split into non-overlapping triangles using diagonals, whatever its side lengths or angles
Correct answer: D
Triangulation does not need equal sides or equal angles. Any polygon can be split into non-overlapping triangles by drawing diagonals from one vertex.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model building: generalisation statement and 'Lesson 7: n = 7, so I will draw ____ triangles'
Q8. You draw diagonals from one corner of a seven-sided plot to every corner that is not next to it. How many triangles do you get?
  1. 7
  2. 6
  3. 5
  4. 4
Correct answer: C
A polygon with n sides gives n − 2 triangles, so 7 − 2 = 5 triangles. The areas of these 5 triangles are then added.
