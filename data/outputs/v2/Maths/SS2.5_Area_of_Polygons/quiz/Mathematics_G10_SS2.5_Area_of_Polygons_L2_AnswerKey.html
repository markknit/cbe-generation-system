Q1. A parallelogram-shaped rooftop panel has a base of 8 cm. Its slanted side is 6 cm long, and its perpendicular height (measured with a set-square) is 5 cm. What is its area?
  1. 20 cm²
  2. 48 cm²
  3. 40 cm²
  4. 30 cm²
Correct answer: C
Area of a parallelogram = base × perpendicular height = 8 × 5 = 40 cm². The slant side (6 cm) is not the height, so 8 × 6 = 48 cm² is wrong.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: dissection of the parallelogram along a perpendicular height line to form a rectangle
Q2. A group cuts a parallelogram along a perpendicular height line and slides the triangle from one end to the other end. What shape do they make, and what does this show about the parallelogram's area?
  1. A rectangle with the same base and slant side, so the area is base × slant side
  2. A triangle, so the area is ½ × base × height
  3. A trapezium, so the area is ½(a + b)h
  4. A rectangle with the same base and height, so the area is base × height
Correct answer: D
The cut-off triangle fits on the other side to form a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height. So the parallelogram's area is bh.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: groups derive the rhombus and kite formulae and compare with the Predict question on shapes B and D
Q3. A rhombus-shaped decorative tile and a kite-shaped festival decoration both have diagonals of 10 cm and 6 cm. Which statement is correct?
  1. The rhombus has the larger area because all its sides are equal
  2. The kite has the larger area because its sides are unequal
  3. Both have an area of 30 cm²
  4. Both have an area of 60 cm²
Correct answer: C
Both areas are ½ × d₁ × d₂ = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm². Equal diagonals in both shapes give equal areas, whatever the side lengths.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: teacher formalises the formulae and asks why the rhombus and kite share the same formula
Q4. Why can the area of both a rhombus and a kite be found using A = ½d₁d₂?
  1. Both shapes have all four sides equal in length
  2. In both shapes, each diagonal cuts the other diagonal exactly in half
  3. Both shapes have two pairs of parallel sides
  4. In both shapes, the diagonals cross at right angles, so one diagonal is a base and the parts of the other diagonal are the heights of the triangles
Correct answer: D
The diagonals of a rhombus and a kite cross at right angles, so one diagonal splits the shape into triangles whose heights add up to the other diagonal. In a kite only one diagonal is bisected, and a kite does not have parallel sides or four equal sides.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Problem 1, the parallelogram-shaped cattle pen in Naivasha
Q5. A parallelogram-shaped cattle pen near Naivasha has a base of 24 m and a perpendicular height of 15 m. What is its area?
  1. 39 m²
  2. 720 m²
  3. 360 m²
  4. 180 m²
Correct answer: C
Area = base × perpendicular height = 24 × 15 = 360 m². Using ½ × 24 × 15 = 180 m² wrongly treats it as a triangle.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Problem 2, the trapezium-shaped tea terrace in Kericho
Q6. A tea-farm terrace in Kericho has a trapezium-shaped cross-section. Its parallel widths are 3.6 m and 2.2 m, and its depth (perpendicular height) is 1.4 m. What is the cross-sectional area?
  1. 8.12 m²
  2. 3.08 m²
  3. 5.04 m²
  4. 4.06 m²
Correct answer: D
Area = ½(a + b)h = ½ × (3.6 + 2.2) × 1.4 = ½ × 5.8 × 1.4 = 4.06 m². Leaving out the ½ gives 8.12 m².
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: decomposing the seven-sided Kericho plot and writing the formula for each sub-shape
Q7. While decomposing a land plot, a group finds a four-sided region with exactly one pair of parallel sides, 12 m and 8 m long, which are 5 m apart. The other two sides are not parallel. Which shape is it, and which formula should they use for its area?
  1. Kite, A = ½d₁d₂
  2. Parallelogram, A = bh
  3. Trapezium, A = (a + b)h
  4. Trapezium, A = ½(a + b)h
Correct answer: D
A four-sided shape with exactly one pair of parallel sides is a trapezium. Its area is ½(a + b)h, and the ½ must not be left out.
