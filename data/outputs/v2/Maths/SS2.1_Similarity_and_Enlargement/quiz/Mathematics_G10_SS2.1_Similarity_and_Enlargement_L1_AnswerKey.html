Q1. A school plan is labelled "Scale 1 : 200". A classroom block that is 10 m wide in real life is drawn 5 cm wide on the plan. How many times shorter is a length on the plan than the real length?
  1. 20 times
  2. 5 times
  3. 2 times
  4. 200 times
Correct answer: D
10 m = 1000 cm, and 1000 ÷ 5 = 200. Every length on the plan is 200 times shorter than the real length, which matches the label 1 : 200.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, Task 2 and Task 3: measuring the A4 and A5 exercise books and recording on the Group Observation Chart
Q2. A group measures an A4 exercise book (29.7 cm by 21 cm) and an A5 exercise book (21 cm by 14.8 cm). Both length ratios come to about 1.4, and every corner of both books measures 90°. What should the group record?
  1. Not similar: the books have different lengths and widths
  2. Similar: corresponding sides are in the same ratio (about 1.4) and corresponding angles are equal
  3. Not similar: only the angles are equal
  4. Similar: because both books are rectangles, no measuring is needed
Correct answer: B
Both pairs of corresponding sides give a ratio of about 1.4 and all corresponding angles are 90°. Both properties hold, so the books are similar.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe, teacher probe: "Have you measured the ratio of heights AND the ratio of diameters?"
Q3. Two tins have heights of 12 cm and 6 cm. A learner says, "The height ratio is 2, so the tins are similar." What must the group still check before they can say the tins are similar?
  1. That the ratio of the diameters is also 2, and that corresponding angles are equal
  2. Nothing more, because one equal ratio is enough
  3. That the difference in heights equals the difference in diameters
  4. That both tins have the same colour of label
Correct answer: A
Similar figures need all corresponding sides in the same ratio, not just one pair, and equal corresponding angles. The group must check the diameters too.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, guided example: the 4 cm × 6 cm and 10 cm × 15 cm rectangles
Q4. A small card measures 4 cm by 6 cm. A larger card measures 10 cm by 15 cm. What is the ratio of the corresponding lengths (larger to smaller), found by dividing 10 by 4 and 15 by 6?
  1. 1.5
  2. 6
  3. 2.5
  4. 2
Correct answer: C
10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 and 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5. Both ratios are equal, so the cards are similar, because the corners are all 90° as well.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, formative check: catching the additive rather than multiplicative comparison error
Q5. Rectangle A is 3 cm by 5 cm and rectangle B is 6 cm by 8 cm. A learner says, "Each side of B is 3 cm longer than the matching side of A, so they are similar." Which statement is correct?
  1. They are not similar, because the angles are different
  2. They are similar, because the differences in length are equal
  3. They are similar, because both are rectangles
  4. They are not similar, because the sides are not in the same ratio (6 ÷ 3 = 2 but 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6)
Correct answer: D
Similarity depends on how many times bigger each side is (the ratio), not on the difference. 6 ÷ 3 = 2 and 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6 are not equal, so the rectangles are not similar even though all their angles are 90°.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain, formalising PROPERTY 1 (equal corresponding angles) and PROPERTY 2 (corresponding sides in the same ratio) – "these two properties must BOTH be true"
Q6. A 4 cm by 4 cm square and a 4 cm by 8 cm rectangle both have four right angles. Why are they NOT similar?
  1. Their corresponding sides are not in the same ratio (4 ÷ 4 = 1 but 8 ÷ 4 = 2)
  2. Squares can never be similar to anything
  3. The square has a smaller area, and similar shapes must have equal areas
  4. Their corresponding angles are different
Correct answer: A
Equal angles alone are not enough. Both properties must be true, and here the side ratios are 1 and 2, which are different.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: posting the formal Driving Question and pink-note questions about area and volume
Q7. Two water tanks on a Kisumu school compound are identical in shape. One holds exactly 8 times as much water as the other, yet it appears only about twice as tall. At the end of this first lesson, which statement is the most honest about what the class knows?
  1. The class already knows the exact rule linking height, area and volume
  2. The tanks are not similar, because their volumes are so different
  3. The class can say the tanks are similar in shape (equal angles, sides in proportion), but how their heights, areas and volumes are related has not yet been explained and stays on the Driving Question Board
  4. The volume must double when the height doubles, so the tank holding 8 times as much must have been measured wrongly
Correct answer: C
Today's lesson establishes what makes shapes similar. The link between lengths, areas and volumes is the Driving Question and will be tested in later lessons, so the predictions stay on the board.
