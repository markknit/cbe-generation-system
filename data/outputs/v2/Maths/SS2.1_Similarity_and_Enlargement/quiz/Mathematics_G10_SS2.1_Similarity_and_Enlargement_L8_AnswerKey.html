Use after: Predict phase — Predict: comparing the Lesson 1 prediction with what learners now know about area and volume when scaling by factor 2
Q1. A solid is enlarged with a linear scale factor of 2 (every length is doubled). Many learners first predicted that its volume would also double. What is the volume scale factor?
  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 8
  4. 4
Correct answer: C
Volume scales by k³. With k = 2, the volume scale factor is 2³ = 8, not 2. The answer 4 is the area scale factor.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: group presentations of similar-solid models (stating L.S.F and verifying A.S.F and V.S.F)
Q2. One group enlarges a small sphere model into a bigger sphere. They find that the volume of the bigger sphere is 27 times the volume of the small one. What linear scale factor did they use?
  1. 27
  2. 9
  3. 13.5
  4. 3
Correct answer: D
V.S.F = k³ = 27, so k = cube root of 27 = 3. Dividing by 2 or by 3 repeatedly gives the wrong values.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Prompt 1 (Why is only the length divided by 200 on the Kisumu school plan?)
Q3. A Kisumu school plan is drawn to Scale 1 : 200. A classroom 10 m wide appears as 5 cm on the plan. Which statement best explains why only the length is divided by 200?
  1. The scale only works for lengths, so area and volume cannot be compared between the plan and the building.
  2. Areas and volumes are also divided by 200, but the plan only shows lengths.
  3. The scale 1 : 200 compares lengths only. Areas on the plan are 200² times smaller and volumes are 200³ times smaller as a result.
  4. Areas are divided by 200 × 2 and volumes by 200 × 3.
Correct answer: C
A scale such as 1 : 200 is a linear scale factor. Because similar shapes keep their proportions, the area ratio follows as k² and the volume ratio as k³. The scale itself only states the length ratio.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Prompt 2 (The plumber says the larger water tank holds 8 times as much water)
Q4. Two water tanks have exactly the same shape. The larger tank is twice as tall as the smaller one. The smaller tank holds 500 litres. How much water does the larger tank hold?
  1. 2 000 litres
  2. 3 000 litres
  3. 1 000 litres
  4. 4 000 litres
Correct answer: D
L.S.F = 2, so V.S.F = 2³ = 8. The larger tank holds 500 × 8 = 4 000 litres, so the plumber is correct.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Prompt 3 (Nairobi architect, Scale 1 : 500, ratio of actual floor area to plan floor area)
Q5. A building plan is drawn to Scale 1 : 500. What is the ratio of the actual floor area to the floor area on the plan?
  1. 1 000
  2. 500
  3. 250 000
  4. 125 000 000
Correct answer: C
The L.S.F from plan to building is 500, so the A.S.F = 500² = 250 000. The actual floor area is 250 000 times the plan floor area.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: writing the group Final Answer Card with the three scale factor rules in symbolic form
Q6. A group writes its Final Answer Card about enlarging a solid by a linear scale factor k. Which set of rules should be written on the card?
  1. Lengths scale by k, areas by k², volumes by k³
  2. Lengths scale by k, areas by k, volumes by k
  3. Lengths scale by k, areas by k³, volumes by k²
  4. Lengths scale by k, areas by 2k, volumes by 3k
Correct answer: A
Area has two dimensions, so it scales by k². Volume has three dimensions, so it scales by k³. Scaling all three by k, or by 2k and 3k, are common misconceptions.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: individual annotated diagram and 'I used to think / Now I know' reflection
Q7. A learner completes the sentence 'I used to think… Now I know…' about doubling every length of a water tank (k = 2). Which version is mathematically correct?
  1. I used to think the volume would double. Now I know it becomes 8 times bigger, because volume depends on three lengths, so V.S.F = 2³.
  2. I used to think the volume would be 4 times bigger. Now I know it is 2 times bigger, because the radius doubles.
  3. I used to think the volume would be 8 times bigger. Now I know it is only 2 times bigger, because the height is 2 times bigger.
  4. I used to think the volume would double. Now I know it becomes 4 times bigger, because V.S.F = 2².
Correct answer: A
Volume depends on three lengths, so doubling every length multiplies the volume by 2³ = 8. The factor 2² = 4 applies to area, not volume.
