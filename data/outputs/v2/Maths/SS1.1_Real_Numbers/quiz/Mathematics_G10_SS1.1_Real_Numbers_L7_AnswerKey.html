Q1. A scientific calculator shows 1/π = 0.3183098862. Which statement about this display is correct?
  1. It is the exact value of 1/π because the calculator is accurate
  2. It shows 1/π is a rational number because the display ends
  3. It is exact because it stops after 10 decimal places
  4. It is a rounded value, so it is only an approximation of 1/π
Correct answer: D
A calculator can only show a limited number of digits, so the display is a rounded approximation. The true decimal for 1/π never ends and never repeats.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Three Methods Sheet, calculator reciprocal of 4.8
Q2. Which key sequence on a calculator correctly gives the reciprocal of 4.8?
  1. 4.8 ÷ 1 =
  2. 1 ÷ 4.8 =
  3. 1 ÷ 48 =
  4. 1 ÷ 4 × 8 =
Correct answer: B
The reciprocal of 4.8 is 1 ÷ 4.8, which can also be found with the x⁻¹ key. Entering 1 ÷ 4 × 8 gives 2, and 1 ÷ 48 drops the decimal point.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Three Methods Sheet, answer rounded to 4 decimal places or 4 significant figures
Q3. The calculator shows 1/4.8 = 0.208333… Write this correct to 4 significant figures.
  1. 0.2084
  2. 0.2083
  3. 0.2833
  4. 0.2080
Correct answer: B
The first four significant figures are 2, 0, 8, 3. The next digit is 3, which is less than 5, so the answer stays 0.2083.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher check 'Does your answer times 4.8 give 1?'
Q4. A learner finds that the reciprocal of 4.8 is about 0.2083. Which check shows that the answer is sensible?
  1. 4.8 ÷ 0.2083 is about 1
  2. 4.8 − 0.2083 is about 1
  3. 4.8 × 0.2083 is about 1
  4. 4.8 + 0.2083 is about 5
Correct answer: C
A number multiplied by its reciprocal gives 1. Here 4.8 × 0.2083 = 0.99984, which is very close to 1.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: discussion 'Which answers repeat, and which never repeat?'
Q5. On a calculator, 1/7 = 0.142857142857… and 1/π = 0.31830988… Which statement is correct?
  1. 1/7 is irrational because its decimal never ends; 1/π is rational
  2. Both are irrational because their decimals go on forever
  3. 1/7 repeats, so it is rational; 1/π never repeats, so it is irrational
  4. Both are rational because every reciprocal is a fraction
Correct answer: C
A decimal that repeats (the block 142857) can be written as a fraction, so 1/7 is rational. 1/π never repeats and cannot be written as a fraction of whole numbers, so it is irrational.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'How many digits of 1/(28π) are sensible if the sufuria rim is measured to the nearest millimetre?'
Q6. A calculator gives 1/(28π) ≈ 0.011368210… A learner measures the rim of a sufuria to the nearest millimetre. Why is it NOT sensible to write all the digits from the calculator in the answer?
  1. Because 1/(28π) is a rational number
  2. The calculator cannot show more than four digits
  3. More digits always make an answer more accurate for any job
  4. Digits beyond the precision of the measurement give a false sense of accuracy
Correct answer: D
The number of digits kept should match the job and the precision of the instrument. Extra digits beyond the nearest millimetre add no real accuracy.

Q7. Which statement about finding reciprocals is correct?
  1. Tables work without batteries but give only about 4 digits; a calculator gives many digits but the display is rounded
  2. Tables need a battery or a device to work
  3. A calculator display is always exact
  4. Tables give more digits than a calculator
Correct answer: A
Tables are handy without a device but limited to about 4 digits. A calculator shows more digits, but they are still rounded and it needs a device.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: quick test, find 1/6.5 with a calculator and check by multiplying back
Q8. Use a calculator to find the reciprocal of 6.5. Give your answer correct to 4 decimal places.
  1. 0.1580
  2. 0.1538
  3. 0.1385
  4. 0.1539
Correct answer: B
1 ÷ 6.5 = 0.153846…, and the fifth decimal digit is 6, so rounding to 4 decimal places gives 0.1538. Check: 6.5 × 0.1538 = 0.9997, close to 1. 0.1539 is a rounding error.
