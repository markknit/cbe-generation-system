Use after: Observe phase — Investigation Steps 1–2: constructing the perpendicular bisector of PP'
Q1. A rotation maps point P to its image P'. The centre of rotation is O. Which statement about O must be true?
  1. O is the midpoint of the segment PP'
  2. O is the same distance from P as from P'
  3. O lies on the line joining P and P'
  4. The line OP is perpendicular to the segment PP'
Correct answer: B
Rotation turns a point about O without changing its distance from O, so OP = OP'. This is why O lies on the perpendicular bisector of PP'. O is the midpoint of PP' only when the angle is 180°.
Use after: Explain phase — Deepening Question Q1: 'Why two and not just one?'
Q2. A group finds the centre of rotation by constructing the perpendicular bisectors of PP' and QQ'. Why do they need TWO bisectors and not just one?
  1. Two bisectors are needed only on the Cartesian plane, never on plain paper
  2. One bisector measures the angle and the second measures the direction
  3. One bisector gives only a line of possible centres, and the second bisector cuts it at one point
  4. One bisector is for the object and the other is for the image
Correct answer: C
Every point on one perpendicular bisector is equidistant from P and P', so one bisector gives a whole line of possible centres. A second bisector crosses it at exactly one point, which is the centre.
Use after: Explain phase — Deepening Question Q3: the 180° case
Q3. A rotation of exactly 180° maps point A(2, 1) to its image A'(6, 5). What are the coordinates of the centre of rotation?
  1. (4, 4)
  2. (4, 3)
  3. (8, 6)
  4. (2, 2)
Correct answer: B
For 180°, the centre is the midpoint of A and A'. Midpoint = ((2+6)/2, (1+5)/2) = (4, 3).
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation Sheet 2, Step 1: perpendicular bisector of AA'
Q4. On a Cartesian plane, a rotation maps A(2, 3) to A'(0, 3). What is the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AA'?
  1. y = 1
  2. x = 2
  3. y = 3
  4. x = 1
Correct answer: D
AA' is a horizontal segment on y = 3 from x = 2 to x = 0. Its midpoint is (1, 3), so the perpendicular bisector is the vertical line x = 1.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation Sheet 2, Step 3: measuring the angle and its direction
Q5. On a Cartesian plane, the centre of a rotation is found to be O(1, 2). The point A(2, 3) maps to A'(0, 3). What is the angle and direction of the rotation?
  1. 180°
  2. 45° anticlockwise
  3. 90° clockwise
  4. 90° anticlockwise
Correct answer: D
OA goes up and to the right (1, 1), and OA' goes up and to the left (-1, 1). These are perpendicular, so the angle is 90°. The turn from OA to OA' is anticlockwise.
Use after: Observe phase — Investigation Step 4: VERIFY by rotating the object
Q6. A learner finds a centre O and an angle by construction and measurement. She rotates vertex P to P' correctly, but vertex R does not land on R'. What is the most sensible conclusion?
  1. The rotation is wrong only for R, because each vertex has its own centre
  2. There was an error in the construction or measurement, so the centre or angle needs to be checked again
  3. Rotations do not always map every vertex onto its image
  4. The image must have been enlarged
Correct answer: B
A rotation uses one centre and one angle for every point, and it keeps shape and size. If one vertex fails to land on its image, the centre or angle found is wrong because of a construction or measuring error.
Use after: End of lesson — Clock extension: Nairobi clock at 12:00 and 12:20
Q7. In a photograph, the tip of a clock's minute hand is at 12:00. Twenty minutes later, at 12:20, it has moved to a new position. Through what angle has the minute hand turned clockwise about the clock's centre?
  1. 90°
  2. 60°
  3. 120°
  4. 180°
Correct answer: C
The minute hand turns 360° in 60 minutes, which is 6° per minute. In 20 minutes it turns 6 × 20 = 120°, which matches the move from 12 to 4 on the clock.
