Q1. A student stands 40 m from the base of a tall mast on level ground. She estimates that the angle from her eye level up to the top of the mast is about 50°. Why can we not yet find the height of the mast using only algebra and Pythagoras' theorem?
  1. The mast is too tall for a right-angled triangle to be formed.
  2. Pythagoras' theorem needs two sides of a right-angled triangle, but we know only one side (40 m) and one angle (50°).
  3. Pythagoras' theorem only works for masts shorter than 40 m.
  4. Algebra cannot be used with angles measured in degrees.
Correct answer: B
Pythagoras links sides to sides, so it needs two known sides. Here only one side and one angle are known, which is the knowledge gap this unit sets out to fill.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: cold-call sharing of attempts (partner share and 'Walk us through your reasoning')
Q2. In the mast problem, a learner writes 'height = 40 + 50 = 90 m'. What is wrong with this working?
  1. The answer should be 40 × 50 = 2 000 m.
  2. It adds a length in metres to an angle in degrees, and these are different kinds of quantities.
  3. Nothing is wrong, because a mast must be taller than the distance to its base.
  4. The answer should be 50 m because the angle is 50°.
Correct answer: B
40 is a distance in metres and 50 is an angle in degrees, so adding them has no meaning. This is the 'mixing the angle and the distance' attempt.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Environment Scan, 'Triangle Hunt' labelling O, A and H
Q3. A wheelchair ramp forms a right-angled triangle with the ground. The sloping ramp surface meets the ground at an acute angle marked θ. The flat ground length runs from θ to the right angle. The vertical height of the ramp stands across from θ. Standing at θ, which labelling of the sides is correct?
  1. Ground length is the hypotenuse, height is the opposite side, and the ramp surface is the adjacent side.
  2. Ground length is the adjacent side, height is the opposite side, and the ramp surface is the hypotenuse.
  3. Ground length is the opposite side, height is the adjacent side, and the ramp surface is the hypotenuse.
  4. Ground length is the adjacent side, height is the hypotenuse, and the ramp surface is the opposite side.
Correct answer: B
The side touching θ (not the longest side) is adjacent, the side across from θ is opposite, and the side facing the right angle (the ramp surface) is the hypotenuse.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Ratio Discovery Activity, three 40° triangles and the group ratio table
Q4. A group draws right-angled triangles that each have a 40° angle. In their first triangle the hypotenuse is 6.0 cm and the side opposite the 40° angle is 3.9 cm, so O ÷ H = 0.65. They then carefully draw a larger triangle with a 40° angle and a hypotenuse of 12 cm. What O ÷ H should they expect for the larger triangle?
  1. About 0.33, because the hypotenuse is twice as long.
  2. About 0.65, nearly the same as the first triangle.
  3. About 6.5, because the sides are measured in centimetres.
  4. About 1.30, because the triangle is twice as big.
Correct answer: B
Groups found that O ÷ H stays nearly the same for triangles sharing the same 40° angle, apart from small measurement error. Here 3.9 ÷ 6.0 = 0.65.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Ratio Discovery Activity, scanning group tables and redirecting groups to re-check the 40° angle
Q5. A group measured three right-angled triangles, each supposed to have a 40° angle. Their O ÷ H values were 0.64, 0.65 and 0.91. What should the group do first?
  1. Delete the 0.64 and 0.65 so that only one value is left.
  2. Add the three values and divide by 3 without checking.
  3. Re-check the 40° angle in the third triangle with the protractor.
  4. Record 0.91 as correct, since triangles of different sizes give different ratios.
Correct answer: C
For the same angle, the ratios should cluster closely (within about ±0.03). A value far from the others most likely comes from an angle drawn or measured wrongly.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: return to the mast problem and write 'height ÷ 40 = tan 50°'
Q6. A mast stands vertically on level ground. A student 40 m from its base sees the top at an angle of 50°. The mast's height is the side opposite the 50° angle, and the 40 m is the side adjacent to it. Which equation links the height to the known values using the correct ratio?
  1. height ÷ 40 = sin 50°
  2. height × 40 = tan 50°
  3. height ÷ 40 = tan 50°
  4. 40 ÷ height = tan 50°
Correct answer: C
Opposite ÷ adjacent is the tangent ratio. With opposite = height and adjacent = 40 m, this gives height ÷ 40 = tan 50°.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: 'Now — what is tan 50°?' and re-opening the knowledge gap
Q7. After writing 'height ÷ 40 = tan 50°', a student says: 'I still cannot find the height.' What is the real reason?
  1. Tangent is a ratio that changes every time a different mast is measured.
  2. The 40 m is too short to be used in a ratio.
  3. We do not yet have a way to find the actual value of tan 50°.
  4. The equation is wrong, because the height cannot be linked to 40 m.
Correct answer: C
The equation is correct, but the number tan 50° is not yet known. Finding the ratio for any angle, not only 40°, is the next step the class needs.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: initial annotated pencil sketch of the mast scenario
Q8. A learner draws a model of the mast problem: a vertical mast, a horizontal ground line of 40 m, and a 50° angle where the student stands. Where should the right angle be marked?
  1. At the top of the mast.
  2. At the student's feet, at the 50° angle.
  3. Halfway up the slanting line to the top of the mast.
  4. At the base of the mast, where the mast meets the ground.
Correct answer: D
The mast stands straight up from level ground, so the right angle is where the mast meets the ground. The 50° angle is at the student's position.
