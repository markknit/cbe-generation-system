Q1. A learner says: 'The antacid tablet and the paracetamol tablet are both white powders, so each one must be a single pure substance.' Which response is best?
  1. Correct, because every tablet is made of only one element.
  2. Wrong, because tablets cannot contain any compounds at all.
  3. Looking the same does not show a substance is pure; we need evidence from tests such as dissolving, heating and reacting.
  4. Correct, because anything that looks like a powder is one substance.
Correct answer: C
Appearance alone cannot tell us whether a substance is one thing or several. Evidence from properties and separations is needed, so 'white powder means pure' is a misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Task 2: re-trace the separation of mixture M and calculate 2.9 + 2.8 + 2.7 = 8.4 g and the loss
Q2. A group separates a mixture of 3 g iron filings, 3 g sand and 3 g salt (total 9 g). They recover 2.9 g of iron, 2.8 g of sand and 2.7 g of salt. How much mass was lost?
  1. 0.3 g
  2. 8.4 g
  3. 1.2 g
  4. 0.6 g
Correct answer: D
Recovered mass = 2.9 + 2.8 + 2.7 = 8.4 g. Loss = 9 - 8.4 = 0.6 g, caused by spillage and material sticking to equipment.
Use after: Observe phase — Task 3: salt dissolved in water passes through the filter paper and needs evaporation
Q3. Salt has dissolved completely in water. Why can the salt NOT be removed from the water by filtration or by a magnet?
  1. The filter paper reacts with salt and destroys it.
  2. The salt has turned into water and no longer exists.
  3. The dissolved salt is mixed in the water and passes through the filter paper; it is not magnetic, so it must be recovered by evaporation.
  4. The salt is magnetic but too heavy to filter.
Correct answer: C
Dissolved salt passes through filter paper together with the water and is not attracted by a magnet. Evaporating the water leaves the salt behind.
Use after: Observe phase — Task 3: each part of mixture M kept its own properties, so M is a mixture
Q4. In a mixture of iron filings, sand and salt, the iron is still pulled by a magnet, the sand is still gritty and does not dissolve, and the salt still dissolves in water. What does this show?
  1. The mixture is a single element.
  2. The substances have lost their properties.
  3. The three substances have joined to form one new compound.
  4. The substances are only mixed, so each keeps its own properties and can be separated by physical methods.
Correct answer: D
In a mixture, substances are not chemically joined, so each keeps its own properties. This is why magnetic separation, filtration and evaporation work.
Use after: Explain phase — Classification card sort: Element, Compound or Mixture (iron E, salt A) with the Substance Information Card
Q5. Iron (Fe) is classed as an element, but salt (sodium chloride) is classed as a compound. Which reason is correct?
  1. Iron is a solid, but salt dissolves in water.
  2. Iron is made of only one kind of substance (iron), while salt has two different elements, sodium and chlorine, joined together.
  3. Salt is a mixture of sodium and chlorine that can be separated with a magnet.
  4. Iron is a compound of iron and sulphur.
Correct answer: B
An element is one kind of substance that cannot be split into simpler ones by ordinary methods. A compound has different elements joined together; salt is sodium and chlorine joined.
Use after: Explain phase — Card sort discussion: 'Why is the antacid tablet F a mixture even though it contains a compound, calcium carbonate?'
Q6. An antacid tablet contains calcium carbonate, which is a compound, together with binders. Why is the whole tablet classified as a mixture?
  1. Because tablets are always made of exactly one element.
  2. Because calcium carbonate is an element.
  3. Because it fizzes in vinegar.
  4. Because it contains an active ingredient and binders that are only mixed together, not joined into one substance.
Correct answer: D
A mixture has different substances just mixed together. The tablet has the active ingredient plus binders, so it is a mixture even though one part is a compound.
Use after: Explain phase — Wanjiru's puzzle: why the antacid relieves heartburn while paracetamol relieves pain (fizz-in-vinegar evidence)
Q7. In a test, an antacid tablet fizzes in dilute vinegar, but a paracetamol tablet shows no fizz. Which conclusion is best supported by this evidence?
  1. Paracetamol is an element and the antacid is a mixture of iron and sulphur.
  2. Both tablets work in the same way because both are white.
  3. The antacid reacts with and neutralises acid, while paracetamol has a different active ingredient that works on pain and fever instead.
  4. Paracetamol is stronger than the antacid because it does not fizz.
Correct answer: C
The fizz shows that the antacid reacts with acid, which explains why it relieves heartburn. Paracetamol does not react this way; it works on pain and fever by a different action.
Use after: Explain phase — Medicine safety: paracetamol label dose and the 4 g per 24 hours maximum with the liver warning
Q8. An adult paracetamol label says: take 1 to 2 tablets of 500 mg every 4 to 6 hours, with a maximum of 8 tablets in 24 hours. What is the maximum total mass of paracetamol in 24 hours, in grams?
  1. 0.4 g
  2. 40 g
  3. 4 g
  4. 8 g
Correct answer: C
8 tablets x 500 mg = 4000 mg. Since 1000 mg = 1 g, the maximum is 4 g in 24 hours. Taking more can damage the liver.
