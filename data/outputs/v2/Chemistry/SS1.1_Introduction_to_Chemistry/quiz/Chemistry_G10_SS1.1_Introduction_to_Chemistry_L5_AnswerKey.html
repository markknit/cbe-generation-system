Use after: Observe phase — Card 1: H sugar and G paracetamol
Q1. A class tests two substances. Sugar melts at about 186 C, turns brown and then chars black with a caramel smell. Paracetamol melts at about 169 C and browns. Both are made of carbon-containing substances. Which branch of chemistry mainly studies carbon-based substances such as these?
  1. Inorganic chemistry
  2. Organic means farming without chemicals, so none of the branches
  3. Organic chemistry
  4. Analytical chemistry
Correct answer: C
Organic chemistry studies carbon-based substances such as sugar and many medicines. Here 'organic' does not mean farming without chemicals.
Use after: Observe phase — Card 2: A salt and E iron filings
Q2. Table salt dissolves completely in water. Iron filings are pulled out by a magnet and turn dull blackish when heated. Salts, metals and minerals like these belong to which branch of chemistry?
  1. Biochemistry
  2. Physical chemistry
  3. Inorganic chemistry
  4. Organic chemistry
Correct answer: C
Inorganic chemistry studies substances such as salts, metals and minerals, so salt and iron filings fit here.
Use after: Observe phase — Card 3: Melting points
Q3. Sulphur melts at about 115 C, paracetamol at about 169 C and sugar at about 186 C. Comparing how matter behaves when heated, such as melting points, is mainly the work of which branch?
  1. Analytical chemistry
  2. Organic chemistry
  3. Physical chemistry
  4. Biochemistry
Correct answer: C
Physical chemistry studies how matter behaves, including melting points and energy changes. Analytical chemistry is about finding out what is present and separating or measuring it.
Use after: Observe phase — Card 4: Mixture M separation (magnet, filtration, evaporation)
Q4. A mixture M has 3 g of iron, 3 g of sand and 3 g of salt (9 g in total). A magnet recovers 2.9 g, filtration recovers 2.8 g and evaporation recovers 2.7 g. This separating and measuring is analytical chemistry. How much mass was lost during the separation?
  1. 1.0 g
  2. 8.4 g
  3. 0.6 g
  4. 0.3 g
Correct answer: C
Total recovered = 2.9 + 2.8 + 2.7 = 8.4 g. Loss = 9 - 8.4 = 0.6 g.
Use after: Explain phase — Puzzle: Wanjiru's question on why the antacid relieves heartburn but paracetamol relieves pain
Q5. An antacid tablet neutralises excess stomach acid and fizzes in dilute vinegar. Paracetamol relieves pain and fever but does not neutralise acid. Which branch of chemistry mainly explains how each of them acts in the body?
  1. Analytical chemistry
  2. Biochemistry
  3. Physical chemistry
  4. Inorganic chemistry
Correct answer: B
Biochemistry is the chemistry of living things, including how medicines act in the body.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Can one substance belong to two branches?
Q6. A learner says, 'Paracetamol can belong to only one branch of chemistry.' Which response is correct?
  1. Correct, because paracetamol is a medicine, so only biochemistry studies it.
  2. Wrong, because paracetamol is studied by organic chemistry for what it is made of and by biochemistry for how it acts in the body.
  3. Wrong, because paracetamol is studied only by physical chemistry for its melting point.
  4. Correct, because each substance is studied by only one branch.
Correct answer: B
One substance can belong to more than one branch. Organic chemistry studies the makeup of paracetamol and biochemistry studies its action in the body.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: groups write a new question a chemist in a specific branch could investigate
Q7. A Nairobi company wants to check that every paracetamol tablet it makes contains 500 mg of paracetamol. Which branch of chemistry would investigate this question?
  1. Inorganic chemistry, because tablets are salts
  2. Physical chemistry, because tablets melt when heated
  3. Biochemistry, because paracetamol acts in the body
  4. Analytical chemistry, because it finds out what is present and measures how much
Correct answer: D
Checking how much of a substance is in a tablet means finding out what is present and measuring it, which is analytical chemistry.
