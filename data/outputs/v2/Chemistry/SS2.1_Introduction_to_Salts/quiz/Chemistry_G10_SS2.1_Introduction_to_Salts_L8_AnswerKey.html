Q1. A container of washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) is left on a laboratory bench. After a few days it has a white powdery crust and the crystals look smaller. What is the correct scientific term for this change?
  1. Precipitation, because a new solid forms in solution
  2. Hygroscopy, because the crystals take in water without dissolving
  3. Efflorescence, because the crystals lose water of crystallisation to the air
  4. Deliquescence, because the crystals absorb water and dissolve
Correct answer: C
Washing soda loses some of its water of crystallisation to the air (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O → Na₂CO₃·H₂O + 9H₂O). This leaves a powdery crust and shrunken crystals, which is efflorescence.

Q2. Table salt (NaCl) kept in an ordinary kitchen store stays dry and unchanged for months, while CAN fertiliser left open becomes wet and sticky. Which explanation for the table salt is correct?
  1. NaCl is insoluble in water, so it can never take in moisture
  2. NaCl takes up water from the air only when the air is very humid (above about 75% relative humidity), so it is unchanged at everyday humidity
  3. NaCl contains water of crystallisation, which keeps it from changing
  4. NaCl is not a salt, so it does not behave like the other two substances
Correct answer: B
NaCl only absorbs water vapour when relative humidity is very high (above about 75%). At ordinary storeroom humidity it stays unchanged, unlike CAN, which absorbs water more readily.

Q3. A group's preparation-pathway flowchart on the poster includes precipitation. Which of these salts would be prepared by precipitation, by mixing two soluble salt solutions?
  1. Potassium nitrate
  2. Ammonium sulphate
  3. Lead(II) sulphate
  4. Sodium chloride
Correct answer: C
Precipitation is used for insoluble salts. Lead sulphate is an exception among the sulphates, so it forms as a solid when, for example, lead nitrate and sodium sulphate solutions are mixed. The other three salts are soluble.
Use after: DQB phase — Summative task part (a): name salt X and classify it
Q4. A Kenyan agrochemical company makes salt X by reacting sulphuric acid with ammonia solution until the acid is completely neutralised. Salt X is ammonium sulphate, (NH₄)₂SO₄. How is ammonium sulphate classified?
  1. Acidic salt
  2. Double salt
  3. Normal salt
  4. Basic salt
Correct answer: C
All the replaceable hydrogen ions of the acid have been replaced by NH₄⁺ ions. The salt has no leftover H⁺ or OH⁻, so it is a normal salt.
Use after: DQB phase — Summative task part (b): write a balanced equation for the preparation of salt X
Q5. Sulphuric acid reacts with ammonia solution to make ammonium sulphate. Which balanced equation shows this reaction?
  1. H₂SO₄ + 2NH₃ → (NH₄)₂SO₄
  2. H₂SO₄ + NH₃ → NH₄SO₄
  3. H₂SO₄ + 2NH₃ → (NH₄)₂SO₄ + H₂
  4. H₂SO₄ + NH₃ → (NH₄)₂SO₄
Correct answer: A
Each H₂SO₄ gives two H⁺ ions, so it needs two NH₃ molecules to form two NH₄⁺ ions. This gives H₂SO₄ + 2NH₃ → (NH₄)₂SO₄ with N, H, S and O balanced. The reaction is not metal + acid, so no hydrogen gas forms.
Use after: DQB phase — Summative task part (c): explain what happens to stored salt X on a very humid night and on an ordinary dry day, and how it should be stored
Q6. Ammonium sulphate fertiliser takes up water vapour only when the air is very humid (above about 80% relative humidity). It is stored in an open warehouse near Lake Nakuru. Which statement is correct?
  1. It is unchanged in all weather because it is a salt, so no special storage is needed
  2. On an ordinary dry day it loses water of crystallisation and forms a powdery crust, so it should be kept in open sacks
  3. On a very humid, rainy night it is hygroscopic and may become damp and clump; on an ordinary dry day it stays dry; it should be kept in sealed bags
  4. On an ordinary dry day it absorbs so much water that it dissolves completely, so it should be kept in the sun
Correct answer: C
The salt absorbs water only at high humidity, so it is hygroscopic. It may get damp and clump on a humid night but stays dry on a dry day. Sealed bags protect it from moist air.

Q7. Which pairing of a salt with a correct Kenyan use is right?
  1. Sodium chloride: preserving and drying fish such as omena and tilapia at Lake Victoria
  2. Washing soda: fertiliser for maize farms in the Rift Valley
  3. Sodium chloride: fertiliser for maize farms in the Rift Valley
  4. Calcium ammonium nitrate (CAN): preserving fish at Lake Victoria
Correct answer: A
Sodium chloride is used to preserve and dry fish such as omena and tilapia. CAN is a fertiliser for maize farms, and washing soda is used for laundry.
