Q1. In Experiment 3, a group weighs a watch glass of anhydrous calcium chloride (CaCl₂) at 2.00 g at the start. After 25 minutes in open classroom air it reads 2.46 g. What is the mass change, Δm = final − initial?
  1. +0.46 g
  2. −0.46 g
  3. +0.54 g
  4. +4.46 g
Correct answer: A
Δm = final − initial = 2.46 − 2.00 = +0.46 g. The positive sign shows the sample gained mass by absorbing water vapour from the air.

Q2. A group weighs 2.00 g of sodium chloride (NaCl) on a watch glass and leaves it open for 25 minutes in a normal classroom. The final reading is 2.01 g and the crystals look unchanged. What is the best conclusion?
  1. There was no significant change, because a difference of 0.01 g is within balance error at everyday humidity
  2. NaCl is efflorescent because its crystals stayed dry
  3. NaCl is deliquescent because its mass increased
  4. NaCl lost its water of crystallisation to the air
Correct answer: A
A 0.01 g difference is within balance error, so NaCl showed no real change. NaCl takes up water only when the air is very humid (above about 75% relative humidity).
Use after: Explain phase — Frayer Model: defining hygroscopic, deliquescent and efflorescent using experimental data
Q3. Which statement correctly describes a deliquescent salt?
  1. It absorbs water vapour from the air until it dissolves in the water it has absorbed
  2. It absorbs some water from the air but never dissolves in it
  3. It does not interact with water vapour in the air at all
  4. It loses water of crystallisation to the air and turns into a powder
Correct answer: A
A deliquescent salt such as CaCl₂ absorbs so much water vapour that it dissolves in it. Absorbing water without dissolving describes a hygroscopic salt, and losing water describes an efflorescent salt.
Use after: Explain phase — Writing the balanced equation for efflorescence with state symbols on the worksheet
Q4. Washing soda crystals (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) left in open air lose water of crystallisation and form a white powder of the monohydrate, Na₂CO₃·H₂O. Which is the correct balanced equation for this efflorescence?
  1. Na₂CO₃·10H₂O(s) → Na₂CO₃·H₂O(s) + 10H₂O(g)
  2. Na₂CO₃·10H₂O(s) → Na₂CO₃·H₂O(s) + 9H₂O(g)
  3. Na₂CO₃·10H₂O(s) + 9H₂O(g) → Na₂CO₃·H₂O(s)
  4. Na₂CO₃·10H₂O(s) → Na₂CO₃·H₂O(s) + 8H₂O(g)
Correct answer: B
The crystal has 10 water molecules and the product keeps 1, so 9 H₂O escape as vapour. The other options are unbalanced or show water being gained.
Use after: Explain phase — Matching each sample to the anchoring phenomenon: 'Sample A = CAN fertiliser because...'
Q5. A bag of CAN fertiliser (calcium ammonium nitrate) is left open overnight in a storeroom. The granules clump together and feel wet and sticky, although no water was added. What is the best explanation?
  1. The ammonium nitrate in CAN absorbed water vapour from the air and dissolved in it, like a deliquescent salt
  2. The ammonium nitrate in CAN is efflorescent and lost water of crystallisation to the air
  3. The limestone in CAN absorbed water from the air and made the granules stick together
  4. The CAN is unchanged like table salt, and the wetness came from the bag
Correct answer: A
The ammonium nitrate in CAN takes in water vapour from the air and dissolves in it, which is deliquescence. The limestone in CAN does not take up water.
Use after: DQB phase — Green card: first complete answer to the driving question, including a storage recommendation
Q6. A learner writes the answer to the driving question on a green card. Which storage advice for Kenyan farms and laboratories is correct?
  1. Sealed containers are only needed for table salt, because it is the salt that changes most in air
  2. Store CAN fertiliser in open bags, because deliquescent salts need air to stay dry
  3. Store CAN fertiliser in sealed, airtight bags and washing soda in sealed containers
  4. Keep washing soda in an open dish so its crystals can take in water
Correct answer: C
CAN is deliquescent, so it must be sealed from moist air to prevent clumping. Washing soda is efflorescent, so it must be sealed to keep its water of crystallisation. NaCl is stable at everyday humidity.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative particle model: Panel 6, side-by-side diagram of deliquescent, efflorescent and stable salt surfaces
Q7. A learner draws a particle diagram for efflorescent washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) left in open air. Which drawing of the water molecules is scientifically correct?
  1. Arrows point away from the crystal into the air, showing separate water molecules leaving the lattice
  2. Arrows point away from the crystal, but the water is drawn as a continuous liquid layer
  3. No arrows, because the water molecules stay still inside the crystal
  4. Arrows point into the crystal, because water molecules are attracted from the air
Correct answer: A
In efflorescence, water molecules leave the crystal lattice and escape into the air as separate particles. This leaves the white powdery residue. Inward arrows would show deliquescent behaviour.
