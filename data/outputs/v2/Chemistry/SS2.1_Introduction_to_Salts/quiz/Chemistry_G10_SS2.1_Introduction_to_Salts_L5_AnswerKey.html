Q1. A teacher pours barium chloride solution (0.1 mol/L) into sodium sulphate solution (0.1 mol/L). Both solutions are clear and colourless. What will you see?
  1. The mixture fizzes as a gas is given off
  2. A white solid forms immediately
  3. A golden-yellow solid forms immediately
  4. The mixture stays clear because both salts are dissolved in water
Correct answer: B
Barium sulphate is one of the insoluble sulphate exceptions, so Ba²⁺ and SO₄²⁻ ions join to form a white precipitate as soon as the solutions mix.
Use after: Observe phase — Primary experiment (barium sulphate): filtering, washing and collecting filtrate in the labelled waste beaker
Q2. After mixing barium chloride and sodium sulphate solutions, a group filters the mixture. Which is the correct way to treat the two parts?
  1. Keep the white residue on the filter paper as the product and collect the filtrate in a labelled waste beaker
  2. Keep the white residue on the filter paper as the product and pour the filtrate down the sink
  3. Collect both the residue and the filtrate in the waste beaker, because neither is useful
  4. Keep the filtrate as the product and throw away the white residue
Correct answer: A
The precipitate (residue) is the insoluble salt that was made. The filtrate is waste and must be collected, not poured down the sink, because barium ions are toxic to aquatic life.
Use after: Explain phase — Layer 1 — Word and symbol equations (balanced molecular equation on mini-whiteboards)
Q3. Which is the correct balanced equation, with state symbols, for the reaction between barium chloride solution and sodium sulphate solution?
  1. BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)
  2. BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + NaCl₂(aq)
  3. BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + NaCl(aq)
  4. BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(aq) + 2NaCl(s)
Correct answer: A
Two Na and two Cl are needed on the right, so the coefficient of NaCl is 2. BaSO₄ is the insoluble precipitate (s) and NaCl stays dissolved (aq).
Use after: Explain phase — Layer 2 — Ionic equations: Think-Aloud on spectator ions and the net ionic equation
Q4. Barium chloride solution reacts with sodium sulphate solution to form a precipitate of barium sulphate. The Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions are spectator ions. What is the net ionic equation?
  1. Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → NaCl(s)
  2. BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)
  3. 2Na⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → Na₂SO₄(s)
  4. Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s)
Correct answer: D
Only the ions that join to form the precipitate appear in the net ionic equation: Ba²⁺ and SO₄²⁻ form BaSO₄(s). Na⁺ and Cl⁻ stay in solution unchanged, so they are cancelled.
Use after: Explain phase — Alternative experiment (lead(II) iodide) and Layer 2 — spectator ions
Q5. Lead(II) nitrate solution is mixed with potassium iodide solution and a golden-yellow precipitate of lead(II) iodide forms. Which ions are the spectator ions?
  1. K⁺ and NO₃⁻
  2. K⁺ and I⁻
  3. Pb²⁺ and NO₃⁻
  4. Pb²⁺ and I⁻
Correct answer: A
Pb²⁺ and I⁻ combine to form the PbI₂ precipitate. K⁺ and NO₃⁻ stay dissolved and unchanged on both sides, so they are the spectator ions.
Use after: Explain phase — Layer 3 — Method Comparison Chart (Precipitation / Double Decomposition column)
Q6. Which of these salts must be prepared by double decomposition (precipitation) rather than by dissolving a metal, base or carbonate in an acid?
  1. Barium sulphate, which is insoluble
  2. Ammonium nitrate, which is soluble
  3. Potassium nitrate, which is soluble
  4. Sodium chloride, which is soluble
Correct answer: A
Insoluble salts are made by mixing two soluble salt solutions so that the insoluble product precipitates. The other three salts are soluble and can be made by neutralisation.
Use after: Explain phase — Layer 3 — key question: 'Why can't we make barium sulphate by reacting barium metal with sulphuric acid directly in the lab?'
Q7. A learner asks: "Why can't we make barium sulphate by reacting barium metal with sulphuric acid in the lab?" Which answer is correct?
  1. Barium sulphate is soluble, so it would dissolve and could not be collected
  2. The reaction would be violently exothermic and dangerous, and insoluble barium sulphate would coat the metal and stop the reaction
  3. Sulphuric acid cannot react with any metal
  4. Barium is a very unreactive metal, so no reaction takes place
Correct answer: B
Barium is a reactive Group 2 metal, so the reaction is violent. The insoluble BaSO₄ formed also coats the metal and stops the reaction.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: 'Kenyan context' annotation on the insoluble-salts branch (barium meal at Kenyatta National Hospital)
Q8. Barium ions are toxic, yet patients at hospitals such as Kenyatta National Hospital swallow barium sulphate in a "barium meal" before stomach X-rays. Why is this safe?
  1. Barium ions are toxic only when they are in a solid
  2. Barium sulphate is soluble, and the body quickly breaks it down into harmless substances
  3. The barium sulphate is mixed with sodium chloride, which cancels the toxic effect
  4. Barium sulphate is insoluble, so it cannot dissolve and be absorbed into the bloodstream
Correct answer: D
Because BaSO₄ is insoluble, it does not release barium ions into the blood. The same property that means it must be made by precipitation also makes it safe in medicine.
