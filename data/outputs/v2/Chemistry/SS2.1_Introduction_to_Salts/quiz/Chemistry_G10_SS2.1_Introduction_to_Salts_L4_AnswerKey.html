Use after: Predict phase — Think-Pair-Share predictions for Experiment 2b (HCl + NaOH)
Q1. Before the experiment, a learner predicts what forms when hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide solution. Which prediction follows the pattern 'acid + base' from Lesson 3?
  1. Sodium chloride, carbon dioxide and water
  2. Sodium oxide and chlorine gas
  3. Sodium chloride and hydrogen gas
  4. Sodium chloride and water
Correct answer: D
An acid reacting with a base (neutralisation) gives a salt and water, so HCl + NaOH gives NaCl + H₂O. Hydrogen is made with acid + metal, and CO₂ only with carbonates.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: neutralisation (NaCl preparation) with universal indicator
Q2. In Part A, universal indicator is added to 25 cm³ of 1 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. Sodium hydroxide solution is then added a little at a time. The final mixture looks like clear water, with no bubbles and no solid. Which observation shows that neutralisation is complete?
  1. The indicator stays red, pH about 1
  2. The indicator turns green, pH about 7
  3. The mixture bubbles and gives off a gas
  4. The indicator turns purple, pH about 14
Correct answer: B
Neutralisation gives no gas or precipitate, so the indicator colour is the evidence. The colour changes from red to orange, yellow and then green, and green (pH about 7) marks the endpoint.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: acid + carbonate (CaCl₂ preparation) with CO₂ collection and limewater test
Q3. In Part B, marble chips (calcium carbonate) are reacted with dilute hydrochloric acid. The gas produced bubbles through limewater, and the limewater turns milky. Which gas is present?
  1. Chlorine
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Hydrogen
  4. Oxygen
Correct answer: B
Carbon dioxide reacts with limewater to form a white suspension, so the limewater turns milky. This is the standard test for CO₂, which is released when an acid reacts with a carbonate.
Use after: Observe phase — Clean-up: waste disposal procedure after Parts A and B
Q4. After the experiment, the used solutions have to be dealt with. Which is the correct way to handle the waste from Experiment 2b?
  1. Leave the waste open on the bench so it evaporates
  2. Pour the waste into the school water tank
  3. Pour the acid waste straight down the sink without any treatment
  4. Neutralise the waste solutions first, then pour them down the sink
Correct answer: D
Acid or alkali waste must be neutralised before disposal so it does not harm people, pipes or the environment. This is the waste disposal procedure used at clean-up.
Use after: Explain phase — Word Equation → Balanced Equation scaffold: Reaction B (HCl + CaCO₃)
Q5. Which is the correct balanced equation for hydrochloric acid reacting with calcium carbonate?
  1. 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + CO₂ + H₂O
  2. 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + H₂ + H₂O
  3. 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl + CO₂ + H₂O
  4. HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + CO₂ + H₂O
Correct answer: A
CaCl₂ needs 2 Cl atoms, so 2HCl is needed. Both sides then have 1 Ca, 2 Cl, 2 H, 1 C and 3 O. The first option has unbalanced Cl and H, the second has the wrong formula for calcium chloride, and the third has no carbon dioxide.
Use after: Explain phase — Phenomenon Connection box: mandazi dough and NaHCO₃
Q6. In mandazi dough, baking soda (sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO₃) reacts with an acid in the dough and the dough rises. What are the products of this type of reaction?
  1. A salt, water and oxygen
  2. A salt and hydrogen gas only
  3. A salt, water and carbon dioxide
  4. A salt and water only
Correct answer: C
An acid reacting with a carbonate or hydrogen carbonate gives a salt, water and carbon dioxide. The CO₂ bubbles make the mandazi dough rise, as in HCl + NaHCO₃ → NaCl + CO₂ + H₂O.
Use after: DQB phase — Mini flow chart cards for acid + base and acid + carbonate, posted in the 'Experiment 2b Evidence' section
Q7. A group makes a mini flow chart for two methods: acid + base and acid + carbonate. Which statement correctly compares the two methods?
  1. Only acid + base makes water, and only acid + carbonate makes a salt
  2. Neither method makes water, but both make a salt
  3. Both make a salt and water, but only acid + carbonate also makes carbon dioxide
  4. Both make a salt, water and carbon dioxide
Correct answer: C
Acid + base gives salt + water. Acid + carbonate gives salt + water + CO₂ gas. Both methods make a salt and water, and only the carbonate method also makes carbon dioxide.
Use after: Model Building phase — Cumulative 'Soluble Salt Preparation Methods' flow chart: adding branches (3) and (4)
Q8. A learner adds the final two branches to the cumulative flow chart of soluble salt preparation methods. Which branch is written correctly?
  1. Acid + Carbonate → Salt + Water only
  2. Acid + Carbonate/Hydrogen carbonate → Salt + CO₂ + Water
  3. Acid + Base → Salt + CO₂ + Water
  4. Acid + Base → Salt + Hydrogen
Correct answer: B
Acid + carbonate or hydrogen carbonate gives a salt, CO₂ and water. Acid + base gives only a salt and water, with no gas.
