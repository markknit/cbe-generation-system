Q1. The first ionisation energies of four period 3 elements are: Na 496 kJ/mol, Mg 738 kJ/mol, Al 578 kJ/mol, Si 786 kJ/mol. Using only this data, which element would you predict to lose its outer electron most easily and so react most vigorously with cold water?
  1. Magnesium, because it has two outer electrons to give away
  2. Silicon, because it has the highest ionisation energy
  3. Sodium, because it has the lowest ionisation energy
  4. Aluminium, because it has the highest nuclear charge of the three metals
Correct answer: C
The lowest ionisation energy (496 kJ/mol) means the outer electron is removed most easily, so sodium forms its ion and reacts most vigorously.

Q2. A water officer drops a small piece of sodium into a beaker of water. It skims across the surface, fizzes and burns with an orange flame. After the reaction stops, what is left in the beaker?
  1. Pure water with solid sodium at the bottom
  2. A neutral solution of sodium chloride
  3. A strongly acidic solution
  4. A strongly alkaline solution
Correct answer: D
Sodium reacts with water to form sodium hydroxide solution and hydrogen gas. Sodium hydroxide is a strong alkali, so the solution is strongly alkaline.

Q3. A strip of aluminium foil placed in dilute hydrochloric acid reacts very slowly at first, and more slowly than magnesium does. What is the main reason for the slow start?
  1. Aluminium is a non-metal, so it cannot react with acids
  2. Aluminium has more outer electrons than magnesium, so it needs more acid
  3. A thin oxide film on the aluminium surface must be removed before the acid reaches the metal
  4. Aluminium has fewer protons than magnesium, so it holds its electrons more weakly
Correct answer: C
Aluminium is covered by a thin, protective aluminium oxide film. The acid has to break through this film before the metal underneath can react.

Q4. Sulfur burns in oxygen to give a colourful flame and a gas with a sharp smell. Which statement about this product is correct?
  1. It is sulfur dioxide, a basic oxide
  2. It is sodium sulfide, a neutral solid
  3. It is sulfur dioxide, an acidic oxide
  4. It is hydrogen sulfide, an acidic gas
Correct answer: C
Sulfur is a non-metal, and non-metals form acidic oxides. Burning sulfur in oxygen gives sulfur dioxide, the sharp-smelling gas, which is acidic.

Q5. Which is the correct balanced equation, with state symbols, for magnesium reacting with dilute hydrochloric acid?
  1. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl(aq) + H2(g)
  2. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + 2H(g)
  3. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + H2(g)
  4. Mg(s) + HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + H2(g)
Correct answer: C
Magnesium chloride is MgCl2, and hydrogen is released as H2 gas. Counting atoms, 1 Mg, 2 H and 2 Cl on each side shows the equation is balanced.

Q6. Magnesium has two outer electrons and sodium has only one, yet magnesium reacts much more slowly with water than sodium does. Which explanation is correct?
  1. Magnesium has 12 protons against 11 in sodium, with similar shielding, so it holds its outer electrons more tightly
  2. Magnesium has a larger atomic mass, so it moves more slowly in water
  3. Magnesium has more electrons, so it has much more shielding than sodium
  4. Two outer electrons are always easier to remove than one
Correct answer: A
Across period 3 the nuclear charge increases while shielding stays almost constant. Magnesium's outer electrons are held more strongly, so its ionisation energy is higher and it loses them less easily than sodium.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: trend arrow on the cumulative annotated periodic table for period 3
Q7. A student draws a trend arrow across period 3 from Na to Al on an annotated periodic table. Which label is correct for the metals Na, Mg and Al?
  1. Reactivity with water stays the same because all three are metals
  2. Reactivity with water increases from Na to Al as the nuclear charge increases
  3. Reactivity with water decreases from Na to Al as ionisation energy generally increases
  4. Reactivity with water decreases from Na to Al because shielding increases sharply
Correct answer: C
Nuclear charge increases with almost constant shielding, so ionisation energy generally rises. The outer electrons are then harder to remove and the metals react less readily with water from Na to Al.
