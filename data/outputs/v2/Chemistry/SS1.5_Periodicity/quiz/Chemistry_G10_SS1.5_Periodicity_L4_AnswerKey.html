Use after: Predict phase — Think-Pair-Share: predicting whether Group VII reactivity increases, stays the same or decreases down the group
Q1. In Group I, reactivity increases down the group because the outer electron is lost more easily from a larger atom. A learner predicts that reactivity in Group VII will also increase down the group, using the same reasoning. What is wrong with this prediction?
  1. Halogens have no outer electrons, so their reactivity cannot be predicted from electron shells
  2. Halogen atoms get smaller down the group, so they hold electrons more tightly and become more reactive
  3. Group VII elements do not change in reactivity because they all have seven outer electrons
  4. Halogens react by gaining an electron, and in a larger atom with more shielding the nucleus attracts an incoming electron less strongly, so reactivity is expected to decrease
Correct answer: D
Group I atoms lose electrons, so more shielding makes reaction easier. Halogens gain electrons, so more shielding and a larger radius make it harder to attract the incoming electron, and reactivity falls down the group.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: Physical Properties Carousel (observation table of colour and physical state)
Q2. Which row correctly describes chlorine, bromine and iodine at room temperature?
  1. Chlorine: grey-black solid; bromine: orange-brown liquid; iodine: yellow-green gas
  2. Chlorine: yellow-green gas; bromine: orange-brown liquid; iodine: grey-black solid
  3. Chlorine: orange-brown liquid; bromine: yellow-green gas; iodine: grey-black solid
  4. Chlorine: yellow-green gas; bromine: grey-black solid; iodine: orange-brown liquid
Correct answer: B
Going down Group VII the physical state changes from gas (Cl₂) to liquid (Br₂) to solid (I₂), and the colour gets darker. Iodine is a grey-black solid that gives off violet vapour.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B, Demo 3: Displacement 1 (Cl₂ displacing Br⁻) with cyclohexane
Q3. Chlorine water is added to potassium bromide solution, then 1 cm³ of cyclohexane is added and the tube is shaken. The cyclohexane layer turns orange-brown. What substance has formed in that layer?
  1. Bromine, Br₂, which was displaced from the bromide ions by chlorine
  2. Potassium chloride, KCl, which dissolves in cyclohexane
  3. Chlorine, Cl₂, which dissolved in the cyclohexane
  4. Iodine, I₂, which was released from the solution
Correct answer: A
Chlorine is more reactive than bromine, so it displaces bromine from potassium bromide: Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂. The orange-brown colour in the organic layer is the bromine.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B, after Demo 4: prediction 'Can I₂ displace Cl⁻?'
Q4. Chlorine can displace bromide ions, and bromine can displace iodide ions. A learner adds iodine solution to potassium chloride solution. What is expected?
  1. Iodine displaces chlorine, forming chlorine gas and potassium iodide
  2. Iodine displaces chlorine because iodine atoms are the largest
  3. A violent reaction, because iodine is the most reactive halogen
  4. No displacement, because iodine is less reactive than chlorine and cannot displace chloride ions
Correct answer: D
A halogen displaces only the halide of a less reactive halogen. Iodine is the weakest oxidising agent of the three, so it cannot displace chloride ions.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Constructing the Explanation using the Electron Affinity Scaffold Card
Q5. The energy released when each atom gains one electron is: chlorine 349 kJ/mol, bromine 325 kJ/mol, iodine 295 kJ/mol. What does this data show about reactivity?
  1. Iodine is the most reactive because it has the lowest electron affinity
  2. Chlorine is the most reactive because it releases the most energy when gaining an electron, so it attracts an incoming electron most strongly
  3. Bromine is the most reactive because its value is in the middle
  4. All three are equally reactive because they each gain one electron
Correct answer: B
Chlorine has the highest electron affinity (349 kJ/mol), so it gains an electron most easily and is the most reactive of the three. Electron affinity falls to iodine as the atoms get larger and more shielded.
Use after: Explain phase — Part C: Equations Practice (balanced equation for iron wool and chlorine)
Q6. Which equation for the reaction of iron with chlorine gas is correctly balanced?
  1. Fe + Cl₂ → FeCl₃
  2. 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃
  3. Fe + 3Cl₂ → FeCl₃
  4. 2Fe + 2Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃
Correct answer: B
Iron(III) chloride is FeCl₃. Two FeCl₃ need 2 Fe and 6 Cl atoms, which is 3 Cl₂ molecules, so 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃ balances.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB bridge statement: why the water officer uses CHLORINE (not bromine or iodine)
Q7. The water officer in Kisumu uses chlorine, not bromine or iodine, to treat Lake Victoria water. Which statement gives the best scientific reason from this lesson?
  1. Chlorine is the strongest oxidising agent of the three because it has the highest electron affinity, and it is also cheap and easy to supply
  2. Chlorine is the only halogen that reacts with metals
  3. Iodine is too reactive to be used safely in water
  4. Bromine has the smallest atoms of the three, so it is the weakest oxidising agent
Correct answer: A
Reactivity and oxidising power decrease from Cl to Br to I, so chlorine is the strongest oxidising agent of the three. Cost and easy supply are also practical reasons. Iodine is the least reactive, not the most.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model Building: adding the Group VII panel (reactivity arrow) to the class cumulative model poster
Q8. A learner adds a Group VII panel to the class model with a reactivity arrow for Cl, Br and I. Which arrow is correct?
  1. An arrow pointing down, because reactivity increases down every group
  2. An arrow pointing down, because iodine has the largest atom and attracts electrons best
  3. No arrow, because all halogens are equally reactive
  4. An arrow pointing up, because chlorine at the top is the most reactive and the strongest oxidising agent
Correct answer: D
In Group VII reactivity decreases down the group, so the arrow points upward towards chlorine. This is the reverse of Group I, where the reactivity arrow points downward.
