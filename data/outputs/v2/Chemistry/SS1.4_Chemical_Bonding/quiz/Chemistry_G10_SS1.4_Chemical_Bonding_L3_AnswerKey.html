Q1. Sodium (electron arrangement 2,8,1) reacts with chlorine (electron arrangement 2,8,7) to form sodium chloride, NaCl. Which statement correctly describes what happens to the electrons?
  1. Sodium and chlorine atoms share one pair of electrons equally
  2. Chlorine transfers one electron to sodium
  3. Both atoms lose their outer electrons and the electrons float freely between them
  4. Sodium transfers its one outer electron to chlorine
Correct answer: D
Sodium is a metal with one valence electron and chlorine is a non-metal needing one more electron. The outer electron moves completely from Na to Cl, giving each a full outer shell. Sharing is a common misconception for ionic bonding.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity A — Electron Transfer Card Model (labelling the ion charges Na⁺ and Cl⁻)
Q2. A sodium atom has the electron arrangement 2,8,1. It loses its one outer electron to form a sodium ion. Which row correctly gives the ion's symbol and electron arrangement?
  1. Na⁻ with 2,8
  2. Na⁺ with 2,8,1
  3. Na⁺ with 2,8
  4. Na⁻ with 2,8,2
Correct answer: C
Losing one negative electron leaves one more proton than electrons, so the charge is 1+. The remaining electrons are arranged 2,8, a full outer shell.
Use after: Observe phase — Activity B — Simulation, pause (ii): just after transfer (ions formed)
Q3. A chlorine atom has 17 protons and 17 electrons. After it receives one electron from a sodium atom, it becomes a chloride ion. Why does the chloride ion have a charge of 1−?
  1. It has lost a proton, so the positive charge is reduced
  2. It has the same number of protons and electrons, but the electrons are arranged differently
  3. It has 17 electrons and 18 protons, so the charge is negative
  4. It has 18 electrons but only 17 protons, so there is one extra negative charge
Correct answer: D
Gaining one electron gives 18 electrons (2,8,8) and still 17 protons. One extra negative charge means the overall charge is 1−.

Q4. Magnesium (Group 2) reacts with oxygen (Group 6) to form magnesium oxide, MgO, which is used to line furnaces. Which description of the dot-and-cross diagram for MgO is correct?
  1. Mg and O share 2 pairs of electrons and no ions form
  2. O transfers 2 electrons to Mg, forming Mg²⁻ and O²⁺
  3. Mg transfers 2 electrons to O, forming Mg²⁺ and O²⁻, each with 8 electrons in its outer shell
  4. Mg transfers 1 electron to O, forming Mg⁺ and O⁻
Correct answer: C
Mg has 2 valence electrons and O has 6. Mg gives away 2 electrons to O, so Mg becomes Mg²⁺ and O becomes O²⁻, and both have full outer shells of 8 electrons.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B — Conceptual Lattice Energy (Nairobi City Market analogy)
Q5. Which statement best describes lattice energy?
  1. The energy released when two atoms share a pair of electrons
  2. The energy needed to remove one electron from a sodium atom
  3. The energy needed to make chlorine atoms form a gas
  4. The energy released when gaseous ions pack together to form a giant ionic lattice
Correct answer: D
Lattice energy is the energy released when separated gaseous ions come together into a lattice. It explains why ionic compounds are stable and why a lot of energy is needed to pull the lattice apart.
Use after: Explain phase — Part C — Property Prediction Table (electrical conductivity as solid / as solution / when molten)
Q6. Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity, but molten sodium chloride and sodium chloride solution do. What is the reason?
  1. Sodium chloride is made of molecules that only separate when heated or dissolved
  2. Free electrons move through solid sodium chloride but are trapped when it melts
  3. Melting or dissolving adds extra electrons to the sodium chloride
  4. In the solid, the ions are held in fixed positions, but when molten or dissolved the ions are free to move and carry charge
Correct answer: D
Electrical conduction in ionic compounds needs charged particles that can move. In the solid lattice the ions cannot move, but they can when the compound is molten or dissolved in water.
Use after: Model Building phase — Bonding Concept Model — Ionic Bonding panel (maize and beans 2-D lattice cross-section)
Q7. Learners build a model of part of a sodium chloride lattice using maize kernels for Na⁺ ions and beans for Cl⁻ ions. They place the maize and beans alternately in a checkerboard pattern. Why is this alternating arrangement correct?
  1. Each ion is attracted to ions of the same charge, so like ions must touch
  2. Each ion is attracted to neighbouring ions of opposite charge, which holds the lattice together strongly in all directions
  3. The ions alternate only to make the lattice look neat; it has no effect on the forces between them
  4. Alternating ions make pairs of molecules that are joined by shared electrons
Correct answer: B
In a giant ionic lattice each Na⁺ is surrounded by Cl⁻ ions and each Cl⁻ by Na⁺ ions. Strong electrostatic attraction between opposite charges acts in all directions, which explains the high melting point.
