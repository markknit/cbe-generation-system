Q1. Aluminium foil is used to wrap mandazi and can be pressed into very thin sheets without breaking. Which statement correctly gives the bond type in aluminium and explains why it can be shaped like this?
  1. Covalent bonding: shared electron pairs between two atoms form small molecules that bend easily
  2. Metallic bonding: layers of aluminium atoms can slide over each other while the delocalised electrons keep holding them together
  3. Weak forces between separate aluminium molecules let the foil be shaped easily
  4. Ionic bonding: strong attraction between positive and negative ions makes the foil flexible
Correct answer: B
Aluminium is a metal held by metallic bonding: a lattice of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons. The layers can slide without the structure breaking, so the metal is malleable.

Q2. A graphite rod from a torch (dry-cell) battery carries electric current. Which feature of graphite's structure explains why it conducts electricity?
  1. Each carbon atom is held by four covalent bonds, so electrons move along the bonds
  2. Each carbon atom has one delocalised electron that is free to move between the layers
  3. Graphite is made of small molecules that carry charge from one to the next
  4. Graphite contains positive and negative ions that are free to move
Correct answer: B
In graphite each carbon atom forms three bonds in a flat hexagonal layer. The fourth electron is delocalised and free to move, so graphite conducts. The covalent bonds themselves do not carry current.

Q3. Silicon dioxide (SiO₂) is melted at a very high temperature to make glass for windows. Which statement best explains why SiO₂ has such a very high melting point?
  1. Its positive and negative ions are attracted by weak forces
  2. It has a giant covalent structure, so a huge number of strong covalent bonds must be broken to melt it
  3. It is made of small molecules held by weak forces that are easy to separate
  4. It has delocalised electrons that make the atoms move apart easily
Correct answer: B
SiO₂ is a giant covalent structure. Every atom is joined to others by strong covalent bonds throughout the whole structure, so a great deal of energy is needed to break them.

Q4. Sodium chloride (NaCl) is rubbed into nyama choma and dissolves in water. It has a high melting point, and solid NaCl does not conduct electricity. Which explanation is correct?
  1. NaCl is an ionic lattice of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions held by strong attractions; in the solid the ions cannot move, so it does not conduct
  2. NaCl is a giant covalent structure with delocalised electrons that are fixed in place
  3. NaCl is a metallic lattice, but its electrons are held too tightly to move
  4. NaCl is made of small covalent molecules, so it melts easily but conducts poorly
Correct answer: A
NaCl is an ionic compound with a lattice of oppositely charged ions. Strong attractions give a high melting point. In the solid the ions are fixed in position, so there are no mobile charges to carry current.

Q5. A grinding wheel in a Kisumu workshop is made of silicon carbide (SiC). It is extremely hard, has a very high melting point and conducts electricity only very poorly. What is the most likely bond type and structure of SiC?
  1. Giant covalent structure, similar to diamond
  2. Simple molecular structure with weak forces between molecules
  3. Ionic lattice of positive and negative ions
  4. Metallic lattice with a sea of delocalised electrons
Correct answer: A
Extreme hardness, a very high melting point and poor conductivity match a giant covalent structure like diamond. An ionic or metallic structure would not fit this pattern, and a simple molecular structure would be soft with a low melting point.

Q6. Diamond and graphite are both made only of carbon. Why does diamond NOT conduct electricity at all?
  1. All four outer electrons of each carbon atom are used in covalent bonds, so no electrons are free to move
  2. Diamond has no electrons because it is transparent
  3. Diamond contains ions that are locked in a lattice
  4. Diamond is too hard for electrons to pass through
Correct answer: A
In diamond each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds in a tetrahedral network. All its outer electrons are used in bonding, so there are no delocalised electrons or ions to carry charge.

Q7. A student says: 'Diamond and graphite behave differently because graphite contains more protons and neutrons than diamond.' Which response is correct?
  1. The student is correct, because graphite has extra electrons that make it conduct
  2. The student is wrong, because both contain only carbon atoms; the difference comes from how the carbon atoms are bonded and arranged
  3. The student is correct, because the number of protons decides how hard a solid is
  4. The student is wrong, because diamond is made of a different element from graphite
Correct answer: B
Both substances are 100% carbon with identical atoms. Diamond has four bonds per atom in a 3-D tetrahedral network, while graphite has three bonds per atom in flat hexagonal layers with one delocalised electron. This is the carbon paradox.

Q8. A jeweller's tile cutter is tipped with diamond. In a model of diamond, an arrow is drawn from a structural feature to this use. Which structural feature should the arrow start from to explain why diamond can cut tiles?
  1. Delocalised electrons that are free to move
  2. Positive and negative ions held in a lattice
  3. Each carbon atom bonded to four others by strong covalent bonds in a 3-D tetrahedral network
  4. Layers of atoms that slide over each other easily
Correct answer: C
Diamond's hardness comes from every carbon atom being strongly bonded to four others in a rigid 3-D network. This makes it hard enough to scratch the ceramic tile.

Q9. A pencil leaves a grey mark on exam paper, but diamond is used to cut tiles. Which statement best explains the pencil's soft, marking graphite?
  1. Graphite has fewer carbon atoms than diamond
  2. Graphite has four strong bonds per carbon atom in a 3-D network
  3. Graphite is an ionic solid with weak attractions between ions
  4. Graphite is made of flat hexagonal layers that can slide off one another and leave a mark
Correct answer: D
Graphite has a layered structure of flat hexagonal sheets. The layers slide over each other and rub off onto paper, so graphite is soft and useful for writing. Diamond's rigid 3-D network does not allow this.
