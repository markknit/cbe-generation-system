Q1. A neutral sodium atom has 11 electrons. Which is its correct electron arrangement in s and p notation?
  1. 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s1
  2. 1s2 2s2 2p6 3p1
  3. 1s2 2s1 2p6 3s2
  4. 1s2 2s2 2p7
Correct answer: A
Fill in order: 1s holds 2, 2s holds 2, 2p holds 6, which makes 10. The 11th electron goes into 3s, giving 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s1.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: checking the Ne, Ar, K and Ca rows of the class data table
Q2. A potassium atom has 19 electrons. Which is its correct energy-level arrangement?
  1. 2,9,8
  2. 2,8,9
  3. 2,8,1,8
  4. 2,8,8,1
Correct answer: D
Level 1 holds 2 and level 2 holds 8. Level 3 is filled to 8 in this lesson, and the 19th electron goes into 4s. This gives 2,8,8,1.

Q3. Lithium (relative atomic mass 7), sodium (23) and potassium (39) are in the same group of the periodic table. What do their atoms have in common?
  1. They have the same mass
  2. They have the same number of occupied energy levels
  3. They have the same number of outer electrons (1)
  4. They have the same total number of electrons
Correct answer: C
Li is 2,1, Na is 2,8,1 and K is 2,8,8,1, so each has 1 outer electron. Their masses, total electrons and number of occupied levels all differ.

Q4. An atom has the electron arrangement 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p4. Which row correctly gives its group and period?
  1. Group 6, Period 3
  2. Group 16, Period 4
  3. Group 4, Period 6
  4. Group 16, Period 3
Correct answer: D
There are 6 outer electrons (3s2 3p4), so group = 6 + 10 = 16. Electrons occupy 3 energy levels, so it is in Period 3. The atom is sulfur.

Q5. A helium atom has 2 outer electrons (1s2), just like magnesium. Yet helium is placed in group 18, not group 2. What is the best reason?
  1. Helium has 18 occupied energy levels
  2. Its only energy level, 1s, is full, so it is placed with the other elements that have full outer levels, such as neon and argon
  3. Helium has 18 electrons in total
  4. Helium is a metal like magnesium, so it is placed far to the right
Correct answer: B
Helium's 1s level holds a maximum of 2 electrons, so it is full. This is why helium is placed in group 18, the exception to the 'group = outer electrons' pattern for groups 1 and 2.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Task 3, counting occupied energy levels and comparing with the period number
Q6. A calcium atom has 20 electrons arranged as 2,8,8,2. In which period of the periodic table is calcium?
  1. Period 3, because the 3rd level is the last full level
  2. Period 4, because its electrons occupy 4 energy levels
  3. Period 2, because it has 2 outer electrons
  4. Period 20, because it has 20 electrons
Correct answer: B
Period number equals the number of occupied energy levels. The arrangement 2,8,8,2 has electrons in 4 levels, so calcium is in Period 4. Outer electrons decide the group, not the period.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the group and period rules to the 'Electron Arrangement Model' poster and the exit slip on chlorine
Q7. Chlorine is used to disinfect drinking water in Nairobi and Mombasa. A chlorine atom has the electron arrangement 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p5. What is the group number of chlorine?
  1. 17
  2. 7
  3. 3
  4. 5
Correct answer: A
The outer level (3s2 3p5) has 2 + 5 = 7 electrons. For groups 13 to 18, group = outer electrons + 10, so 7 + 10 = 17.
