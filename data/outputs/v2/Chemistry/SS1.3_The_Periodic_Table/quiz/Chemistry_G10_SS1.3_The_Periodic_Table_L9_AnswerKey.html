Q1. A small piece of sodium is dropped into water. The gas given off burns with a pop, and red litmus paper turns blue in the remaining solution. Which pair of products fits this evidence?
  1. Sodium hydroxide and oxygen
  2. Sodium hydroxide and hydrogen
  3. Sodium chloride and hydrogen
  4. Sodium oxide and oxygen
Correct answer: B
A gas that burns with a pop is hydrogen, and red litmus turning blue shows an alkaline solution, which is sodium hydroxide (NaOH). Oxygen and chloride are not supported by the evidence.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: atom-counting table for Na + H2O -> NaOH + H2
Q2. Look at the unbalanced equation Na + H2O -> NaOH + H2. How many hydrogen atoms are on the product (right-hand) side?
  1. 3
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 1
Correct answer: A
NaOH has 1 hydrogen atom and H2 has 2 hydrogen atoms, so 1 + 2 = 3. The left side has only 2 hydrogen atoms, so the equation is not balanced.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: balancing rule - change coefficients, never subscripts
Q3. A learner tries to balance Na + H2O -> NaOH + H2 by changing NaOH to NaOH2. Why is this not allowed?
  1. The subscript should be changed on H2O instead of NaOH
  2. Hydrogen atoms cannot be placed in a hydroxide
  3. Subscripts must always be even numbers
  4. Changing a subscript makes a different substance; only the big numbers in front (coefficients) may be changed
Correct answer: D
Subscripts are part of a substance's formula, so changing them creates a different substance. Balancing is done only by changing coefficients.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: groups copy the pattern to write the K and Li equations
Q4. Which is the correctly balanced equation for potassium reacting with water?
  1. 2K + 2H2O -> 2KOH + H2
  2. K + H2O -> KOH + H2
  3. K + 2H2O -> 2KOH + H2
  4. 2K + H2O -> 2KOH + H2
Correct answer: A
2K + 2H2O -> 2KOH + H2 has 2 K, 4 H and 2 O on each side. The other equations have unequal numbers of atoms on the two sides.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: worksheet balancing Ca + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + H2
Q5. Calcium reacts with hydrochloric acid: Ca + __HCl -> CaCl2 + H2. What number must go in the blank to balance the equation?
  1. 1
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 2
Correct answer: D
CaCl2 contains 2 chlorine atoms, so 2 HCl are needed. This also gives 2 hydrogen atoms on the left to match the H2 on the right.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: new panel 'Equations as models of reactions' and exit prompt on why Li, Na and K have the same pattern
Q6. Lithium (mass 7), sodium (mass 23) and potassium (mass 39) have very different masses, but they react with water by the same equation pattern. What is the best reason?
  1. They all have the same number of energy levels
  2. They have nearly the same mass
  3. They all have the same number of protons
  4. Each has one outer electron and forms a 1+ ion
Correct answer: D
Li, Na and K each have one outer electron, lose it, and form a 1+ ion that combines with OH- in a 1:1 ratio. The pattern depends on outer electrons, not on mass.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: writing state symbols on the balanced sodium equation
Q7. In the equation 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) -> 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g), what does the symbol (aq) after NaOH tell us?
  1. NaOH is a pure liquid
  2. NaOH is dissolved in water
  3. NaOH is a solid
  4. NaOH is a gas given off
Correct answer: B
The symbol (aq) means aqueous, which means dissolved in water. The symbols (s), (l) and (g) mean solid, liquid and gas.
