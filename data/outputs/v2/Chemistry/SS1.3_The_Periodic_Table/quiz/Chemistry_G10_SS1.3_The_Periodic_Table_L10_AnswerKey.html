Q1. Strontium (Sr, atomic number 38) has the electron arrangement 2,8,18,8,2. In which group and period of the periodic table does it belong?
  1. Group 38, period 2
  2. Group 5, period 2
  3. Group 2, period 5
  4. Group 8, period 5
Correct answer: C
The outer level has 2 electrons, so Sr is in group 2. Five energy levels are occupied (2,8,18,8,2 has five numbers), so it is in period 5.
Use after: Observe phase — Pattern Finder task 3: use Ca and Mg to forecast Sr's ion
Q2. Calcium (2,8,8,2) and magnesium (2,8,2) both form 2+ ions. Strontium has the electron arrangement 2,8,18,8,2. Which ion will strontium form?
  1. Sr2+, by losing its 2 outer electrons
  2. Sr2-, by gaining 2 electrons
  3. Sr8+, by losing all 8 electrons in its fourth level
  4. Sr+, by losing 1 electron
Correct answer: A
Sr has 2 outer electrons, like Mg and Ca. It loses these 2 electrons to reach a stable noble gas arrangement, forming Sr2+.
Use after: Observe phase — Placing the Ar (A_r 40) and K (A_r 39) cards in order
Q3. Argon has a relative atomic mass of 40 and atomic number 18. Potassium has a relative atomic mass of 39 and atomic number 19. Argon is placed before potassium in the periodic table. What decides this order?
  1. Relative atomic mass, as used by Mendeleev in 1869
  2. Atomic number, as shown by Moseley in 1913
  3. The number of reactions the element takes part in
  4. The number of energy levels only
Correct answer: B
Modern elements are ordered by increasing atomic number (Moseley, 1913). Argon (18) comes before potassium (19) even though its mass is higher.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A, Box 2: SAME COLUMN, DIFFERENT MASS (Li, Na, K)
Q4. Lithium, sodium and potassium have relative atomic masses of 7, 23 and 39, yet all three react vigorously with water. What is the best explanation?
  1. They each have 1 electron in the outer energy level
  2. They all have the same number of occupied energy levels
  3. They have almost the same mass
  4. They all have the same number of protons
Correct answer: A
Li (2,1), Na (2,8,1) and K (2,8,8,1) each have 1 outer electron, so they are in group 1 and react alike. Mass and the number of levels differ.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A, Box 3: STABILITY AND IONS (why Ne and Ar do not react)
Q5. Neon (2,8) and argon (2,8,8) do not react with other substances. What is the reason?
  1. They have a full outer energy level, which is a stable arrangement
  2. They have no electrons in their outer level
  3. They have a very high relative atomic mass
  4. They have only one outer electron
Correct answer: A
Neon and argon have full outer energy levels (8 electrons), so they have no tendency to lose or gain electrons and are unreactive.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A, Box 3: why chlorine gains 1 electron to form Cl-
Q6. A chlorine atom has the electron arrangement 2,8,7. It forms the chloride ion, Cl-, used in compounds such as NaCl. What happens, and why?
  1. It loses 1 electron to become 2,8,6, the same as sulphur
  2. It loses 7 electrons to become 2,8, the same as neon
  3. It gains 1 electron to become 2,8,8, the same as argon
  4. It gains 1 electron to become 2,8,8, the same as neon
Correct answer: C
Chlorine gains 1 electron, giving 2,8,8, the same arrangement as argon. Gaining one electron gives a 1- charge, so the ion is Cl-.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A, Box 4: PREDICTION (formulae SrCl2 and SrO)
Q7. Strontium forms the ion Sr2+ and chlorine forms the ion Cl-. What is the formula of strontium chloride?
  1. SrCl2
  2. SrCl3
  3. Sr2Cl
  4. SrCl
Correct answer: A
One Sr2+ (charge +2) needs two Cl- (charge -1 each) to balance, so the formula is SrCl2, like CaCl2.
Use after: Model Building phase — Exit ticket: predict group, ion and formula with chlorine for barium
Q8. Barium (Z56) has 2 outer electrons. Using the same reasoning as for calcium and strontium, which row correctly gives its group, ion and formula with chlorine?
  1. Group 56, Ba2+, Ba2Cl
  2. Group 2, Ba2-, BaCl2
  3. Group 1, Ba+, BaCl
  4. Group 2, Ba2+, BaCl2
Correct answer: D
Two outer electrons put barium in group 2. It loses 2 electrons to form Ba2+, and one Ba2+ combines with two Cl- to give BaCl2.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB review: sorting questions into ANSWERED, PARTLY ANSWERED and NEW/OPEN
Q9. At the end of the periodic table unit, learners sort their Driving Question Board questions. Which question is still OPEN and will be studied in Sub-Strand 1.4 Chemical Bonding?
  1. How do chlorine atoms stay joined together as Cl2?
  2. Why do Li, Na and K react in a similar way?
  3. Why does argon come before potassium in the table?
  4. What ion does strontium form?
Correct answer: A
The first three questions are answered using atomic number, outer electrons and ion formation. How atoms share electrons and stay joined is the focus of chemical bonding.
