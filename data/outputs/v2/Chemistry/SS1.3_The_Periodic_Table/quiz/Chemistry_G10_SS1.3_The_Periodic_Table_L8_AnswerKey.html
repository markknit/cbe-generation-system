Q1. Aluminium forms Al3+ ions and oxygen forms O2- ions. What is the formula of aluminium oxide, and why?
  1. Al3O2, because the charge on aluminium is 3 and the charge on oxygen is 2
  2. AlO, because one aluminium atom joins one oxygen atom
  3. Al2O2, because both ions must have the same number of atoms
  4. Al2O3, because 2 x (+3) = +6 balances 3 x (-2) = -6
Correct answer: D
A compound is neutral, so total positive charge must equal total negative charge. Two Al3+ give +6 and three O2- give -6, so the formula is Al2O3.

Q2. Calcium forms Ca2+ ions and the nitrate radical is NO3-. Which is the correct formula of calcium nitrate, the compound used in some fertilizers?
  1. Ca(NO3)2
  2. CaNO6
  3. CaNO3
  4. Ca2NO3
Correct answer: A
One Ca2+ (+2) needs two NO3- (-2) to balance. Brackets show that the whole radical is doubled, giving Ca(NO3)2.
Use after: Observe phase — Station 3: building Fe2O3, FeCl2 and CuO using oxidation numbers
Q3. Iron can form Fe2+ and Fe3+ ions. Iron(II) chloride contains Fe2+ and Cl- ions. What is its formula?
  1. FeCl2
  2. FeCl
  3. Fe2Cl3
  4. Fe2Cl
Correct answer: A
One Fe2+ (+2) needs two Cl- (-2) to make the total charge zero, so the formula is FeCl2.
Use after: Explain phase — Criss-cross method practice: testing it on Mg2+ and O2-
Q4. A learner uses the criss-cross method for Mg2+ and O2- and gets Mg2O2. What should the learner do next?
  1. Leave it as Mg2O2, because the criss-cross method always gives the final formula
  2. Simplify to MgO, because one Mg2+ already balances one O2-
  3. Change it to Mg2O, because oxygen has the smaller number
  4. Change it to MgO2, because oxygen is written with the larger number
Correct answer: B
Mg2O2 has the ratio 2:2, which simplifies to 1:1. One Mg2+ (+2) balances one O2- (-2), so the formula is MgO.
Use after: Explain phase — Challenge: predict the formula of calcium phosphate, then check with the criss-cross method
Q5. Calcium phosphate is made from Ca2+ ions and PO4 3- radicals. Which is its correct formula?
  1. CaPO4
  2. Ca2(PO4)3
  3. Ca3PO8
  4. Ca3(PO4)2
Correct answer: D
Criss-crossing the charges gives 3 Ca2+ and 2 PO4 3-. The total charge is 3 x (+2) = +6 and 2 x (-3) = -6. The radical needs brackets, so the formula is Ca3(PO4)2.
Use after: Explain phase — Linking back to the phenomenon: why Li, Na and K form similar compounds
Q6. Lithium, sodium and potassium have very different masses, yet they form similar compounds: LiCl, NaCl and KCl. What is the best explanation?
  1. They all have the same number of electron energy levels
  2. Each has 1 outer electron, so each forms a 1+ ion
  3. They all have the same number of protons in the nucleus
  4. They have nearly the same mass, so they behave alike
Correct answer: B
Li, Na and K each have one electron in the outer energy level. Each loses it to form a 1+ ion, which combines with Cl- in a 1:1 ratio.
Use after: Model Building phase — Class Model revision: adding the arrow 'ion charge -> neutral compound formula (charges balance)'
Q7. Which chain best shows how electron arrangement helps chemists predict the formula of a compound?
  1. Formula of the compound, then ion charge, then electron arrangement
  2. Electron arrangement, then ion charge, then formula in which the total charges balance
  3. Electron arrangement, then the number of atoms in the compound, then ion charge
  4. Mass of the element, then ion charge, then formula of the compound
Correct answer: B
Electron arrangement decides the charge of an ion. Because a compound is neutral, the ion charges decide the ratio of ions in the formula.
