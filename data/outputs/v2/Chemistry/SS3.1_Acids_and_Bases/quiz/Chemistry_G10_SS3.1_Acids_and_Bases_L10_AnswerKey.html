Q1. A Kisumu kitchen has four products with these pH values: lemon juice 2.0, vinegar 2.8, baking soda solution 8.3 and bar soap solution 10.0. Which product has the most OH- (aq) ions and is the most alkaline?
  1. Bar soap solution, because it has the highest pH
  2. Lemon juice, because it is the sourest
  3. Baking soda solution, because it is used in mandazi
  4. Vinegar, because it is used for pickling
Correct answer: A
The higher the pH above 7, the more alkaline the solution and the more OH- (aq) it has. Soap at pH 10.0 is the highest of the four.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Evidence Gallery Walk, Station 1 (pH graph data and the 10-fold H+ change per pH unit)
Q2. Each drop of one pH unit in a solution means the H+ (aq) concentration becomes 10 times greater. How many times more H+ (aq) ions does lemon juice at pH 2.0 have than a solution at pH 4.0?
  1. 100 times
  2. 2 times
  3. 1000 times
  4. 20 times
Correct answer: A
The pH difference is 4.0 - 2.0 = 2 units, so the H+ concentration differs by 10^2 = 100 times. Many learners wrongly subtract or multiply the pH values instead of using powers of 10.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: Evidence Gallery Walk, Station 4 (reactions: vinegar + baking soda and Mg + HCl)
Q3. Vinegar is added to baking soda and the mixture fizzes. The gas given off turns limewater milky. Which gas is it?
  1. Water vapour, because the mixture is wet
  2. Hydrogen, because it gives a squeaky pop
  3. Oxygen, because it relights a glowing splint
  4. Carbon dioxide, because acids react with carbonates to release it
Correct answer: D
Acids react with carbonates and hydrogen carbonates to give a salt, water and carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky, while hydrogen (from a metal and acid) gives a squeaky pop.

Q4. A learner says, 'Hydrochloric acid at pH 1 is a strong acid, so it must be the same as a concentrated acid.' Which statement best corrects this idea?
  1. Strength and concentration mean the same thing, so the learner is correct
  2. Strength tells how fully an acid forms H+ (aq) ions in water, while concentration tells how much acid is dissolved in a given volume
  3. A strong acid always has a pH above 7
  4. Strength tells how much acid is dissolved in a litre, while concentration tells how fully it forms ions
Correct answer: B
Strength is about how completely an acid ionises to release H+ (aq). Concentration is the amount of acid per litre, so a strong acid is not automatically a concentrated one.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning Final Explanation on why a patient with hyperacidity is given magnesium hydroxide
Q5. A patient in Nairobi has hyperacidity after eating spicy nyama choma and is given magnesium hydroxide. Why does this help?
  1. It only hides the pain and does not change the acid in the stomach
  2. It adds more acid so the stomach digests the food faster
  3. The base neutralises the excess hydrochloric acid, forming a salt and water and raising the stomach pH
  4. It turns the stomach acid into a stronger acid
Correct answer: C
Magnesium hydroxide is a base. It reacts with excess HCl (acid + base gives salt + water), so the stomach pH rises from about 1.5 to 2 up to about 3.5 to 5.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: addressing the limits of the claim (strength and safety, why not sodium hydroxide at pH 13)
Q6. Sodium hydroxide (pH 13) is a base that could neutralise stomach acid. Why is magnesium hydroxide used in antacids instead?
  1. Sodium hydroxide cannot react with acids
  2. Sodium hydroxide is a strong base that is unsafe to swallow, while magnesium hydroxide is a mild base that dissolves only slightly
  3. Sodium hydroxide is a weak base, so it does not work quickly enough
  4. Magnesium hydroxide is an acid, so it is safe
Correct answer: B
Sodium hydroxide at pH 13 is strongly alkaline and harmful if swallowed. Magnesium hydroxide dissolves only slightly, so it is a mild base that can safely neutralise excess acid.

Q7. Wanjiru asked, 'Why is soap slippery?' Which answer is best supported by the evidence from the unit?
  1. Soap solution is slippery because it is a strong acid
  2. Soap solution is alkaline (pH 10.0) and contains OH- (aq) ions, which give the slippery feel
  3. Soap solution is neutral at pH 7.0, like water
  4. Soap solution contains many H+ (aq) ions, as lemon juice does
Correct answer: B
Soap at pH 10.0 is alkaline, so it has more OH- (aq) than H+ (aq). Sourness is linked to H+ (aq) in acids, and the slippery feel to alkalis.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: applying the final model to lime for Kericho soil (Station 6 and the final particle model)
Q8. In Kericho, Mr Kamau adds lime to soil with pH about 4.5 and raises it to about 6.0 for growing maize. Which explanation matches the particle model?
  1. Lime is a base that neutralises H+ (aq) ions in the soil, so H+ + OH- forms H2O and the pH rises
  2. Lime changes the soil into a strong acid
  3. Lime removes all ions so the soil becomes pH 14
  4. Lime adds more H+ (aq) ions to the soil
Correct answer: A
Lime is a base. It reacts with H+ (aq) in acidic soil (H+ + OH- gives H2O), reducing acidity so the pH rises towards neutral, which suits maize.
