Q1. A patient in Nairobi has too much acid in the stomach (hydrochloric acid, pH about 1.5 to 2). The pharmacist gives an antacid containing magnesium hydroxide, which is a base. What will happen to the pH of the stomach contents?
  1. The pH rises because the base removes some of the H+ ions
  2. The pH falls because the base adds more particles to the stomach
  3. The pH stays the same because the base only mixes with the acid
  4. The pH jumps straight to 14 because a base is added
Correct answer: A
The base supplies OH- ions that react with H+ ions, so there are fewer H+ ions and the pH rises, but only to about 3.5 to 5 and not to 14.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Step 1: adding magnesium hydroxide suspension to hydrochloric acid with universal indicator
Q2. Dilute hydrochloric acid contains universal indicator and looks red (pH about 1). Magnesium hydroxide is added a little at a time with stirring. Which colour sequence will you see?
  1. Red, purple, blue, green
  2. Red, orange, yellow, green
  3. Green, yellow, orange, red
  4. Red stays red, because the base only dilutes the acid
Correct answer: B
As the base removes H+ ions the pH rises from about 1 towards 7, so universal indicator changes from red through orange and yellow to green.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe Step 3 (control): adding the same volume of water to the acid
Q3. In the neutralisation investigation, learners add the same volume of water (instead of a base) to a beaker of the acid with universal indicator. What is the reason for doing this?
  1. To make the acid stronger so that the base reacts faster
  2. To show that water is also a base
  3. To wash the indicator out of the beaker
  4. To show that the colour change is caused by the base and not just by dilution
Correct answer: D
The water control changes the colour very little, so the large pH rise with the base must be due to the base reacting with the acid and not only to dilution.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: teacher demonstration of copper(II) oxide with warm dilute sulphuric (VI) acid
Q4. A teacher warms dilute sulphuric (VI) acid and adds black copper(II) oxide powder. The black solid dissolves and the solution turns blue. Which word equation correctly describes the reaction?
  1. Copper(II) oxide + sulphuric (VI) acid gives copper + sulphuric (VI) oxide
  2. Copper(II) oxide + sulphuric (VI) acid gives copper(II) sulphate + hydrogen
  3. Copper(II) oxide + sulphuric (VI) acid gives copper(II) sulphate + carbon dioxide
  4. Copper(II) oxide + sulphuric (VI) acid gives copper(II) sulphate + water
Correct answer: D
A metal oxide reacts with an acid like a base: acid + metal oxide gives salt + water. The blue colour shows copper(II) sulphate has formed.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: particle diagrams and the ion equation H+ (aq) + OH- (aq) gives H2O (l)
Q5. Which equation shows what happens to the particles when an acid is neutralised by a base such as magnesium hydroxide?
  1. OH- (aq) + OH- (aq) gives H2O (l)
  2. H+ (aq) + H+ (aq) gives H2 (g)
  3. H+ (aq) + OH- (aq) gives nothing, because the two cancel out and disappear
  4. H+ (aq) + OH- (aq) gives H2O (l)
Correct answer: D
In neutralisation, H+ ions from the acid and OH- ions from the base join to form water molecules. They do not simply cancel out or vanish.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: writing the word equation for hydrochloric acid + magnesium hydroxide
Q6. Which word equation correctly shows the reaction between stomach acid (hydrochloric acid) and the antacid magnesium hydroxide?
  1. Hydrochloric acid + magnesium hydroxide gives magnesium chloride + hydrogen
  2. Hydrochloric acid + magnesium hydroxide gives magnesium oxide + chlorine
  3. Hydrochloric acid + magnesium hydroxide gives magnesium chloride + water
  4. Hydrochloric acid + magnesium hydroxide gives magnesium + water
Correct answer: C
Acid + base gives salt + water. The salt from hydrochloric acid and magnesium hydroxide is magnesium chloride.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: why the pharmacist does not give sodium hydroxide as an antacid
Q7. Why does a pharmacist not give a patient with hyperacidity a strong base such as sodium hydroxide (pH 13) as an antacid?
  1. Sodium hydroxide cannot react with hydrochloric acid
  2. Sodium hydroxide would make the stomach more acidic
  3. Sodium hydroxide is harmful, like soap but much stronger, so it is not safe to swallow
  4. Sodium hydroxide has no OH- ions
Correct answer: C
Sodium hydroxide would neutralise the acid, but it is a very alkaline and harmful substance that is unsafe to swallow. Antacids use mild bases such as magnesium hydroxide.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: adding the stomach before and after antacid panel to the particle model
Q8. A learner revises their particle model of the stomach before and after taking a magnesium hydroxide antacid. Which model panel is correct?
  1. After the antacid there are fewer H+ ions, H+ and OH- join to form H2O, and the pH is about 3.5 to 5
  2. After the antacid there are fewer H+ ions, and the pH falls to about 1
  3. After the antacid there are more H+ ions, and the pH is about 1.5 to 2
  4. After the antacid the H+ ions have simply disappeared, and no water forms
Correct answer: A
The antacid supplies OH- ions that join with H+ ions to form water, so the H+ ions decrease and the stomach pH rises from about 1.5 to 2 to about 3.5 to 5.
