Q1. Pure water has pH 7.0 and H⁺ concentration of 1 x 10⁻⁷ mol/L. Which statement about the H⁺ ions in pure water is correct?
  1. It has a tiny amount of H⁺, far less than lemon juice
  2. It has more H⁺ than vinegar
  3. It has the same amount of H⁺ as lemon juice
  4. It has no H⁺ ions at all
Correct answer: A
1 x 10⁻⁷ mol/L is very small but not zero, and it is much less than lemon juice (1 x 10⁻² mol/L) or vinegar (about 1.6 x 10⁻³ mol/L).
Use after: Observe phase — Part A (data): 'How many times more H+ does lemon juice have than vinegar?'
Q2. Lemon juice has pH 2.0 and H⁺ = 1 x 10⁻² mol/L. Vinegar has pH 2.8 and H⁺ about 1.6 x 10⁻³ mol/L. About how many times more H⁺ does lemon juice have than vinegar?
  1. About 0.8 times
  2. About 10 times
  3. About 6 times
  4. About 60 times
Correct answer: C
Divide the concentrations: (1 x 10⁻²) ÷ (1.6 x 10⁻³) = 6.25, which is about 6 times. The pH difference of 0.8 does not mean 0.8 times more H⁺, because each pH unit is a 10-fold change.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B (simulation or paper model): acid molecules dissolving in water and releasing H+ (aq) ions
Q3. In a particle simulation, acid molecules from lemon juice dissolve in water. What do learners see happen?
  1. The acid molecules stay whole and release nothing
  2. The acid molecules turn into sugar particles
  3. The acid molecules remove all the water particles
  4. The acid molecules release H⁺ (aq) ions into the water
Correct answer: D
An acid dissolving in water releases H⁺ (aq) ions. This is the particle-level change that explains the properties of acids.
Use after: Explain phase — Pairs' CER explanation and the class definition recorded on the board
Q4. Which statement is the Arrhenius definition of an acid?
  1. A substance that is harmful if swallowed
  2. A substance that contains no water
  3. A substance that produces H⁺ (aq) ions when dissolved in water
  4. A substance that tastes sour in the laboratory
Correct answer: C
The Arrhenius definition says an acid produces H⁺ (aq) ions in water. Being harmful or tasting sour is not the definition, and we never taste chemicals in the laboratory.
Use after: Explain phase — Claim-evidence-reasoning: 'Why is lemon juice sour, and why does it have a lower pH than vinegar?'
Q5. Lemon juice (pH 2.0) and vinegar (pH 2.8) are both acids. Why does lemon juice have the lower pH?
  1. It contains no water, but vinegar does
  2. It has fewer H⁺ ions than vinegar
  3. It has a higher concentration of H⁺ ions than vinegar
  4. It contains more sugar than vinegar
Correct answer: C
A lower pH means a higher H⁺ concentration. Lemon juice has about 6 times more H⁺ (1 x 10⁻² mol/L) than vinegar (about 1.6 x 10⁻³ mol/L).
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: linking notes to the hyperacidity question
Q6. Stomach acid is hydrochloric acid. Which particle does it release when it dissolves in water, making it an acid?
  1. No particles, because it is a liquid
  2. Only water particles
  3. H⁺ (aq) ions
  4. Sugar particles
Correct answer: C
Hydrochloric acid is an acid, so by the Arrhenius definition it releases H⁺ (aq) ions in water, just like the acids in lemon juice and vinegar.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model page: three boxes for lemon juice, vinegar and water showing H+ (aq) ions
Q7. In a Model drawing, lemon juice (H⁺ = 1 x 10⁻² mol/L) is shown with 6 H⁺ dots. Which drawing for vinegar (about 1.6 x 10⁻³ mol/L) and water (1 x 10⁻⁷ mol/L) is best?
  1. Vinegar 6 dots, water 0 dots
  2. Vinegar 1 dot, water almost no dots
  3. Vinegar 1 dot, water exactly 0 dots
  4. Vinegar 12 dots, water 6 dots
Correct answer: B
Lemon juice has about 6 times more H⁺ than vinegar, so 6 dots against 1 dot. Water has a tiny amount of H⁺, so it is drawn with almost none, not exactly zero.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: linking H+ ions to the litmus and indicator colours from Lessons 2-3
Q8. Lemon juice turns blue litmus paper red. Which explanation uses the idea from this lesson?
  1. Lemon juice contains H⁺ (aq) ions released by its acid in water
  2. Lemon juice is red in colour
  3. Lemon juice has no water particles
  4. Lemon juice contains sugar particles
Correct answer: A
Acids release H⁺ (aq) ions in water. These ions explain the low pH, the sour taste and the colour change of indicators such as litmus.
