Q1. Niels Bohr's model of the atom says something important about where electrons can be found. Which statement matches Bohr's key idea?
  1. Electrons can only exist at specific energy levels (shells), not in between them
  2. Electrons can have any amount of energy, so they can be anywhere around the nucleus
  3. Electrons sit inside the nucleus together with the protons
  4. Electrons move randomly in a cloud around the nucleus with no fixed positions
Correct answer: A
Bohr's key idea is that electrons occupy fixed energy levels (shells) and cannot exist in between them. This is what links atomic structure to flame colours.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Drawing Practice, Bohr models for elements 1–10 (checkpoint element carbon)
Q2. A carbon atom has 6 electrons. Using the shell rules (Shell 1 holds a maximum of 2 electrons, Shell 2 holds a maximum of 8), how are its electrons arranged?
  1. 6
  2. 2, 4
  3. 2, 2, 2
  4. 4, 2
Correct answer: B
Shell 1 fills first with 2 electrons. The remaining 4 electrons go into Shell 2, giving 2, 4.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Drawing Practice, Bohr models for elements 1–10 (Shell 2 begins filling from lithium)
Q3. A lithium atom has 3 electrons. Where must the third electron be placed in a Bohr model?
  1. In Shell 3, because Shell 2 must stay empty until Shell 1 has 3 electrons
  2. In Shell 2, because Shell 1 is already full with 2 electrons
  3. Inside the nucleus with the protons
  4. In Shell 1, because it is closest to the nucleus
Correct answer: B
Shell 1 holds a maximum of 2 electrons, so it is full after two. The third electron starts Shell 2, giving lithium the arrangement 2, 1.
Use after: Explain phase — Part A: Think-Pair-Share, the three steps of a sodium electron in a cooking fire
Q4. In a cooking fire, the outermost electron of a sodium atom absorbs heat energy and jumps to a higher energy level. What happens when that electron falls back to its original level?
  1. It leaves the atom and the sodium turns into a different element
  2. It releases the energy only as sound, so no colour is seen
  3. It releases the energy it absorbed as a photon of light, which is yellow for sodium
  4. It absorbs even more energy from the flame and becomes more stable
Correct answer: C
The excited electron is unstable at the higher level. When it falls back, it releases the absorbed energy as a photon of light, and for sodium this light is yellow.
Use after: Explain phase — Part B: Connecting to the Driving Question, why potassium gives violet and sodium gives yellow
Q5. Sodium gives a yellow flame and potassium gives a violet flame. Using Bohr's model, what is the best explanation for this difference?
  1. Potassium atoms have no electrons, so they cannot give out yellow light
  2. The flame changes its own colour depending on how hot it is, not on the substance
  3. Each type of atom has different spacings between its energy levels, so the energy released when electrons fall back is different and gives a different colour
  4. Sodium and potassium electrons release exactly the same energy, but potassium releases it more slowly
Correct answer: C
The colour depends on how much energy is released when an electron falls back. This depends on the spacing between energy levels, which is different for each type of atom.
Use after: Explain phase — Part C: Worked Example, electron arrangements (phosphorus 2, 8, 5, then elements 11–15)
Q6. A silicon atom has 14 electrons. What is its electron arrangement?
  1. 8, 6
  2. 2, 6, 6
  3. 2, 8, 8
  4. 2, 8, 4
Correct answer: D
Shell 1 holds 2 and Shell 2 holds 8, which is 10 electrons. The remaining 14 − 10 = 4 electrons go in Shell 3, giving 2, 8, 4.
Use after: DQB phase — DQB update: green 'I now understand' sticky notes under 'Lesson 3 — Bohr's Model'
Q7. A learner is writing a green 'I now understand' note about the yellow flame at a cooking fire. Which note explains the phenomenon using Bohr's model, rather than only describing it?
  1. Electrons in sodium jump to higher shells when they absorb heat and release yellow light when they fall back
  2. Atoms have a nucleus and electrons, as we learned in Lesson 2
  3. Sodium makes a yellow flame and potassium makes a violet flame
  4. The fire is hot, so the salt changes colour when it burns
Correct answer: A
Only this note describes the mechanism: absorbing energy, jumping to a higher shell, then falling back and releasing light. The other notes only describe the observation or repeat earlier ideas.
