Use after: Predict phase — Predict: Think-Pair-Share prediction on classifying 6 unfamiliar organisms from Kericho, Kisumu or Mombasa
Q1. Learners from a school in Kisumu receive 6 unfamiliar organisms and must group them exactly as a school in Kericho does. What would help them most to get the same groups?
  1. Use the same observable characteristics and a shared dichotomous key
  2. Group them by size, from the biggest to the smallest
  3. Group them by how useful each one is as food
  4. Group them by colour so that similar-looking ones are together
Correct answer: A
Shared, observable characteristics and a key let any learner reach the same groups. Size, colour and use as food vary from person to person and give different groupings, as seen in Lesson 1.
Use after: Observe phase — Final classification challenge: one-line note on viruses (why they are not placed in either kingdom)
Q2. Why are viruses not placed in Kingdom Plantae or Kingdom Animalia?
  1. Viruses are too small to be seen with any instrument
  2. Viruses are not made of cells, so they do not fit into either kingdom
  3. Viruses only live in water and not on land
  4. Viruses are single cells that have no nucleus
Correct answer: B
Viruses are non-cellular. Plants and animals are made of cells, so viruses cannot be placed in either kingdom. The idea that 'viruses are cells' is a common misconception.
Use after: Observe phase — Final classification challenge, Task 1: sorting cards into Divisions (Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Spermatophyta)
Q3. A fern is in Division Pteridophyta and moss is in Division Bryophyta. Which feature separates the fern from the moss?
  1. The fern has vascular tissue, but the moss does not
  2. The moss makes seeds, but the fern makes spores
  3. The fern has flowers, but the moss does not
  4. The moss has vascular tissue, but the fern does not
Correct answer: A
Ferns have vascular tissue and reproduce by spores. Mosses lack vascular tissue. Neither makes seeds or flowers.
Use after: Observe phase — Final classification challenge, Task 1: sorting maize and bean into monocot and dicot
Q4. A plant has seeds enclosed in a fruit, leaves with net veins, and seeds with two cotyledons. Which group does it belong to?
  1. Gymnospermae
  2. Bryophyta
  3. Angiospermae, dicotyledon
  4. Angiospermae, monocotyledon
Correct answer: C
Seeds enclosed in a fruit mean Angiospermae. Net veins and two cotyledons mean a dicotyledon, like the bean. Maize has parallel veins and one cotyledon, so it is a monocotyledon.
Use after: Observe phase — Final classification challenge, Task 1: sorting into Phyla and Classes (Chordata and Arthropoda)
Q5. A mosquito has a jointed exoskeleton, three pairs of jointed legs and no backbone. Which phylum does it belong to, and why?
  1. Arthropoda, because it is small and harmful to people
  2. Chordata, because it is an animal that can fly
  3. Arthropoda, because it has a jointed exoskeleton and jointed legs
  4. Chordata, because it has three pairs of legs
Correct answer: C
Arthropods have a jointed exoskeleton and jointed legs. Chordates have a backbone, which the mosquito lacks. Size and harmfulness are not classification characteristics.
Use after: Observe phase — Final classification challenge, Task 2: build or complete a dichotomous key for at least 6 organisms and exchange it with another group
Q6. Which pair of statements is best for one step in a dichotomous key for insects?
  1. Is liked by learners / is not liked by learners
  2. Is big / is small
  3. Has wings / has no wings
  4. Looks beautiful / does not look beautiful
Correct answer: C
A key needs paired statements that are observable and clear, so any user gets the same answer. 'Has wings / no wings' can be checked by anyone. Beauty, size and liking are opinions or vary between people.
Use after: Explain phase — Final Explanation: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) answering 'How can we identify and classify living organisms accurately?'
Q7. A learner writes: 'Maize has one cotyledon and parallel veins, but bean has two cotyledons and net veins.' Which reasoning best explains why this evidence helps to classify them accurately?
  1. Any learner can observe and check these features, so everyone gets the same groups
  2. Maize is eaten more than bean, so it needs its own group
  3. The teacher said that maize and bean are different, so they are
  4. Maize is bigger than bean, so they must be separate groups
Correct answer: A
Good reasoning links evidence to the principle that shared, observable characteristics give the same grouping for any learner. Size, use as food and authority do not.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: writing at least one new question beyond the classroom
Q8. After sorting the Driving Question Board, a group wants to add a new question that goes beyond the classroom. Which question is the best fit?
  1. Which organism in our collection is the prettiest?
  2. How can a dichotomous key help identify crop pests in Nakuru?
  3. Why do we have a Driving Question Board?
  4. Is a goat bigger than a chicken?
Correct answer: B
The best new question applies classification knowledge to a real situation such as farming and can be investigated. The others are opinions, simple comparisons or about classroom routine.
Use after: Model Building phase — Final model: 'Can your model place a new organism you have never seen, say a tilapia or a cypress tree?'
Q9. A learner finds a tilapia from Lake Victoria. It has a backbone, gills and fins. Where should the learner's final classification model place it?
  1. Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Arthropoda
  2. Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Birds
  3. Kingdom Plantae, Division Spermatophyta
  4. Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Fish
Correct answer: D
Animals with a backbone are in Phylum Chordata, and a fish with gills and fins belongs to the fish class. Arthropods lack a backbone, and birds are a different class of Chordata.
Use after: End of lesson — End of lesson: importance of viruses in health, agriculture and biotechnology (one harm and one benefit)
Q10. Which statement about the importance of viruses to Kenyans is correct?
  1. Viruses can cause diseases such as measles and maize streak, but are also used in vaccines and biotechnology
  2. Viruses affect only people and never crops
  3. Viruses make food for plants by photosynthesis
  4. Viruses only cause disease and have no use
Correct answer: A
Viruses cause harm in health (for example measles) and agriculture (maize streak virus), but are also used in vaccines and biotechnology.
