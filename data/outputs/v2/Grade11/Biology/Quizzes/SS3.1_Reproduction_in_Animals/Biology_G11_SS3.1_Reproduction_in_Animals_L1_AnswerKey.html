Use after: Predict phase — Predict: viewing the photos of frogspawn in a Kisumu wetland pond and a hen on eggs
Q1. Near Kisumu, a learner looks at frogspawn in a pond and a hen's egg in a nest. Which statement correctly describes what is seen?
  1. Both frog eggs and hen's eggs are hard-shelled and laid on land.
  2. Frog eggs are covered in jelly and released into water, while a hen's egg is hard-shelled and laid on land.
  3. Frog eggs are hard-shelled and laid on land, while a hen's egg is covered in jelly and released into water.
  4. Both frog eggs and hen's eggs are covered in jelly and released into water.
Correct answer: B
The images show jelly-covered frog eggs released into pond water and a hen's hard-shelled egg laid on land. This is only a description of what is seen, not an explanation.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: teacher checklist for misconceptions such as 'all animals lay eggs'
Q2. A learner claims that 'all animals lay eggs'. Which animal from the lesson photos does NOT fit this claim?
  1. A frog in a pond near Kisumu
  2. A cow with her calf
  3. A tilapia in Lake Victoria
  4. A hen sitting on her eggs
Correct answer: B
A cow gives birth to a live calf after months of development inside her body, so the claim 'all animals lay eggs' does not fit. Frogs, hens and tilapia all produce eggs.
Use after: Predict phase — Predict: prediction 'Why do frogs need thousands of eggs but a cow produces one calf?'
Q3. Which statement matches what is described about the number of young in the anchoring phenomenon?
  1. A frog gives birth to a few live young, while a cow lays one hard-shelled egg.
  2. A cow lays thousands of eggs, while a frog gives birth to one live calf.
  3. A frog releases thousands of eggs into water, while a cow gives birth to one live calf after months of development inside her.
  4. A frog and a cow both produce thousands of young at one time.
Correct answer: C
Frogs release thousands of jelly-covered eggs, while a cow has one calf after months of internal development. At this stage learners only record this pattern, and why it happens is still an open question.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: observing external fertilisation at the school pond or wetland edge
Q4. Your group is going to observe frogspawn at the edge of a school pond. Which rule is correct?
  1. Collect the spawn in your pockets so you can study it in class.
  2. Wade into the pond and pick up the spawn to count the eggs.
  3. Touch the spawn with bare hands, but only for a short time.
  4. Observe from the edge, do not touch the spawn with bare hands, and wash your hands afterwards.
Correct answer: D
The lesson rules are to observe from the edge, not to touch spawn with bare hands, and to wash hands afterwards.
Use after: Observe phase — Part A: recording where eggs are released, jelly coating and number of eggs in the observation table
Q5. A group is filling in its Part A observation table for frog spawn. Which entry is an observation, not an explanation?
  1. Water helps the sperm to reach the eggs.
  2. The eggs are released into the water and are surrounded by a jelly layer.
  3. The jelly stops the eggs from drying out.
  4. Frogs need thousands of eggs because many are eaten.
Correct answer: B
Saying where the eggs are and what surrounds them is a direct observation. The other entries claim reasons for what is seen, and the table is for recording what is seen.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: sorting animals into oviparous, viviparous and ovoviviparous columns on the classification chart
Q6. In Part B, a group sorts a hen and a cow on the classification chart. Which placement is correct?
  1. Hen: ovoviviparous; cow: oviparous
  2. Hen: oviparous (eggs laid, development outside the body); cow: viviparous (young develop inside the mother and are born live)
  3. Hen: viviparous; cow: ovoviviparous
  4. Hen: viviparous; cow: oviparous
Correct answer: B
A hen lays eggs that develop outside her body, so it is oviparous. A cow carries the young inside her and gives birth to a live calf, so it is viviparous.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: sorting shark/guppy into the ovoviviparous column on the classification chart
Q7. A source says that in a female guppy the eggs develop and hatch inside her body, and she does not feed them through a placenta-type connection. In which column should the group place the guppy?
  1. Ovoviviparous, because eggs develop and hatch inside her body without placenta-type feeding
  2. Oviparous, because the eggs hatch
  3. Viviparous, because the young develop inside her
  4. Oviparous, because it produces eggs
Correct answer: A
Ovoviviparous animals keep the eggs inside the body where they develop and hatch, without placenta-type nourishment from the mother. This matches the guppy description.
