Q1. A farmer in Kakamega plants bean seeds on the same day after rain. One healthy-looking seed has not germinated because it is still dormant. Which hormone could help break its dormancy and start germination?
  1. Abscisic acid
  2. Ethylene
  3. Gibberellins
  4. Auxin from the stem tip only
Correct answer: C
Gibberellins break dormancy and promote germination. Abscisic acid maintains dormancy, so it would not start germination.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: hormone evidence table, row for abscisic acid
Q2. Which hormone keeps a seed dormant and also causes the stomata of a leaf to close?
  1. Ethylene
  2. Cytokinin
  3. Abscisic acid
  4. Gibberellin
Correct answer: C
Abscisic acid is responsible for dormancy and stomatal closure. Gibberellins do the opposite for dormancy, and cytokinins act in cell division.
Use after: Observe phase — Observe: highlighting an example for each hormone (ethylene: bananas or mangoes sold in Mombasa)
Q3. Green bananas bought by a trader in Mombasa turn yellow and soft as they ripen. Which plant hormone controls this ripening?
  1. Cytokinin
  2. Gibberellin
  3. Auxin
  4. Ethylene
Correct answer: D
Ethylene is the hormone that stimulates fruit ripening, for example in bananas and mangoes.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER for (C) bean plants that develop side branches while maize stays unbranched, and the teacher's modelled CER on the tip of the bean stem
Q4. A farmer cuts off the tip of a bean stem and soon side shoots start to grow from the lateral buds. What best explains this?
  1. The tip makes abscisic acid that feeds the side buds, so removing it stops branching
  2. The tip absorbs the rainwater, so removing it leaves more water for the side buds
  3. Cutting the tip makes the plant stop making any hormones
  4. The tip makes auxin that suppresses lateral buds, so removing it lets side buds grow
Correct answer: D
This is apical dominance: auxin from the tip suppresses lateral buds. When the tip is removed, auxin falls and lateral buds grow, helped by cytokinins.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER rubric oral probes on misconceptions (hormones are food for the plant)
Q5. A student writes: 'Hormones are food for the plant, so a plant with more hormones has more energy to grow.' What is wrong with this idea?
  1. Hormones only work on seeds and never on shoots or roots
  2. Nothing is wrong; hormones are the main food of a plant
  3. Hormones are chemical messengers that control growth processes; they are not food
  4. Hormones are found only in animals, not in plants
Correct answer: C
Plant hormones are chemical messengers that coordinate processes such as cell division, elongation and germination. Plants make their own food by photosynthesis.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER for (D) farmers in Kericho or Nyeri rooting tea or tree cuttings with natural hormone sources
Q6. Farmers in Kericho root tea cuttings using natural sources such as bulb onion or Aloe extract. Which hormone group do these sources help to supply so that roots form on the cuttings?
  1. Abscisic acid, which keeps the cutting dormant
  2. Ethylene, which ripens the cutting
  3. Gibberellins, which close the stomata
  4. Auxins, which promote the rooting of cuttings
Correct answer: D
Auxins promote root formation on cuttings, which is why natural rooting sources are used in tree nurseries.
Use after: Model Building phase — Model: revising the Day 1 model to add hormones and links to dormancy and germination conditions
Q7. Two maize seeds are planted on the same day with the same rain. One germinates in three days and the other takes longer. What is the best explanation that includes hormones?
  1. Hormones respond to conditions such as water, oxygen and temperature, so seeds can differ in when they germinate
  2. Only seeds without hormones can germinate late
  3. Rain alone decides when every seed germinates, so hormones play no part
  4. Hormones replace water, oxygen and temperature, so these factors do not matter
Correct answer: A
Hormones do not replace environmental conditions; they respond to them. Differences in hormone balance, such as gibberellins versus abscisic acid, can change germination time even with the same rain.
