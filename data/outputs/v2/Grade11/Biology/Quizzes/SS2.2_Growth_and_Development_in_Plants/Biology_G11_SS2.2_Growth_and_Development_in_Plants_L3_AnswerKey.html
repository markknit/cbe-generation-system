Q1. Mr. Wafula planted Rosecoco beans and some healthy-looking seeds did not germinate even though the rain, soil and planting day were the same. Which statement best describes a dormant seed?
  1. A living seed that fails to germinate even when conditions are favourable
  2. A seed that has been eaten by pests in the soil
  3. A dead seed whose embryo has rotted
  4. A seed that fails because it was planted in waterlogged soil
Correct answer: A
Dormancy means a viable (alive) seed does not germinate even in favourable conditions. Dead seeds, pest damage and waterlogging are different causes of failure.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Sort the non-germinating seeds (data card table for beans)
Q2. Of 100 Rosecoco bean seeds planted, 20 did not germinate. The reasons were: 8 had a hard impermeable seed coat, 6 were in a waterlogged patch, 4 were old seed with dead embryos, and 2 were eaten by pests. How many of the 20 non-germinating beans failed for reasons OTHER than dormancy?
  1. 12
  2. 14
  3. 8
  4. 6
Correct answer: A
Only the 8 hard-coated seeds were dormant. The others are 6 + 4 + 2 = 12, which is also 20 - 8 = 12.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: Sort the non-germinating seeds (comparing maize with 0 dormant and beans with 8 dormant)
Q3. In Mr. Wafula's field, 12 maize seeds did not germinate: 5 were eaten or rotted by fungi or pests, 4 were old seeds with dead embryos, and 3 were in a waterlogged patch. Among the 20 non-germinating beans, 8 had a hard impermeable seed coat. Which conclusion is supported by this data?
  1. Maize seeds can never be dormant
  2. Dormancy caused all the bean failures but none of the maize failures
  3. Dormancy explained 8 of the 20 bean failures and none of the 12 maize failures in this field
  4. The hard seed coat caused most of the maize failures
Correct answer: C
The table shows 8 dormant beans out of 20 failures and 0 dormant maize out of 12. The data shows that not germinating has several causes, and it cannot show that maize never has dormancy.
Use after: Observe phase — Part C: Field result (7 of 8 germinated after scarification)
Q4. The 8 hard-coated bean seeds were scarified (nicked) and replanted. By Day 7, 7 of the 8 had germinated. What percentage of the 8 seeds germinated?
  1. 70%
  2. 7.8%
  3. 87.5%
  4. 12.5%
Correct answer: C
Percentage = 7 ÷ 8 × 100 = 87.5%. The 12.5% option is the share that did not germinate.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: CER explanation of why 8 healthy-looking seeds did not germinate and why 7 of 8 germinated after scarification
Q5. Why did scarifying (nicking or rubbing) the hard seed coat of the dormant bean seeds help them germinate?
  1. It added food to the seed so the embryo could grow
  2. It made the embryo larger before planting
  3. It made a small opening in the impermeable testa so water and oxygen could reach the embryo
  4. It killed the pests living inside the seed
Correct answer: C
A hard impermeable testa blocks water and oxygen. Scarification damages the testa gently so that water and oxygen can enter and the living embryo can start to grow.
Use after: Explain phase — Explain: matching each cause of dormancy to a way of breaking it
Q6. A farmer finds that some seeds stay dormant because of a chemical inhibitor, abscisic acid, in the seed. Which way of breaking dormancy matches this cause best?
  1. Planting the seeds in a waterlogged patch
  2. Soaking the seeds in water for some hours so the inhibitor is washed out
  3. Rubbing the seed coat with sandpaper
  4. Storing the seeds in sunlight and adding fertiliser
Correct answer: B
The way of breaking dormancy must match the cause. A chemical inhibitor can be washed out (leached) by water, whereas sandpaper is for a hard testa.
Use after: DQB phase — Driving Question Board: moving 'Why do healthy-looking seeds not germinate?' to 'Partly answered' and the exit ticket
Q7. A learner says, 'A seed that does not germinate must be dead.' Which group of non-germinating bean seeds from Mr. Wafula's field shows that this idea is wrong?
  1. The 4 old seeds with dead embryos
  2. The 8 hard-coated seeds, of which 7 germinated after scarification
  3. The 5 maize seeds eaten or rotted by fungi
  4. The 2 seeds eaten by pests
Correct answer: B
The hard-coated seeds were alive but dormant, and 7 of the 8 germinated once the testa was nicked. The other groups were dead, eaten or rotted.
Use after: End of lesson — Explain: significance of dormancy to the plant and to the farmer
Q8. Why can dormancy be useful to a wild plant but a problem for a farmer like Mr. Wafula?
  1. Dormancy helps seeds survive dry or cold seasons and germinate over time, but the farmer wants all seeds to germinate together for a uniform stand
  2. Dormant seeds always die, so wild plants make more seeds, but the farmer loses money
  3. Dormancy stops pests from eating seeds in the wild but not in a farm
  4. Dormancy makes seeds grow faster, which wild plants need but farmers do not
Correct answer: A
For wild plants, dormancy keeps seeds alive through unfavourable seasons and spreads germination over time. A farmer needs even germination, so scarification is used to break dormancy.
