Q1. A lone avocado tree in a Nairobi compound is covered in flowers but sets no fruit. A second avocado tree is planted 10 m away. Which outcome is most likely once both trees flower?
  1. The second tree gives the first tree extra water, so its flowers will make fruit
  2. The first tree will start making fruit from its flowers by mitosis alone
  3. The two trees join their roots, so no pollen is needed to make fruit
  4. Pollen can be carried between the two trees, so cross-pollination and fertilisation can happen and fruit can form
Correct answer: D
A lone tree may fail to fruit because of barriers to self-pollination. A second tree supplies compatible pollen, so cross-pollination, fertilisation and fruit development can follow.
Use after: Observe phase — Station A: vegetative propagation charts and photos of cassava, sugarcane and sweet potato cuttings
Q2. A farmer in Kisumu plants a cassava stem cutting. It grows roots and shoots without any flower or pollen. Which statement explains this correctly?
  1. The cutting needs pollen from another cassava plant before it can grow roots
  2. The cutting is a type of seed, so it grows like a seed
  3. Cells in the stem divide by meiosis, so the new plant is different from the parent
  4. Cells in the stem divide by mitosis to form roots and shoots, so the new plant is a clone of the parent
Correct answer: D
Vegetative propagation uses mitosis in vegetative tissue. No gametes are involved, so the offspring is genetically identical to the parent.
Use after: Observe phase — Station B: labelled flower diagrams and classification tables, including features that hinder self-pollination
Q3. Which of these is a feature that hinders self-pollination in flowering plants?
  1. Anthers and stigma of the same flower ripen at the same time and touch each other
  2. The flower produces a lot of nectar
  3. The flower has a large ovary with many ovules
  4. The stigma of a hibiscus flower matures at a different time from its anthers
Correct answer: D
Different times of maturity of anthers and stigma (dichogamy) make it hard for a flower to pollinate itself. Nectar and a large ovary do not prevent self-pollination.
Use after: Observe phase — Station C: double fertilisation animation screenshots and group summary of zygote and endosperm formation
Q4. During double fertilisation in a flowering plant such as maize, what happens to the two sperm nuclei from the pollen tube?
  1. One sperm nucleus forms the seed coat, and the other forms the fruit wall
  2. One sperm nucleus fuses with the egg cell to form the zygote, and the other fuses with the polar nuclei to form the endosperm
  3. Both sperm nuclei fuse with the polar nuclei to form the endosperm
  4. Both sperm nuclei fuse with the egg cell to form two zygotes
Correct answer: B
One sperm nucleus fertilises the egg to make the diploid zygote. The second joins the polar nuclei to make the endosperm, which feeds the embryo.
Use after: Observe phase — Station D: fruit sections of tomato, passion fruit and bean pod with placentation and fruit type classification
Q5. A learner cuts open a tomato fruit and finds small seeds inside. Which statement about how the fruit and seeds formed is correct?
  1. The petals became the fruit and the anthers became the seeds
  2. The ovule became the fruit and the ovary became the seeds
  3. The whole flower became the fruit and the seeds came from the stigma
  4. The ovary became the fruit and the ovules became the seeds
Correct answer: D
After fertilisation, ovules develop into seeds and the ovary wall develops into the fruit.
Use after: DQB phase — Sorting DQB sticky notes into 'Answered', 'Partly answered' and 'New question', including unresolved confusion between pollination and fertilisation
Q6. A student looks at a pawpaw flower and sees pollen grains on the stigma. Which conclusion is correct?
  1. Neither pollination nor fertilisation can happen without a second tree
  2. Pollination and fertilisation mean the same thing, so the fruit will form
  3. Pollination has happened, but fertilisation has not necessarily happened yet
  4. Fertilisation has definitely happened, because pollen is on the stigma
Correct answer: C
Pollination is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. Fertilisation happens later, when the male gamete fuses with the egg, and only if the pollen is compatible and a pollen tube grows.
Use after: Model Building phase — Final model and unit assessment tasks: advantages and disadvantages of asexual and sexual reproduction
Q7. A sugarcane farmer in Mumias plants setts (stem pieces) instead of seeds. What is an advantage and a disadvantage of this method?
  1. Advantage: it needs double fertilisation. Disadvantage: it makes fewer plants
  2. Advantage: the crop grows fast and is uniform. Disadvantage: the plants are clones, so a disease can spread to all of them
  3. Advantage: it needs pollen from another farm. Disadvantage: it makes the plants different from each other
  4. Advantage: it creates a lot of genetic variation. Disadvantage: it needs pollinators
Correct answer: B
Setts produce clones by mitosis. This gives fast, uniform crops, but the lack of variation means disease can affect all plants.
