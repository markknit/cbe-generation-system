Nutrition in Animals — Materials Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
DG1 — Trace food and secretions separately
Construct a labelled flow model from these organ names:mouth,pharynx,oesophagus,stomach,small intestine(duodenum then later regions),large intestine,rectum,anus. Add salivary glands,liver,gallbladder and pancreas as accessory sources,using a different arrow style for secretions. Trace a fictional starch/protein/fat meal;do not eat or bring food to demonstrate swallowing. Distinguish ingestion,digestion,absorption,assimilation and egestion. Predict whether food passes through the liver or pancreas.
Teacher key and preparation
Food follows the alimentary tube in the listed order;saliva enters mouth,bile made by liver/stored and released by gallbladder enters duodenum,pancreatic juice enters duodenum. Food does not travel through liver/pancreas. Ingestion entry;digestion breaks food into absorbable molecules;absorption crosses gut lining into blood/lymph;assimilation uses absorbed materials in cells;egestion removes unabsorbed residues through anus. Urinary excretion is a different process. Use arrow styles consistently;this is a flow model,not a scale anatomical map.
DG2 — What changes during mechanical and starch digestion?
Evidence statements:chewing breaks food pieces;saliva moistens food;salivary amylase acts on starch;muscle waves move a swallowed bolus;acidic stomach conditions reduce salivary-amylase activity. Sort mechanical processing,transport and chemical digestion. Build a starch→smaller sugars→glucose chain,locating the contributing mouth/small-intestine stages. Explain why chewing alone cannot be claimed to turn starch into glucose. Draw a particle-size versus molecule-change comparison.
Teacher key and preparation
Chewing is mechanical;peristalsis transports/mixes;amylase hydrolyses starch to smaller carbohydrates such as maltose. Pancreatic amylase contributes in small intestine and intestinal enzymes complete digestible carbohydrate breakdown to absorbable simple sugars including glucose. Chewing increases exposed area without by itself changing starch molecules into glucose. Do not claim saliva digests all food classes or starch digestion is completed in the mouth. No saliva sampling,food tasting or human-body experiment.
DG3 — Protein digestion is a sequence,not a stomach finish line
Compare three claims: A stomach churning is an enzyme; B stomach acid alone converts all protein directly to amino acids; C protein breakdown begins with stomach protease and continues in small intestine. Use the information that pepsin works in acidic stomach conditions,while pancreatic/intestinal proteases continue protein digestion to absorbable amino acids. Correct the claims and explain the roles of muscular wall,acid and protective lining. Draw a protein→peptides→amino-acids pathway.
Teacher key and preparation
C is the supported summary. Muscular mixing is mechanical,pepsin is a protease,acid helps establish its working environment and disrupts protein structure;acid itself is not a protease. Pepsin produces shorter peptides;later proteases/peptidases complete breakdown. Mucus/bicarbonate and epithelial protection reduce damage;do not claim a model proves immunity to acid or give health diagnosis/advice. No acid/enzyme body simulation is required.
DG4 — Why does fat digestion need more than a mixing arrow?
Use these facts to make a duodenum model:bile is made by liver and stored in gallbladder;it disperses fat into small droplets;pancreatic lipase breaks fat molecules down;pancreatic bicarbonate helps neutralise incoming acid. Distinguish emulsification from enzyme digestion. Locate most nutrient absorption in small intestine and compare a villus's large surface,thin lining,blood capillaries and lacteal with a smooth thick wall. State that absorbed materials enter blood or lymph,not all directly into blood. Explain the large intestine's role without claiming all water absorption happens there.
Teacher key and preparation
Emulsification increases contact area without hydrolysing fat molecules;bile is not an enzyme. Lipase yields fatty acids and glycerol-containing products,principally monoglycerides in small-intestinal digestion;school summaries often use fatty acids/glycerol. Villi/microvilli enlarge area;thin lining reduces crossing distance;blood/lymph transport carries absorbed nutrients away. Much lipid is routed via lacteals/lymph;simple sugars/amino acids mainly enter blood. Water is absorbed in both small and large intestines;colon recovers water/electrolytes and forms faecal material. Avoid treating a cardboard villus as a working absorption experiment.
DG5 — Plan controlled tests before touching reagents
Plan separate tests for starch,reducing sugar,protein and lipid using fresh labelled portions of teacher-prepared samples. For each include water/process blank,a known positive control,an unknown,and a repeat. Make an empty record with columns sample,test,initial observation,final observation,control verdict,inference and action actually performed. Explain why taste/colour of the original food cannot replace a test and why a negative result does not prove absolute absence. Identify hazards and stop conditions before practical work.
Teacher key and preparation
Approve a school-specific risk assessment and actual reagent SDS/supplier procedure before practicals. Prepare dilute known standards and anonymous safe extracts in advance;avoid allergenic/unknown foods,biological fluids and tasting. Goggles,lab coat/apron,closed footwear,clean separate droppers,racks and spill/eyewash arrangements. Teacher controls reagent stocks,hot bath and waste;never mouth-pipette. No flames. Keep ethanol in a separate unheated lesson/area away from heat/sparks. If facilities,supervision or approved reagents are unavailable,use clearly labelled supplied data and record practical skill NOT OBSERVED;do not substitute written work for outcome c's performed experiment. Blank should be negative,positive control positive;failed controls invalidate unknown inference.
DG6 — Starch test — actual observations
Only after teacher approval:label a white spotting tile for water blank,known starch,unknown and repeat. Apply the teacher-provided sample portions and2–3drops of dilute iodine solution to each using separate droppers. Record actual colours,control validity and a cautious inference;use no heat. Stop for spills or contact and notify the teacher. Never taste samples/reagents. If using a supplied observation record instead,write DATA INTERPRETATION,NOT A PERFORMED TEST.
Teacher key and preparation
Use approved dilute iodine in potassium iodide per supplier guidance. A blue-black change supports starch detected;unchanged yellow/brown is negative under these conditions. Check positive/blank before interpreting unknown;opaque/strongly coloured samples may obscure a change and require a teacher-prepared clearer extract/repeat. Iodine is an irritant/stains;protect eyes/skin and follow school spill/waste instructions. Observe individual safe labelling/dispensing/recording,not just group product;retain authentic results without inventing expected colours for a food.
DG7 — Reducing-sugar test — supervised hot bath
Use teacher-prepared clear extracts and standards. Label blank,known glucose,unknown and repeat;combine equal5mLvolumes of sample and prepared Benedict reagent in suitable open tubes. The teacher places/handles the rack in an80°Cwater bath for10minutes and manages cooling. Observe from the designated position;record before/after appearance after safe cooling and judge controls. Learners do not carry hot water or heat sealed tubes. No flames,ethanol or tasting. Record whether you actually prepared a test or only observed/interpreted it.
Teacher key and preparation
Use tubes with capacity safely above10mL;teacher confirms supplier protocol,water bath stability,tube rack/holder and eye protection. Positive reducing-sugar test may give green/yellow/orange/red precipitate depending on conditions;blank remains blue. A negative result does not exclude non-reducing sugars such as sucrose;do not add an acid-hydrolysis extension. Different food colours are not a quantitative concentration scale without calibration/controls. Let tubes cool before learner contact;collect copper-containing waste under school guidance. Teacher-controlled heating is compatible with observing individual learner preparation/recording;record exactly which actions were performed.
DG8 — Protein test — room-temperature controls
After teacher approval,put5mLof each prepared blank,protein standard,unknown and repeat into separate labelled tubes. Add5mLof ready-prepared biuret reagent as instructed by the teacher,gently mix and inspect after about2minutes at room temperature. Record colour/control verdict/inference. Keep tubes in racks,protect eyes/skin,and report splashes immediately. Do not prepare strong alkali or copper-salt stocks. If a teacher must dispense reagent for local safety,record the actions you actually perform.
Teacher key and preparation
Ready-prepared alkaline copper reagent is not a food. Purple/lilac supports protein(peptide bonds)detected;blue is negative under test conditions. Check blank and known protein before the unknown;clear teacher-prepared samples help avoid misleading turbidity/colour. Biuret reagent can be corrosive/harmful;teacher follows actual SDS and controls dispensing/waste;no heating or mouth pipetting. Verify learner sample preparation,labelling and inference individually;an observation-only alternative cannot certify performance of that action.
DG9 — Lipid test — separate cold teacher demonstration
This is a teacher demonstration unless local risk assessment explicitly permits supervised learner handling. Watch a labelled ethanol-water process blank,a known oil standard and a prepared unknown. The teacher mixes sample with3mLethanol,allows solids to settle2–3minutes,then transfers clear extract into a separate tube half-filled with water. Record transparency/cloudiness and whether controls worked. No heat,flames,sparks or hot bath is present. Never taste or inhale deliberately. Label your record DEMONSTRATION OBSERVED unless you actually performed an authorised action.
Teacher key and preparation
A white/milky emulsion after transfer supports lipid detection;initial sample particles or a cloudy blank invalidate that inference. Ethanol is highly flammable;teacher dispenses small quantities in a ventilated flame/spark-free area,keeps stock closed and away from hot equipment,protects eyes and collects ethanol-containing waste appropriately. Do not combine with DG7's heated practical. Clear process blank carries ethanol through the same steps;known oil positive checks procedure. Demonstration is evidence of observation/interpretation,not individual hands-on lipid testing. Different-nutrient performance evidence may come from the separately observed learner starch/sugar/protein tests.
DG10 — Build a model and verify a saved enzyme claim
Use clean reused card,string,tape and teacher-precut pieces to build a digestive-system model with correctly ordered alimentary tube,accessory secretion arrows and one close-up adaptation such as a villus or stomach wall. Mark what the model cannot show. With an approved device,open a teacher-selected source on digestion,retrieve and save one enzyme/site/substrate/product claim with title,URL/date and source credit;share it in a short digital slide or saved display. Cross-check against supplied DG2–DG4. If no device is available,make a printed evidence panel and label digital retrieval/sharing NOT OBSERVED. Approved starting source,if the teacher authorises live access:https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works . Save/share only the short original claim panel,not personal information or a copied whole page.
Teacher key and preparation
Supply preapproved NIDDK/NCI/OpenStax page or an authorised offline copy;do not require personal accounts,downloads from unknown sites or personal health information. Teacher observes each learner retrieving/saving and sharing when available;print substitution supports content but is not evidence of digital competence. Assess a physically made model and oral explanation,not a claim that a sketch demonstrates construction. Blunt scissors only if safe/approved;teacher precuts tougher parts,no sharp wire/glass/hot glue/food waste. Model limitations include no real peristalsis,enzyme reactions,scale or absorption;cardboard arrows represent explanations.
DG11 — Audit controls,mechanisms and models
Fictional records for interpretation only: A iodine blank blue-black,starch control blue-black,unknown blue-black; B Benedict blank blue,glucose control orange precipitate,unknown blue; C biuret blank blue,protein control purple,unknown purple; D ethanol-water blank milky,oil control milky,unknown milky. Which inferences are justified and which must be repeated? Correct these claims:'bile is an enzyme','food enters pancreas','all digestion finishes in stomach','a negative Benedict result proves no sugar','all water absorption is in colon'. Review one constructed model and name each organ's contribution respectfully.
Teacher key and preparation
A blank contamination/invalid run;repeat before unknown inference. B controls valid,no reducing sugar detected under those conditions;cannot exclude sucrose or levels below detection. C valid supports protein detected. D cloudy blank invalidates lipid inference;repeat process/clarifyextract. Correct physiological claims using DG1–DG4. A written audit supports reasoning,not experiment performance. Model/appreciation evidence is a specific role-and-benefit explanation;do not infer a lasting attitude. Preserve real earlier practical observations even if they conflict with predictions.
DG12 — Independent digestion and food-test data
Fictional assessment observations,not your own practical results. Sample X:iodine blank yellow-brown,starch control blue-black,Xblue-black;Benedict blank blue,glucose control orange precipitate,Xblue;biuret blank blue,protein control purple,Xpurple. Sample Y ethanol-process blank milky,oil standard milky,Ymilky. Tests used correctly prepared separate portions as described unless a control shows a problem. Fresh independent model brief:trace a meal containing starch,protein and fat through the human digestive system;distinguish food and secretion routes. Use these data and the final prompts only. No reagents,teaching cards or answer discussion during this written assessment.
Teacher key and preparation
Xstarch detected,protein detected,no reducing sugar detected under those conditions;cannot say no carbohydrate or no sugar. Ylipid inference invalid because process blank is cloudy;repeat with clean apparatus/clear controls. Written final does not replace the separately observed performance evidence in DG6–DG8. Physiology/model keys use ordered tube,accessory secretion routes,starch/sugar and protein/amino-acid pathways,bile emulsification versus lipase,and structural adaptations linked to absorption/protection. Final data and exemplars withheld until assessment.
