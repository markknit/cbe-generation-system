Nutrition in Animals — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
DG1 — Trace food and secretions separately
Construct a labelled flow model from these organ names:mouth,pharynx,oesophagus,stomach,small intestine(duodenum then later regions),large intestine,rectum,anus. Add salivary glands,liver,gallbladder and pancreas as accessory sources,using a different arrow style for secretions. Trace a fictional starch/protein/fat meal;do not eat or bring food to demonstrate swallowing. Distinguish ingestion,digestion,absorption,assimilation and egestion. Predict whether food passes through the liver or pancreas.
DG2 — What changes during mechanical and starch digestion?
Evidence statements:chewing breaks food pieces;saliva moistens food;salivary amylase acts on starch;muscle waves move a swallowed bolus;acidic stomach conditions reduce salivary-amylase activity. Sort mechanical processing,transport and chemical digestion. Build a starch→smaller sugars→glucose chain,locating the contributing mouth/small-intestine stages. Explain why chewing alone cannot be claimed to turn starch into glucose. Draw a particle-size versus molecule-change comparison.
DG3 — Protein digestion is a sequence,not a stomach finish line
Compare three claims: A stomach churning is an enzyme; B stomach acid alone converts all protein directly to amino acids; C protein breakdown begins with stomach protease and continues in small intestine. Use the information that pepsin works in acidic stomach conditions,while pancreatic/intestinal proteases continue protein digestion to absorbable amino acids. Correct the claims and explain the roles of muscular wall,acid and protective lining. Draw a protein→peptides→amino-acids pathway.
DG4 — Why does fat digestion need more than a mixing arrow?
Use these facts to make a duodenum model:bile is made by liver and stored in gallbladder;it disperses fat into small droplets;pancreatic lipase breaks fat molecules down;pancreatic bicarbonate helps neutralise incoming acid. Distinguish emulsification from enzyme digestion. Locate most nutrient absorption in small intestine and compare a villus's large surface,thin lining,blood capillaries and lacteal with a smooth thick wall. State that absorbed materials enter blood or lymph,not all directly into blood. Explain the large intestine's role without claiming all water absorption happens there.
DG5 — Plan controlled tests before touching reagents
Plan separate tests for starch,reducing sugar,protein and lipid using fresh labelled portions of teacher-prepared samples. For each include water/process blank,a known positive control,an unknown,and a repeat. Make an empty record with columns sample,test,initial observation,final observation,control verdict,inference and action actually performed. Explain why taste/colour of the original food cannot replace a test and why a negative result does not prove absolute absence. Identify hazards and stop conditions before practical work.
DG6 — Starch test — actual observations
Only after teacher approval:label a white spotting tile for water blank,known starch,unknown and repeat. Apply the teacher-provided sample portions and2–3drops of dilute iodine solution to each using separate droppers. Record actual colours,control validity and a cautious inference;use no heat. Stop for spills or contact and notify the teacher. Never taste samples/reagents. If using a supplied observation record instead,write DATA INTERPRETATION,NOT A PERFORMED TEST.
DG7 — Reducing-sugar test — supervised hot bath
Use teacher-prepared clear extracts and standards. Label blank,known glucose,unknown and repeat;combine equal5mLvolumes of sample and prepared Benedict reagent in suitable open tubes. The teacher places/handles the rack in an80°Cwater bath for10minutes and manages cooling. Observe from the designated position;record before/after appearance after safe cooling and judge controls. Learners do not carry hot water or heat sealed tubes. No flames,ethanol or tasting. Record whether you actually prepared a test or only observed/interpreted it.
DG8 — Protein test — room-temperature controls
After teacher approval,put5mLof each prepared blank,protein standard,unknown and repeat into separate labelled tubes. Add5mLof ready-prepared biuret reagent as instructed by the teacher,gently mix and inspect after about2minutes at room temperature. Record colour/control verdict/inference. Keep tubes in racks,protect eyes/skin,and report splashes immediately. Do not prepare strong alkali or copper-salt stocks. If a teacher must dispense reagent for local safety,record the actions you actually perform.
DG9 — Lipid test — separate cold teacher demonstration
This is a teacher demonstration unless local risk assessment explicitly permits supervised learner handling. Watch a labelled ethanol-water process blank,a known oil standard and a prepared unknown. The teacher mixes sample with3mLethanol,allows solids to settle2–3minutes,then transfers clear extract into a separate tube half-filled with water. Record transparency/cloudiness and whether controls worked. No heat,flames,sparks or hot bath is present. Never taste or inhale deliberately. Label your record DEMONSTRATION OBSERVED unless you actually performed an authorised action.
DG10 — Build a model and verify a saved enzyme claim
Use clean reused card,string,tape and teacher-precut pieces to build a digestive-system model with correctly ordered alimentary tube,accessory secretion arrows and one close-up adaptation such as a villus or stomach wall. Mark what the model cannot show. With an approved device,open a teacher-selected source on digestion,retrieve and save one enzyme/site/substrate/product claim with title,URL/date and source credit;share it in a short digital slide or saved display. Cross-check against supplied DG2–DG4. If no device is available,make a printed evidence panel and label digital retrieval/sharing NOT OBSERVED. Approved starting source,if the teacher authorises live access:https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works . Save/share only the short original claim panel,not personal information or a copied whole page.
DG11 — Audit controls,mechanisms and models
Fictional records for interpretation only: A iodine blank blue-black,starch control blue-black,unknown blue-black; B Benedict blank blue,glucose control orange precipitate,unknown blue; C biuret blank blue,protein control purple,unknown purple; D ethanol-water blank milky,oil control milky,unknown milky. Which inferences are justified and which must be repeated? Correct these claims:'bile is an enzyme','food enters pancreas','all digestion finishes in stomach','a negative Benedict result proves no sugar','all water absorption is in colon'. Review one constructed model and name each organ's contribution respectfully.
