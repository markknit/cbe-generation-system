## Source discrepancies requiring explicit scientific clarification

Preserve the official wording in its source record. Do not silently correct it or teach a source inconsistency as established science.

- **Respiration p10:** project is anaerobic, while the Integrity value refers to food products utilising *aerobic* respiration. Record the mismatch. Source also explicitly says “CAUTION! No brewing of alcoholic substances.” Fermentation activities must respect that constraint.
- **Chemical Families p22:** copper, iron, zinc and lead are grouped as selected transition elements. Explain the classification distinction: lead is p-block/group 14; zinc is group 12 with a filled d subshell and is excluded by the usual introductory incomplete-d criterion. The first 20 elements contain no d-block transition elements, so additional transition examples extend beyond that classification scope. Primary checks: [IUPAC definition](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/T06456/plain), [RSC zinc](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/30/zinc), [RSC lead](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/82/lead).
- **Chemical Bonding pp23–24:** a list labelled common compounds includes elemental hydrogen, oxygen, iodine, diamond and graphite. A bond-types list combines ionic, covalent, dative-covalent, hydrogen, metallic and van der Waals. Distinguish elements/compounds and structural bonding/intermolecular interactions in teaching material.
- **Magnetism pp40–41:** the simple electric-bell project fits an electromagnetism unit, but a conventional bell works through a current-driven electromagnet, attraction and contact cycling. Do not claim generator-style induction is its operating mechanism; separately demonstrate induced emf.

## Unit scope, practical evidence and constraints

### 1.4 Transport in Plants

Required scope includes absorption of water/mineral salts, translocation, transpiration and its structural/environmental factors and importance. Experiences include specimens, cohesion/adhesion resources, investigations and community watering/manuring/fertiliser contexts, naming DAP/CAN/urea. Distinguish xylem uptake/transpiration from phloem translocation: dye-in-water observations are not automatically phloem evidence. Uptake apparatus measures uptake under assumptions, not exact transpiration. Use controls and time-series records. Community tasks should not become unsupervised pupil chemical application.

### 1.5 Respiration

Include aerobic/anaerobic respiration, substrates, respiratory quotient (RQ), factors and economic applications. Finite RQ calculations require nonzero oxygen uptake; substrate inference needs stated assumptions and is not unique under arbitrary mixed metabolism. Yeast/fermentation/acidity work needs an approved procedure compatible with the no-brewing caution. Do not direct sealed pressure-vessel, improvised biogas or tasting activities. Actual observations must remain separate from supplied/simulated data.

### 1.6 Plant Growth and Development

Include dormancy/causes/breaking, germination conditions/types, primary/secondary growth, hormones and growth factors. Plan multi-day observations, prepared specimens or attributed existing time-series evidence; do not claim instant growth observation during one short lesson. Light is not a universal germination requirement for every species. Distinguish germination requirements from subsequent growth factors, and avoid unapproved chemical/hormone treatments.

### 1.7 Microorganisms

Include fungi, bacteria, viruses, transmission/infections, prevention/control and economic importance. Project investigates mould growth on food; community activity concerns preservation. Author must provide an approved biosafety route rather than asking pupils to open/sniff unknown mould or culture human/environmental pathogens. Teacher-controlled observation or genuine supplied evidence can support study when direct work is inappropriate, with honest labels. Distinguish acellular viruses from bacteria/fungi, beneficial from harmful microorganisms and antibacterial from antiviral claims. Avoid identifiable personal health data in community work.

### 2.1 The Periodic Table

First-20-element shell arrangement, groups/periods, stability, electron affinity, ion formation, valency, oxidation numbers, formulae and equations are within scope. Projects model any five of the first 20 and make common-substance name/formula charts. In the listed context, source “radicals” means polyatomic ions, not free radicals. Distinguish valency from oxidation number. Balance with coefficients while preserving valid formula subscripts, despite the source's valency/balancing wording. Account for helium's duet and the introductory shell model's limits; electron affinity is not merely a desire for a full shell.

### 2.2 Chemical Families

Cover alkali/alkaline-earth/halogen/noble-gas/transition examples, properties and uses. Reaction experiences name sodium, magnesium and chlorine with air/oxygen, water and dilute acids **where applicable**; do not invent a universal positive-reaction matrix. Sodium/chlorine/lead contexts need an approved demonstration or existing-evidence route rather than uncontrolled pupil handling or hazardous gas preparation. Preserve research/road-lighting contexts without requiring hazardous visits. Resolve classification as above.

### 2.3 Chemical Bonding

Cover formation, types, structures and property/use links, including diamond, graphite and aluminium. Dot-and-cross/model tasks must distinguish ionic lattices, metals and giant covalent networks from discrete molecules. Distinguish O–H covalent bonds from hydrogen bonds. Melting/boiling, solubility and electrical/thermal tests need safe selected substances, controlled heating and low-voltage circuits rather than arbitrary-material testing. Models and experiments require observable action evidence; a written assertion alone is not evidence of making or measuring.

### 2.5 Rates of Reactions

Include experiments, temperature, pressure, catalyst, light, concentration and surface area, plus optimum industrial conditions. Distinguish physical-process rates such as dissolving from chemical-reaction rates, and rate from extent. Pressure effects depend on system, especially gaseous reactants; light is context-specific and enzyme rate does not increase indefinitely with temperature. Use controlled comparisons, measurements, units and timing. Written interpretation alone does not evidence the outcome to perform experiments. Avoid improvised pressure vessels or harmful gas-generating procedures.

### 3.1 Turning Effect of Force

Include moments at a point, **anti-parallel forces**, principle of moments and metre-rule balance, numericals and applications. Use perpendicular lever arms, explicit directions and correct mass/force units. State rule-weight, centre-of-mass and pivot assumptions. Not every anti-parallel pair is a pure couple: a couple needs equal opposite forces with separated lines of action. Use small secured loads and safe apparatus, distinguishing actual balance observations from ideal results.

### 3.2 Linear Motion

Constant-acceleration equations and free-fall applications are required; source explicitly says **no derivations needed**. Do not make derivation a required assessed hurdle. State sign conventions and distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity. Ideal mass-independent fall assumes negligible air resistance; rolling/sloping-road motion is not automatically free fall. Source dropping activities need approved low-height, supervised setups rather than rooftops/traffic hazards. Human timing precision limits conclusions. Retain p37 PCIs.

### 3.3 Waves

Include terms, wave equation/calculations, properties and applications. Source uses echoes, day/night sound refraction, hearing round corners and community/marine/infrastructure impacts/mitigation. Account for echo round-trip distance and distinguish spatial from time graphs. Sound refraction depends on temperature/wind gradients, not a universal day/night slogan; around-corner sound may also involve reflections. Avoid harmful sound levels and hazardous fieldwork. Label observed demonstration, video study and supplied-data analysis accurately.

### 3.4 Magnetism and Electromagnetic Induction

Include magnetisation/demagnetisation, fields, induced emf/factors and applications. Factor treatment is explicitly **qualitative**, not compulsory advanced numerical Faraday-law work. Source methods include stroking, induction, electrical, hammering and heating contexts; these require approved safe apparatus/supervision rather than generic directions to heat/strike metal. Use low-voltage limited-current setups, avoiding mains/overheating coils. Distinguish emf from current, which requires an appropriate closed circuit. Diagram field/motion orientation; stationary-conductor statements must account for whether relevant magnetic conditions change. Actual induction observation is separate from paper prediction. Include the bell project with the mechanism distinction above and p42 responsibility/PCIs.
