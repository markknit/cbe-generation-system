Transport in Plants — Materials Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
PT1 — Observe an approved root specimen
Teacher supplies a healthy pre-grown seedling with roots supported moist on a tray and an approved hand lens. Observe without pulling/root cutting. Draw only visible root features and label root hairs only if resolved;record specimen,tool and an observation limit. Use supplied conceptual description separately:many young absorbing regions bear thin root-hair extensions that increase contact with soil water. No assumed microscopic detail from an unaided view.
Teacher key and preparation
Teacher prepares seedlings days beforehand,checks species/sap allergies and hygiene;no mouldy cultures/unknown toxic plants/soil aerosols. Actual observable root/branch/hair detail depends on sample and lens;NOTSEEN does not mean absent. Model root hairs increase absorbing area/shorten distance to membrane;visible morphology alone does not demonstrate ion transport mechanism. Clean hands and trays after use.
PT2 — Water and ions need different explanations
Conceptual cases,not measured plant data:Asoil has higher water potential than root cell under suitable conditions;Broot cells accumulate an essential ion against its concentration gradient;Csoil solution becomes very concentrated. Explain water entry,possible energy-dependent ion uptake and a reason excess dissolved salts may hinder water uptake. Draw labelled soil→root→xylem pathway and distinguish a water-potential gradient from an ion-concentration gradient.
Teacher key and preparation
Water crosses selectively permeable cell membranes by osmosis down water potential under stated conditions;ion uptake uses selective transporters,with energy-dependent uptake possible against gradients. Not every ion movement is always active. Mineral ions are not food/sugars and do not enter by osmosis. Concentrated soil can lower water potential and reduce/reverse entry. Root pathways ultimately supply xylem;avoid claiming every water molecule crosses every cortex cell membrane.
PT3 — Cohesion,adhesion and transpiration pull
Use this supplied mechanism model:water evaporates from moist leaf cell-wall surfaces,vapour diffuses through stomatal openings,and tension is transmitted through connected xylem water. Explain how cohesion between water molecules and adhesion to walls support the column. Compare with a teacher-approved digital transport model;record what you actually viewed and one model limit. If no device,use this printed model and mark actual digital viewing NOT OBSERVED.
Teacher key and preparation
Cohesion/adhesion support water columns;evaporation and water-potential gradients generate a transpiration pull. Capillarity alone is not a complete explanation for tall-tree ascent;no claim leaves pump by muscular action. Root pressure may contribute in some conditions but is not the sole general driver. Digital animation is a representation,not an observation of the learner’s specimen. No water experiments with electricity.
PT4 — Source and sink can change
Draw two labelled source→phloem→sink models:(1)mature photosynthesising leaf supplying growing roots and shoot tip;(2)sprouting storage organ supplying developing shoot before its leaves photosynthesise sufficiently. Explain why phloem transport need not always point downward. State the role of sugars,loading/unloading,water and pressure differences without treating all loading strategies as identical.
Teacher key and preparation
Sucrose/other organic solutes travel in living phloem from source to sink;different tubes/routes may carry in different directions simultaneously. Stored reserves can make a tuber/bulb a source and a developing organ a sink;roles change. Loading/unloading and water movement contribute to pressure-driven bulk flow;energy may be used in loading/maintenance,not a tiny pump at every segment. Xylem water/mineral transport differs;phloem also contains water.
PT5 — What evidence tests a phloem model?
Research context:Knoblauch and colleagues(2016),Testing the Münch hypothesis of long distance phloem transport in plants,eLife5:e15341,DOI10.7554/eLife.15341. The researchers combined pressure,flow and anatomical resistance measurements in morning glory to test whether pressure-driven transport was physically plausible. This short account gives no copied numerical result. Separately consider fictional teaching evidence:labelled sugar appears in sinks connected by intact phloem,whereas an interrupted phloem path changes its arrival. What is supported,and which alternative damage/source effects need controls? Does coloured water in xylem prove sugar translocation?
Teacher key and preparation
Primary research DOIhttps://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.15341;abstract/archive search evidence supports this narrow design description,not a claim all transport questions were settled. Fictional tracer case supports a phloem-path association with suitable sham/healthy controls,not direct proof from colour or uncontrolled injury. Coloured water usually illustrates a water route,not organic sugar phloem translocation. No learner ring-barking,radioactive tracer,aphid handling or destructive sap sampling;research interpretation is not performed phloem experimentation. Background physiologyhttps://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-5-transport-of-water-and-solutes-in-plants;use qualified statements because some textbook absolutes are overgeneralised.
PT6 — Plan a mass-loss investigation
Teacher prepares stable leafy-shoot mass potometers:each safe plastic water vessel is sealed around the shoot and every exposed water surface is sealed,leaves dry;a matched sealed vessel without shoot tests background mass loss/leaks. Teacher alone cuts/assembles supports shoots and keeps all electronics dry. Plan airflow comparison and a separate structural-area comparison. Record start/end mass(g),duration(min),background loss,conditions,leaf-area proxy,replicateID and named actions. Explain why mass loss and bubble displacement measure different quantities.
Teacher key and preparation
Primary method contexthttps://www.saps.org.uk/teaching-resources/resources/1263/investigating-transpiration-with-a-potometer/ distinguishes mass loss and bubble water uptake,and recommends technician assembly. This is an independently specified low-risk mass-loss design;teacher must pilot actual plant/balance sensitivity,seals and duration. Net mass loss mainly reflects water loss over short runs with negligible other mass exchange,not exact transpiration under every condition. Bubble potometer records uptake as proxy;distance/time is not itself water volume. No untested equipment is assumed ready.
PT7 — Perform an environmental-factor comparison
With teacher-approved preassembled mass potometers,compare still air with gentle hand-fan airflow for10minutes using similar shoots and sealed no-shoot backgrounds. Keep light,temperature,humidity,duration,starting hydration and leaf-area proxy as comparable as possible;record remaining differences. Take actual start/end masses on a dry stable balance away from drafts;stop fan during each weighing. Use at least3replicates per condition across class groups and retain raw values. Each learner performs a named weighing/timing/recording/check action. Do not invent the expected direction if change is too small.
Teacher key and preparation
Teacher pilots10minute detectability and supplies sufficient matched equipment/backgrounds;extend session/opportunities if necessary,not fabricate data. Hand fan keeps electricity away from water;standardise distance/cadence and acknowledge airflow not calibrated. Compute netloss=(start-end)-backgroundloss,rate/netduration. Positive netloss under fan often supports greater water loss under measured conditions;humidity/water-stress/stomatal responses may differ. Class replicates are not automatically independent if same shoot reused;report design. Demonstration/supplied values cannot certify individual investigation.
PT8 — Perform a structural leaf-area comparison
Use teacher-prepared comparable shoots with full leaf area versus reduced area;teacher removes selected leaves before class and seals/standardises cut sites where appropriate. Keep hydration,time,ambient conditions,shoottype and setup comparable;trace remaining leaf outlines on squared paper for one-sided area proxy. Collect10minute mass losses and sealed no-shoot backgrounds,at least3replicates per condition across class. Record actual named actions and compare total loss plus rate per area if areas known. Identify a confound from plant variation,wounding or time rather than calling this a perfect isolated test.
Teacher key and preparation
Leaf area is a structural factor;larger area can support more total water loss when other factors comparable. One-sided outline is a stated proxy,not exact full stomatal surface;donot silently double and treat both sides equivalent. Reduced-leaf pairedshoot design can confound size/wounding/physiology;use replicates/randomised assignment and report limits. Teacher does all cutting;no blades/pupil sap exposure. Teacher pilots sensitivity and may extend timing;unperformed actions NOTOBSERVED. Separate this from environmental comparison rather than interpreting uncontrolled leaf differences as pure airflow effect.

Explain how light,humidity,temperature,stomatal aperture,cuticle or leaf area may affect loss without claiming all responses are universally monotonic.
Teacher key and preparation
Correctedstill0.18,0.20,0.16g,mean0.18g,rate0.018g/min;fan0.33,0.35,0.34g,mean0.34g,rate0.034g/min. Fan association in this fictional controlled comparison,not realclass evidence. Structural rates0.02and0.01g/min,each0.0005g/(mincm²). Moretotal area neednot higher per-area rate. Higher humidity often reduces vapour gradient;light/heat/air movement responses depend on stomata/water supply. If background large/drifting,repeat/check leaks;negative corrected loss is uncertainty/failure evidence,not forced positive transpiration.
PT10 — Water transport supports life but has costs
Explain how transpiration-linked transport supplies water/mineral movement,cooling and tissue hydration while excessive loss can cause wilting/stomatal closure and reduce carbon uptake. Prepare a short checked awareness message for a teacher-approved class/school audience on appropriate watering and nutrient management. Name source-listed fertilisers DAP,CAN,urea as nutrient sources,not plant food or universal cures. Explain why soil/plant need,label/local expert guidance and avoiding runoff/overapplication matter. Do not handle fertilisers/manure or give dose prescriptions.
Teacher key and preparation
Use transport reasoning and one explicit benefit/limit. Fertilisers provide mineral nutrients rather than replacing photosynthesis;organic manure needs safe managed use. Concentrated salts/runoff can harm plants/environment;watering also must fit conditions,not constant flooding. Teacher reviews the message and arranges any actual class/school sharing;record named presentation/contribution,not claim community impact. No automatic external messages or chemical handling. Source suggested communityawareness is adapted through approved classroom audience;actual community engagement remains teacher confirmation.
PT11 — Audit transport claims
Correct:(1)roots absorb sugars as mineral food by osmosis;(2)phloem flows only down;(3)bubble speed directly equals transpiration volume;(4)coloured water proves sugar transport;(5)all plants should always maximise water loss;(6)reading supplied mass data proves you performed the experiment. For each give a corrected mechanism/evidence limit and link one correction to a useful watering/nutrient message.
Teacher key and preparation
Ion uptake differs from osmosis and plant photosynthate food;phloemsource/sinkroutescanpointdifferentdirections;bubblespeedrequirescrosssection/uptake assumptions andisnotdirectevaporationvolume;dyedxylemrouteisnotsugarphloem;waterlossbenefits/risksmustbalance;performedactionneedsactualobservation. A specific supported benefit may evidence reasoned appreciation,not lasting attitude. No reagent/clinical recommendation.
