The Cell — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
CL1 — Choose an instrument for a question
Make a comparison table for conventional light microscopy,TEM andSEM:illumination,what image information is obtained,specimen preparation/living-state limits and likely resolving detail. Decide which is appropriate to observe a living cell moving,to examine internal membranes in a prepared thin section,and to examine surface form. Explain why an electron image is not automatically a natural-colour picture of a living cell. These are instrument-choice cases,no pupil vacuum/electron apparatus experiment.
CL2 — Bigger image or more resolved detail?
Hypothetical instrument specifications for reasoning only:Aresolves separations of0.2µm,Bresolves0.005µm. Two features are0.05µmapart. Which instrument could resolve them under these stated limits? If an Aimage is enlarged10times on a screen,does this create new detail? Convert0.2µmto nm. Separate calibration exercise,not a measurement of the supplied photos:a2µmscale bar prints20mmlong,a feature prints50mmlong;estimate feature length and explain why resizing both together preserves the ratio.
CL3 — Plant light evidence:an onion root tip
Observe the genuine supplied light micrograph. Draw a small evidence outline and identify at least two defensible features,then list two things this image does not resolve. Distinguish stained nuclei/chromosomes and cell boundaries from a separately resolved plasma membrane. Explain why absent visible chloroplasts in this nonphotosynthetic root sample do not mean every plant cell lacks them. Caption:onion(Allium cepa)root-tip whole mount,acetocarmine stain,brightfield. State one function using supplied biological knowledge,not a claim that a still image directly measures it. Explain why the photo does not establish every organelle's presence/absence. CL7 — Function knowledge is not the same as a visual observation
Use these supplied function facts to build a structure→function→cell-benefit table:nucleus contains most cell DNA and supports gene regulation;plasma membrane selectively regulates exchange;cytoplasm provides a medium for many reactions;ribosomes synthesise proteins;roughERsupports synthesis/processing/transport of some proteins;Golgimodifies/sorts/packages products;mitochondria support ATP production in aerobic respiration. Match each to a defensible model location. Mark each entry OBSERVED IN A SPECIFIC IMAGE,KNOWN BIOLOGICAL FUNCTION orMODEL REPRESENTATION,as appropriate. Do not pretend an image of a fold directly proves a biochemical rate.
CL8 — Compare generalised models without erasing real exceptions
Draw two explicitly labelled generalised models:a photosynthetic plant cell and a nucleated animal cell. Add the shared structures/functions from CL7and these plant features:cell wall for support/protection,chloroplasts for photosynthesis,large sap vacuole/tonoplast system contributing storage and turgor. Use CL5/CL6as limited evidence rather than pretending each diagram is a traced complete cell. Compare wall with plasma membrane;explain why CL3root tissue need not show chloroplasts and why selected model features are not universal in every specialised cell.
CL9 — Make the levels-of-organisation links explicit
Construct two concept maps with labelled relationships:mouse cardiac muscle cell→cardiac muscle tissue→heart→circulatory system→mouse;onion root cell→root tissue→root→root system→onion plant. State how a tissue differs from an organ and why organelles are parts of cells,not tissues. Add the case'a unicellular organism consists of one cell':must it have tissues and organs? Explain a specific reason cells are basic units in these examples without claiming the fixed micrographs demonstrate living processes.
CL10 — Construct two models and show what they cannot do
Using clean reused card,paper,string,tape or safe modelling material,construct a relief/3Dgeneralised plant model and a nucleated animal model with labelled compartments and a function key. Base features on CL7–CL8and state which selected micrographs support which parts. Record each learner's actual contribution and at least three limits:scale,colour,section/3Dgeometry,organelle positions or lack of living activity. Revise a model after a peer's evidence-based criticism. Drawings/plans alone must be marked NOT CONSTRUCTED.
CL11 — Find,save and credit a checked cell claim
With an approved device,search a teacher-selected primary repository/source for one plant/animal cell structure as viewed by electron microscopy. Approved starting records:https://www.cellimagelibrary.org/images/39755 andhttps://doi.org/10.1186/s40529-026-00499-x . Record actual search/retrieval steps,title,organism/tissue,method,one structure claim,URL/date,licence/credit and a saved short original evidence panel. Share it with a partner and revise if metadata does not support your claim. Do not enter personal data or create accounts. If using the supplied offline packet only,label actual online search/retrieval NOT OBSERVED rather than inventing it.
CL12 — Audit an overconfident cell poster
Audit these claims:Azooming a blurred image creates new resolved organelles;Bno chloroplast is visible in the onion root image,so it is not plant tissue;Callanimal cells have exactly the model's shape and nucleus;Dthe mouse TEMshows a whole generic cell;Eevery structure's function was directly observed in a still micrograph;Fa one-celled organism must have tissues. Repair each with evidence/limits. Review your actual constructed-model/source log separately from written explanations. Give one supported benefit of understanding cells as basic units of organisms.
