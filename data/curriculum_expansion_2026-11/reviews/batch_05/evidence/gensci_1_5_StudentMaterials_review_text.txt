Respiration — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
RS1 — Respiration is a cellular energy-transfer process
Sort these concepts:breathing/gas exchange,cellular respiration,photosynthesis,ATP-dependent active transport and heat loss. Explain how living plant and animal cells transfer chemical energy from substrates to ATP-linked processes. Give one specific use in each organism. Do not claim energy is created or that plants only photosynthesise.
RS2 — Aerobic glucose:atoms and energy
Use C6H12O6+6O2→6CO2+6H2Owith energy transferred to ATP-linked processes and heat. Check carbon,hydrogen and oxygen atoms on each side. Explain oxygen’s role in the usual aerobic pathway and distinguish cytoplasmic glycolysis from later mitochondrial stages in a typical eukaryotic cell. Explain why total ATPyield is not a universal fixed number across all cells/conditions.
RS3 — Two fermentation examples are not every anaerobic pathway
Compare school examples:(A)glucose→2lactate with net2ATPfrom glycolysis;(B)glucose→2ethanol+2CO2 with net2ATPfrom glycolysis. Explain that fermentation regenerates the carriers needed for glycolysis and has lower ATPyield than complete aerobic oxidation. Compare with microorganisms that use nonoxygen terminal acceptors in true anaerobic respiration;do not label every anaerobic process identical. State which example is theoretical only under the source caution.
RS4 — RQ calculation and its limits
For same-condition corrected gas amounts,calculate RQ=CO2produced/O2consumed:(A)12mL/12mL;(B)7mL/10mL;(C)8mL/10mL;(D)5mL/0mL;(E)0mL/0mL. Relate A/B/Ccautiously to approximate complete-aerobic carbohydrate/fat/protein or mixed-substrate models. Use glucose stoichiometry6/6and palmitate C16H32O2+23O2→16CO2+16H2Oto compare ratios. Explain why zero denominators and mixed/nonsteady conditions prevent a unique substrate diagnosis.
RS5 — Factors and a fair rate comparison
Fictional corrected gas-production records for a defined same-mass yeast preparation over5min:15°C5mL,25°C12mL,35°C15mL. Calculate rates and state a qualified temperature association. Identify controls for substrate concentration,organism amount,time,pH and apparatus. Explain why raising temperature indefinitely need not increase respiration and why CO2rate alone does not prove aerobic/anaerobic pathway or ATPyield. Discuss activity/growth-related energy demand in general without personal health measurements.
RS6 — Audit supplied yeast and acidity evidence
Fictional teaching record:not your experiment. Defined yeast+sugar at25°Cproduces12mLgas/5min;no-sugar preparation2mL;heat-inactivated preparation0.5mL. All start volumes/masses matched;oxygen was not measured or excluded. Another supplied lactic-culture record changes pH6.6to4.5. Explain what each supports and what is still unknown. Is pHdrop alone proof of a specific microbe,safe food or ATPyield?
RS7 — Plan an approved lactic-fermentation project
Choose only the teacher-approved commercial yoghurt-starter/pasteurised-milk route,not yeast/alcohol or waste-biogas culture. Prepare a record:batchID,milk/starter source and dates,teacher preparation/time/temperature checkpoints,pHbefore/after,consistency from outside container,actual named learner action,exceptions/costs and disposal. Label every school-lab container NOT FOR CONSUMPTION. School/teacher biosafety approval,allergy exclusion,controlled equipment and an adult6–8hour monitoring plan are required before active preparation;otherwise use supplied records and mark actual project NOT OBSERVED.
RS8 — Start and log the actual approved project
Only after teacher approval:teacher prepares a small covered batch with commercially pasteurised milk,fresh plain labelled live-culture commercial yoghurt starter,clean sanitised equipment and a temperature-controlled yoghurt maker. Teacher handles all heating/cooling and culture handling. Learners perform safe approved labelling,timing/temperature-record checks and initial pH-record observations using teacher-dispensed cooled aliquots,without tasting,touching culture or returning tested material. Record actual actions and source/time data. Incubation continues under adult monitoring between lessons;no pupil takes a batch home.
RS9 — Follow up without claiming a safe food product
At the next scheduled lesson,teacher brings the approved chilled labelled batch and complete adult time/temperature log. From outside the container observe consistency;teacher dispenses cooled tiny test aliquots for pHpaper or calibrated meter readings and collects waste. Record actual before/after pH,tool resolution,dates/temperature deviations and each named checking/recording action. Never taste,smell closely,sell or distribute school-lab products. Compare results with the separate supplied record only if actual evidence exists;failure or unavailable culture remains honest data/NOT OBSERVED.
RS10 — Economic value,real costs and biogas limits

Explain a useful lactic-fermentation application and a separate biogas benefit/limit using a paper process model. No school product is sold and no gas is generated or ignited.
RS11 — Audit six respiration claims
Correct:(1)plants only photosynthesise;(2)respiration means breathing;(3)every anaerobic process produces CO2;(4)RQ5/0=0and identifies fat;(5)more warmth always gives more ATP;(6)acidic school yoghurt is safe to taste and confirms you performed every project step.
