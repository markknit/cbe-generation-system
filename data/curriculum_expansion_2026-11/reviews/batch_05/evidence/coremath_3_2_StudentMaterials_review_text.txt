Probability I — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
PI1 — Range depends on a defined event/model
For an ideal fair six-face die with sample space{1,2,3,4,5,6},find probabilities of getting7,getting an integer1–6,and getting an even number. Locate each on a0to1line. Explain why -0.2and1.2cannot be probabilities. Does saying an event is possible establish its numerical probability?
PI2 — Perform and record a coin experiment
Use a teacher-approved clean Kenyan coin or two-labelled token and a shallow tray on a stable table. Identify the coin as currency respectfully;denomination/value is irrelevant to the chance model. Perform20safe low tosses,record every H/Tresult in order and cumulative counts. Declare beforehand how an edge/escaped/obstructed trial is recorded invalid and repeated;keep invalid count. Each learner performs a named toss/record/check action. Calculate actual Hrelativefrequency;do not force10heads or invent missing trials.
PI3 — Perform and record a die experiment
Use a teacher-approved six-face die and tray. Perform30valid gentle rolls;record full ordered outcomes,frequencies1–6and count even results. Predeclare invalid cocked/escaped rolls and retain count. Compare actual even relativefrequency with the ideal fair-die model1/2and with another group’s honest record. Each learner performs a named roll/record/check action;do not alter results to fit the model.
PI4 — Generate a complete probability space
For independent ideal fair coin and fair die,list all12ordered outcomes(HorT,dieface). Define eventA=heads and even die,eventB=tail or die6. Find each probability by counting equally likely outcomes. Explain why this counting would need different weights if the coin/die were biased or dependent.
PI5 — When may probabilities simply add?
One ideal fair die:eventsA={1},B={6};findP(AorB)andP(AandB). Then C=even={2,4,6},D=prime={2,3,5};findP(CorD)andP(CandD). State which pair is mutually exclusive,and useP(CorD)=P(C)+P(D)-P(CandD).
PI6 — Independence is a probability relationship
Two independent ideal fair coin tosses:compareA=firstHwithB=secondH;list four ordered outcomes and testP(AandB)=P(A)P(B). One fair die:compareC=evenwithD=greaterthan3;test independence. Explain why mutually exclusive and independent mean different things.
PI7 — Replacement changes the stages
An opaque bag contains3redand2blueequal-size tokens;blind uniform draw,replace and mix between draws. FindP(two red),P(red then blue)andP(exactly one red). Compare onlyP(two red)if first token is not replaced,assuming uniform remaining draw. Explain which stages are independent and why.
PI8 — Draw an independent two-stage tree
Draw a full probability tree for two independent fair coin tosses. Label first/secondH/Tbranches,all four ordered paths and path probabilities. FindP(exactlyoneH),P(atleastoneH)andP(noH). Explain why branch probabilities at a node sum1,why a path multiplies,and why disjoint selected terminal paths add.
PI9 — Tree weights need not be equal
Draw the full tree for the replaced/mixed3red2bluebag in PI7. Label all branch and path probabilities. DetermineP(atleastone red)using selected paths and using1-P(no red). Compare withP(exactlyone red).
PI10 — Make and play an honest no-stakes game
In pairs design a tray-die game with two declared winning faces and four nonwinning faces under an ideal fair-die model. State rules,all outcomes,theoretical win probability and a predeclared invalid-roll rule. Play12valid rounds with no money,prizes,wagers or personal advantage;retain ordered actual outcomes and winrelativefrequency. Rotate actual roll/record/check roles and accept honest results. Explain why a losing streak does not make a win due.
PI11 — Useful probability does not excuse unsafe choices
Explain one useful probability application to a modelled game,planning or repeated sampling,and one assumption/limit. Check a teacher-approved digital/printed explanation of an addition/tree claim against PI5orPI8;record actual device action separately. For a fictional road-awareness message,explain why unsafe crossing must not be justified by a guessed chance of no crash. No numerical crash probability is supplied or inferable here;do not collect roadside data or encourage risk-taking.
