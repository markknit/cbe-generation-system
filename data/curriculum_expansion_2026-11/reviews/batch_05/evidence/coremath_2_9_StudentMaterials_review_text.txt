Linear Motion — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
LM1 — Path length and signed change
On a fictional straight path,east is positive. A toy starts atx=0m,moves to6m,then returns to2m. Total time5s. Draw the path with arrows and find distance travelled,displacement,average speed and average velocity. Explain why the last two differ. Distinguish each from acceleration;no final velocity is supplied. This is a paper scenario,not a road activity.
LM2 — Units before arithmetic
Convert36km/hto m/s and12m/sto km/h. A fictional traveller covers18km in30minutes;find average speed in km/handm/s. A different object's displacement is−24m in6s;find average velocity and explain what cannot be inferred about its distance or final velocity. Show conversions using units,not an unexplained factor.
LM3 — A timed activity cannot supply unmeasured acceleration
Teacher-approved activity on a clear level school area:walk a short marked straight distance at a comfortable pace,or use a safely guided tabletop trolley where access needs require. Roles are mover/timer/recorder;keep identifiers anonymous and avoid ranking learners. Record measured positions,times,repeats and uncertainty. Determine average velocity over each measured interval. Do not infer acceleration from only total distance and elapsed time. If no safe practical opportunity,use this fictional example and label it supplied data:displacement2m in4s. Name additional velocity observations needed for acceleration.
LM4 — Draw a displacement-time model from a table
Fictional straight-line model withxmeasured from fixed originO,east positive. Time(s):0,2,4,6,8. Displacement fromO(m):0,4,4,0,−4. Draw axes with units and join successive points by straight lines as the stated piecewise-uniform model. Find velocity on each interval,rest intervals,total distance,change in displacement and average speed/velocity over8s. State what the sharp corners do not describe about real turning/braking.
LM5 — Read the supplied displacement graph
Use the printed graph,where displacement is measured from fixedO,not automatically from the starting point. Determine initial/final positions,change in displacement,each interval velocity,total distance and the meaning of its horizontal section. Compare thegraph's height with slope. Explain why ending at−2mdoesnot imply the journey's displacement was−2m. The graph is a mathematical model with straight segments between points.

LM6 — Acceleration is a signed rate of velocity change
Compute average acceleration for these fictional intervals:Avelocity+2to+8m/sin3s;Bvelocity−6to−2m/sin2s;Cvelocity+3to−1m/sin2s. State direction of acceleration and whether speed increases,decreases or changes through zero under a constant-acceleration assumption. Explain why 'negative acceleration always means slowing' is false. Show velocity change and time separately.
LM7 — Draw a velocity-time graph and use its area
Fictional model,time(s):0,2,4,6;velocity(m/s):0,4,4,0. Draw the graph using straight segments between entries as the stated constant-acceleration/constant-velocity model. Find accelerations on each interval and displacement from signed areas. Find total distance and explain why it equals displacement magnitude here. Do not call a horizontal nonzero velocity segment 'stopped'.
LM8 — A velocity graph can cross zero
Use the supplied velocity-time graph. Find acceleration on each interval,the time velocity becomes zero,net displacement and total distance over6s. Split area where velocity changes sign. Identify an interval when negative acceleration occurs while speed increases. Explain why graph height and area answer different questions.

LM9 — Closing or separating depends on directions and positions
Paper scenarios only. A starts100mbehindB;both move east at20and15m/srespectively. Find closing speed and ideal catch-up time. In a second scenario,Cstarts atx0andmoves east10m/s,Dstarts atx90mandmoves west8m/s:find closing speed,meeting time and meeting position. If they instead move away from each other at those speeds,does the same speed sum describe closing? State assumptions and units.
LM10 — An ideal passing time is not a safe-overtaking decision
Fictional one-line model:vehicleAis4mlong at20m/s,vehicleBis8mlong at15m/s,both east. At time0A'sfront is level withB'srear. Find relative distance A must gain for its rear just to clearB'sfront,and ideal time. Draw the start/end alignments. List missing real-road information that prevents using this number as permission to overtake. Observe only a teacher-selected recording/paper diagram,never live traffic from a road edge.
LM11 — Audit graph claims and the origin of the data
Audit:Aa horizontal displacement-time graph means rest;Ba horizontal velocity-time graph always means rest;Cnegative velocity means negative distance;Ddistance/time alone determines acceleration;Earea below a velocity axis is subtracted for displacement but added by magnitude for distance. Explain each decision with a sketch/example. With an approved tool,plot one supplied table and compare slopes/areas with paper;record actual tool/inputs/output. Review your actual timed-activity log separately from fictional graph records. Paper work alone does not establish a digital action.
