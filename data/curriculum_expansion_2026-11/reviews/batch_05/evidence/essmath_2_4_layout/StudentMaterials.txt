Area of Polygons — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy


PA1 — Two sides must enclose the angle
Draw a triangle with sides8cm and10cm enclosing30°. Drop a perpendicular to show
why its area is(1/2)ab sinC. Compute area. Explain why using a30°angle elsewhere
without checking which sides enclose it would be invalid. Compare with side
pair6cm,9cm, included60°;round final area to2decimalplaces.


PA2 — Measure a real paper triangle
On clean blank paper construct triangle with two legs from a common vertex of
approximately8cm and10cm enclosing approximately30°using ruler/protractor;teacher
may supply a safe triangle cut-out instead. Each learner measures both chosen sides
and their included acute angle,records instrument/resolution,and computes a measured
area with the sine rule. Independently measure a perpendicular height for a base-height
comparison. Do not replace actual readings with the ideal design values.


PA3 — Heron uses three valid sides
For a triangle with sides13,14,15cm,find semiperimeter s=(a+b+c)/2and A=sqrt[s(s-a)(s-
b)(s-c)]. State every substitution and units. Repeat for3,4,5cm,and check the latter by a
right-triangle base-height method. Explain why perimeter and semiperimeter differ.


PA4 — Check feasibility before a square root
Test side sets2,3,6cm and2,3,5cm:can each form a nondegenerate triangle? For a safe
paper triangle each learner measures all three sides and computes Heron area with the
actual values;compare with a base-height check if possible. Do not ignore a
negative/zero radicand or force a physical result.


PA5 — Parallelogram:sloping side is not height
A parallelogram has adjacent sides8cm and5cm with included acute angle30°. Draw and
compute perpendicular height to the8cmbase and area. Compare with another
parallelogram of the same sides but60°included angle. Explain why a constant perimeter
does not force constant area.


PA6 — Rhombus:two compatible area routes
A rhombus has side6cm and included acute angle30°. Compute area withs²sinθ.
Separately a rhombus has perpendicular diagonals10cm and8cm;compute area using
half their product and explain the perpendicular-triangle partition. Can you assume those
diagonal lengths belong to the first rhombus?

PA7 — Regular pentagon:from side to apothem
Use the supplied regular-pentagon schematic,not a scale measurement. Join centre to
vertices and side midpoints. Explain central angle360/5=72°and half-angle36°. For
side4cm,derive apothem a=(s/2)/tan36°and area(1/2)(5s)a. Round final area
to2decimalplaces. Explain which equal-angle/side assumptions depend on regularity.




PA8 — Regular hexagon splits into six equilateral triangles
Use supplied regular-hexagon schematic. Explain central angle60°and why centre-to-
vertex radius equals side s. For side4cm,compute area6*(1/2)s²sin60°and compare
with(1/2)perimeter×apothem,whereapothem=s cos30°. Explain why a six-sided figure
alone is not enough.

PA9 — A measured classroom design application
Choose teacher-approved regular pentagon/hexagon paper templates or safe flat
patterns;check approximate equal sides/angles before using regular formulas. Each
learner measures side length and records tool/precision;estimate area
and,wherepossible,check measured apothem. For a separate exact fictional regular-
hexagon tile side10cm,find one tile area;ten nonoverlapping whole tiles cover how much
area ignoring gaps? Explain why dividing a room area by tile area alone does not prove
an exact tiling or purchasing count.


PA10 — Audit the area claims
Correct:(1)Heron uses the full perimeter as s;(2)parallelogram area is always product of
adjacent side lengths;(3)any five-sided shape with equal sides has the regular-pentagon
area formula;(4)twice the length scale gives twice the area. Justify with a formula/model
and give one useful practical area application with a limitation.
