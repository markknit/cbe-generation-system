## Confirmed findings

### EM22-C1 — plural fixed-point claim is false

Lesson 7 `knowledge` says: “A distinct point-image pair determines its perpendicular bisector;fixed points alone are insufficient.” The same sentence appears in its `summary.learned`. Two **distinct** fixed points determine the mirror uniquely: it is the line through them. For example, (1,1) and (2,2) fixed imply y=x. One fixed point alone leaves infinitely many unrestricted plane mirror lines through that point. RF7’s teacher key uses that narrower single-pair statement correctly.

The false plural sentence propagates into the actual Compact Teacher Guide. Correct the knowledge and learned summary to specify a single fixed point/pair, preserving the distinction from two distinct fixed points. When discussing nonuniqueness, make clear the general plane interpretation: if candidates are restricted to the three syllabus lines, even one fixed point may select a unique candidate (for example (2,2) selects y=x). The final’s general single-fixed-point reasoning is defensible on the unrestricted interpretation, but should not be presented as a universal statement about every restricted candidate set.
