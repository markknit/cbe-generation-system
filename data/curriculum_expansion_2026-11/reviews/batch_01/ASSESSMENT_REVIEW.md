## General Science 1.1

Lesson 7:4 minutes reading/procedure,28 minutes for sections 1–4 (16 for sections 1–2 and 12 for sections 3–4),4 minutes for a separate still-open-question reflection, and 4 minutes checking/collection; total 40. Work independently using GS17 only; basic calculator optional. Explain your reasoning, distinguish observations from inferences and label suggested uses/career activities as examples. No teaching cards.

### 1. What is General Science useful for?

Explain General Science as a learning area. Use the new material-testing case to connect evidence to a possible technology decision, and give one different life/environment example. State one limitation of applying the test result.

**Teacher exemplar:** General Science connects inquiry and knowledge about living things, matter and physical processes. Testing material properties can inform a proposed design choice, but water-rise data alone cannot show that a material is always best. A garden comparison can inform questions about plant conditions, but needs comparable conditions to isolate a cause. Accept another reasoned life/environment link.

### 2. Observation, analysis and inference

Calculate both means. State one observation, an inference supported by the test and an alternative explanation for the difference. Evaluate the poster’s “always best” claim and propose one design change that would improve the comparison.

**Teacher exemplar:** Means X14 mm and Y8 mm. Water-line readings and recorded conditions are observations; greater mean rise for X under the tested conditions is supported. Unequal sunlight may contribute to the difference; it is not ruled out. “Always best” exceeds both the property measured and the tested conditions. Test both materials under comparable light with fresh repeated strips, keeping other relevant conditions comparable. Other justified alternatives accepted. Scoring clarification: the computed mean comparison may be called descriptive data analysis rather than inference. Accept that distinction. A suitable tentative inference is that material properties or exposure conditions may contribute to the difference; this confounded design cannot isolate either cause. Do not require a confident causal explanation.

### 3. Career evidence

Use both printed source summaries to identify two related career directions. For each give a possible science question or evidence type, label it as your proposed example, and say what the source actually supports. State one thing about training these summaries cannot establish.

**Teacher exemplar:** Weather/climate science can use instrument records to investigate rainfall patterns; the source states observation/analysis/forecast roles but the question is a proposed example. Human-health research can investigate a properly governed health-study question; the source supports a human-health research role rather than a specified personal duty. The summaries do not establish an entry qualification, duration, salary or guaranteed employment.

### 4. Reasoned reflection

Explain one way careful scientific reasoning could benefit your daily life. Identify one next learning action and explain why it would help. You may choose any relevant context; no particular career choice is required.

**Teacher exemplar:** An appropriate answer links a concrete decision to checking evidence and limits, then proposes an attainable action such as practising observation/inference classification or verifying a training question with an official source. Judge the reasoning and specificity, not the chosen preference or a claim to lasting attitude change.

## Essential Mathematics 1.1

Lesson 8,40 minutes including 4 minutes procedure and 4 minutes final checking. Work independently using RN8 and a calculator; no teaching cards. Give exact fractions where requested, show intermediate operations and use≈ for approximations. Teacher observes calculator use without providing calculation hints.

### 1. Classify exact numbers

Classify all five exact RN8 values as rational or irrational. Give fraction representations for rational values; identify which are integers. Explain why a rounded calculator display does not establish that√3 is rational.

**Teacher exemplar:** Rational:0/1,−7/4,0.272727…=3/11,√25=5/1. Integers 0 and 5. √3 irrational. A finite display represents an approximation rather than the full exact expansion of√3.

### 2. Combined operations

Calculate A=5/6−[(1/3)÷(−2)] and B=8÷4×3−(−2)². Show working, justify equal-priority processing and give one independent check.

**Teacher exemplar:** A=5/6−(−1/6)=1. B=(8÷4)×3−4=6−4=2. Multiplication/division are processed left to right at equal priority. A reverse check 1+(−1/6)=5/6 or a correct equivalent check is accepted.

### 3. Reciprocals and calculator evidence

Find the exact reciprocal of 2/5 and the reciprocal of√3 in exact form. Use a calculator to approximate the latter to 3 decimal places and record your entry/key sequence. Verify the rational reciprocal by multiplication and explain why 0 has no reciprocal.

**Teacher exemplar:** 5/2=2.5;1/√3=√3/3≈0.577. Entry equivalent to 1÷√(3); actual model sequence can vary. (2/5)×(5/2)=1. Zero times any real number equals 0, never 1. Observe actual calculator use separately from written correctness.

### 4. Interpret the context

Use RN8 to find the final liquid volume and signed credit balance. If the final liquid is poured into full 2/5 L portions, how many full portions fit and what volume remains? Show units, state an assumption and explain how exact rational reasoning improves this decision.

**Teacher exemplar:** Volume 5/6−1/4+1/3=11/12 L. Balance−20+65−18=27 KES. (11/12)÷(2/5)=55/24≈2.292 portion-equivalents, so only 2 full portions. Remainder 11/12−4/5=7/60 L. Assume exact stated volumes and no spills. Exact calculation distinguishes a fractional portion-equivalent from the whole-number count of full portions; accept another specific decision/check explanation.

## Core Mathematics 2.6

Lesson 12,40 minutes including 3 minutes procedure and 4 minutes final checking. Work independently with CM212 and a scientific calculator in degree mode. Show a labelled region/decomposition and an exact area expression; approximate to 3 decimal places where requested. Formula support on CM212 is allowed; no teaching cards. Use square units. ### 1. Ring area and a check

Find annulus A area exactly. Explain why the difference of squared radii is appropriate and verify by recombining with the inner disk.

**Teacher exemplar:** π(8²−5²)=39π cm²;39π+25π=64π restores the outer disk. Both circular boundaries share a centre; subtract the inner disk, not a disk of radius 8−5.

### 2. Sector and annular-sector comparison

Find B and C areas exactly and explain the difference between the two decompositions. Check that adding C to the matching radius 6 sector restores B.

**Teacher exemplar:** B=(80/360)π9²=18π cm². C=(80/360)π(81−36)=10π cm². Matching radius 6 sector 8π;10π+8π=18π. The hole removes the smaller sector with the same centre and angle.

### 3. Minor segment

Find D’s minor-segment area exactly and approximately. Explain the triangle subtraction and check that the result is positive and less than the sector.

**Teacher exemplar:** Sector 25π cm²; right central triangle 50 cm²; segment 25π−50≈28.540 cm². The chord bounds a region outside the triangle but within the 90° sector.0<25π−50<25π.

### 4. Intersecting-circle overlap

Find E’s common area exactly and approximately. Explain why two segment areas are added and why doubling is valid in this case. State a size check.

**Teacher exemplar:** Each segment(60/360)π36−(1/2)36 sin 60°=6π−9√3. Overlap 12π−18√3≈6.522 cm². The common chord divides the lens into congruent segments because equal radii/central angles give equal geometry. It is positive and less than one 36π cm² disk.

### 5. Explore a safe design use

Choose A or C as a proposed paper ornament. Explain what its computed area would estimate, one benefit of that estimate and one further datum/assumption needed for real material planning. Describe one safe next step to explore the proposed use. This is a written plan; no physical making is required.

Do not infer actual practical skill from the written plan.
