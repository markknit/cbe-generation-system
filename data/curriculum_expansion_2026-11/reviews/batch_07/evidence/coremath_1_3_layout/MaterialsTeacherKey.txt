Quadratic Expressions and Equations —
Materials Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy


CQE1 — What makes an expression quadratic?
Form an expression for each statement: twice the square of x plus three times x minus5;
the area of a rectangle with sides x and x+4; the product of two numbers x+1 and x−1.
Expand and collect terms. In each case identify the coefficient of x² and distinguish an
expression from an equation. Is x²−x²+3 quadratic after simplification? Here a quadratic
in x has form ax²+bx+c with a≠0.

Teacher key and preparation
Expressions2x²+3x−5,x(x+4)=x²+4x,(x+1)(x−1)=x²−1. Leading coefficients2,1,1. An
expression has no equality to solve; an equation states two expressions are equal.
x²−x²+3=3 is constant,not quadratic. A paper rectangle interpretation requires positive
side lengths; the algebraic expansion is an identity for every realx.


CQE2 — Derive a square identity from area
Use plain/reused paper and a ruler. Draw a square of side a+b with a,b positive lengths.
Mark distance a from the same corner on each adjacent side and draw parallel division
lines,creating four regions. Label each region’s two side lengths and its area. Use the
sum of all four areas to derive(a+b)². Check the construction with a=4cm,b=2cm,then
state the general identity. In pairs write the derivation on a chart,retaining each learner’s
labelled drawing. No cutting is required.

Teacher key and preparation
Four regions have areasa²,ab,ab,b²,so(a+b)²=a²+2ab+b². With4cm,2cm,the
total36cm²=16+8+8+4. Both cross rectangles must be counted. The area construction
assumes positive lengths; expanding(a+b)(a+b) confirms the identity for all reala,b. A
group chart does not prove each learner drew/derived it; inspect individual labels and
reasoning.


CQE3 — Subtract strips without losing their overlap
Draw a square side a with0<b<a. To leave the corner square side a−b,remove a strip
width b along the top and another along the right;explain the overlap square and
derive(a−b)². Separately remove only a b-by-b corner square from an a-by-a square.
Partition the remaining L into rectangles of dimensions a×(a−b) and b×(a−b),then
describe rearranging them into(a+b)×(a−b). Derive a²−b². Check both models with
a=5cm,b=2cm. Draw and label;no cutting is required.

CQE4 — Use identities for numerical calculations
Without a calculator,choose an identity and evaluate102²,98²,51×49 and2.5². Show the
nearby convenient number and every cross term or product used. Verify one result by
direct multiplication. Explain why(a+b)² cannot be replaced by a²+b².

Teacher key and preparation
102²=(100+2)²=10000+400+4=10404;98²=(100−2)²=10000−400+4=9604;51×49=(50+1)
(50−1)=2500−1=2499;2.5²=(2+0.5)²=4+2+0.25=6.25. Example check102×102=10404.
Omitting2ab loses both cross regions;use a=b=1 as a counterexample:4≠2.


CQE5 — Factor out the entire common factor
Factor6x²+9x,x²−5x and−2x²+8x,then expand each answer to check. Explain why taking
out a factor is different from dividing an equation by that factor,and why an x factor must
not be discarded when solving a later equation.

Teacher key and preparation
6x²+9x=3x(2x+3);x²−5x=x(x−5);−2x²+8x=−2x(x−4). Equivalent correctly expanded
factors are accepted;complete integer factorisation is preferred. These are identities
includingx=0. Dividing an equation byx would requirex≠0 and could lose a valid zero
solution;factoring alone does not make that restriction.


CQE6 — Factor monic quadratics by expanding candidate pairs
For x²+5x+6,x²−x−6,x²−9 andx²+6x+9,find integer factor pairs and verify each by
expansion. Explain how the sum/product of candidate constants controls the
middle/constant terms. Identify a repeated factor and a difference of squares.

Teacher key and preparation
(x+2)(x+3),(x−3)(x+2),(x−3)(x+3),(x+3)². For(x+p)(x+q),the expansion isx²+(p+q)x+pq. A
repeated factor does not create two different numerical roots when the expression is
later set to0. Factoring an expression is not yet solving an equation.


CQE7 — Factor a quadratic whose leading coefficient is not1
Factor2x²+7x+3,6x²−x−2 and2x²−8,checking by expansion. In the first two,show either a
split middle term/grouping or explicit candidate-product reasoning. Does the absence of
an integer-factor pair prove a quadratic is meaningless? Explain the limitation using
x²+1,which is positive for every realx. No quadratic formula or complex-number method
is required.

CQE8 — Form an equation from roots or a model
Form one monic quadratic equation whose roots are2 and−3,and one whose repeated
root is3. Show the factors and expansion. A fictional rectangle has widthx and
lengthx+2,with area15square units:form its equation in zero form,stating the side-length
condition. Do not assume roots are known merely because the equation is formed.

Teacher key and preparation
(x−2)(x+3)=0 givesx²+x−6=0;(x−3)²=0 givesx²−6x+9=0. Other nonzero multiples have the
same roots,but monic form fixes the requested representative. The rectangle
equationx(x+2)=15 becomesx²+2x−15=0,withx>0. Forming an equation from a context
precedes solving and checking it.


CQE9 — Why does a zero product split into cases?
For real factors,the product is0 exactly when at least one factor is0. Use this to solve
x²−5x+6=0 and2x²−6x=0,showing factors and substitution checks. Audit a learner who
divides the second equation byx and keeps onlyx=3. Can(x−2)(x+3)=7 be solved by
setting each factor to0? Test the proposedx=2 andexplain the condition;you need not
solve that different equation here.

Teacher key and preparation
(x−2)(x−3)=0 givesx=2,3. 2x(x−3)=0 givesx=0,3;substitution verifies both. Dividing byx
without considering0 loses a solution. For the product equal7,neither factor-zero case
works:atx=2 the product is0,not7. The zero-product law needs a zero right-hand side;this
observation does not claim the equation equal7 has no solutions.


CQE10 — Rearrange, factor and keep every valid root
Solve by factorisation and check each distinct real
root:2x²+7x+3=0;x²=25;x²+2x=15;x²+4x+4=0. First write a zero-form equation where
needed. Explain the difference between two factor occurrences and two distinct
numerical roots.

Teacher key and preparation
(2x+1)(x+3)=0 gives−1/2,−3;x²−25=(x−5)(x+5)=0 gives±5;x²+2x−15=(x+5)(x−3)=0
gives−5,3;(x+2)²=0 gives one distinct root−2. Substitute into the original equations,not
merely the rearranged factors. A repeated factor yields a repeated root,not two different
values.

CQE11 — Which algebraic roots describe the paper plan?
Two fictional paper planning models: A rectangle has widthx metres,lengthx+5metres
and area36m²; B a rectangular dot array hasx rows andx+1columns,total20dots. Form
and solve both quadratic equations by factorisation. State domain constraints,check the
selected values with units/counts,and explain why rejected algebraic roots still solve the
equation but not the stated model. No actual building or measurement is required.

Teacher key and preparation
A:x²+5x−36=(x+9)(x−4)=0 gives−9,4;choosewidth4m,length9m,check36m².
B:x²+x−20=(x+5)(x−4)=0 gives−5,4;choose4rows,5columns,check20dots. Physical
lengths must be positive and array counts positive integers. Negative roots are
algebraically valid but infeasible for these definitions. State exact idealised
dimensions/counts.


CQE12 — Explore when an area model is quadratic
Use these supplied teacher-prepared fictional case briefs as the actual paper
resource,not a claimed external search. A square page has unknown side xcm and
area49cm². B an inner rectangle xcm byx+3cm has a fixed1cm border outside all four
sides;the border alone has area30cm². Form each model,solve it andcompare degrees
after collecting terms. Explore changing A’s area to64cm² by posing and answering your
own question. Optional:if approved books/devices are supplied,locate and record one
actual further use,with its real title/tool and the mathematical model;otherwisemark
external/digital research NOT OBSERVED.

Teacher key and preparation
A:x²=49,roots±7,physicalside7cm;changedarea64 givesphysical8cm.
B:outerdimensionsx+2,x+5;borderarea(x+2)(x+5)−x(x+3)=4x+10,so4x+10=30
givesx=5cm. Check outer7×10=70,inner5×8=40,border30cm². B reduces to a linear
equation,so an area context is not automatically quadratic. The comparison and
learner’s answered variation are substantive paper exploration;do not invent a
book/device source or completed search.


CQE13 — Audit factors, missing roots and feasible answers
Repair each claim with evidence: A x²+6x+9=(x+3)(x−3); B ifx(x−4)=0,divide byx to get
the only root4; C a rectangle with widthx,lengthx+5 and area36 can usex=−9 because it
solves the equation. Show an expansion for A,a complete solution/check for B,and a
context constraint/check for C. Then revise one individual earlier error;only
afterwardscompare reasoning respectfully with a partner.

Teacher key and preparation
A correctfactorisation(x+3)² expands tox²+6x+9;the proposed product isx²−9. B
roots0and4;dividing byx discards0. C width−9m andlength−4m are not positive
lengths;usewidth4m,length9m despite−9 also solving the algebraic equation. Keep
expression identity,solution set andcontext feasibility distinct. Actual peer discussion
must be observed rather than inferred from a worksheet.
