Reflection and Congruence — Student
Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy


CRG1 — Exact symmetry and an observed object
Use ideal figures with these complete descriptions:
a6cmby4cmrectangle,a4cmsquare,an equilateral triangle,a scalene triangle with
vertices(0,0),(4,0),(1,2),and a circle. Draw or sketch each and identify every line of
symmetry,stating a count and reason. For a centred square include diagonal as well as
horizontal/vertical lines. If a safe teacher-selected desk object is available,observe it and
distinguish an ideal model from manufacturing/shape deviations;otherwise mark object
observation NOT OBSERVED. Do not collect glass,sharp litter or objects outside the
approved area. For an ideal2cmby3cmby4cmcuboid,describe one plane of symmetry
halfway between opposite faces and distinguish this3Dplane from a line on a2Dfigure.


CRG2 — An actual plane mirror and a mathematical prediction
Teacher supplies an approved plane mirror,preferably acrylic,and a small labelled object
on a paper surface. Mark the mirror line and place the object3cm in front along a
perpendicular. Individually predict the image location,then actually view and sketch the
apparent image when equipment is provided. Describe whether it is in front or behind the
mirror,and distinguish the virtual image from a physical object that could be touched
behind it. Use the plane-reflection model to label equal perpendicular distances;do not
claim a direct ruler measurement of an inaccessible virtual point. Teacher records
observed/not observed action. If no mirror is provided,complete only the paper prediction
and mark actual observation NOT OBSERVED.


CRG3 — Fold a line into a perpendicular bisector
Draw equal-unit axes and triangleA(1,1),B(4,1),C(2,3). The mirror line isx=0. On thin
paper,fold along the line and trace corresponding vertices when teacher-provided
materials permit;otherwise draw the coordinate construction and mark tracing NOT
OBSERVED. Join each object vertex to its image and locate the midpoint. State what
happens to distances,angles,points on the mirror and clockwise/counterclockwise order
of the labelled triangle. No cutting is required.


CRG4 — Horizontal and vertical mirror lines
Use equal-unit axes. ReflectP(0,1)andQ(3,−1)inx=2;reflectR(2,3)andS(−1,−1)iny=−1.
Separately reflect triangleT(0,0),U(2,0),V(1,2)iny=1. Draw the mirror lines and join
corresponding vertices. Explain the fixed point and the formulas x′=2a−x,y′=y
forx=a,andx′=x,y′=2b−y fory=b.

CRG5 — Diagonal reflections are not negative enlargements
Reflect triangleA(2,−1),B(3,1),C(1,4)firstiny=xandseparatelyiny=−x. Plot and label both
images on equal-unit axes. State the coordinate
rules,verifyP(2,2)isfixediny=xandQ(2,−2)isfixediny=−x. Compare the reflection
of(3,1)iny=−xwith its signed-factor−1enlargement about the origin.


CRG6 — Find a mirror line from two point pairs
TriangleA(0,0),B(2,0),C(0,1) maps by one reflection toA′(−1,1),B′(−1,3),C′(0,1). Plot all
points. Find midpoints ofAA′andBB′,construct their perpendicular bisectors with ruler/set
square,and check they give one mirror line. Determine its equation using the midpoint
coordinates,rise/run and intercept. Check every pair and explain why the fixed pointC
alone would not determine the line.


CRG7 — A mirror line with a different slope
One reflection mapsP(1,1)toP′(5,3)andQ(0,3)toQ′(4,5). On equal-unit axes,find both
midpoints and construct perpendicular bisectors using a ruler/set square. Identify the
common line. Use the two midpoint coordinates to find rise/run and the equation
y=mx+c. Verify it bisects both segments at right angles. Linear-line scaffolding:between
two different x values,m=change iny/change inx;then substitute one point to findc.


CRG8 — Congruence and labelled orientation
Use triangleA(0,0),B(3,0),C(1,2). Copy/plot its direct imageA′(2,1),B′(5,1),C′(3,3)and
opposite imageA″(0,0),B″(−3,0),C″(−1,2),using the stated correspondenceA→A′orA″,etc.
Compare corresponding lengths,angles and clockwise/counterclockwise order. With
teacher-provided paper shapes or safely precut labelled copies,check superposition with
and without turning over;record actual cutout use separately. Explain why identical area
alone cannot prove congruence.


CRG9 — Establish SSS and SAS with complete data
Pair1:trianglesABCandDEF haveAB=DE=3cm,BC=EF=4cm,CA=FD=5cm.
Pair2:AB=DE=4cm,AC=DF=6cm,and the included anglesAandD are50°. Draw or
construct the two cases with ruler/protractor as supplied,compare correspondence,and
name/justify a sufficient congruence test for each. Explain why SAS requires the angle
between the two given sides. Merely saying two sides and any angle is not the same
criterion.

third angle and state whether the side is included between the two originally given

CRG11 — Why RHS is a right-triangle criterion
TrianglesABCandDEF are right-angled atCandF,with hypotenusesAB=DE=5cm and
corresponding legsAC=DF=3cm. Identify the right angles,hypotenuses and given
matching legs;find the remaining legs and justify congruence. Would a right angle and
one leg alone suffice? Compare right triangles with legs3and4versus3and5. Then make
a four-test checklist for SSS,SAS,ASA/AAS andRHS,including the essential condition for
each.


CRG12 — Use reflection and congruence responsibly
Write a short advice card for a classmate with two specific checked uses:one ideal
plane-mirror/reflection example andone congruent-copy/stencil example justified by a
valid triangle test. Include the limit that many driving mirrors are curved,so the plane-
mirror equal-size coordinate model does not apply automatically. Discuss road-safety
uses only as a classroom example;no road visit or driving instruction. If approved digital
plotting is supplied,enter triangle(1,1),(3,1),(2,3),reflect iny=0,record the real
tool/inputs/output and check on paper. If no device is supplied,mark digital reflection
NOT OBSERVED. Share the advice only when directed,andrecordactualsharing
separately.
