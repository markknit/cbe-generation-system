Similarity and Enlargement — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
CSE1 — Similar is more than the same general shape
Use these complete descriptions. A andB are right triangles with corresponding side lengths(3,4,5) and(6,8,10). RectanglesC,D,E have sides2by3,4by6 and4by5 respectively,all angles90°. A rhombusF has four sides2and angles60°,120°,60°,120°;squareG has four sides2and four right angles. Decide which specified pairs A/B,C/D,C/E,F/G are similar. Give a correspondence,common positive length ratio and angle reason where appropriate.
CSE2 — Find a centre from matching vertices
Under one nonidentity enlargement,triangleA(2,1),B(4,1),C(2,3) maps toA′(3,1),B′(7,1),C′(3,5). Draw equal-unit perpendicular axes and plot all six points. Extend linesAA′ andCC′ to their intersection O;check BB′ passes through it. Find the image/object ratio of corresponding lengths and compare directed centre-to-point distances. Label the centre and linear scale factor. If no squared paper is supplied,draw axes with0.5cm per coordinate unit using a ruler.
CSE3 — Construct a positive enlargement
Use equal coordinate scales,one unit0.5cm on paper. O(0,0) is the centre;triangleP(1,1),Q(3,1),R(1,2) has scale factor2. Draw rays from O through each vertex and locate each image at twice its original centre distance. Join and label the image. Independently check with coordinates and compare corresponding side lengths and angles. Explain why simply adding2to every coordinate is a different transformation.
CSE4 — A reduction away from the origin
CentreO(2,1),scale factor1/2;triangleP(4,3),Q(8,3),R(4,7). Plot the original and place each image halfway from O to its vertex. Record image coordinates and verify each centre-to-point distance ratio. Explain why halving the original coordinates alone would give the wrong result. Use equal coordinate scales and a labelled centre.
CSE5 — A negative coordinate factor and physical lengths
On equal-unit axes,enlargeP(1,1),Q(3,1),R(1,2) aboutO(0,0) with signed factor−2. Draw the opposite rays and locate all images. Compare a corresponding side’s positive length ratio and the signed coordinate factor. Is this a reflection in a line? Explain why neither an area nor a volume becomes negative. State what factors0and1 would do.
CSE6 — Area follows two length directions
Compare similar rectangles3cmby2cm and9cmby6cm. Compute their areas,the common length ratio and the area ratio. Compare triangles with base4cm,height3cm and their factor2images with base8cm,height6cm. Also compare total surface areas of cubes with edges2cm and6cm. Derive why a positive length ratio k gives area ratio k²,including surface areas of geometrically similar solids.
CSE7 — Volume follows three length directions
Compare cubes with edges2cm and6cm,then similar cuboids2cmby3cmby4cm and6cmby9cmby12cm. Find both volumes and their ratios,and relate them to the common positive length ratio. A cube2cmperedge and a cuboid1cmby2cmby4cm both have volume8cm³:does equality of volume make them similar? Explain.
CSE8 — Recover a length ratio from area or volume
For geometrically similar figures with small-to-large area ratio4:25,find the positive large/small length ratio. For similar solids with small-to-large volume ratio8:125,find the same length ratio and corresponding area ratio. For a positive length ratio3,relate length,area and volume ratios. Explain why the assumption of similarity and the chosen ratio direction must be stated.
CSE9 — A model scale needs unit checks
A fictional rectangular model is4cmby6cm at scale1:50,meaning1modelcm represents50actualcm in every length direction. Find actual dimensions in metres,the model area in cm²,the actual area in m² and the actual/model area ratio after using the same area units. Explain why multiplying the model area by50 alone fails. No actual building,site visit or measurement is implied.
CSE10 — Design two similar learning models
Project design stage only. Plan two ideal closed paper cubes with target edge lengths2cm and4cm. For each cube,draw a net of six equal squares:use cell positions(row0,col1),(row1,col0),(row1,col1),(row1,col2),(row2,col1),(row3,col1). Each cell is one face with the target edge length;rows run downward,columns rightward. The four cells incol1 form a vertical strip,and the extra two attach left/right of its second cell. Add optional small fastening tabs outside faces without changing face dimensions. Label target lengths and calculate ideal surface area and volume for each. List clean reused paper/card,ruler,pencil and teacher-approved fastening/precutting provision. A plan is not a completed model. State the large/small length,area and volume scale factors and explain their relationship.
CSE11 — Make and check the cube pair honestly
Only after teacher preparation/approval,use the CSE10nets to fold and fasten the two models. Teacher provides safely precut nets or supervises blunt scissors;no knives,hot glue or staples. Mark faces and corresponding edges,check actual edge lengths with a ruler and record the measurements and any deviations from the ideal targets. Compare the ideal length/area/volume ratios with what the target models represent;do not claim exact measured similarity if construction errors disagree. Each learner records their own contribution and actual completed/not completed status. If making resources are unavailable,complete the paper analysis and mark the actual project NOT OBSERVED.
CSE12 — Audit the scale argument and its usefulness
Individually repair: A doubling every length doubles area and volume; B a signed coordinate factor−2 gives negative volume; C equal volumes prove two solids similar; D the model area in CSE9scales by50rather than2500. Give a checked numerical example or condition for each. Write a short reasoned statement explaining one useful scale decision from this unit and one limitation or assumption. A reasoned preference for an alternative method is accepted;enthusiasm is not graded. Then compare corrections respectfully with a peer if directed.
