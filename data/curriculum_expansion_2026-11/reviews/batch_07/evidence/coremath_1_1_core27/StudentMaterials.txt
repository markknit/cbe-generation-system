Real Numbers — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
CRN1 — Whole-number claims
For each of 0,1,2,3,4,9,11,15,21, state odd or even, and prime, composite or neither. Give the positive factor pairs for9 and15. Decide whether every odd whole number is prime and whether0 is even. Work individually first, then compare reasons with a partner. Whole numbers here are0,1,2,…; a prime is an integer greater than1 with exactly two positive divisors.
CRN2 — Exact real numbers and finite displays
Classify −5,0,2/3,0.125,0.777…,√16,√2,π,22/7 as rational or irrational;0.777… repeats7 forever and π denotes the exact constant. Give integer-fraction forms for rational values and identify integers and whole numbers. Explain why a finite calculator display of√2 does not prove√2 is rational. A displayed decimal is not the full exact value of an irrational number.
CRN3 — One divided by what?
Define reciprocal of nonzero real x as1÷x. Use division to find reciprocals of4,−5,2/3 and√2. Check each by multiplying by its original value. Can a real reciprocal of0 exist? Does reciprocal mean additive opposite? Give a counterexample. Keep exact expressions for√2.
CRN4 — Fractions, mixed numbers and decimals
Find exact reciprocals of1½,−2½,0.25,−0.4 and1/9. First express mixed numbers and terminating decimals as fractions; verify by multiplication. A learner proposes that the reciprocal of−2½ is−2/3 because the digits can be swapped. Audit the claim.

CRN6 — Actual calculator and table comparison
After the teacher supplies an approved calculator or supervised equivalent, individually enter1÷3.6,1÷(−2.5),1÷(2÷3) and1÷√(2). Record the actual key sequence, full display copied, and result to4significant figures where not exact. Compare1÷3.6 with CRN5. Predict1÷0, then follow only the normal approved calculation to see how the device reports it; an error message is not a real number. Teacher records observed or not observed device use. If devices are unavailable, complete the exact paper calculations and mark calculator use NOT OBSERVED; a written key sequence alone does not prove use.
CRN7 — Division as multiplication by a reciprocal
Paper planning tasks only: (a) A fictional4½L supply is packed into exact3/4L portions without spills. Use division and multiplication by a reciprocal to find the number of full portions. (b) An exact2.4m strip is cut into0.3m pieces without cutting loss; find the count. (c) Find (−3/5)÷(2/7) exactly. Show the nonzero divisor condition, units for each context, and one product check.
CRN8 — Explain a useful number choice
Using CRN7, write a short labelled advice card intended for a classmate: explain how classification prevents one specific mistake and how a checked reciprocal calculation supports one everyday decision. Include your numerical evidence, units, assumptions and the limit0 has no reciprocal. Exchange and explain the card with a peer only when the teacher directs; each learner records the peer question and their own revision. If not actually shared, mark presentation NOT OBSERVED. Reuse clean paper and respect different explanations.
CRN9 — Repair five plausible shortcuts
Individually audit and repair: A every odd whole number is prime; B all real numbers are rational because calculators show finite decimals; C reciprocal of−2½ is−2/3; D multiplying the input by10 multiplies its reciprocal by10; E a division-by-zero display error means the reciprocal is0. Give a counterexample or calculation for each. Then ask one question about number classification, exactness or reciprocal use that the class can answer with evidence.
