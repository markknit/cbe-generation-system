Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
CTG1 — Ratios belong to a named acute angle
Draw triangleABC with right angleC,AC=4cm,BC=3cm,AB=5cm. Relative to acute angleA,label opposite,adjacent and hypotenuse and find sine,cosine and tangent. Repeat relative to angleB and explain which sides swap roles. Define ratios for an acute angle in a nondegenerate right triangle and explain why the hypotenuse stays the same. Use only this paper model unless a safe teacher-selected right-angle object is actually provided.
CTG2 — A supplied mathematical ratio table
Use this complete teacher-prepared instructional table,not an official book extract. All ratio entries are rounded to4decimal places: 10°: sin 0.1736, cos 0.9848, tan 0.1763; 20°: sin 0.3420, cos 0.9397, tan 0.3640; 30°: sin 0.5000, cos 0.8660, tan 0.5774; 40°: sin 0.6428, cos 0.7660, tan 0.8391; 45°: sin 0.7071, cos 0.7071, tan 1.0000; 50°: sin 0.7660, cos 0.6428, tan 1.1918; 60°: sin 0.8660, cos 0.5000, tan 1.7321; 70°: sin 0.9397, cos 0.3420, tan 2.7475; 80°: sin 0.9848, cos 0.1736, tan 5.6713. Read sin40°,cos50°,tan30°andtan80°. Explain why reading the wrong column or angle unit changes the result and why≈is needed for rounded values. Do not infer that a displayed decimal is an exact irrational ratio.
CTG3 — Actual calculator mode and table evidence
When an approved calculator/supervised equivalent is supplied,individually select/confirm degree mode and obtain sin40°,cos50°andtan30°. Record the actual mode,key sequence,full display and4decimal-place result;compare with CTG2. Teacher records observed/not observed action. If no device is supplied,read the supplied table and mark calculator use NOT OBSERVED;do not invent a display or entry record. Explain why a radians-mode calculation of the same numeral is a different angle.
CTG4 — Complementary angles swap opposite and adjacent
For a right triangle with acute anglesθandφ,explain whyθ+φ=90°. Use the same triangle to derive sinθ=cosφ andcosθ=sinφ. Use CTG2to form and compare angle/complement rows10°/80°,20°/70°,30°/60°,40°/50°and45°/45°. State the exact identity separately from approximate table evidence.
CTG5 — Relate all three ratios without confusing rounded data
Use the exact3–4–5triangle fromCTG1to show tanθ=sinθ/cosθ andsin²θ+cos²θ=1. Derive tanθ=(O/H)/(A/H)=O/A for any acuteθ. Then compare the CTG2rounded sin40°/cos40°with its tan40°entry;explain why a small rounding difference does not refute the exact identity.
CTG6 — Derive45°from an isosceles right triangle
Draw an isosceles right triangle with perpendicular legs1unit each. Use Pythagoras to derive the hypotenuse,then derive exact sin45°,cos45°andtan45°. Explain why equal acute angles sum to90°. Compare with the rounded CTG2row. Each learner keeps a labelled drawing before any group chart is shared.
CTG7 — Derive30°and60°from an equilateral triangle
Draw an equilateral triangle of side2units and bisect it by its perpendicular altitude. Label each half-base1unit,hypotenuse2units,and derive the altitude by Pythagoras. Identify the30°/60°angles and derive all three ratios for both acute angles. Compare complementary pairs and the supplied table.
CTG8 — Why90°needs a limiting or coordinate case
The source includes90°among special angles. Explain why a nondegenerate right triangle cannot have another90°angle. Let an acute-angle right triangle have hypotenuse1andimagine the chosen acute angle approaching90°:describe opposite and adjacent lengths. At the unit-circle coordinate endpoint(0,1),state sin90°,cos90°and whether tan90°has a real value. Do not call a collapsed triangle an ordinary right triangle or treat a device’s finite error as a definition.
CTG9 — Choose the side ratio before calculating
Paper right-triangle cases: A hypotenuse10cm,acute angle30°,find opposite and adjacent sides. B opposite6cm,hypotenuse10cm,find the acute angle using the supplied teacher-prepared inverse cue sinθ=0.6→θ≈36.87°. C opposite3cm,adjacent4cm,find the acute angle using tanθ=0.75→θ≈36.87°. State the named angle,ratio,units and a check;use approved calculators only when supplied,recording actual action separately. Before solving A,read sin30°andcos30°fromCTG2andcompare with their exact special-angle values;label the rounded table calculation separately.
CTG10 — Elevation starts at an observer’s horizontal
Fictional paper observations on level ground:an observer’s sight point is1.5m above ground,a target is12m away horizontally,and elevation to its top is30°. Draw the right triangle,find the rise above the sight point and total height to2decimal places. Separately,a target is6m above a sight point and8m away horizontally;use the supplied inverse cue tanθ=0.75→θ≈36.87°. Explain why a slope distance or an angle from the vertical cannot be substituted silently.
CTG11 — Depression and elevation share parallel horizontals
Paper cases: A an observation point is15m above a target’s level and the angle of depression is45°;find horizontal distance. B observation height above the target level12m,depression30°;find horizontal and line-of-sight distances. Draw the two parallel horizontals and explain the equal acute elevation/depression angles. The given heights already refer to the sight point,so no extra body height is added.
CTG12 — Make and calibrate a simple clinometer
Use the supplied author-created0–180°template. Teacher provides clean card/paper,a straight sight edge or clean straw,string,a secure light soft weight and safe fastening;teacher prepares any attachment hole/precut pieces. Mount the sight edge along the straight baseline and hang the weighted string freely from the centre dot. Keep the instrument in the vertical plane of the sight line. Check a horizontal sight against a teacher-selected level reference:the string should cross90°. For a selected reading r,the angle magnitude is|90°−r|;record UP or DOWN separately from the target’s actual direction. Explain how r60°and r120°both represent30°magnitude on opposite scale sides. Actual making/calibration is teacher-observed;without resources complete only the paper plan and mark NOT OBSERVED. Paper calibration/application check only:r60°,targetUP,horizontal5m;state the angle and rise5tanθ,without calling these example numbers observations.

CTG13 — Use the instrument and separate real data from examples
Only after teacher approval/calibration,use the clinometer from stable level ground toward a safe stationary target. No climbing,roadside work or direct sun sighting. Record the actual raw reading r,UP/DOWN direction,angle|90°−r|,horizontal baseline distance and sight-point height,with units. Repeat readings and state their spread/measurement limits. Use rise=d tanθwhen assumptions fit and report actual uncertainty,not false precision. If equipment/access is unavailable,mark actual make/use NOT OBSERVED and analyse only this fictional example:r60°,UP,horizontal10m,sightpoint1.4m. Never label the example as a pupil measurement. Choose a nonzero horizontal baseline and a target with0°<θ<90°;a directly overhead/degenerate case does not fit the finite tangent model.
CTG14 — Audit a model and reflect on its usefulness
Individually repair: A opposite and adjacent do not depend on the named angle; B degree/radian modes are interchangeable; C rounded sin/cos table divisions must exactly match every rounded tangent; D tan90°is a finite very large number; E any line-of-sight distance can be used as a horizontal baseline; F a written clinometer plan proves actual making/use. Give a concrete mathematical or observation-based reason for each. Explain one specific useful decision fromCTG10/11/13andtwo assumptions/limits. If approved books/manuals/journals/devices are supplied,record an actual title/page/tool and one checked mathematical claim;otherwisemark external/digital research NOT OBSERVED.
