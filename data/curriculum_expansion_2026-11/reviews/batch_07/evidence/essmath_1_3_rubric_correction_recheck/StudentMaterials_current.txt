Quadratic Equations I — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
QE1 — Say what the letter measures
A fictional rectangular sign has width x cm and length (x+3) cm, where x>0. Write its perimeter and area as expressions, expand each, and evaluate both for x=4. A hall has n rows of chairs with (n+2) chairs per row; write the total. Explain why adding the two dimensions does not give an area. Label units and distinguish an expression from an equation.
QE2 — When does a condition create a quadratic equation?
A paper rectangle has width x cm and length (x+5) cm. Its area is36 cm². Write an equation without solving, expand it, and rearrange with zero on one side. A display has n rows and (n+1) cards per row,72 cards in all. Model that condition and state the possible values allowed for n before solving. Contrast each equation with the corresponding perimeter expression. Draw labelled rectangles on paper.
QE3 — Factor as an inverse operation
Expand to check each proposed factorisation:6x²+9x=3x(2x+3);x²−25=(x−5)(x+5);x²+25=(x+5)(x+5). Identify which are true for every x and repair the false claim by stating why that proposed product fails. Factor4y²−12y and9t²−16. Explain the difference between factoring an expression and solving an equation. No equation is imposed on these expressions.
QE4 — Choose a factor pair by product and sum
Factor x²+7x+12, x²−x−12 and x²−7x+12. For each, record the integer pair with the required product and sum, then expand your factors to verify. A learner uses (x+6)(x+2) for the first expression. Explain exactly what coefficient fails. Do not assume positive constants require positive signs in both factors.
QE5 — When the leading coefficient is not one
Factor2x²+7x+3 by splitting the middle term and grouping. Show why the two split coefficients must add to7 and multiply to6. Then factor3x²−2x−1 and verify by multiplication. Compare the first result with the incorrect proposal(2x+3)(x+1). Record a check that catches the error without guessing roots.
QE6 — Zero product and the root that division can lose
Solve by factorisation:x²+7x+12=0;2x²+7x+3=0;x²=5x;(x−3)²=0. State all real roots and substitute each in the original equation. Explain why dividing x²=5x by x at the outset can lose a solution, and why x(x+2)=12 does not permit setting either factor to zero.
QE7 — Algebraic roots and feasible dimensions
A fictional rectangular garden bed is x m wide and (x+3) m long with area54 m². Model, factor and solve; test both roots in the original equation and decide which is feasible. A separate display has n rows and(n+1) cards per row,72 cards total. Solve and interpret. Explain why an algebraic root is rejected by the context, rather than silently deleting it. No field measurement or digging is required.
QE8 — Audit an application before trusting its answer
Audit these fictional claims: A a square with side x cm and area25 cm² has side−5 cm because x²−25=0; B a rectangle with sides x and(x+2) has area x²+2; C x²−6x=0 has only root6; D(x−2)(x+5)=0 has roots2 and−5. Correct the reasoning and check it. In pairs, make one fresh factorable area/array application with labelled variables and restrictions; exchange and check by expansion/substitution. If an approved device/print reference is available, record one named source and the precise claim it supports; do not enter personal data or treat an unverified search result as evidence.
