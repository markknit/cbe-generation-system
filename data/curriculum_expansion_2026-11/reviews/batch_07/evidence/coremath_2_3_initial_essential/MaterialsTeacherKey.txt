Rotation — Materials Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
CRT1 — A rotation needs a centre,angle and direction
Use an actual teacher-supplied clock or a safe improvised paper clock with12equally spaced marks and one fixed-length pointer. Mark the pivot and compare the same pointer at12,2and4. State each clockwise angle from12 and check that the pointer length stays fixed. Trace one paper shape and rotate it around a marked pivot without resizing it;record actual apparatus action only when performed. If apparatus is unavailable,complete the specified paper predictions and mark observation NOT OBSERVED. Explain why different hour/minute hands are not automatically images of one another.
Teacher key and preparation
Angles from12are60°and120°clockwise;each neighbouring clock mark is30°. A rotation fixes its centre and preserves centre distances,shape,size,angles and orientation of a fixed labelled correspondence. The same fixed-length pointer is compared with itself;hour/minute hands may have different lengths. Actual manipulation/observations remain learner/teacher records,not invented data. Use a teacher-prepared safe pivot or precut pieces;no sharp improvised fasteners.
CRT2 — Quarter turns about the origin
On equal-unit axes,rotate triangleA(1,1),B(3,1),C(1,2)through90°counterclockwise aboutO(0,0),then separately90°clockwise. Draw and label both images. Derive/check the coordinate rules by tracking horizontal/vertical movement and compare corresponding distances.
Teacher key and preparation
Counterclockwise:(−1,1),(−1,3),(−2,1),rule(x,y)→(−y,x). Clockwise:(1,−1),(1,−3),(2,−1),rule(x,y)→(y,−x). The direction and centre must be stated. Do not swap the two rules or call the image a reflection merely because one coordinate changes sign.
CRT3 — Half turns,full turns and equivalent endpoints
RotateP(1,2),Q(−2,3),R(0,−1)through180°abouttheorigin and draw the image. Compare360°,450°counterclockwise,90°counterclockwise and270°counterclockwise endpoints for a chosen point. Explain which clockwise turn matches270°counterclockwise,and why a matching endpoint does not disclose how many full turns occurred.
Teacher key and preparation
Half-turn images(−1,−2),(2,−3),(0,1),rule(x,y)→(−x,−y). A360°turn returns every point to itself. A450°counterclockwise turn has the same endpoint image as90°counterclockwise;270°counterclockwise matches90°clockwise. From endpoint data alone,the angle is determined only modulo360°unless the motion is specified.
CRT4 — Move relative to a nonorigin centre
CentreO(1,1);triangleA(2,1),B(4,1),C(2,3). Rotate90°counterclockwise by marking each point’s displacement fromO,turning that displacement and addingO back. Draw the original,image and centre. Explain why applying an origin rule directly to the original coordinates would be wrong.
Teacher key and preparation
ImagesA′(1,2),B′(1,4),C′(−1,2). Relative differences(1,0),(3,0),(1,2)turnto(0,1),(0,3),(−2,1),thenadd(1,1). Verify centre distances and the90°direction. Applying(−y,x)to the original point without shifting the reference would rotate about a different centre.
CRT5 — Recover a centre and a nonidentity angle
One rotation mapsA(2,1),B(3,1),C(2,2)toA′(1,2),B′(1,3),C′(0,2). Plot all points and construct perpendicular bisectors ofAA′andCC′with ruler/set square. Find their intersection,then checkBB′. Measure/reason the directed angle between one object radius and its image radius,and check another. Explain why two coincident bisectors would not alone locate a unique centre.
Teacher key and preparation
AA′midpoint(1.5,1.5),bisectory=x;CC′midpoint(1,2),bisectorx=1;centreO(1,1). BB′also has bisectory=xandactsasacheck,notanindependentintersection. RadiusOA points east andOA′north,so90°counterclockwise;OCnortheast→OC′northwestagrees. The angle is specified modulo360°from endpoint data.
CRT6 — A half-turn shortcut and the identity limit
Under a rotation,P(1,2)maps toP′(5,4),Q(2,5)toQ′(4,1),andR(0,3)toR′(6,3). Find every object-image midpoint and determine a centre and angle,checking all radii. Contrast this with an identity image in which every labelled point stays unchanged. Can that identity alone identify a unique centre or distinguish0°from360°?
Teacher key and preparation
All midpoints are(3,3),so this is a180°rotation about(3,3);corresponding radii are opposite and equal. For an identity endpoint map,any centre could give0°or a full-turn multiple;the centre is not uniquely identified and endpoint data do not reveal the completed-turn count. Do not invent a unique centre for an identity.
CRT7 — Rotation symmetry is not merely moving to a congruent copy
For ideal unmarked figures,a nonsquare rectangle,square,equilateral triangle,regular pentagon,scalene triangle and circle,find the rotational-symmetry order about the natural centre. State the smallest positive matching angle when a finite one exists. Count matches over0<angle≤360°so the full turn is included once. Explain why every rotated image being congruent is different from a shape matching its own original position.
Teacher key and preparation
Finite orders/minimumangles:rectangle2/180°,square4/90°,equilateraltriangle3/120°,regularpentagon5/72°,scalenetriangle1/360°. The ideal circle matches every angle and has continuous rotational symmetry,with no smallest positive matching angle. A general rotation preserves congruence but need not place the figure on itself. Marks,colours or labels can lower the symmetry of a real object.
CRT8 — A cube has more than one type of symmetry axis
For an ideal unmarked cube,describe/sketch three axis types using opposite face centres,opposite vertices and opposite edge midpoints. For one representative of each,state the order and smallest positive matching angle. Use a teacher-provided safe model if available;otherwise use the stated ideal cube and record physical-model observation NOT OBSERVED. Do not treat a vertex of a drawing as the axis itself:an axis is a line through the solid.
Teacher key and preparation
Oppositefacecentres:order4,90°. Oppositevertices(bodydiagonal):order3,120°. Oppositeedgemidpoints:order2,180°. The representative line passes through the cube’s centre. There are3face-axis lines,4vertex-axis lines and6edge-axis lines,but identifying a representative of each and its order is the required task;do not confuse number of axes with order about an axis.
CRT9 — Compare finite and continuous solid symmetry
Use these ideal unmarked descriptions: a regular square pyramid has its apex above the square-base centre and four matching triangular side faces; a regular triangular prism has two parallel congruent equilateral bases and three matching rectangular side faces; a regular tetrahedron has four congruent equilateral faces; a right circular cone has its apex on the perpendicular axis through the circular base centre. Describe one rotational-symmetry axis and its order for each. For the tetrahedron also test the line through opposite edge midpoints. Use safe teacher-selected models/objects only when provided;record actual collection/observation separately and do not collect hazardous litter.
Teacher key and preparation
Squarepyramid:apex-to-basecentreaxis,order4. Regulartriangularprism:linejoiningbasecentres,order3. Regulartetrahedron:vertex-to-oppositefacecentreaxis,order3;oppositeedge-midpointaxis,order2. Rightcircularcone:apex/basecentreaxis with continuous symmetry at every angle,no finite least positive matching angle. These are geometric ideals;markings or construction deviations may reduce actual-object symmetry. Actual environmental collection is not inferred from correct paper descriptions.
CRT10 — Deduce direct congruence from rotation
Rotate triangleA(0,0),B(3,0),C(1,2)through90°counterclockwise about the origin. Compare all three corresponding side lengths and angles,keepingA→A′,B→B′,C→C′. Explain why rotation gives direct congruence. Compare the labelled reflection inx=0andstate what differs about clockwise/counterclockwise order even though both preserve size and shape.
Teacher key and preparation
RotatedA′(0,0),B′(0,3),C′(−2,1). Corresponding lengths3,√5,√8match,andanglesarepreserved. Reflection gives(0,0),(−3,0),(−1,2). Rotation preserves the orientation of the stated vertex order;reflection reverses it,so the former is direct and the latter opposite congruence under these labels. Do not relabel vertices to disguise the correspondence. Congruence is not the same as rotational symmetry of a figure in place.
CRT11 — Use a checked turn and report actual digital work
On paper,rotate triangle(1,0),(3,0),(1,2)through90°clockwise about the origin and check one pair of lengths. Write a specific useful application of rotation,using a correct clock/pattern/model example and a condition such as fixed centre,angle,direction or unchanged size. If an approved plotting tool is supplied,perform the same rotation and record the actual tool,inputs and output,then compare with paper;otherwisemark digital action NOT OBSERVED. Discuss resource care and actual peer sharing only when observed. No road visit or real machinery operation is required.
Teacher key and preparation
Paper image(0,−1),(0,−3),(2,−1);adjacent2-unitperpendicularsidesremain2andrightangled. Accept a concrete mathematically correct usefulness explanation and model limit,including a reasoned preference for another approach. Actual digital inputs/output and sharing are learner/teacher records,not invented displays. A fixed-length clock pointer can illustrate rotation,whereas different-length hands are not automatically congruent images.
CRT12 — Independent final rotation data
Use only this card and the separate prompts. TriangleP(1,0),Q(3,0),R(1,2)isrotated90°clockwise aboutO(0,0). Under a separate nonidentity rotation,A(1,0)→A′(3,2),B(2,1)→B′(2,3).
