Sub-Strand 1.3: Quadratic Equations (I)

Driving Question

Which algebraic answers describe a possible design? KICD: How are quadratic equations applied in real life?

Lesson # and Title

What did I observe?

What did I learn?

Lesson 1
What does the letter
measure?

Side labels lead to different operations for
area and perimeter.

Multiplying two linear dimensions can form
a quadratic expression.

A variable definition and units make an
expression interpretable.

Lesson 2
Which condition makes
an equation?

The same side model can be equated to a
specified total.

Equivalent quadratic equations can be
written with zero on one side.

A contextual restriction is part of a model,
not a later guess.

Lesson 3
Can multiplication be
undone?

A proposed product can insert an
unintended middle term.

Common factors and square differences
have verifiable product forms.

Expansion checks the full identity;
evaluating one input cannot prove it.

Lesson 4
Which pair has both the
right sum and product?

Signed pairs must satisfy two constraints
simultaneously.

A monic trinomial factors using the middleterm sum and constant product.

Expansion links pair selection to all three
coefficients.

Lesson 5
How does a non-unit
leading coefficient
change factoring?

A non-unit leading coefficient changes
cross-term products.

Middle-term splitting and grouping can
reveal two linear factors.

The product expansion checks the split,
factors and signs.

Lesson 6
Which roots does zero
product permit?

Division by x removes the valid case x=0.

Zero-product solving requires zero on one
side and all factor branches.

Substitution checks roots, while the factor
branches support a complete set.

Lesson 7

Both algebraic roots can satisfy a product
equation.

Positive dimensions and integer counts
restrict the feasible solutions.

A complete answer preserves the algebraic
roots and justifies its contextual choice.

Lesson 8
Can another team
reproduce our model
audit?

Different flaws survive different kinds of
checking.

The whole modelling chain needs
mathematical and contextual checks.

A respectful audit makes its correction and
its evidence reproducible.

Lesson 9
Independent quadratic
design audit

New data reveal each learner's modelling
and solving choices.

Teacher records demonstrated strengths
and specific errors from collected work.

Feedback distinguishes
equation,factorisation,root and context
errors.
