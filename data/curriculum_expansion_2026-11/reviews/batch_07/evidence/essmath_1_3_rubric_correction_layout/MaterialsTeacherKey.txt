Quadratic Equations I — Materials
Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy


QE1 — Say what the letter measures
A fictional rectangular sign has width x cm and length (x+3) cm, where x>0. Write its
perimeter and area as expressions, expand each, and evaluate both for x=4. A hall has
n rows of chairs with (n+2) chairs per row; write the total. Explain why adding the two
dimensions does not give an area. Label units and distinguish an expression from an
equation.

Teacher key and preparation
Sign perimeter 2x+2(x+3)=4x+6 cm; area x(x+3)=x²+3x cm². At x=4: perimeter22 cm,
area28 cm². Chairs n(n+2)=n²+2n for positive integer n. An expression names a quantity;
an equation states an equality. Addition combines lengths; area counts unit squares via
a product. Paper-only models, no personal measurements.


QE2 — When does a condition create a quadratic equation?
A paper rectangle has width x cm and length (x+5) cm. Its area is36 cm². Write an
equation without solving, expand it, and rearrange with zero on one side. A display has n
rows and (n+1) cards per row,72 cards in all. Model that condition and state the possible
values allowed for n before solving. Contrast each equation with the corresponding
perimeter expression. Draw labelled rectangles on paper.

Teacher key and preparation
x(x+5)=36 gives x²+5x−36=0 with x>0. n(n+1)=72 gives n²+n−72=0 with n a positive
integer. Perimeters are4x+10 and4n+2 respectively (the card count rectangle is an array
model, not a measured physical perimeter unless spacing/size is specified). Do not solve
here; later factors give x4 and n8. Accept any equivalent correct equation.


QE3 — Factor as an inverse operation
Expand to check each proposed factorisation:6x²+9x=3x(2x+3);x²−25=(x−5)
(x+5);x²+25=(x+5)(x+5). Identify which are true for every x and repair the false claim by
stating why that proposed product fails. Factor4y²−12y and9t²−16. Explain the difference
between factoring an expression and solving an equation. No equation is imposed on
these expressions.

Teacher key and preparation
First two identities true. (x+5)²=x²+10x+25, so third false; x²+25 does not factor into real
linear factors.4y(y−3);(3t−4)(3t+4). Factoring rewrites a product equal to the original

QE4 — Choose a factor pair by product and sum
Factor x²+7x+12, x²−x−12 and x²−7x+12. For each, record the integer pair with the
required product and sum, then expand your factors to verify. A learner uses (x+6)(x+2)
for the first expression. Explain exactly what coefficient fails. Do not assume positive
constants require positive signs in both factors.

Teacher key and preparation
(x+3)(x+4), pair3,4; (x−4)(x+3), pair−4,3; (x−3)(x−4), pair−3,−4. Proposed6,2 has
product12 but sum8, giving8x rather than7x. Both middle and constant terms must
agree.


QE5 — When the leading coefficient is not one
Factor2x²+7x+3 by splitting the middle term and grouping. Show why the two split
coefficients must add to7 and multiply to6. Then factor3x²−2x−1 and verify by
multiplication. Compare the first result with the incorrect proposal(2x+3)(x+1). Record a
check that catches the error without guessing roots.

Teacher key and preparation
2x²+6x+x+3=2x(x+3)+(x+3)=(2x+1)(x+3). Split6,1.3x²−3x+x−1=3x(x−1)+(x−1)=(3x+1)
(x−1). Incorrect proposal expands2x²+5x+3. Accept another valid factorisation method
with full expansion. This lesson establishes a simple non-monic case; no quadratic
formula is required.


QE6 — Zero product and the root that division can lose
Solve by factorisation:x²+7x+12=0;2x²+7x+3=0;x²=5x;(x−3)²=0. State all real roots and
substitute each in the original equation. Explain why dividing x²=5x by x at the outset can
lose a solution, and why x(x+2)=12 does not permit setting either factor to zero.

Teacher key and preparation
Roots−3,−4;−1/2,−3;0,5;3 (one distinct repeated root). For x²=5x write x(x−5)=0; division
by x assumes x≠0 and excludes the valid zero root. Zero-product reasoning applies to a
product equal to0, not12. Exact
substitution:9−21+12=0;16−28+12=0;2(1/4)−7/2+3=0;18−21+3=0;0=0;25=25;0²=0.


QE7 — Algebraic roots and feasible dimensions
A fictional rectangular garden bed is x m wide and (x+3) m long with area54 m². Model,
factor and solve; test both roots in the original equation and decide which is feasible. A
separate display has n rows and(n+1) cards per row,72 cards total. Solve and interpret.
Explain why an algebraic root is rejected by the context, rather than silently deleting it.
No field measurement or digging is required.

QE8 — Audit an application before trusting its answer
Audit these fictional claims: A a square with side x cm and area25 cm² has side−5 cm
because x²−25=0; B a rectangle with sides x and(x+2) has area x²+2; C x²−6x=0 has
only root6; D(x−2)(x+5)=0 has roots2 and−5. Correct the reasoning and check it. In
pairs, make one fresh factorable area/array application with labelled variables and
restrictions; exchange and check by expansion/substitution. If an approved device/print
reference is available, record one named source and the precise claim it supports; do
not enter personal data or treat an unverified search result as evidence.

Teacher key and preparation
A algebraic roots±5 but physical side5cm; B area x(x+2)=x²+2x; C x(x−6)=0 gives0 and6;
D correct. Review peer-created products/equations for consistency and feasible positive
dimensions, not just arithmetic. Use teacher-selected trusted reference or a supplied
print textbook, documenting actual title/page/access; do not fabricate a digital action or
source. Respectful critique targets claims.


QE9 — Independent modelling data
Release only with the final assessment. A fictional sign has width x cm and length(x+4)
cm; its area is77 cm². A separate array has n rows with(n+2) tokens per row, where n is
a positive integer. These are separate models. Show all working using the final task
prompts. Use ordinary paper and pencil; a calculator may check arithmetic after your
factorisation, but no teaching cards, peer help or worked examples are available.

Teacher key and preparation
For final section1 array n(n+2)=n²+2n; at n3 total15. Section2:3t²+10t+3=(3t+1)(t+3)
gives−1/3,−3; y²=4y gives0,4. Section3:x(x+4)=77 gives x²+4x−77=(x+11)
(x−7)=0;roots−11,7,physical sides7cm and11cm. Area77cm²,perimeter36cm. Section4
reflection assessed for a specific supported application/limitation, not enduring attitude
change. Withhold all teacher results.
