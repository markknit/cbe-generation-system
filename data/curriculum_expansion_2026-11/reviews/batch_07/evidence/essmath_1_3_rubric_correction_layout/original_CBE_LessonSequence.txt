SUB-STRAND OVERVIEW
Grade Level

10

Subject

Essential Mathematics

Strand

1.0 Numbers and Algebra

Sub-Strand

Sub-Strand 1.3: Quadratic Equations (I)

Total Duration

9 lessons × 40 minutes = 360 minutes

Sub-Strand Content

Formation of quadratic expressions; Factorisation and solve quadratic equations; Applications of quadratic equation

Learning Outcomes

form algebraic expressions from different situations,
form quadratic expressions and equations from real life situations,
factorise quadratic expressions in different situations,
solve quadratic equations by factorisation,
recognise use of quadratic expressions in real life situations.

Core Competencies

Communication and collaboration: the learner collaborates with peers as they discuss with peers and work out factors of quadratic
expressions.
Critical thinking and Problem solving: the learner develops open-mindedness, discusses with peers and works out factors of
quadratic expressions.

Core Values

Respect: the learner displays humility by listening to others opinions while discussing areas where quadratic equations are used in
real life situations.

Science & Engineering
Practices

Define variables and restrictions, build a model, factor, solve and test the interpretation.

Pertinent & Contemporary
Issues (PCIs)

Online safety: the learner observes online safety procedures and guidelines while searching on the application of quadratic
equations using digital resources.

Career Connections

Illustrative design, display planning and area estimation; no employment or professional competence claim.

Focus for Lessons

Form quadratic expressions/equations and solve factorable examples, retaining every algebraic root before applying contextual
restrictions.

Driving Question / Key Inquiry
Questions

Which algebraic answers describe a possible design? KICD: How are quadratic equations applied in real life?

Anchoring Phenomenon

Two sign designs satisfy the same area equation on paper, but one answer assigns negative side lengths.

Storyline Thread

Variables → model equations → factors → signed pairs → non-monic grouping → zero product → feasible roots → audit →
independent model.

Supporting Phenomena

Labelled rectangles, row arrays, inverse expansion checks and lost-zero-root audits.

LESSON 1 (40 minutes): What does the letter measure?
A. SPECIFIC LEARNING OUTCOMES
Category

Specific Learning Outcomes

Purpose

Represent stated dimensions and counts with meaningful algebraic expressions.

Knowledge

Perimeter adds lengths; area and row arrays use products. Units and restrictions give a variable meaning.

Skills

Label, translate, expand and substitute.

Attitudes

Listen to a partner's interpretation and correct an unsupported model.

Key Inquiry Question

Why does an area model contain x²?

Purpose in Storyline

The design team defines its quantities before looking for an answer.

Safety Notes

Paper-only fictional data; use no personal measurements and keep devices on approved resources.

Category

Specific Learning Outcomes

Purpose

Form equivalent quadratic equations from area and array conditions.

Knowledge

An equation asserts the model equals a known quantity; moving every term to one side preserves equality.

Skills

Translate conditions, expand and rearrange to zero.

Attitudes

State assumptions explicitly and check another person's reading fairly.

Key Inquiry Question

What information turns a model into a solvable equation?

Purpose in Storyline

The design brief now specifies an area or a total count.

Safety Notes

Use paper diagrams rather than moving furniture or arranging learners physically.

LESSON 3 (40 minutes): Can multiplication be undone?
A. SPECIFIC LEARNING OUTCOMES
Category

Specific Learning Outcomes

Purpose

Factor common-factor and difference-of-squares expressions and verify identities.

Knowledge

A factorisation must expand to the entire original expression; a sum of squares is not a difference of squares.

Skills

Extract common factors, recognise a square difference and expand to verify.

Attitudes

Use a counterexample to challenge a claim respectfully.

Key Inquiry Question

How can we know a proposed factorisation is correct for every x?

Purpose in Storyline

An unverified formula sheet introduces an extra middle term.

Safety Notes

Use paper and pencil; printed symbols have no physical measurement meaning unless stated.

B. LESSON OVERVIEW
Audit identities and factor two fresh expressions on QE3.
Materials: QE3. Use the companion Student Materials and Teacher Materials Key. Pacing: Predict 4 min, Observe 16 min, Explain 10 min, Driving Question Board

LESSON 4 (40 minutes): Which pair has both the right sum and product?
A. SPECIFIC LEARNING OUTCOMES
Category

Specific Learning Outcomes

Purpose

Factor monic quadratic trinomials with positive and negative terms.

Knowledge

In(x+p)(x+q),p+q is the middle coefficient and pq is the constant.

Skills

List signed factor pairs, choose both conditions and verify.

Attitudes

Value a checked method over a quick unverified guess.

Key Inquiry Question

Why is the right product alone insufficient?

Purpose in Storyline

Two plausible pairs give the same constant but different middle terms.

Safety Notes

Fictional symbolic problems use paper only.

B. LESSON OVERVIEW

LESSON 5 (40 minutes): How does a non-unit leading coefficient change factoring?
A. SPECIFIC LEARNING OUTCOMES
Category

Specific Learning Outcomes

Purpose

Factor simple non-monic quadratics by middle-term splitting and grouping.

Knowledge

Split coefficients sum to b and multiply to ac; grouping exposes a shared binomial.

Skills

Split, group, extract and expand.

Attitudes

Explain a checked sequence so a partner can inspect it.

Key Inquiry Question

Why must the split pair multiply to6 rather than3?

Purpose in Storyline

The design audit encounters a leading coefficient of2.

Safety Notes

Use labelled working on paper; no unrelated digital search is needed.

B. LESSON OVERVIEW
