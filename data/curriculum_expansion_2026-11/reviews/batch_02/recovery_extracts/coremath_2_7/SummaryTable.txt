Lesson 1
Which faces cover a prism?
A net separates faces that cover the solid.
Surface area sums exposed face areas in square units.
A triangular prism needs its two triangular ends plus three rectangles.
Lesson 2
Which height belongs to a pyramid face?
A pyramid face has a different altitude from the solid’s vertical height.
Face triangles use slant altitude;the base area must also be counted.
Four 15 cm² faces plus 36 cm² base give 96 cm².
Lesson 3
How does a curved surface become a flat area?
Curved surfaces can have different flat representations.
Cone curved area uses slant height;closed surfaces include their bases.
The 216° sector of radius 5 matches a radius 3 base circumference.
Lesson 4
What surface remains after a cone is cut?
Removing a similar cone leaves a tapered circular surface.
Frustum curved area isπ(R+r)s;total includes exposed ends.
Similarity supplies the original/removed lengths needed for subtraction.
Lesson 5
Can a sphere have the same kind of net?
A hemisphere has a curved half-sphere surface and may have a flat circular face.
Surface scaling is quadratic in radius.
Flat-strip approximations do not provide an exact sphere net or proof.
Lesson 6
Which faces disappear inside a composite solid?
Joining solids changes which component faces are external.
Composite surface area counts exposed regions only.
Hidden joining disks/squares are omitted even though each standalone solid had them.
Lesson 7
What does capacity measure inside a prism?
An outer shape and its usable inside space need not match.
Right-prism volume uses cross-section area×perpendicular length.
The 3 L capacity follows from internal dimensions,not an external label alone.
Lesson 8
How much does a cone or pyramid hold?
Matching ideal cone/cylinder and pyramid/prism pairs have a 1:3 volume model.
Use one third of base area×perpendicular height for cone/pyramid volume.
A practical/printed comparison illustrates the model without proving it universally.
Lesson 9
Which cone volumes make the frustum?
A frustum is the difference of two similar cones with different heights.
V=(πh/3)(R²+Rr+r²) agrees with that decomposition.
Cylinder bounds provide a useful size check without treating the frustum as a prism.
Lesson 10
How do sphere volumes scale?
Doubling radius multiplies a sphere’s volume by 8.
A hemisphere volume is half the sphere volume.
Area and volume have different units,scale factors and practical meanings.
Lesson 11
When does a composite volume add or subtract?
Assemblies and cavities require different component operations.
Add non-overlapping solid volumes;subtract a genuine removed volume.
A contact face is an area boundary,not a 3 D region to remove.
Lesson 12
Does this decision need area,capacity or both?
Open-top material and internal storage space use different calculations.
A planning decision needs the right quantity,units and assumptions.
The illustrative cost is not a real purchasing quote or a waste-inclusive amount.
Lesson 13
Can we defend a usable solid model?
Similar-looking errors can confuse heights,units or contact geometry.
A valid estimate needs sufficient data and the right decomposition.
The evidence record must distinguish actual making/measuring from diagram analysis.
Lesson 14
Independent solids commission
New dimensions reveal individual area/volume choices.
Teacher records actual demonstrated reasoning after collection.
A formula sheet alone cannot establish valid geometry or practical competence.
