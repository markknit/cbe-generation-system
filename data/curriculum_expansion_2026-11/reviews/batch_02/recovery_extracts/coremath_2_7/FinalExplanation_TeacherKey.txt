FINAL EXPLANATION: SURFACE AREA AND VOLUME OF SOLIDS
Teacher Key: Exemplar Answers (do not give to students)

INSTRUCTIONS GIVEN TO STUDENTS
Lesson 14:3 minutes procedure,20 minutes sections 1–2,11 minutes sections 3–4,2 minutes private check/limitation reflection and 4 minutes final checking/collection,total 40. Work independently with SV14 and a scientific calculator. Show which faces/solid parts are included,formulas,working and units. Give exactπ forms where appropriate and final approximations to 3 decimal places when requested. Formula support is allowed;no teaching cards. This written task does not certify physical making/measuring.

1. A closed frustum
For F,calculate total exposed surface area and volume exactly and approximately. Explain which circular ends are included and why vertical height 4,not slant 5,belongs in the volume formula. Give one geometric/data check.
Curved 45π plus ends 36π+9π gives 90π≈282.743 cm². V=(4π/3)(36+18+9)=84π≈263.894 cm³. Both circular ends are exposed because the frustum is closed. Volume uses perpendicular height 4;slant 5 belongs to curved surface. Check 5²=4²+(6−3)² or a valid bounding-cylinder/positive-area check.

2. A capped cylinder
For G,identify exposed faces and compute total surface area and volume exactly and approximately. Explain why the joined disks do not count as external surfaces,and why their contact area is not subtracted as a volume.
Exposed area cylinder side 24π+hemisphere curved 8π+bottom 4π=36π≈113.097 cm². V=cylinder 24π+hemisphere 16π/3=88π/3≈92.153 cm³. Joining disks are internal;the two solids touch at a plane rather than overlap in 3 D volume. Do not add a top-cylinder base or a hemisphere flat base to external area.

3. Capacity and the stated dimensions
Find H’s capacity in cm³ and litres. Explain why the specification says internal dimensions and what information would be missing if only outside dimensions were supplied.
25×12×8=2400 cm³=2.4 L. Capacity uses available inside space;outside dimensions alone do not provide wall thickness/internal geometry. Do not silently equate an outside box volume with usable capacity.

4. Explore a practical design decision
Choose F or G as a proposed ideal container. Explain one use of its computed surface area and one use of its volume. Name one further datum/assumption needed for actual material/capacity planning,and describe a safe next step to explore the design. Do not invent a price or claim a measurement you did not perform.
Surface area can estimate intended covering/material quantity;volume can estimate ideal storage space. Real planning might need internal/external dimensions,wall thickness,joining/cutting allowance,measurement precision or an applicable price. A safe next step is an accurately labelled paper model or teacher-approved measurement of a clean stable container,recording actual versus assumed dimensions. Judge explicit quantity-to-use reasoning,not a particular decoration or physical action inferred from writing.
