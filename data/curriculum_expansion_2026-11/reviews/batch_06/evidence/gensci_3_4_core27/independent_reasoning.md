# Independent qualitative solutions (core27)

Reviewed all 15 student cards and corresponding teacher keys, all 15 lesson records (75 phases/300 phase cells), the complete blueprint, four final prompts/exemplars and four four-level rubric criteria. No numerical registry entries exist. This is qualitative reasoning, not a claim that zero registered calculations prove correctness.

- GEM1: magnetic domains already exist; changing relative alignment changes net magnetisation. Soft iron has relatively low remanence, not necessarily zero. The model does not show electric charge creation.
- GEM2–3: external coil field, nearby-magnet induction, consistent stroking and field-assisted mechanical disturbance can change net alignment. Field, material and history matter. Decreasing alternating field, suitable thermal disorder or mechanical disturbance without a strong aligning field can reduce net magnetisation. Mechanical disturbance in an aligning field may do the opposite. None is a learner mains/heating/hammering procedure here.
- GEM4: bar-magnet field direction is N→S externally and S→N internally, with continuous noncrossing lines; compass north-seeking end is tangent. Drawings represent direction, not literal threads or measured strength.
- GEM5: A static magnet/static loop with steady flux has no induced emf. B relative motion that changes linked flux can induce. C an appropriately moving open conductor can have induced voltage, without a complete conducting current path. D changing primary current can induce voltage in a stationary linked secondary. Input energy is necessary.
- GEM6–8: choose x right, y up, z out of page. B=(B,0,0), v=(vx,vy,0), so v×B=(0,0,-vy B). Front-to-back active conductor points -z. Its directed motional-emf contribution is proportional to vy B times active length. Stationary and purely horizontal motion give zero; upward perpendicular has maximum magnitude for the equal-speed comparisons; an upward oblique motion with smaller perpendicular component gives smaller nonzero magnitude. Absolute meter direction requires a lead convention. Changing v to -v OR B to -B reverses the sign; changing both restores it. Actual lead movement/fringing fields/sensitivity can confound observations. A coherent supplied table cannot establish actual experiment performance.
- GEM9: stronger field, faster relevant motion and greater correctly active length increase the ideal motional contribution under controlled geometry. Increasing similarly linked turns adds coil contributions for equal flux-change rate per turn. Arbitrary wire length and unlinked turns are insufficient. Qualitative records alone do not supply numerical multipliers.
- GEM10: generator converts mechanical input via changing linked flux; transformer requires changing primary current and linked flux, with no sustained ideal secondary emf after a steady-DC transient; induction heating needs changing fields and designed conducting workpiece conditions. Ordinary battery bell is primarily current-fed electromagnet action, despite possible switching induction.
- GEM11: all five claims are false as written. Existing domains may cancel; mere presence of a magnet is insufficient; relevant transverse velocity/active conductor orientation matter; transformer output needs input; a battery bell is not an induction generator. Corrected claims remain conditional on geometry and device design.
- GEM12–13: supply–switch–normally closed contact–coil–return is a series path. Energised soft-iron core attracts armature, opening contact; current and attraction fall, spring returns armature and recloses contact. Mechanical linkage is essential. Low retention supports release. Actual assembly/test must establish this feedback or honestly record a failed cycle; a drawing/off-the-shelf buzzer proves no design-and-make action. Current limiting, compatible ratings, brief tests, power-off adjustments and heat/insulation stop conditions are necessary as stated.
- GEM14: supplied table ≠ performed experiment; drawing ≠ ringing prototype; stationary permanent magnet supplies no free energy; hammering does not guarantee permanent soft-iron magnetisation. Generator and transformer examples need mechanical or changing electrical input. The task does not ask for induced-magnitude factor reasoning.
- GEM15/final I1–I4: final I1 requires two magnetisation and two demagnetisation descriptions, domain/retention qualifications, and directed field diagram. I2 applies the motion/reversal/current distinctions above; I3 requires four qualitative factors and controls; I4 requires a generator/transformer example, bell contrast and honest unperformed-action scope. GEM15 teacher key contradicts I1's required method counts (four/three versus two/two).
