Rates of Reactions — Materials Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
RR1 — What quantity changes per time?
Supplied records: A forms24cm³ gas in30s; B forms24cm³ in60s. Calculate average gas-volume formation rates with units and compare. Distinguish final amount from rate. Define chemical reaction rate through reactant consumption or product formation. Classify rusting as chemical change and salt dissolving as a physical process; both can have rates without both making a new substance. Reference: OpenStax Chemistry2e12.1,https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/12-1-chemical-reaction-rates.
Teacher key and preparation
A:0.8cm³/s; B:0.4cm³/s; A has twice the interval-average volume rate. Equal final amounts need not imply equal rates. Volume formation is a measured proxy under stated conditions,not automatically a molar reaction rate. Dissolving salt alone is not new-substance evidence.

Calculate each10s interval average and overall0–40s average. Explain the declining slope and plateau without claiming an instantaneous rate or identifying an exhausted reactant. No gas apparatus is supplied.

RR3 — Approved practical plan and actual-performance record
Proposed school practical,conditional on teacher approval of product,local risk assessment,equipment and supervision. Use identical non-medicinal citric-acid/bicarbonate teaching tablets; no medicines or unknown chemicals. Teacher supplies6 identical clear open plastic cups,6 same-batch/same-size tablets,approved protection,timer,thermometer and measuring equipment. Teacher prepares100mL water per cup at nominal20°C or30°C,both within5–35°C; no learner heating. Run3 trials per temperature,one whole tablet in each fresh cup. Start timing on addition; stop when no new visible bubbles appear for5s. Record actual initial temperature and elapsed time. Do not stir; hold cup,water amount,tablet batch/size and endpoint definition constant. Stagger supervised trials. No tasting,crushing,dust,flames,gas collection or sealed containers. Wash hands,report spills and follow teacher disposal. If any approval/provision is missing,use RR4 and record not performed. Actual record headings: date,observer/role,approval,product/batch,water amount,trial temperature,time,endpoint deviations,cleanup. Nothing is prefilled. Adapted from the effervescence teaching idea at ACS Chicago,https://chicagoacs.org/articles/183; its original medicinal-tablet/hot-water procedure is not used here.
Teacher key and preparation
Teacher checks ingredients,allergies and suitable safe product; no medicinal substitutes. Observe each learner’s actual addition,timing,recording and safe handling before crediting outcome b. Actual temperatures may differ from targets and must be measured. A supplied record cannot replace performance. The defined endpoint combines dissolution,chemical effervescence and gas release,so1/time is an apparent comparison index for equal amounts/endpoints,not an intrinsic rate constant. No claim of complete chemical conversion at the visual endpoint. Cleanup/disposal require supervision.
RR4 — Replicates and an apparent-rate index
Fictional endpoint trials:20°C times60,64,62s;30°C times40,42,44s. Calculate means,ranges and1/mean time to3significant figures with s−1 units. Compare the defined endpoint processes. Explain why this index is neither gas volume per second nor a proven intrinsic chemical rate. Name two controls and two improvements; label all records supplied.
Teacher key and preparation
Means62s and42s; ranges4s each; indices0.0161s−1 and0.0238s−1.30°C has a shorter mean time in these fictional conditions. Equal batch/size,water amount,cup,no stirring and endpoint matter. Measure temperature/timing uncertainty and add repeats or clearer endpoint observation. Dissolution and gas release affect timing; do not claim a universal chemical law.
RR5 — Temperature and biological limits
Fictional enzyme product after a fixed1min:10°C→2units,20→4,30→7,40→8,60→1. Plot your own temperature/product graph,identify the highest tested condition,and explain why this does not establish the exact optimum. Compare kinetic-energy/activation-barrier reasoning for many chemical reactions with enzyme loss of activity at high temperature. Apply to cooling food without claiming sterilisation. No culture or food test. General reference: OpenStax12.2,https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/12-2-factors-affecting-reaction-rates.
Teacher key and preparation
Highest tested40°C,8units; an exact optimum needs intermediate conditions and uncertainty. Higher kinetic energy can increase successful reaction encounters,but not every reaction doubles per10°C. Enzyme structure can change at high temperature and activity decline; this dataset does not directly measure denaturation. Cooling can slow spoilage processes without killing every microorganism or certifying food safe.
RR6 — Concentration and fair comparison
Fictional controlled records: concentrations0.5,1.0,1.5mol/L give initial gas-volume rates2,4,6cm³/s. Temperature,solid amount and other conditions are stated equal. Describe the tested relationship and a particle-level reason; explain why it does not prove every reaction is first-order in this reactant. A faulty comparison also changes water temperature and solid particle size. Identify the confounders and propose controls. No actual solution handling.
Teacher key and preparation
Rate is proportional to concentration over these three supplied conditions,consistent with more frequent reactant encounters. It is not a universal order law. Hold temperature,solid mass,surface area,mixing and apparatus constant. When several factors change,one cause cannot be assigned uniquely. Distinguish concentration units mol/L from observed volume-rate units cm³/s.
RR7 — Surface area versus amount
Paper geometry model: a solid cube of side2cm is divided into8 separate cubes of side1cm without material loss. Calculate original and final total volumes and exposed areas,assuming every small-cube face is exposed. Explain how increased interface area can increase a heterogeneous reaction rate without increasing mass. Smaller same-mass sugar pieces may also dissolve faster; explain why dissolution alone is a physical-rate example. No grinding,dust,flame or actual reaction.
Teacher key and preparation
Large cube8cm³ and24cm²; eight small cubes total8cm³ and48cm². Area doubles while volume/mass stay fixed for the same material. More exposed interface can permit more encounters,without guaranteeing an exact rate doubling. Clumping changes exposed area. Sugar dissolution does not itself form a new chemical substance.
RR8 — Pressure in a relevant gas system
Gas model: fixed particle number N and temperature,initial volume V; compress to V/2. Calculate the number-density change and explain why it can affect an appropriate gas reaction without proving a rate multiplier. Compare adding inert gas at fixed V/T,which leaves reactant number density unchanged. Explain why sealed tablet bottles are not an appropriate pressure investigation. No compression apparatus is supplied.
Teacher key and preparation
N/(V/2)=2N/V. Greater reactant concentration can increase suitable reaction rates,but an exact multiplier needs a kinetic relationship. Inert gas at fixed V/T does not change reactant concentration,so total pressure alone does not give the same argument. Never seal a gas-generating reaction or improvise pressure equipment.
RR9 — Catalysts and enzyme activity
Fictional product records starting at0: with catalyst,20units at10s and40at20s; without,10at10s and20at20s. Calculate0–20s average rates. Explain a catalyst as an alternative pathway with a lower activation barrier,not a reagent consumed overall or a source of extra equilibrium yield. Relate enzymes to catalysis and name relevant conditions. No catalyst chemicals or enzymes are supplied.
Teacher key and preparation
Average2units/s with catalyst and1unit/s without. A catalyst can participate in steps and be regenerated; it is not consumed overall. It changes the pathway/rate without changing the equilibrium constant or composition at fixed conditions. Enzymes are biological catalysts and may depend on temperature/pH; not every catalyst is an enzyme and pH was not measured here.
RR10 — Light and physical/chemical process boundaries
Fictional photosensitive system at equal temperature/time: dark1product unit,low light3,higher light5. Explain why suitable light can affect this system without claiming every reaction requires light. A brighter-lamp comparison also warms the mixture; identify the confounder. Contrast chemical product formation with water evaporation,where the substance remains H2O. No UV,laser,heat lamp or photosensitive chemical is used.
Teacher key and preparation
The records are consistent with a light effect in this system,not a universal rule or direct mechanism proof. Control temperature to distinguish light from heating. Photosynthesis also depends on light with other limiting factors; rates need not increase indefinitely. Evaporation is physical phase change,not formation of a new substance. Reading these data is not performed experimental evidence. General mechanism: absorption of suitable light can excite a reactive species or initiate a photochemical pathway,providing energy needed for relevant changes. This explains a possible light-sensitive mechanism;the fictional correlations alone do not establish which pathway actually operates.
RR11 — Optimum conditions and real decisions
Fictional plant choices: A produces8units/hour at4tokens/hour; B produces9 at10tokens/hour. Quality,safety and emissions are unknown. Explain why fastest need not be most appropriate. If budget is5tokens/hour and both are hypothetically approved,make a qualified choice and state the real evidence missing. Distinguish a biological optimum from the highest tested temperature,and physical dissolution from reaction. Optional actual digital research: open an approved rate-factors page and record title/link/claim/limit; print notes do not establish navigation.
Teacher key and preparation
Only A fits the fictional budget; output alone does not establish profit,quality,safety or emissions. Tokens are fictional,not local prices. Optimum depends on the objective and constraints; enzymes can lose activity at high temperature. Dissolution is not automatically new-substance formation. Digital credit needs actual observed use.
RR12 — Audit all six factor claims
Audit six fictional claims using RR5–RR10: temperature always increases enzyme activity; total pressure increases every reaction rate; a catalyst increases final equilibrium yield; every reaction needs light; larger same-mass pieces always expose more area; increasing concentration always doubles rate. Give a condition-specific correction and supporting record for each. Explain one daily decision where a manipulated factor helps without guaranteeing safety or the best overall outcome.
Teacher key and preparation
Use enzyme60°C decline; gas reactant density versus inert gas; catalyst pathway/rate versus equilibrium; relevant photochemical process only; separate small cubes expose more area; concentration relationship is reaction/range dependent. A cooling example needs spoilage and safety limits. Six reasoned corrections are required,not six unsupported statements of “not always.”
