MI12 — Integrated infection and daily-life case
FINAL-ONLY HELD-OUT CASE. All events and business facts fictional; source summaries are paraphrases checked14Nov2026. A food-service record names Vibrio cholerae, a bacterium, and states that ingestion of contaminated water/food can transmit cholera, a bacterial infection (WHO Cholera, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cholera). A reported water contamination alert has not been independently investigated; no actual person is diagnosed here. Available risk actions: use an authority-verified safe water supply; keep that water in clean covered containers using clean utensils; follow current public-health instructions for the suspect supply. Covered storage does not treat contaminated water, and safe water can be recontaminated. Separate food-stock case: a vendor has fresh cut melon and reliable monitored refrigeration, but no validated drying, salting or fermentation process. CDC Preventing Food Poisoning https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention/index.html lists cut melon among riskier foods and recommends appropriate chilling. Cooling slows microbial growth; it does not sterilise food or establish the safety of any particular batch. Fictional business record: spoiled stock cannot be sold and must be replaced; no quantities/prices are given. For the three final sections, cite which supplied source/fact supports each answer and identify what remains unknown. This case provides no proof of a real outbreak, consumption, testing, investigation or community action.Task: For a fictional food-service case, identify the microorganism group and stated infection category, trace the supported transmission route, recommend route-matched prevention/control and food-preservation actions, and explain one economic effect and one evidence limit.
