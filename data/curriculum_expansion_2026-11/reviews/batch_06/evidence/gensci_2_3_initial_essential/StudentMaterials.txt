Chemical Bonding — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
CB1 — Valence and levels of explanation
Supplied outer-electron counts:H1,O6,N5,Na1,Cl7,Al3. Classify these explanatory records:Na transfers one electron toCl and the resulting opposite ions attract;twoH atoms share an electron pair;Al solid contains positive ion cores and delocalised electrons;separate water molecules attract through hydrogen bonding. Identify ionic,covalent,metallic and intermolecular descriptions. Explain why the source's list includes both bonds within structures and attractions between molecules. Source lists H2,O2,iodine,diamond,graphite among examples;these are elements,not compounds. Water/NaCl are compounds. Draw one labelled representation and name what it omits.
CB2 — Ionic transfer and extended structure
Draw before/after outer-electron dot/cross diagrams forNaCl andMgO usingNa1,Cl7,Mg2,O6. Use dots for nonmetal-original electrons,crosses for transferred metal electrons,brackets and charges. Inner shells may be omitted if stated. Then draw a small labelled alternating-ion lattice fragment and explain why a NaCl formula unit is an ion ratio,not a single molecule. Predict conductivity of solid versus molten NaCl from fixed versus mobile ions;no melting is performed.
CB3 — Covalent dot and cross drawings
Draw dot/cross outer-electron diagrams forH2,H2O,O2,N2 andI2. Counts:H1,O6,N5,I7. Show single,double/triple shared pairs as appropriate and all lone pairs. Explain bonding within each molecule separately from attractions between molecules. ClassifyH2O as compound andH2,O2,N2,I2 as elemental molecules. State why boiling molecular water does not normally break its internalO–H bonds intoH/O atoms.
CB4 — Dative-covalent formation and equivalent bonds
Supplied record:NH3 has threeN–H bonds and a nitrogen lone pair;H+ has no electron. Draw a dot/cross diagram forNH3 then an electron-pair donor arrowN→H+ and bracketedNH4+. Show how the donated pair forms a coordinate/dative covalent bond. Count all valence electrons and total charge. Explain why in the formed ammonium ion all fourN–H bonds are equivalent even though a formation diagram labels one pair's origin. No ammonia or acid is handled.
CB5 — Intermolecular attractions and property claims
Supplied diagrams to draw:two water molecules,each with solidO–H covalent connections;markpartialδ−onO andδ+onH,then a dottedO···H attraction between molecules. A second record is twoI2 molecules with strong internalI–I covalent bonds and weaker dispersion attractions between molecules. Label hydrogen bonding and van der Waals/dispersion attractions separately. Explain why all molecules can have dispersion forces but not all can hydrogen-bond like water. Compare the forces overcome on boiling water versus separating iodine molecules,without claiming the internal bonds disappear. References:IUPAC hydrogen-bond recommendations https://publications.iupac.org/pac/83/8/1637/index.html;van der Waals forces https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/V06597/pdf. Definitions are paraphrased for this level,not a universal strength ranking.
CB6 — Metallic bonding as a collective model
Supplied aluminium model:manyAl positiveioncores in an extended structure with delocalised valence electrons. Draw and label a small continuing fragment with a mobile-electron sea and attraction between cores/electrons. Explain electrical conduction,thermal transfer and malleability at this level;do not claim every atom forms an isolatedAl3+molecule. Compare moving electrons in metal with moving ions in moltenNaCl. No circuit or heating is operated.
CB7 — Structure comparison from complete reference data
Supplied text table:NaCl—giantionic lattice,solid doesnot conduct,molten conducts,high melting point,soluble in water under ordinary conditions;I2—simple molecular solid,weakdispersion between molecules,poor conductor,low solubility in water;diamond—giantcovalent3D network,hard,electricalinsulator;graphite—giantcovalent layeredcarbon,conducts along layers,layers slide;Al—giantmetallic,conducts in solidstate,malleable. Classify each structure;relate one property to particle arrangement/carriers or forces. Explain why 'all covalent substances have low melting points' and 'all high melting substances are ionic' fail. Reference:OpenStax Chemistry2e https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/10-5-the-solid-state-of-matter (structure categories);specific records here are teaching summaries.
CB8 — Investigating properties with provenance
Supplied reference table; no high-temperature tests: NaCl melts at about801°C; Al about660°C; pure water melts about0°C and boils about100°C at101kPa. NaCl dissolves in water; iodine only sparingly. Pure water has low electrical conductivity compared with ionic salt solution; Al and graphite conduct. Design fair tests for solubility,electrical conductivity and thermal conductivity,and explain what the melting/boiling records show. Optional actual low-risk solubility observation,only with teacher approval: known food-grade salt in room-temperature water using approved clean equipment. Record actual amounts,conditions and outcome; those are not supplied here. No tasting,iodine handling,flames,hot objects,mains electricity or molten-substance heating. If approval/resources are unavailable,use the supplied data and mark actual investigation not performed. Pressure,purity,temperature and measurement conditions matter.
CB9 — Diamond and graphite:one element,different structures
Supplied structure records: diamond—each C covalently linked to4 others in a3D network,no ordinary mobile electrons,very hard. Graphite—each C linked to3 within layers,delocalised electrons in layers,weaker attractions between layers. Draw labelled fragments and compare bond type,structure,electrical behaviour and hardness/sliding. Select one for a cutting abrasive and one for a pencil marking material; justify and state a limit. Do not scratch,sample or heat diamonds or unknown powders. Reference: RSC Carbon,https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/6/carbon.
CB10 — Aluminium cookware:properties and product limits
Supplied decision facts: Al metal conducts heat,is lightweight and malleable,and has a protective oxide surface. Actual cookware may be alloyed,coated or anodised. The product's instructions,stove compatibility and condition must be checked; bond type alone does not certify every product for every food or use. Explain metallic structure links to two useful properties. Draft questions for a manufacturer without inventing medical claims. Audit this fictional message: 'Any Al pan works on every hob; bond type proves all coatings safe; clean it with anything.' Correct each claim. Reference: RSC Aluminium,https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/13/aluminium,for general properties; no product-specific manual is supplied.
CB11 — Select materials from supplied constraints
Fictional jobs: A needs an electrical contact in a solid device; B needs a hard abrasive; C needs a sliding marking material; D needs a light,formable heat-transfer container with manufacturer approval. Available materials with the supplied CB7/CB9/CB10 properties: NaCl,I2,diamond,graphite,Al. Choose a supported material for each job,link structure/carriers/forces to its useful property,and state one practical uncertainty. No prices,dimensions or product certificates are given. Reflect on one way an initial choice based only on bond type changed after considering structure and conditions. No device is assembled.
CB12 — Build actual molecule and lattice models
Project1: plan then construct a H2O or O2 molecular model AND a NaCl alternating-ion lattice fragment using approved clean scrap card/paper and safe connectors. Include a labelled key,correct charges,molecular bond multiplicity and lattice continuation. Explain why the ionic fragment is not a NaCl molecule and why ball/stick sizes are schematic. Peer-check and revise actual products; record materials used without inventing savings. If construction cannot occur,submit a plan and mark project not performed. Optional actual digital activity requires a teacher-approved virtual bond lab or simulation with title,link and action record. The printed CB2–CB6 representations are a paper alternative and do not prove digital navigation. Label each product as simple molecular or giant ionic structure,and explain the distinguishing particle arrangement.
CB13 — Prepare an honest community-care message
Project2: use CB10's facts and limits to draft an Al cookware use/care message for a fictional audience. Include one property-use benefit,two questions needing an actual manufacturer's instructions,one practical care limit,and the line 'Draft: not yet delivered.' Plan review and permission before any school-approved presentation. Record an actual audience,date and feedback only if delivery occurs. Audit: 'Our draft proves we changed community behaviour and all such pans are safe.' Explain why unperformed outreach remains unperformed and why a bond model cannot certify product health or safety.
