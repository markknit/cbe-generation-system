Turning Effect of Force — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
TF1 — Perpendicular distance is the lever arm
Definition for this plane model: moment magnitude=force×perpendicular distance from pivot to the force's line of action. SI unit N m. Draw the following three records: a horizontal bar pivot O at x=0; a downward10N force at x=0.30m; a horizontal10N force along the bar through O; and a slant10N force whose line has stated perpendicular distance0.18m from O. Calculate the three magnitudes. For the slant case,application distance is0.30m but must not replace the given perpendicular distance. Which additional direction information is needed to assign its clockwise/anticlockwise sense? Source: OpenStax Torque,https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/10-6-torque.
TF2 — Units and rearranging the relation
Paper records with perpendicular forces: A12N at25cm; B a2.4N m target with0.40m lever arm; C a1.8N m target from6N. Calculate A's moment,B's required force,C's required perpendicular distance. Convert all distances to metres. Explain why12×25 is N cm rather than N m. State that these are ideal model values,not actual tool recommendations.
TF3 — Turning sense about one stated pivot
Use anticlockwise positive. In a horizontal plane drawing x points right and y up,on the page. Around O at x=0,calculate signed moments for A down4N at x=+0.20m; B down4N at x=−0.20m; C up3N at x=+0.40m; D up3N at x=−0.40m. Draw all force arrows. Then add A+B+C and state why signed total0.0 or nonzero moment alone does not establish full static equilibrium.
TF4 — Principle of moments with a weightless bar model
Ideal weightless horizontal bar,pivot at x=0,downward2N at x=−0.30m,downward unknownF at x=+0.20m. Find F for zero net moment and the upward pivot reaction for zero resultant force. Explain why pivot reaction has no moment about that pivot. Then include bar weight1N acting at x=+0.10m;recalculate F and reaction. Draw a force diagram for each case. No real bar or loads are implied.
TF5 — Approved metre-rule balance and honest records
Actual demonstration only after teacher approval of stable low bench support,light metre rule and secured labelled loads each no more than1N. Find the unloaded rule's actual balance point under supervision;do not assume50cm. Support there,so its own weight has approximately zero moment about the pivot. Place a secured labelled0.5N load20cm to one side and predict where a secured1N load on the opposite side would balance. Teacher checks clear drop/pinch zones and stable support before release;no fingers below loads,no sharp pivots,no heavy hanging masses. Measure actual perpendicular distances/forces and near-horizontal balance;repeat with0.5N on each side at15cm. Record date,actual pivot,roles,loads,distances,observations and deviations. If approved provision is missing,use supplied TF6 records and mark demonstration not performed. Do not leave apparatus unattended;teacher supervises removal. A written prediction is not an actual balancing action.
TF6 — Read a balance record with uncertainty
Fictional supplied metre-rule comparison,pivot through actual unloaded balance point:trial1,left0.5N at0.20m,right1.0N at0.10m;trial2,left0.5N at0.15m,right0.5N at0.16m. Calculate each side's moment and net signed moment with anticlockwise positive (downward left load is positive). Is each ideally balanced? Explain why a small mismatch does not prove dishonesty and why these supplied trials do not prove your group balanced a rule.
TF7 — Equal anti-parallel forces form a couple
Paper force pair:down6N at x=−0.20m and up6N at x=+0.20m on one rigid body. Calculate resultant force and net signed moment about O at0,then about P at−0.50m. Use anticlockwise positive;draw both pivots and perpendicular distances. Find separation of lines and verify couple magnitude F×separation. Explain why equal opposite separated forces can have zero resultant force but a nonzero moment;do not call them a balanced complete static system without another opposing moment.
TF8 — Unequal anti-parallel forces are not a pure couple
Paper pair:down4N at x=−0.20m and up6N at x=+0.20m. Compute resultant force and moments about O0 and P−0.50m. Compare TF7. Explain why multiplying one chosen force by the line separation does not give a pivot-independent couple for this unequal pair. Draw force lines and use signed contributions. No apparatus operated.
TF9 — Apply the turning-effect model to tools
Fictional door requires8N m moment about hinges. Compare perpendicular pushes at0.20m and0.80m from hinge:find required forces. Explain why pushing along a line through hinges is ineffective in this model. A fictional wrench requires6N m with a0.30m perpendicular arm:calculate force. State practical constraints that prevent recommending improvised extensions or excessive force. No real door movement,repair or loaded tool use required.
TF10 — School applications with honest evidence origin
If school-approved,walk only the designated safe route with teacher supervision to identify an accessible door hinge,handle or lever;do not operate equipment,enter traffic/workshops or photograph people without approval. Record actual object,pivot,line of force,useful lever arm and a limitation. Otherwise analyse these supplied fictional cards:door with handle far from hinge;scissors with central pivot and force applied to handles;tap knob with a twist from two opposed fingers. Draw one card and explain moment/application,then compare the tap's possible couple to a single handle force. Name two daily benefits with a practical limit;state whether the walk actually occurred. Optional digital research needs an approved moments/couple animation,title/link/action record;print drawings are not observed animation use.
TF11 — Audit moment claims
Correct four fictional claims with TF1–TF10 evidence:A any handle length is the perpendicular lever arm;B zero resultant force always means no turning effect;C equal clockwise and anticlockwise moments alone prove full static balance;D unequal anti-parallel forces always have the same moment about all pivots. Include a numerical counterexample for B/D and a force-balance condition for C. Explain one everyday use and its limitation without claiming actual practical work.
