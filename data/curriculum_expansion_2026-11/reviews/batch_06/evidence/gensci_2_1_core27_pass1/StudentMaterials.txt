The Periodic Table — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
PT1 — Atomic bookkeeping
Supplied neutral isotope records: sodium-23, atomic number11; oxygen-16, atomic number8; calcium-40, atomic number20. Atomic number Z counts protons; mass number A counts protons+neutrons, not average atomic mass. Complete p,n,e for each and explain why a neutral atom has equal p/e. Audit: 'oxygen-16 has16electrons' and 'changing electron number changes the element'. Draw a labelled nucleus/electron-region model for one example and name a model limit.
PT2 — First twenty element reference and shell task
Reference in atomic-number order (symbol,name,shell populations):1H hydrogen1;2He helium2;3Li lithium2,1;4Be beryllium2,2;5B boron2,3;6C carbon2,4;7N nitrogen2,5;8O oxygen2,6;9F fluorine2,7;10Ne neon2,8;11Na sodium2,8,1;12Mg magnesium2,8,2;13Al aluminium2,8,3;14Si silicon2,8,4;15P phosphorus2,8,5;16S sulphur2,8,6;17Cl chlorine2,8,7;18Ar argon2,8,8;19K potassium2,8,8,1;20Ca calcium2,8,8,2. Cover the population column; predict and draw neutral-atom shell arrangements for Li,O,Na,Cl,K,Ca, then uncover and compare. Explain why2,8,9 is not the ground-state school-shell arrangement of K. Shell model scope is these first20 ground-state atoms; the third shell is not universally limited to8. Reference for ground-state configurations: NIST Atomic Properties periodic table https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2019/07/10/nist_periodictable_june_2019-nocrop.pdf; shell populations here are a pedagogical summary.
PT3 — Construct a first-twenty periodic table
Use PT2 to place all20 elements on a blank grid you draw: columns labelled1,2,13,14,15,16,17,18 and rows periods1–4. In period1 only H column1 and He column18 are occupied; in period4 only K/Ca are included in this first20 subset. Explain what period counts in this shell model; compare Li/Na/K, Be/Mg/Ca and F/Cl. Explain He's exception to a simple 'group18 means8outer electrons' rule. State the modern periodic law using atomic number and recurring properties, then explain why atomic structure supports classification.
PT4 — Stability and electron affinity are distinct
Supplied model facts: He2,Ne2,8,Ar2,8,8 have closed occupied outer shells and are relatively unreactive. A simple outer-shell model helps explain many common ions, but atoms do not want electrons and not every element makes a simple ion. Electron affinity describes an energy change for adding an electron to an isolated gaseous atom, not a count of missing outer electrons. In this task positive affinity means energy released: fictional atoms X and Y release30 and10 arbitrary energy units respectively on electron attachment. Which releases more? Can that alone prove a solid compound forms? Explain why neutral Na losing one electron is not the same process as electron affinity. Source definition: IUPAC Electron affinity https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/E01977/pdf (qualitative paraphrase).
PT5 — Ion formation with conserved nuclei
Use PT2. For Na→Na+,Mg→Mg2+,Al→Al3+,F→F−,O→O2−,Cl→Cl−,K→K+,Ca→Ca2+, give electrons lost/gained, new electron total and shell populations, leaving proton count fixed. Write electron-transfer equations with e− on the correct side. Scope notes: H+ and H− occur in different contexts; Li commonly formsLi+,Be formallyBe2+ but many compounds have appreciable covalent character; B,C,Si commonly form covalent bonds rather than simple±4ions; N3−,P3−,S2− occur in appropriate compounds; He,Ne,Ar do not normally form simple ions. Explain why a first20 ion task should not force every element to form an ion.
PT6 — Draw dot and cross ion diagrams
Draw before/after outer-electron dot/cross diagrams for NaCl and MgO using PT2 populations. Dots denote the non-metal's original outer electrons and crosses the metal's transferred electrons; brackets and ion charges must be labelled. Inner shells may be omitted only if stated. Na transfers1 toCl;Mg transfers2 toO. For MgCl2 extension distribute Mg's2electrons to TWO Cl atoms. Explain why symbols do not represent different kinds of electron and why an ionic solid is not a set of isolated molecule-pairs.
PT7 — Valency, ionic charge and oxidation number
Working rules for selected cases: valency is combining capacity written here without sign; a monatomic ion's oxidation number equals its signed charge; an uncombined element has oxidation number0; H usually+1 and O usually−2 in the ordinary compounds here (exceptions exist). For NaCl,MgO,H2O,CO2,NH3, find named atoms' oxidation numbers using total0. For elementalO2 give O oxidation number. Explain why oxygen valency2 does not mean oxidation number+2 and why oxidation number need not be an actual ionic charge in a covalent molecule. Use common valencies:H1,O2,Na1,Mg2,Al3,Cl1 in these tasks. IUPAC oxidation-state guidance: https://iupac.org/recommendation/comprehensive-definition-of-oxidation-state/. Finally use the supplied valencies to formulate sodium chloride and magnesium oxide from element symbols; show the simplest combining ratio instead of merely copying a supplied formula.
PT8 — Polyatomic ions and common compound formulae
Supplied ion reference: ammoniumNH4+, sulphateSO4^2−, nitrateNO3−, hydroxideOH−, carbonateCO3^2−, phosphatePO4^3−, hydrogen carbonateHCO3−. Here source 'radicals' means polyatomic ions, not unpaired-electron free radicals. Cations:Na+,K+,Mg2+,Ca2+,Al3+,NH4+. Write smallest whole-ion neutral ratios and formulae for sodium chloride,magnesium oxide,calcium hydroxide,ammonium sulphate,aluminium nitrate,calcium phosphate,sodium hydrogen carbonate. Explain parentheses. Do not change a polyatomic ion's internal subscripts to balance charge.
PT9 — Check a substance chart
Draft chart with errors: table salt NaCl; calcium carbonate CaCO3; magnesium hydroxide MgOH2; ammonium nitrate NH4NO3; sodium carbonate NaCO3; potassium sulphate KSO4; calcium phosphate CaPO4; sodium hydrogen carbonate NaHCO3. Use PT8 to correct every formula, show charge totals for each correction and name each compound. Explain whether a chemical name/formula alone proves the composition or safety of a household product; products may be mixtures and labels must be checked.
PT10 — Balanced equations conserve atoms
Supplied unbalanced skeletons with correct product formulae:Mg+O2→MgO;H2+O2→H2O;Na+Cl2→NaCl;N2+H2→NH3. Gas elemental molecules here are O2,H2,Cl2,N2 (atomicity2); He/Ne/Ar gases are monatomic. Insert smallest positive whole-number coefficients and make an element-count table before/after. Audit 'Mg+O2→MgO2 is how to balance magnesium oxide'. These are symbolic tasks, not demonstrations; never mix or ignite substances.
PT11 — Equations with compound groups
Balance these supplied symbolic skeletons with formulae held fixed:CaCO3→CaO+CO2;HCl+NaOH→NaCl+H2O;Al+O2→Al2O3;Ca(OH)2+HCl→CaCl2+H2O. For every equation give coefficients and full atom tally; in the last one count O/H carefully. State why a coefficient multiplies every atom in a formula. No chemicals are supplied or used.
PT12 — Make and critique five electron models
Project1: choose ANY FIVE DIFFERENT elements1–20 from PT2. Record symbols/Z/shell populations, plan and construct five labelled models with clean scrap card/paper and drawn dots; alternative large tactile tokens require teacher approval and no small loose parts where unsuitable. For each chosen atom state its group and period with He correctly placed. Check every model against PT2 with a partner, correct errors and explain two limits. Write an inventory of reused/new materials; do not invent a money saving. If construction cannot occur, submit a clearly labelled plan and record project not performed. Optional digital activity requires a teacher-approved ion/atomicity animation and actual title/link/action evidence; printed electron-transfer frames in PT5/PT6 and PT10's gas list provide the equivalent paper option but do not prove animation viewing.
PT13 — Produce a common-substance chart
Project2: create a chart with names/formulae for at least seven supplied examples, covering all seven polyatomic ions: ammonium chloride,NH4Cl; sodium sulphate,Na2SO4; potassium nitrate,KNO3; calcium hydroxide,Ca(OH)2; calcium carbonate,CaCO3; calcium phosphate,Ca3(PO4)2; sodium hydrogen carbonate,NaHCO3. Use PT8 to verify charge balance and show one correction trail from an initial error or an explicit successful check. Explain two ways atomic structure helps organise the periodic table and supports these formula decisions; distinguish this ionic model from covalent compounds. Learner creates the actual chart; copying this reference alone does not establish independent formula skill.
PT14 — Integrated model audit
Audit five fictional claims using PT2/PT4/PT7/PT8/PT10: A calcium20 has2,8,10; B electron affinity is the number of outer-shell vacancies; C every first20 element forms a simple octet ion; D aluminium sulphate isAlSO4; E H2+O2→H2O is balanced. Correct each and explain the relevant evidence/rule; show an atom or charge count. Prepare one new explanation about why electron arrangement matters, with one model limit. This is formative practice, not the final question set.
