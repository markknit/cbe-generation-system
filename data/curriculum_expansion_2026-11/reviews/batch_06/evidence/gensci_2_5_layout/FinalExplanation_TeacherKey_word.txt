2. Factors with conditions and limits
Explain how each of temperature,pressure,catalyst,light,concentration andsurfacearea can affect an appropriate process,with a relevant limitation for each. Use RR13 enzyme data to identify the highest tested condition and explain why an exact biological optimum is not established. Distinguish one physical-rate process from chemical product formation.
Temperature can increase kinetic energy and the fraction of encounters overcoming activation barriers,but enzymes may lose activity at high temperature. Compressing a relevant gas can increase reactant concentration and encounters;adding inert gas at fixed volume/temperature does not. A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation barrier,not a changed equilibrium. Suitable absorbed light can excite species or initiate photochemical changes,not every reaction. Greater reactant concentration can increase encounter frequency,depending on the reaction/range. Greater exposed surface gives more interface sites for a heterogeneous reaction at fixed mass,not a universal exact rate multiplier. Enzyme35°C has highest supplied8units;intermediate temperatures and uncertainty are needed for an exact optimum. Dissolution or evaporation can be physical without new substances. These mechanisms are explanatory models,not directly proven by the fictional records.
