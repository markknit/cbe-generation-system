Rates of Reactions — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy


RR1 — What quantity changes per time?
Supplied records: A forms24cm³ gas in30s; B forms24cm³ in60s. Calculate average gas-
volume formation rates with units and compare. Distinguish final amount from rate.
Define chemical reaction rate through reactant consumption or product formation.
Classify rusting as chemical change and salt dissolving as a physical process; both can
have rates without both making a new substance. Reference: OpenStax
Chemistry2e12.1,https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/12-1-chemical-
reaction-rates.


RR2 — Graph and interval rates
Fictional gas record under constant measurement conditions: time/s0,10,20,30,40,50;
volume/cm³0,18,30,38,42,42. Draw labelled time-volume axes and plot every point.
Calculate each10s interval average and overall0–40s average. Explain the declining
slope and plateau without claiming an instantaneous rate or identifying an exhausted
reactant. No gas apparatus is supplied.


RR3 — Approved practical plan and actual-performance record
Proposed school practical,conditional on teacher approval of product,local risk
assessment,equipment and supervision. Use identical non-medicinal
citric-acid/bicarbonate teaching tablets; no medicines or unknown chemicals. Teacher
supplies6 identical clear open plastic cups,6 same-batch/same-size tablets,approved
protection,timer,thermometer and measuring equipment. Teacher prepares100mL water
per cup at nominal20°C or30°C,both within5–35°C; no learner heating. Run3 trials per
temperature,one whole tablet in each fresh cup. Start timing on addition; stop when no
new visible bubbles appear for5s. Record actual initial temperature and elapsed time. Do
not stir; hold cup,water amount,tablet batch/size and endpoint definition constant.
Stagger supervised trials. No tasting,crushing,dust,flames,gas collection or sealed
containers. Wash hands,report spills and follow teacher disposal. If any
approval/provision is missing,use RR4 and record not performed. Actual record
headings: date,observer/role,approval,product/batch,water amount,trial
temperature,time,endpoint deviations,cleanup. Nothing is prefilled. Adapted from the
effervescence teaching idea at ACS Chicago,https://chicagoacs.org/articles/183; its
original medicinal-tablet/hot-water procedure is not used here.

RR5 — Temperature and biological limits
Fictional enzyme product after a fixed1min:10°C→2units,20→4,30→7,40→8,60→1. Plot
your own temperature/product graph,identify the highest tested condition,and explain
why this does not establish the exact optimum. Compare kinetic-energy/activation-
barrier reasoning for many chemical reactions with enzyme loss of activity at high
temperature. Apply to cooling food without claiming sterilisation. No culture or food test.
General reference: OpenStax12.2,https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/12-2-
factors-affecting-reaction-rates.


RR6 — Concentration and fair comparison
Fictional controlled records: concentrations0.5,1.0,1.5mol/L give initial gas-volume
rates2,4,6cm³/s. Temperature,solid amount and other conditions are stated equal.
Describe the tested relationship and a particle-level reason; explain why it does not
prove every reaction is first-order in this reactant. A faulty comparison also changes
water temperature and solid particle size. Identify the confounders and propose controls.
No actual solution handling.


RR7 — Surface area versus amount
Paper geometry model: a solid cube of side2cm is divided into8 separate cubes of
side1cm without material loss. Calculate original and final total volumes and exposed
areas,assuming every small-cube face is exposed. Explain how increased interface area
can increase a heterogeneous reaction rate without increasing mass. Smaller same-
mass sugar pieces may also dissolve faster; explain why dissolution alone is a physical-
rate example. No grinding,dust,flame or actual reaction.


RR8 — Pressure in a relevant gas system
Gas model: fixed particle number N and temperature,initial volume V; compress to V/2.
Calculate the number-density change and explain why it can affect an appropriate gas
reaction without proving a rate multiplier. Compare adding inert gas at fixed V/T,which
leaves reactant number density unchanged. Explain why sealed tablet bottles are not an
appropriate pressure investigation. No compression apparatus is supplied.


RR9 — Catalysts and enzyme activity
Fictional product records starting at0: with catalyst,20units at10s and40at20s;
without,10at10s and20at20s. Calculate0–20s average rates. Explain a catalyst as an
alternative pathway with a lower activation barrier,not a reagent consumed overall or a
source of extra equilibrium yield. Relate enzymes to catalysis and name relevant
conditions. No catalyst chemicals or enzymes are supplied.

RR10 — Light and physical/chemical process boundaries
Fictional photosensitive system at equal temperature/time: dark1product unit,low
light3,higher light5. Explain why suitable light can affect this system without claiming
every reaction requires light. A brighter-lamp comparison also warms the mixture;
identify the confounder. Contrast chemical product formation with water
evaporation,where the substance remains H2O. No UV,laser,heat lamp or
photosensitive chemical is used.


RR11 — Optimum conditions and real decisions
Fictional plant choices: A produces8units/hour at4tokens/hour; B produces9
at10tokens/hour. Quality,safety and emissions are unknown. Explain why fastest need
not be most appropriate. If budget is5tokens/hour and both are hypothetically
approved,make a qualified choice and state the real evidence missing. Distinguish a
biological optimum from the highest tested temperature,and physical dissolution from
reaction. Optional actual digital research: open an approved rate-factors page and
record title/link/claim/limit; print notes do not establish navigation.


RR12 — Audit all six factor claims
Audit six fictional claims using RR5–RR10: temperature always increases enzyme
activity; total pressure increases every reaction rate; a catalyst increases final
equilibrium yield; every reaction needs light; larger same-mass pieces always expose
more area; increasing concentration always doubles rate. Give a condition-specific
correction and supporting record for each. Explain one daily decision where a
manipulated factor helps without guaranteeing safety or the best overall outcome.
