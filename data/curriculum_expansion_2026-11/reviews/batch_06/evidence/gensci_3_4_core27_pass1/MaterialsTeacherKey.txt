Magnetism and Electromagnetic Induction — Materials Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
GEM1 — Domains and soft iron
Supplied model:within each ferromagnetic domain,many magnetic dipoles are aligned;an unmagnetised sample can have differently oriented domains with little net magnetisation. An applied field changes the balance/alignment of domains. Soft iron magnetises/demagnetises readily and usually retains relatively little magnetisation after the field is removed compared with a permanent-magnet material. Draw before/applied-field/removed-field domain cartoons and explain one limit. Do not claim that unmagnetised iron has no domains or that soft iron always retains exactly zero magnetism. Source:OpenStax Ferromagnets and Electromagnets,https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/22-2-ferromagnets-and-electromagnets.
Teacher key and preparation
Cartoons show existing internally aligned regions,net orientations largely cancelling before,more aligned under field,less retained afterwards for the soft material. Diagrams are explanatory,not a domain photograph. Distinguish atomic/domain alignment from creation of new poles or electric charge. Residual magnetisation depends on material/history.
GEM2 — Compare magnetisation methods
Supplied paper methods:electrical magnetisation with a controlled direct-current coil;magnetic induction with soft iron near a magnet;stroking with one pole repeatedly in a consistent direction;mechanical tapping in an applied field can assist alignment. Describe each through the domain model and compare control,retention and conditions. Explain why hammering alone does not specify a reliable polarity/result and why magnetic induction of a piece of iron differs from electromagnetic induction of e.m.f. These are descriptions,not live instructions;no striking,heating or coil power is supplied.
Teacher key and preparation
DCcoil provides directed field;nearby magnet induces domain alignment/polarity;consistent stroking supplies a directional field;mechanical disturbance plus field can favour alignment. Result depends material/field/history;do not guarantee a strong permanent soft-iron magnet. Magnetic induction concerns magnetisation,not necessarily induced voltage/current. Best method depends criteria,apparatus and safety,not a universal ranking.
GEM3 — Demagnetisation with explicit conditions
Supplied descriptive records:an alternating magnetic field whose amplitude is gradually reduced can reduce net magnetisation;mechanical disturbance away from a strong aligning field can reduce ordered domain orientation;appropriate heating can disrupt ferromagnetic order. Explain these through the model,contrast hammering in an aligning field,and state why source methods cannot be executed with improvised mains coils,hot metal or unsupervised hammering. Source practicals remain unperformed unless separately approved by qualified staff with suitable apparatus;this card provides no live heating/striking procedure.
Teacher key and preparation
Alternating diminishing field can leave low net alignment;disturbance/heat effects depend material and external fields. Hammering in a field may instead help magnetise. Domains are not physically erased by every mild tap;not every temperature demagnetises all materials. No student mains/electrical-demagnetiser construction,heating or striking. Electrical alternative is descriptive unless approved purpose-designed low-voltage teaching equipment/protocol is provided.
GEM4 — Map magnetic fields safely or use a model
Draw a bar magnet labelledN/S and several continuous field lines:outside the magnet arrows runN→S,insideS→N as a closed-loop model;lines do not cross. A small compass north-seeking end follows the local external field tangent. Optional actual mapping only after teacher approves a low-strength school bar magnet and compass:place at several marked locations,record directions before moving,keep fingers clear and follow equipment/access requirements. No loose filings,strong small magnets,devices dismantled or power circuits. Otherwise use the supplied direction model and mark mapping not performed. Explain why field-line density in a schematic is qualitative,not a measured field-strength scale.
Teacher key and preparation
External directionNtoS,continuous loops,noncrossing lines and compass tangency. Greater representative density may indicate stronger field only under a consistent drawing convention;line count alone is not a measurement. Actual mapping requires observed positions/directions;Earth/background fields and alignment matter. No material streams travel along drawn lines.
GEM5 — What does induced e.m.f. mean?
Supplied scenarios:A closed wire loop and magnet remain stationary with steady field;B magnet moves relative to a loop and its magnetic flux changes;C an open conductor moves appropriately across a steady field;D current in a separate coil changes the field through a stationary loop. Describe when induction can occur. Define e.m.f. as energy transfer per charge associated with the source process (unit volt),not a mechanical force measured in newtons. Distinguish induced voltage from current:current additionally needs a conducting closed path and depends on circuit conditions. Qualitative treatment only;no numerical flux derivation. Reference:OpenStax Induced Emf,https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-1-induced-emf-and-magnetic-flux.
Teacher key and preparation
A no induced e.m.f. from unchanged flux;B changing flux can induce;C motional voltage may exist across the open conductor but no closed-loop current;D changing field can induce with no motion. Avoid 'a magnet's mere presence always produces current' or 'only moving magnets induce'. Energy is supplied by mechanical/electrical source,not created for free. No live circuit operated.
GEM6 — Fix conductor geometry before predicting
Paper apparatus:U-magnetNpoleleft,Spoleright gives field left→right in the page. The active straight copper conductor runs perpendicular to the page,front-to-back,with flexible leads to a sensitive centre-zero galvanometer in a closed path;other leads held outside the strong gap as far as practicable. Movement is in the page plane. Compare stationary,parallel-to-field horizontal,perpendicular upward,and angled motion with the same speed,active length and field. Predict zero/nonzero relative effects and explain why a wire moving in an unspecified orientation is insufficient evidence. Reference:OpenStax Motional Emf,https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/23-3-motional-emf.
Teacher key and preparation
Stationary/parallel motion gives no motional e.m.f. in this steady geometry;vertical perpendicular gives largest effect among equal-speed cases;angled with a nonzero perpendicular component gives smaller nonzero effect. Conductor length must be aligned with the motion×field direction;the stated front/back orientation provides that. Motion along field does not cut field in this model. Absolute meter left/right depends lead polarity;do not invent it before defining wiring.
GEM7 — Actual induction experiment and source procedure
Conditional actual experiment:teacher provides a stable school U-magnet,straight copper conductor,flexible insulated connecting leads and sensitive centre-zero galvanometer,prechecks connections,zero,sensitivity and safe clear movement. No external power source or mains is connected to this induction circuit. Use GEM6 geometry and fixed lead polarity;draw the actual apparatus before testing. Teacher supervises small controlled movements without striking magnet/meter or pulling leads. Observe and record actual readings for (1)stationary between poles,(2)parallel to field,(3)vertical upward perpendicular to field,(4)angled in-plane motion at comparable speed. Include a return/reversed-motion trial and baseline;repeat observations. Record date,observer/role,field/conductor/movement directions,lead convention,qualitative deflection and uncertainties,including any undetectable result. Do not fabricate expected deflection. If equipment/approval/sensitivity is missing,use GEM8 supplied record and mark experiment not performed. This matches the source's four motion conditions;qualitative predictions are not performance evidence.
Teacher key and preparation
Actual setup/drawing/roles/movements/records required for outcome d. Expect stationary/parallel baseline,perpendicular response,angled smaller response only with comparable speed/geometry;meter sensitivity and lead movement may obscure it. Reversal reverses a reproducible response if wiring/field unchanged. Zero/undetectable readings need honest diagnosis rather than invented result. Teacher precheck is needed;paper protocol is not approval/equipment provision.
GEM8 — Direction records and competing explanations
Fictional supplied fixed-wiring record:stationary centre;parallel motion centre;upward motion right;downward motion left;angled upward with smaller perpendicular component smaller right response. Predict what happens when magnet poles are reversed but upward motion retained,then when both poles and motion reverse. Explain why these left/right labels are not universal without the fixed wiring. An unexplained meter kick occurs when a lead is moved near the gap;name a confounder and an improvement. No actual observations supplied by learners.
Teacher key and preparation
Reverse field alone flips right→left;reverse both field/motion restores original right for this convention. Relative signs,not an absolute polarity rule. Lead movement can change loop flux/contribute induced response;hold return leads outside gap and control geometry/speed. Supplied qualitative records do not prove an actual experiment or field-strength measurement.
GEM9 — Qualitative factors need controlled comparisons
Supplied same-geometry comparisons:move a conductor faster across the field;use a stronger gap field at the same speed;increase active conductor length correctly cutting field;change movement from parallel to perpendicular;for a separate coil example increase linked turns while keeping the same flux-change rate per turn. Explain expected magnitude trends and what must be held constant. State why exact numerical multipliers cannot be claimed from these qualitative records and why coil-turn reasoning must not be applied to an arbitrary loose-wire shape without linked flux. No circuit or magnet strength is changed here.
Teacher key and preparation
Greater relevant rate of flux change can increase induced e.m.f.;faster perpendicular component,strongerB,greater properly oriented active length and more appropriately linked coil turns support larger magnitudes under stated controls. Parallel movement giveszero in GEM6;perpendicular maximises equal-speed case. Keep other geometry,meter/circuit,speed/field comparisons controlled. Source requests qualitative treatment;no numerical derivation or universal multiplier inferred.
GEM10 — Generators and transformers need change
Supplied application cards:generator converts mechanical input to electrical output through changing linked flux;transformer uses changing current/field in one coil to induce voltage in another;induction heating uses induced currents with suitable designed equipment. Explain each application's change and energy source,and why steadyDC through an ideal transformer primary does not sustain secondary induction after the switching transient. Contrast an ordinary electromagnet or bell powered by a battery,whose main action is current-generated magnetism rather than electricity produced from a stationary magnet. No appliance,mains circuit or cooker is opened or tested.
Teacher key and preparation
Mechanical work supplies generator output;changing electrical input supplies transformer/heating systems. No energy creation and no sustained ideal-transformer secondary e.m.f. from perfectly steady primaryDC;switching transients can occur. Conventional bell is primarily electromagnet make-break action,not a generator;self-induction can occur at switching but is not its primary intended application here. Product ratings/safety require qualified provision.
GEM11 — Diagnose induction and magnetisation claims
Correct five fictional claims:A unmagnetised soft iron contains no magnetic domains;B a stationary magnet always gives current in any nearby wire;C parallel/perpendicular movement gives the same effect regardless of geometry;D transformer output needs no energy source;E every battery bell is an induction generator. Give a relevant model/record and one uncertainty for each. Outline one useful daily induction application with its conditions and limits. Reading these records does not establish actual apparatus use.
Teacher key and preparation
A existing domains can have cancelling net orientations;B appropriate change/motionalgeometry and circuitconditions matter;C perpendicularcomponent/activeorientation matters;D energyinputnecessary;E electromagnet make-break primarily,not generator. Suitableapplicationgenerator/transformer with fluxchange and qualifieddesign/productlimits. Distinguish magnetic induction of iron from electromagnetic induction of e.m.f.
GEM12 — Design a simple electric bell without claiming a generator
Project design reference:teacher-approved low-voltage current-limited teaching supply compatible with coil (nominal1.5–3V only if its rating permits),switch,normally closed make-break contact,insulated electromagnet coil on soft-iron core,springy soft-iron armature carrying a light striker,quiet gong and stable frame. Circuit topology:supply+→switch→closed contact→coil→supply−;armature movement opens the contact,then its spring returns to close it. Draw a labelled circuit AND mechanical layout;explain the cycle and why soft iron's low retention matters. Use clean card/wood frame and rated school components;no mains,strong magnets,sharp scrap,soldering,hot work or direct battery short. Teacher approves coil/current limit,insulation,contacts,spacing and mechanical safety before any energising. This is design work only;actual making/testing is GEM13 and can remain not performed. Compare the bell with one generator or transformer application from GEM10,identifying the change and energy source in each.
Teacher key and preparation
Closed contact permits current,magnetising the core and attracting the armature/striker. The armature opens the contact,current falls,the core loses most magnetisation and the spring returns to close the contact. The cycle can repeat. A mechanical feedback connection is required,not merely a battery attached to an independent buzzer. Series topology and armature-contact linkage must be explicit. Coil rating,current limit,insulation and short test duration all matter;nominal voltage alone is insufficient. This is electromagnet make-break action rather than induction generation.
GEM13 — Make,test and record the bell project honestly
Conditional project build:after teacher approves GEM12 drawings,components,current limit,stable mounting and insulated connections,assemble the designed low-voltage make-break prototype with power disconnected. Use only approved rated components and safe connectors;teacher handles any component preparation. Teacher inspects before a brief supervised test (no more than5s at a time,or shorter rated limit),disconnects and checks for heat/mechanical/contact problems before any adjustment. Stop on heating,damaged insulation or unstable motion;no bypass of current limit or contact with live connections. Use a quiet striker and protect fingers. Record actual build/date/roles,diagram,observed cycle or failure,one justified power-off adjustment and retest if approved. If resources/approval absent,submit a labelled model/design and mark making/testing not performed. Record reused material inventory;do not invent savings or a working outcome. Explain how current magnetises the soft-iron core and how field removal permits release;contrast this with one induction application from GEM10.
Teacher key and preparation
Actual make-break prototype and observed test evidence are required before claiming project completion. A plan or off-the-shelf buzzer does not establish this design. A failed cycle is an honest result requiring diagnosis;continuous coil current without interruption may heat it,so stop and disconnect rather than prolong a test. Ratings,inspection,current/time limits and power-off adjustments all matter. Written answers guarantee neither construction nor performance.
GEM14 — Synthesis:what exactly was established?
Audit this fictional report:'We read a deflection table,so everyone performed induction;our bell drawing proves it rang;the stationary magnet powered the bell without a source;hammering always makes soft iron permanently magnetic.' Correct all claims using the source-required actions and models. Explain two daily induction applications with appropriate change/energy conditions. Separate actual observations from supplied/predicted records and describe one next approved evidence opportunity if unperformed. No actual experiment or project is implied by this audit.
Teacher key and preparation
Actual induction needs GEM7 setup,movements and records;the bell needs GEM13 actual prototype/testing. A stationary magnet does not provide free energy. Hammering outcomes depend on field,material and history;soft iron has relatively low retention. Generator/transformer examples need mechanical or changing electrical input. New opportunities require approved school provision,not automatic permission. Task-level reflection does not establish lasting responsibility.
GEM15 — Final-only magnetic and induction reference
HELD-OUT FINAL,paper models only. Soft-iron domains already have internal alignment;net orientation varies and retention is relatively low. Bar magnet N left,S right;external arrows N→S. U-gap field left→right in page,straight conductor front-to-back,movement in page plane,fixed lead convention. Supplied readings:stationary centre,parallel centre,upward right,downward left,angled upward smaller right. Reversing field flips polarity for the same upward motion. Qualitative comparisons change one factor:faster perpendicular movement,stronger field,greater correctly active length,or more linked turns in a separate coil model. Bell:limited low-voltage battery current powers coil,armature opens contact,current falls,spring recloses contact;not a generator. Records are supplied,not actual student experiments. No working bell,product certification or numerical e.m.f. derivation is given.
Teacher key and preparation
I1:two magnetisation and two qualified demagnetisation descriptions use the domain model;soft-iron retention is bounded. Field drawing has closed noncrossing loops,outside N→S. I2:stationary/parallel zero,vertical nonzero,angled smaller under equal conditions. Meter sign depends on wiring;field reversal flips it. Closed path is needed for current,not voltage existence. I3:explain qualitative flux-change factors and controls without exact multipliers. I4:generator/transformer energy/change and bell make-break distinction;written final proves no actual experiment or project. Outcome d requires separate observed GEM7 evidence.
