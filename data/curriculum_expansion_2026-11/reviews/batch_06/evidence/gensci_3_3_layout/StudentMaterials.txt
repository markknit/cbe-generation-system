Waves — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy


GWV1 — Read spatial and time wave records
Supplied transverse-wave profile at one instant:crests at x0.20,1.00,1.80m with
displacement+0.030m;trough displacement−0.030m;equilibrium0. A fixed point has
successive crests at t0,0.25,0.50s. Draw a labelled spatial profile and a separate
displacement-time sketch. Find amplitude,peak-to-peak displacement,wavelength,period
and frequency. Explain why a spatial crest separation and a time between crests have
different units. Contrast transverse displacement with longitudinal sound compressions
in air.


GWV2 — Interpret v=fλ rather than recite it
A periodic wave advances one wavelength during one period. Explain v=λ/T=fλ using
distance/time and f=1/T. For a model with v12m/s,f3Hz,findλ andT. If frequency
becomes6Hz while speed in the same nondispersive model stays12m/s,find newλ/T.
Explain why doubling frequency need not double speed and why amplitude is absent
from this linear relation.


GWV3 — Wave-equation calculations and units
For supplied models calculate:A speed
fromf50Hz,λ0.40m;Bfrequencyfromv340m/s,λ0.85m;C
wavelengthfromv300m/s,f1.5kHz;Dperiodfromf25Hz. Convert kHz toHz
andshowunits/rearrangement. Explain why using1.5 directly in C gives a thousand-fold
error. None is an actual measured wave.


GWV4 — A boundary can change speed and wavelength
Supplied periodic source frequency5Hz;wave speed20m/s in mediumA and10m/s in
mediumB. Assuming stationary boundary and unchanged source frequency,calculate
wavelengths and periods in both. Draw schematic equally timed wavefronts with closer
spacing inB. Explain which properties change and why frequency stays fixed. Refraction
means direction changes when entering obliquely under suitable speed change;normal
incidence can change speed/λ without bending.


GWV5 — Reflection with a defined normal
Draw a flat reflecting surface and a normal at the incident point. A ray makes35° with the
normal;draw and label the reflected ray and both angles. Compare reflection of light in a
mirror with sound echo at a wall. Explain that incident/reflected angles are measured
from the normal,not surface,and ideal reflection stays in the original medium. No laser or
mirror-glare experiment.

GWV6 — Echo method with an actual-action boundary
Conditional actual observation:teacher selects a quiet,approved level area away from
roads/traffic/hearing-sensitive activity,with a suitable distant reflecting wall and safe
standing zone. Use one moderate hand clap,not amplified sound,shouting,impulses near
ears or repeated noise escalation. Record whether a distinct delayed echo is heard;an
absent/unclear echo is a valid observation,not permission to get louder. Assign actual
source/listener/recorder roles and record site/date/conditions. If no approved suitable
site,use supplied records and mark not performed. Supplied fictional echo round-trip
time0.40s with sound speed340m/s:find wall distance. Compare a second record
wall17m away:find round-trip time. Explain why reverberation in a small room may not be
a separately resolved echo and why ordinary reaction timing cannot precisely measure
such short delays. No actual speed/distance is prefilled.


GWV7 — Sound refraction:day/night is a conditional model
Supplied temperature-only profiles,ignoring wind: D has warmer ground air and cooler
air above; N has cooler ground air with a warmer layer above. Sound travels faster in
warmer air. Draw height diagrams with speed-gradient labels and initially near-horizontal
rays; predict bending in each and explain possible ground-audibility differences. Qualify
the common day/night association: clock time or one listening report does not establish
gradients,wind,humidity or audibility. Reference:Penn State
Acoustics,https://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/demos/refract/refract.html. Optional
approved video needs teacher provision and an actual viewing record; diagrams are not
a watched clip.


GWV8 — Diffraction depends on relative scale
Gap0.50m;sound A wavelength0.85m,B0.085m. Calculate wavelength/gap ratios and
predict which tends to spread more in this simplified geometry. Draw spreading
wavefronts. Distinguish diffraction around edges from reflection at surfaces and
refraction due to speed variation. Explain why visible light,with wavelength far smaller
than a doorway,usually shows much less obvious doorway spreading than ordinary
sound.


GWV9 — Around-corner sound investigation
Conditional approved observation:in a quiet supervised school area,a speaker uses a
low conversational voice while a listener stands safely around an approved corner,out of
direct view. Do not block corridors,enter traffic,shout,use earphones or increase volume.
Record positions,heard/not heard,conditions,date and actual roles. Repeat a small safe
position change only with permission. If unsuitable,use this supplied fictional
record:voice audible out of view near a doorway,with hard walls present. Explain
possible diffraction and reflected alternatives;draw both paths. A single observation does
not isolate diffraction. Mark actual experiment not performed when using only the record.

GWV10 — Environmental wave cases and source distinctions
Supplied cases:coastal water waves can move sediment and erode or reshape
shores,affecting habitats,communities,infrastructure and port operations. Underwater
human-generated sound from ships/construction can affect marine-animal
communication or behaviour depending on level,frequency,duration and species.
Distinguish water-surface waves from acoustic waves. Explain possible benefits/harms
and two missing local facts. No visit or measured local impact is claimed.
References:NOAA
OceanService,https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/wavesinocean.html;NOAA
Fisheries,https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/national/science-data/ocean-noise.


GWV11 — Mitigation needs evidence and trade-offs
Fictional options:A restrict access to an already identified unsafe shore during official
warnings;B install an engineered breakwater without study;C reduce avoidable shipping
noise through qualified planning. Match each to water-wave/acoustic cases. Explain why
B needs engineering/ecological assessment and C has no quantified benefit from a
general summary. Request evidence covering communities,marine
ecosystems,infrastructure and operations. No construction,marine visit or emergency
activity occurs.


GWV12 — Waves and safer road-use reasoning
Supplied cards:rear-view mirrors reflect light;optical lenses refract light;horn/siren sound
can diffract around edges and also reflect. Explain a use and limit for each.
Correct:hearing around a corner proves the road clear,and any mirror removes every
blind spot. Draw a relevant model and identify evidence needed before real decisions.
No road visit,vehicle operation,laser or loud sound. Explain how wave knowledge helps
while proper road procedures and attention remain necessary.
