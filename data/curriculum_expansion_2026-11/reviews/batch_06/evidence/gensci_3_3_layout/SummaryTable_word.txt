Sub-Strand
Sub-Strand3.3: Waves
Driving Question
How are waves applicable in our day-to-day life? How do waves affect our environment?

INSTRUCTIONS
FOR TEACHERS: This is the teacher reference version — each row is pre-filled with expected student responses. Use it to assess student Summary Tables, identify gaps, and prepare discussion questions. The student version has blank cells for students to complete after each lesson.

Lesson # and Title
What did I observe?
What did I learn?
How does this explain the phenomenon?
Lesson 1
Which graph gives which wave quantity?
Explain amplitude,wavelength,period and frequency using distinct spatial/time records.
Spatial repetition and temporal repetition have different dimensions;particle motion differs from propagation.
Can a distance between crests also be a period? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 2
What relationship does the wave equation express?
Interpret propagation per period and calculate linked quantities.
v=fλ relates propagation speed,frequency and wavelength;fixed speed gives inverse f/λ variation.
Must greater frequency mean greater speed? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 3
Can units check a wave calculation?
Calculate speed,frequency,wavelength and period with correct prefixes.
Rearrangement and unit conversion are needed to interpret wave quantities.
What error follows from treating kHz as Hz? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 4
What changes across a stationary boundary?
Calculate boundary wavelengths and explain unchanged frequency.
At a stationary boundary/source model,frequency stays fixed while speed and wavelength can change.
Can speed change without bending at normal incidence? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 5
How is reflection represented?
Draw a normal-based reflection model and compare light/sound examples.
Reflection angles are measured from the normal;surface and medium conditions matter.
Why does the normal matter to an angle claim? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 6
What would demonstrate an echo safely?
Observe a conditional quiet echo and calculate supplied out-and-back records.
An echo is reflected sound;round-trip distance differs from one-way wall distance.
Can every room provide a distinct timed echo? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 7
How can a temperature gradient bend sound?
Draw conditional sound-refraction profiles without universal clock-time rules.
Spatial sound-speed gradients can curve rays;day/night labels do not establish the actual gradient.
Why might a night listening report need more evidence? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 8
When is diffraction most obvious?
Compare relative wavelength/opening scale and draw spreading wavefronts.
Diffraction depends on relative scale and geometry;it differs from bouncing or speed-gradient bending.
Why can ordinary sound spread through a doorway more obviously than light? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 9
What does hearing around a corner establish?
Observe a conditional quiet corner effect and compare possible mechanisms.
Audibility out of view can involve diffraction and reflection;one observation does not isolate cause.
Can an audible hidden voice prove diffraction alone? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 10
How can different waves affect environments?
Distinguish water-wave and acoustic cases and request relevant local evidence.
Effects depend on wave type,exposure,location and affected systems.
Does every marine-wave claim refer to the same phenomenon? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 11
What makes a mitigation proposal defensible?
Evaluate hypothetical exposure/engineering/noise options with trade-offs.
Mitigation requires qualified local evidence and may affect other systems.
Can a general wave summary choose the best structure? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 12
How can wave knowledge support road-use reasoning?
Explain reflection/refraction/diffraction uses and practical limits.
Wave models aid understanding but do not remove blind spots or certify traffic conditions.
Can an absent sound establish a clear road? Use a relevant mechanism and state an evidence limit.
Lesson 13
Can wave evidence support an independent application?
Independently connect wave characteristics,mechanisms and use limits.
Wave conclusions need model conditions and evidence origin.
Defend an application without claiming an unperformed demonstration.
