Linear Motion — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
GSLM1 — Distance and displacement on one line
A fictional walker travels4m east then3m west in7s. Take east positive. Draw the path and find distance,total displacement,average speed and average velocity. Explain why a nonzero distance can accompany zero displacement on a return trip. No actual walk is recorded.
GSLM2 — Position and velocity records
Supplied straight-line position record:time/s0,2,4,6;position/m0,6,12,18. Plot labelled position-time axes and calculate interval velocities. Compare a second record at the same times:positions18,12,6,0m. Explain negative velocity without calling speed negative. Explain the position-time slope and whether these tables alone resolve every motion between observations.
GSLM3 — Acceleration is change of velocity
Supplied constant-acceleration record:initial velocity+5m/s,final−1m/s,elapsed3s. Calculate acceleration,find time at which velocity is zero,and explain how speed can first decrease then increase while acceleration keeps the same negative sign. If needed use v=u+at;do not confuse acceleration with speed. Draw a labelled velocity-time line through the endpoints.
GSLM4 — Use v=u+at with stated assumptions
For straight motion with constant acceleration find:A final velocity fromu3m/s,a2m/s²,t4s;B time fromu3,v15,a2;C acceleration fromu3,v11,t4. Show substitution,units and direction convention. Audit a claim that this equation with one constant a automatically describes a vehicle whose acceleration changes halfway. No derivation is required.
GSLM5 — Displacement under constant acceleration
Givenu2m/s,a1.5m/s²,t4s,calculate displacement with s=ut+½at² and final velocity with v=u+at. Check displacement using average velocity (u+v)/2 times time. Explain when displacement equals distance in this example. Then audit the incorrect calculation2×4+½×1.5×4,which omits the time square.
GSLM6 — Equations without time and stopping models
Use v²=u²+2as for a forward-moving object withu4m/s,a3m/s²,s8m. Find final speed and explain why squared velocity alone does not determine direction without the context. Separately,a fictional object slows uniformly from12m/s witha−3m/s² until first rest:find time and stopping displacement. No actual vehicle/braking test or road stopping-distance recommendation.
GSLM7 — Piecewise motion and graph area
A fictional object moves at+4m/s for3s,then accelerates uniformly from+4 to+10m/s during the next3s. Draw its velocity-time graph using these intervals. Calculate displacement of each interval,total displacement,total average velocity and acceleration in each interval. Explain why one constant acceleration does not describe all6s. All velocities remain positive.
GSLM8 — Approved low-drop investigation
Actual observation only after teacher approves a light soft ball,stable floor-level catch tray,clear exclusion zone and maximum drop height0.50m;no climbing,stairs,windows,roads,hard/heavy objects or people below. Teacher marks a visible scale,supervises a release from rest without throwing,and retrieves only after rest. Repeat3times,recordactualheight,object,release condition,observer/date/role and downward motion. If approved video/frame capture is available,record actual frame times and positions against scale to examine increasing spacing for equal intervals;ordinary human stopwatch timing over this low height cannot establish precise g. Do not claim that looking alone measures acceleration or that ordinary air-resisted motion is exact free fall. If approval/provision missing,use GSLM9 supplied frame data and mark actual investigation not performed. Compare an ideal gravity-only model with real air resistance;no actual values are prefilled.
GSLM9 — Supplied free-fall frame positions
Ideal fictional downward-positive frame record:time/s0,0.10,0.20,0.30;position below release/m0,0.05,0.20,0.45. Calculate the three interval average velocities and their change per0.10s. Explain why these interval averages belong to intervals rather than measured instantaneous endpoint speeds. Check the positions against s=½gt² withg10m/s² andrestrelease. State two ways an actual GSLM8 recording could differ. These are supplied ideal values,not actual video.
GSLM10 — Downward free-fall calculations
For PAPER MODELS ONLY use downward positive,g10m/s²,negligible air resistance. A rest release falls for1s:find displacement and speed. B rest release falls1.25m:find time and speed at that point. C initial downward speed2m/s for0.40s:find displacement and velocity. Explain why these calculation heights are not instructions for actual drops and why mass does not enter the ideal equations.
GSLM11 — An upward object still accelerates downward
Paper model,upward positive,u+10m/s,a−10m/s²,no drag,return to same launch height. Find time to highest point,maximum displacement,total return time and return velocity. At the highest point state both velocity and acceleration. Find displacement and velocity at0.50s and1.50s;explain why same position does not mean same velocity. No launch is performed.
GSLM12 — Real-life applications and safety reasoning
Fictional statements to review:A a rolling vehicle on a steep road is in ideal free fall;B a falling object’s ideal impact speed doubles if fall height doubles;C a calculation alone proves a fall-protection system safe;D at the top of a thrown object's path gravity stops. Correct each using the model and conditions. Explain two relevant real-life precautions conceptually (for example preventing falls/excluding people from a drop zone and qualified road-speed/braking controls). Do not visit roads/heights,drop objects or prescribe real stopping distances. Present a short group explanation with evidence and actual participation status. Optional approved research needs title/link/action evidence;print work is not digital use.
