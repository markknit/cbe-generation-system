Microorganisms — Materials Teacher Key
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
MI1 — Microorganism groups and observable evidence
Classify each source-approved example as a fungus, bacterium or virus. For each, distinguish what the image/model shows from what it cannot show, and identify one way it can affect people.
Teacher key and preparation
Use teacher-previewed sources and age-appropriate examples. Fungi, bacteria and viruses are distinct groups; a picture alone does not establish that an organism causes a particular infection. Avoid diagnosing people or implying all members of a group are harmful.
MI2 — Helpful and harmful effects
Sort supplied examples into useful, harmful or context-dependent effects of microorganisms on people. Explain one economic benefit and one cost, naming the evidence needed for each claim.
Teacher key and preparation
Examples may include food fermentation, decomposition and spoilage or infection. Not all microorganisms are pathogens; preserve distinctions between economic examples and measured local impacts.
MI3 — Transmission route matching
For four teacher-approved fictional scenarios, match the stated infection and contact history to a possible transmission route (direct contact, respiratory particles, contaminated food/water or a vector). Identify one uncertainty in each scenario.
Teacher key and preparation
Use medically accurate, reviewed examples; routes are organism-specific. A scenario does not establish diagnosis or causation. Do not ask learners to disclose personal illness or contacts.
MI4 — Infection categories and evidence
Sort reviewed examples of fungal, bacterial and viral infections. For each example, state the source that supports the category and one prevention or control measure that matches its transmission route.
Teacher key and preparation
Use reliable public-health or curriculum sources selected by the teacher. Do not present antibiotics as treatment for viral infections; no learner diagnosis or treatment recommendation.
MI5 — Match prevention to transmission
Evaluate a set of fictional prevention plans. Match handwashing, safe food/water handling, ventilation or avoiding shared contaminated items to the stated route; reject measures that do not address the route and explain why.
Teacher key and preparation
Measures are examples, not a complete medical protocol. Emphasise route-specific prevention, access and reputable guidance; no stigma or blame.
MI6 — Control and prevention are related
Compare a prevention action before exposure with a control action after an identified risk. Explain how sanitation, safe handling, isolation under professional guidance or vaccination where applicable can reduce risk, and name a limit of each example.
Teacher key and preparation
Keep vaccination/general health information aligned to current local public-health sources; do not give personal medical advice or imply one measure prevents every infection.
MI7 — Economic roles of microorganisms
Use supplied evidence cards to explain two ways microorganisms contribute to economic activity and one way microbial growth can cause loss. Separate a general example from a measured local claim.
Teacher key and preparation
Possible examples include fermentation, decomposition, food spoilage and infection-related costs. Do not invent local prices or economic measurements.
MI8 — Food preservation choices
Compare drying, cooling, heating, salting, sugaring and fermentation using a provided information table. Recommend one method for a fictional food and explain the conditions, benefits and limitations using the supplied evidence.
Teacher key and preparation
Do not treat a classroom comparison as proof that food is safe. No tasting, consumption or home-preservation instruction; use teacher-reviewed food-safety sources.
MI9 — Plan a safe mould-growth investigation
Review a teacher-prepared plan for observing mould growth on food substrates. Identify the comparison, observation schedule, records and safety controls. Use supplied dated images/data if no approved sealed observation is available; state clearly whether any growth was actually observed.
Teacher key and preparation
Any sample must be prepared and sealed by the teacher under school approval, allergy-aware material choice and a disposal plan. Learners do not open, touch, smell, incubate at body temperature or take samples home. A supplied record is not student performance evidence.
MI10 — Read observation records without overclaiming
Compare supplied dated observations from two sealed food-substrate samples. Describe the visible change, identify what was held constant or changed, and explain why the observations do not identify the microorganism or prove a general rule.
Teacher key and preparation
Label all records as supplied unless genuinely observed. No organism identification from appearance alone and no opening of containers. Discuss uncontrolled conditions and replicate limits.
MI11 — Review a community food-preservation message
Draft a short, evidence-based food-preservation message for a fictional community audience. Check that it states a practical benefit and limitation, uses a reliable source and avoids claiming that one method prevents all spoilage or infection.
Teacher key and preparation
A learner draft is not a community sensitisation event. Actual distribution or presentation requires school/community approval and a reviewed message.
MI12 — Integrated infection and daily-life case
For a fictional food-service case, identify the microorganism group and stated infection category, trace the supported transmission route, recommend route-matched prevention/control and food-preservation actions, and explain one economic effect and one evidence limit.
Teacher key and preparation
All case facts are fictional/supplied. Mark each inference against the stated evidence; do not diagnose a real person or claim that learners performed an investigation or community activity.
