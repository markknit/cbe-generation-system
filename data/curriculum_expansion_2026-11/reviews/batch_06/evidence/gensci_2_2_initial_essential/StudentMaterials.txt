Chemical Families — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only curriculum draft • teacher review copy
CF1 — Family names and definition check
Supplied family reference: group1 alkali metals excludes hydrogen; group2 alkaline earth metals; group17 halogens; group18 noble gases. IUPAC's transition-element definition requires an incomplete d subshell in the atom or a cation; suitable examples include iron and copper. Locate these five categories on a table sketch. Explain why being a metal alone is not enough for transition classification. Sources: IUPAC https://old.goldbook.iupac.org/html/T/T06456.html; RSC element pages https://periodic-table.rsc.org/. This source's suggested 'transition' examples include copper,iron,zinc,lead; preserve that printed list as a discrepancy: lead is group14 p-block,while zinc is d-block but excluded by the incomplete-d atom/common-cation definition. Some broad region labels include zinc. State which definition you use; do not teach lead as a transition element.
CF2 — Sort all first twenty without forcing families
Supplied atomic-number list:H1,He2,Li3,Be4,B5,C6,N7,O8,F9,Ne10,Na11,Mg12,Al13,Si14,P15,S16,Cl17,Ar18,K19,Ca20. Family positions:Li/Na/Kg1;Be/Mg/Cag2;F/Clg17;He/Ne/Arg18;H is distinctive and not an alkali metal. Sort all20 into these four families plus 'other first20 elements'; identify whether the transition category has any member in this set. Give one placement reason beyond the symbol itself.
CF3 — Physical property investigation or supplied evidence
Actual low-risk option,only after teacher approval: inspect known clean copper wire and uncoated steel (iron alloy),without bending sharp ends;record colour,state and any teacher-approved low-voltage battery conductivity evidence. No mains,heating,unknown scrap,lead,batteries opened or chemical tests. If equipment/specimen approval unavailable,read supplied reference records instead and mark actual investigation not performed. Supplied at20°C:Na silvery solid;Mg silvery solid;Cl2 yellow-green gas;Ar colourless gas;Cu reddish solid;Fe grey solid. Metals here conduct electrically; elemental chlorine/argon gases are not ordinary metallic conductors. Compare two physical similarities/differences and explain why a colour alone cannot establish identity/family. Reference:RSC element fact boxes;no learner sees or handlesNa/Cl2/Ar gas. Design a fair conductivity comparison naming same safe circuit/contacts and a reference check;a printed circuit record is not actual testing.
CF4 — Sodium reaction records with conditions
SUPPLIED REACTION RECORDS,not experimental directions: sodium is highly reactive. RecordA water reaction gives sodium hydroxide and hydrogen; recordB oxygen-rich combustion gives sodium peroxideNa2O2. Balance Na+H2O→NaOH+H2 and Na+O2→Na2O2;count all atoms and explain why specifying oxygen product/conditions matters. Sodium can produce different oxides under different conditions;do not assume one product in all air reactions. For dilute acid mark learner reaction NOT APPROPRIATE;an acid reaction must not be attempted. Explain how the reference evidence differs from a personal investigation. No quantities,handling,cutting,storage or ignition procedure supplied. Reference:RSC Sodium https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/11/sodium.
CF5 — Magnesium with oxygen,water and acid
Supplied context-specific records:Mg heated in oxygen→MgO;Mg reacts only slowly with cold water under ordinary classroom conditions;with steam it can produce MgO+H2;with diluteHCl it produces MgCl2+H2. Balance Mg+O2→MgO,Mg+H2O(g)→MgO+H2,and Mg+HCl→MgCl2+H2. Label required conditions and compare cold-water/steam statements without changing the supplied products. Explain why a slow cold-water record is not 'never reacts'. Paper task only;do not burn magnesium,look at bright burning metal,generate gas or mix acid. Source:RSC Magnesium https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/12/magnesium (general properties);equations are supplied standard chemistry records,not observed here.
CF6 — Chlorine records and unsafe combinations
Supplied records:chlorineCl2 is a toxic reactive halogen gas;in water a simplified equilibrium is Cl2+H2O⇌HCl+HClO. Show atom balance and explain reversible-arrow meaning at this level. No ordinary direct chlorine–oxygen reaction is supplied. The phrase 'where applicable' in the source does not mean every named material must react with every other one. Chlorine/dilute-acid combinations are NOT a learner investigation;never mix bleach with acids or other cleaners. Explain why acidifying a chlorine-containing cleaner is hazardous rather than an ordinary class reaction. Source:RSC Chlorine https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/17/chlorine;CDC bleach safety https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/cleaning-and-disinfecting-with-bleach.html. No chlorine generation,smelling,collection or handling.
CF7 — Compare properties with evidence limits
Use CF3–CF6 supplied records to build a matrix forNa,Mg,Cl2,Ar:physical state;family;oxygen/water/acid behaviour where a valid record exists;one uncertainty. Ar is a noble gas and relatively unreactive under ordinary conditions,no listed reaction is expected in this school comparison. Distinguish missing record from confirmed absence and 'slow' from 'impossible'. Describe a safe investigation question,what observation would answer it and why the paper matrix does not establish performed chemical investigation.
CF8 — Named metals and uses,with source discrepancy
Supplied researched entries:Cu,group11,d-block,good electrical conductor,wiring;Fe,group8,d-block,strong structural steel alloys (pure iron differs from steel);Zn,group12,d-block,used as coating to protect iron;Pb,group14,p-block,dense/toxic,industrial shielding in controlled settings,no classroom handling. ClassifyFe/Cu with CF1 definition,explainZn/Pb qualification and link each use to a relevant property. Distinguish the element from an alloy/coating/product. These entries address all four source-named metals without calling every one a transition element. Sources:RSC https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/29/copper, /26/iron, /30/zinc, /82/lead. Optional actual digital research requires opening a teacher-approved page and recording title/link/claim;reading these print notes is not observed digital use.
CF9 — Noble gases:property-use reasoning
Supplied use cards:He is low-density and relatively unreactive,used in some balloons;Ne produces characteristic light in electrical discharge signs;Ar is relatively unreactive and used as shielding gas in welding. Match a relevant property to each use and state a limitation. Explain why a gas can emit light when electrically excited yet remain relatively chemically unreactive. Sources:RSC helium/neon/argon element pages;no gas cylinders,discharge apparatus,balloon inhalation or welding. Compare an unsupported claim 'all noble gases are completely inert and safe to breathe'.
CF10 — Elements and compounds in daily use
Supplied examples:elemental sodium vapour in certain lamps;NaCl compound used as food salt under ordinary food standards;Mg metal in lightweight alloys;magnesium compounds in some materials;chlorine chemistry used in controlled water treatment;Cu element in conductors. For four examples identify element/compound/alloy context,link property to use and state an important limit. Explain why the properties/hazards of sodium metal cannot simply be assigned toNaCl. Source:RSC element uses;these are comparison facts,not permission for food/cleaner handling.
CF11 — Road-lighting decision with limits
Fictional road-lighting tender:OptionS is a sodium-vapour lamp with yellow-orange output;OptionL is an LED fixture giving broad white light. Both are stated to meet the fictional site's required illumination level,but no energy/cost/glare/maintenance measurements are supplied. RSC Sodium describes its characteristic lamp light;this is an elemental-use example,not proof every modern road uses sodium. Explain the sodium property/use link,identify two further criteria relevant to road safety and state why neither colour alone nor these notes choose the best lamp. Draft a short evidence request to the road authority;no visits near traffic,live electrical work or lamp dismantling.
