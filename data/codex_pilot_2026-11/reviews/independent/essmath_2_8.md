### Coverage, assessment, pacing and classroom differences

Both units substantively teach all six source outcomes. Baseline offers additional calculation variety and stronger explicit overhead-cost treatment, but some purported resources are unspecified and its contextual conclusions contain the errors above. Baseline source wording is a grammatical adaptation rather than a verbatim transcription; it does not insert the pilot’s “the different situations” error. Baseline final has richer connected reasoning and actual quotation comparison, but still does not independently require unknown percentage commission, reverse currency conversion or a genuine loss calculation; earlier formative work covers these. No teacher exemplars were found in its student FinalExplanation, though prompts legitimately carry forward known data. Baseline teacher summary remains clearly labelled.

Baseline role-plays are classroom simulations using dummy money, so no real transaction safety risk is intrinsic. Respect for private finances is explicit. Its teacher-preparation burden is larger than the pilot’s because of B6. The baseline40-minute lessons have many prescribed spoken scripts, cold calls, waits, worksheet and movement transitions. L8’s specified blocks plus non-overlapping waits are approximately39.5 minutes **before** most teacher speech, learner oral answers, distributing/collecting materials and correcting work; this is a substantial classroom pacing concern, not a stopwatch-verified failure for every class. No separate final assessment duration or calculator/access rule is provided. Pilot arithmetic is simpler and gives30 minutes for a narrower assessment, while its own exact40-minute final also needs handling-time confirmation.
