                                 GENERAL SCIENCE GRADE 10: ACIDS BASES AND SALTS
                                  OpenAI-only evaluation • Sub-Strand 2.4 • 10 lessons • teacher review copy


                                                                SUB-STRAND OVERVIEW
Grade Level                       10

Subject                           General Science

Strand                            2.0 Matter and Chemical Reactions

Sub-Strand                        Sub-Strand 2.4: Acids, Bases and Salts

Total Duration                    10 lessons × 40 minutes = 400 minutes

Sub-Strand Content                Acids and bases; Reactions of acids and bases; Salts.

Learning Outcomes                 distinguish between acids and bases using the universal indicator and pH chart,
                                  explain the role of acids and bases in biological and chemical processes,
                                  identify products of chemical reactions of acids and bases,
                                  classify salts according to their behaviour when exposed to air,
                                  outline applications of salts in real-life situations,
                                  promote proper use of salts in day-to-day life.

Core Competencies                 Communication and collaboration: as the learner sensitises the community on the acidity and basicity of substances used in day-
                                  to-day life such as bleaching agents, vinegar, salts, soda, juices, water, wood ash, antacids, lemons among others.
                                  Self-efficacy: as the learner creates awareness on the importance of putting required amounts of salt on food to avoid cases of
                                  high blood pressure.
                                  Digital literacy: as the learner uses digital devices to research on the applications of salts in day-to-day life.

Core Values                       Responsibility: as the learner carefully uses digital devices to research on the applications of salts in day-to-day life.
                                  Respect: as the learner respects each other’s opinion while discussing with peers the meaning of acids, bases and salts.

Science & Engineering             Planning comparisons, observing, using simple models and arguing from evidence.
Practices

Pertinent & Contemporary          Socio-economic and environmental education: as the learner discusses the effect of salts on the environment.
Issues (PCIs)                     Safety and security: as the learner carries out experiments and presents findings on chemical properties of dilute acids and bases.
                                  Learner support programme (peer education): as the learner discusses with peers the functions of acids and bases in biological
                                  and chemical processes (digestion, respiration, bleaching action).

Career Connections                Laboratory support, agriculture, food processing and environmental monitoring.

Focus for Lessons                 Explain aqueous acidity, selected reactions and salt-air behaviour with distinct models and safe-use evidence.

Driving Question / Key Inquiry    Which evidence helps us explain and use these substances responsibly? KICD: Why are salts important in day-to-day life? How do
 Questions                                salts behave when exposed to air?

 Anchoring Phenomenon                     A fictional school store contains acidic vinegar, a mildly basic bicarbonate solution, a salt that becomes wet and washing-soda
                                          crystals that become powdery. Can one explanation account for all of them?

 Storyline Thread                         pH classification -> biological/chemical roles -> neutralisation -> carbonate -> metal -> salt-water exchange -> fair comparison ->
                                          uses/risks -> communication -> assessment.

 Supporting Phenomena                     Microscale indicator/carbonate activities, teacher-controlled acid/base and acid/metal demonstrations, and labelled illustrative salt-
                                          exposure data.

                                                            A. SPECIFIC LEARNING OUTCOMES
Category                       Specific Learning Outcomes

Purpose                        Demonstrate the six outcomes independently using supplied evidence.

Knowledge                      Classify, explain and apply the unit’s chemical and water-exchange models.

Skills                         Separate observation, inference and safe-use reasoning.

Attitudes                      Work independently, record reasoning honestly, and check the stated assumptions.

Key Inquiry Question           Which model is justified by the evidence?

Purpose in Storyline           Learners independently apply taught models to the supplied assessment card. Reaction identities are recall/application checks; changed
                               salt masses provide limited numerical variation, not a broad unfamiliar-context transfer test.

Safety Notes                   Paper assessment only; no tasting, chemical handling or personal health advice.
