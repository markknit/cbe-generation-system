Commercial Arithmetic 1 — Student
Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only pilot • teacher review copy


E1 — A club needs a workable budget
Illustrative school-club case. Funds available: 30 members contribute KES 200 each.
Planned costs: venue KES 1200; 30 notebooks at KES 80 each; transport KES 1800;
reserve KES 600. Compute income, each cost and the total. Is the plan affordable?
Suggest one justified adjustment if needed.


E2 — Discount offers
Illustrative quotations for identical notebooks, with equal quality and no delivery charges:
Vendor P lists KES 80 each and offers 10% off; Vendor Q lists KES 75 each and offers
5% off. Find the saving and net price per notebook for each; compare the cost of 30.
Extension: why is the larger percentage discount not enough to choose a vendor?


E3 — Commission and percentage commission
Illustrative sales agreement: a salesperson earns 4% of KES 15000 in sales. Calculate
commission. A second salesperson earns KES 450 on sales of KES 18000. Find the
rate. For this exercise there is no salary, tax or other payment. Explain what changes if
sales double and the rate stays fixed.


E4 — Profit and loss use cost as the base
Illustrative stall records: Batch A costs KES 2400 altogether and sells for KES 3000;
Batch B costs KES 2000 and sells for KES 1700. Calculate profit or loss and its
percentage of cost for each. The stated cost includes all costs for this task. Explain why
dividing A profit by selling price answers a different question.


E5 — Read a currency board from the dealer’s perspective
Illustrative rates, NOT current rates. Currency USD; dealer buys at KES 128 per USD;
dealer sells at KES 132 per USD. No extra fees. (a) A visitor gives the dealer USD 50:
how many KES? (b) A customer wants to buy USD 50: how many KES must they pay?
Write the direction of each exchange before calculating.


E6 — Two-way conversion and checking units
Use the E5 board, with no fees. (a) Convert KES 6600 into USD. (b) Convert KES 6400
received from selling USD 50 back into USD. Round a final currency amount to 2
decimal places. Is a round trip free? Show one unit check.

E7 — Revise the club’s purchasing plan
Return to E1. Choose the cheaper E2 quotation for 30 notebooks. Replace only the
notebook cost; keep venue 1200, transport 1800 and reserve 600. Compute revised total
planned allocation and unallocated funds. A salesperson’s employer separately pays the
E3 commission: should the club subtract it from its funds? Explain.


E8 — A fair quotation and consumer information card
In groups of four, prepare a one-page quotation for a fictional school club: include
marked price, discount, quantity, all charges, final total, rate assumptions and
expiry/date if relevant. Exchange cards with another group for checking. Write two
sentences explaining why arithmetic and transparent assumptions help people make
everyday decisions. No real buying, fundraising or posting is required.


E9 — Final independent assessment data
All figures are illustrative. A club has KES 4000. It plans 20 books marked KES 120
each, with a 15% discount, plus transport KES 500 and reserve KES 300. Separate
sales agreement: 3% commission on KES 8000 sales. A stall buys stock for KES 1600
and sells it for KES 2000. Dealer buys USD at KES 125 and sells USD at KES 130; no
fees.
