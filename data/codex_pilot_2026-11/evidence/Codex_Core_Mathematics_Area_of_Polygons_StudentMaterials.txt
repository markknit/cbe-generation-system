Area of Polygons — Student Materials
Grade 10 • OpenAI-only pilot • teacher review copy


C1 — The triangular bed
Illustrative school-garden plan, not a surveyed site. Triangle ABC has AB=10 m, AC=8 m
and included angle BAC=30°. Sketch it and label the perpendicular height from C to AB.
Can its area be 40 m²? Explain what information a gardener needs before buying cover
material.




C2 — Build the triangle-area rule
Draw a triangle with a horizontal base b, adjacent side a, and an acute included angle θ.
Drop a perpendicular height h from the far end of side a. Write sinθ in terms of h and a,
then substitute into A=bh/2. Practice: a=12 cm, b=7 cm,θ=60°.


C3 — Three sides, no measured angle
Triangle with sides 13 m, 14 m, 15 m. Use Heron’s formula A=√[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)],
where s=(a+b+c)/2. First verify the sides can form a triangle. Find s and A. Compare with
a triangle with sides 3 m, 4 m, 8 m.


C4 — A scale plan and a measurement limit
A triangular plot has measured side lengths 65 m, 70 m, 75 m. A drawing uses 1 cm to 5
m. Find the drawn side lengths and the plot area using Heron. Explain why the numeric
result is a model estimate if the field measurements were rounded.
C5 — Quadrilaterals from triangles
Sketch and label:(a) parallelogram base 12 m and perpendicular height 5 m;(b)
trapezium parallel sides 8 m and 14 m, perpendicular height 4 m. Decompose into
triangles or rectangle plus triangles. Find each area and explain why a sloping side
cannot replace perpendicular height.




C6 — Diagonals and a general quadrilateral
(a) A rhombus has diagonals 10 cm and 8 cm. They meet at right angles and bisect one
another. Find its area by four triangles.(b) A kite has perpendicular diagonals 12 cm and
7 cm; find area.(c) A general quadrilateral is split by a 10 m diagonal; perpendicular
heights of the other two vertices to its line are 3 m and 4 m on opposite sides. Find total
area.


C7 — Regular polygons and their centre
For a regular n-sided polygon of side s and apothem r (perpendicular from centre to a
side), draw n congruent triangles. Derive A=nsr/2. A regular hexagon has side 4 cm and
apothem 2√3 cm. Find its area.


C8 — The regular heptagon and octagon
Two regular polygons each have side 4 m:(a) heptagon, n=7;(b) octagon, n=8. Split a
centre triangle into two right triangles to show r=(s/2)/tan(180°/n). Calculate the apothem
and area, keeping intermediate digits. Give areas to 1 decimal place.


C9 — An irregular L-shaped bed
An L-shaped region is an outer 10 m by 8 m rectangle with a 4 m by 3 m rectangular
corner removed. Draw its boundary and split it into two nonoverlapping rectangles. Find
the area by two methods. Then explain why adding all outer side lengths would answer a
different question.
C10 — An irregular polygon from a plan
Plot the ordered vertices(0, 0),(8, 0),(8, 3),(5, 3),(5, 6),(0, 6) on squared paper, 1 unit=1
m. Join in order and return to(0, 0). Find area by decomposition, then by a bounding
rectangle minus a missing corner. Explain the units.




C11 — Choose and defend a covering estimate
A fictional garden contains separate, nonoverlapping beds C1 and C9. Find total area.
Cover material costs KES 150 per m²; for this exercise buy exactly the area needed, with
no waste, tax or delivery. Find cost. State two reasons a real order might differ.
