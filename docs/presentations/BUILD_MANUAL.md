**Science Lesson PowerPoint Build Manual**

*Guidance for building one teacher-ready PowerPoint per lesson from any Grade 10 science Scheme of Work: Biology, Chemistry and Physics*

Version 1.0 · October 2026 · Maths has its own separate guidance.

> **How this manual is used**
>
> • AI tools build the decks, lesson by lesson, following this manual exactly.
>
> • A teacher checks every deck before it is released (Section 14).
>
> • Every rule applies to every science unit. Examples come from different subjects to show how a rule applies; they are illustrations, not content to copy.

# 1. Why these decks exist

SeaVuria decks follow the Carbon TIME model (Michigan State University, NSF). Teachers used slide decks with embedded resources far more than long lesson plans. So the deck IS the lesson plan.

Every deck does two jobs at once:

- **The slides face the students.** Short, clear, visual. Only what students need to see.

- **The speaker notes teach the teacher.** They explain what to do, what to say, what to look for, and why the strategy works. A teacher who has never used this pedagogy should be able to teach the lesson well and learn the pedagogy by doing it.

Five principles run through every deck:

- **One phenomenon carries the unit.** Every lesson returns to the unit's anchor phenomenon, e.g. a healing cut (Biology), a rusting bicycle (Chemistry), a phone charger that gets warm (Physics).

- **Evidence before explanation.** Students predict and gather evidence before the teacher explains. Vocabulary, equations and laws come after the experience.

- **Check, don't hope.** Formative checks sit at the moments where learning can go wrong.

- **Plain English.** Many teachers and students use English as an additional language. Short sentences. Simple words.

- **Works offline.** Decks, simulations and resources must run in a classroom with no internet, through ARES.

# 3. Before building

The builder reads every lesson in the SoW first and notes:

- The anchor phenomenon and the unit's driving question.

- The title of every lesson (for the unit map).

- Which lessons are practicals or demonstrations (they need a safety slide).

- Any surprise, result or law a later lesson depends on, so no earlier slide or resource gives it away.

- Lesson length: 40 minutes (single) or 80 minutes (double).

## The golden rule

The deck teaches the SoW. It does not redesign it. Do not reorder lessons, add or cut activities, or change the phenomenon. If the SoW has a problem, follow Section 12.

# 6. Phase slides

## Predict

- Students write their own idea, alone and silent, BEFORE anything is taught. Then pair talk.

- Ask for a prediction AND a reason (e.g. "Which will heat up faster, and why?" "What colour will the indicator turn?").

- Put a visible timer on the slide. Follow with a sharing slide: list predictions on the board under "BEFORE — Our Predictions". No corrections.

- Where the lesson has a surprise, record a count (hands up) to return to in Explain.

## Observe

- Students gather evidence and record what they see. Evidence can be a practical, a teacher demonstration, data tables, graphs, a story, images, or an offline simulation from ARES (e.g. PhET).

- For practicals: a teacher demonstration slide with numbered steps (think-aloud), then a "Your turn" slide.

- Give students a recording format: a drawing guide, a results table with units in the headings, or axes for a graph.

- Students record ONLY what they actually see or measure. Never show the expected result before they record.

## Explain

- Students return to their predictions and mark them right or wrong in a different colour.

- Build the explanation with a chain of small questions, one click at a time. Name the idea, law or equation AFTER students understand what it describes.

- Where there is data, students look for the pattern first, then explain it.

- End with a short written explanation using Claim–Evidence–Reasoning or sentence starters. Claim = answer to the question; Evidence = data or observations; Reasoning = the science idea that links them.

- Comparison images or expected results go here, never before Observe.

## Driving Question Board

- Updated EVERY lesson. Groups post: evidence we now have and questions we still need to answer (two colours of notes).

- Evidence notes must use something seen or measured today, not the textbook. Include a quick class vote on whether the evidence supports a claim.

## Model Building

- Each student keeps ONE model for the whole unit and revises it in a NEW colour each lesson. The model can be a diagram, a particle model, a circuit or energy diagram, or a flow chart, whatever the SoW uses.

- List the specific revisions to make. Ask students to cite their evidence ("This model is based on evidence from…").

- End with a one-sentence prediction for the next lesson.

# 7. Speaker notes

Every slide has notes in the same order, with the labels in capitals. Leave out a label only if it has nothing to say on that slide. Start each phase's first slide with the phase total, e.g. "OBSERVE PHASE (35 minutes total)".

| **Label**                | **What it contains**                                                                                                                                             |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **TIME**                 | Minutes for this slide. All slide times must add up to the lesson length.                                                                                        |
| **DO**                   | What the teacher does, step by step, in short bullets. Include grouping and materials.                                                                           |
| **SAY**                  | Words the teacher can read aloud. Teachers may adapt them.                                                                                                       |
| **WAIT TIME**            | How long to stay silent after a question (usually 5–15 seconds; the full time for silent writing).                                                               |
| **LOOK FOR**             | The formative check: what to look and listen for, and how many students or books to check. Be specific (e.g. "results table has units in every column heading"). |
| **IF…**                  | What to do when students are stuck, give a common wrong answer, or equipment fails. Include the exact words to say.                                              |
| **WHY THIS WORKS**       | One or two sentences on the reason behind the strategy, so the teacher can use it in other lessons.                                                              |
| **ANSWER KEY**           | A model student answer for every task students write, including worked calculations with units.                                                                  |
| **SCIENCE NOTE FOR YOU** | Optional. Background the teacher needs to answer likely questions accurately or avoid a misconception.                                                           |
| **TEACHER REFLECTION**   | Last slide only. 3–5 questions to judge the lesson and adjust the next one.                                                                                      |

## Writing rules for notes

- Short sentences. Common words. One instruction per bullet.

- Write SAY lines as natural speech the teacher could read straight out.

- Cold-calling: say who to choose and why (e.g. one student who predicted X, one who did not).

- Name the common misconception for the lesson and how the slide addresses it.

- Link back to the anchor phenomenon whenever it fits.

> **Example — notes for an Explain slide (Biology, Cell Structure L6)**
>
> TIME: 6 minutes
>
> DO • Students write three sentences in their practical book, alone. • Circulate and read 4–5 responses as students write. Do not collect.
>
> LOOK FOR (formative check) • Each response mentions (a) no chloroplasts, (b) the onion layers get no light, and (c) specialisation or Amina's finger. • If a student writes only "they don't need them", prompt: "Why don't they need them? What does a chloroplast DO? Where does the onion grow?"
>
> WHY THIS WORKS • Moving from "I saw X" to "X tells us something about how the world works" is the most important thinking skill in the unit.
>
> ANSWER KEY • "Under the microscope I saw a cell wall, a nucleus and cytoplasm, but no chloroplasts. This makes sense because the onion layers grow inside the bulb where light does not reach…"

# 8. Formative assessment

- Mark every slide that carries a check with a "CHECK FOR UNDERSTANDING" label at the top.

- Place checks where learning can go wrong: after predictions, during data collection, after the explanation, at the Driving Question Board, and at Model Building.

- Every check needs a LOOK FOR and an IF… in the notes. A check with no teacher response is not a check.

- If the SoW has no check where one is needed, insert a PLACEHOLDER slide with a dashed border and the text "Formative check to be added". Do not invent an assessment; list it in the build log.

# 9. Practicals and demonstrations

- Add a Safety slide before any practical or demonstration. Five rules maximum, specific to today's hazards: chemicals (goggles, handling, disposal), heat (burners, hot water), glass, and electricity (low-voltage supplies only, dry hands).

- The glance slide lists every material per group, with quantities and concentrations, and a fallback if equipment is short (rotate groups; teacher demonstration; ARES video or simulation as a last resort, clearly labelled).

- Use low-cost, locally available materials where the SoW allows, and name substitutes.

- Demonstrate first with a think-aloud, then students work in groups of 3–4. Every student records in their own book.

- Set realistic expectations. Tell the teacher what students will and will not be able to see or measure with school equipment (e.g. faint colour changes, meter readings that vary, structures that are rarely visible). Honest results are valid evidence.

- Protect the discovery. If a slide or resource would give away the result, warn in the title-slide notes: "Do not show slide X until after students have recorded their results."

# 10. Resources and ARES

- Resources go on the hidden glance slide as ARES buttons, never on student slides.

- Each resource gets a match note: Best match · Preview first ("not an exact match") · Do not use (with reason).

- Never let a resource spoil a later discovery. Say when it fits better (e.g. "after Lesson 7").

- Never invent a resource or link. Until the ARES link format is confirmed, use http://ares.local/REPLACE-WITH-REAL-ARES-LINK and list every placeholder in the build log.

# 11. Visuals, notation and language

## Visuals

- Use drawn illustrations and diagrams. No photographs that identify students or children, and no graphic injury images.

- When an illustration stands in for a real observation, say so ("This is a drawing, not a photograph").

- Analogies: one mapping only, the same in every table, sketch and slide. State the analogy's limits in the notes.

- One idea per slide, large text, visible timers for timed tasks.

## Scientific notation

- SI units throughout, with units in every table heading and on every graph axis.

- Chemical formulas with correct subscripts; balanced equations with state symbols where the SoW uses them.

- Standard circuit symbols and diagram conventions as used in KICD materials.

- Worked calculations show the equation, substitution, answer and unit.

## Language and context

- CBE terms: Grade 10, strands, sub-strands, learning outcomes. Never "Form 1".

- Current, correct local names and institutions (e.g. KALRO, not KARI).

- Contexts familiar to Kenyan students, with local names and everyday settings.

- No deficit framing of communities, and no framing that casts outsiders as rescuers.

- British spelling to match KICD materials (e.g. colour, specialisation, aluminium).

# 12. When the SoW has a problem

The builder fixes only what it must, and records everything in a build log.

| **Situation**                                 | **What to do**                                                                                         |
|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Clear science error**                       | Correct it in the deck. Add a SCIENCE NOTE if teachers might see the old version elsewhere. Record it. |
| **Activities overrun the period**             | Trim times to fit 40 or 80 minutes without cutting an activity. Record what was trimmed.               |
| **Inconsistent analogy, labels or symbols**   | Use one consistent version. Record which was chosen.                                                   |
| **Missing formative check**                   | Insert a PLACEHOLDER slide (Section 8). Record it.                                                     |
| **Outdated term or local name**               | Correct it. Record it.                                                                                 |
| **Unsafe or unrealistic practical**           | Do not change it. Flag it for the teacher checker.                                                     |
| **Storyline, sequence or phenomenon problem** | Do NOT fix it in the deck. Flag it for the SoW's teacher owner.                                        |

The build log lists, for each item: lesson, slide, what the SoW said, what the deck does, and why. It goes to the teacher checker with the deck, then to the SoW owner so the SoW is corrected to match.
