Use after: Explain phase — Formal definition P = F/A, after the book vs pencil demonstration
Q1. A book and a pencil are pressed onto your palm with the same force. Why does the pencil tip hurt more?
  1. The pencil is heavier than the book
  2. The same force acts on a much smaller area, so the pressure is greater
  3. The pencil pushes with a bigger force
  4. The book spreads the force over a smaller area
Correct answer: B
P = F/A. With F the same, a smaller contact area gives a larger pressure. The masses and forces were deliberately kept equal.
Use after: Observe phase — Part B: shoe-pressure investigation (cm² to m² conversion)
Q2. A shoe sole covers 150 cm². What is this area in square metres?
  1. 1.5 m²
  2. 0.15 m²
  3. 0.015 m²
  4. 15 m²
Correct answer: C
1 m² = 10 000 cm², so 150 ÷ 10 000 = 0.015 m². This conversion is the error the lesson plan flags as most common.
Use after: Explain phase — Problem 1: maize bag on a pallet (Eldoret warehouse)
Q3. A maize bag on a pallet pushes 225 N onto each leg. Each leg touches the floor over 0.0025 m², so the pressure is 90 000 ____. Which unit completes the answer?
  1. Newtons (N)
  2. Pascals (Pa), which are N/m²
  3. Kilograms (kg)
  4. Square metres (m²)
Correct answer: B
Pressure = force ÷ area, so its unit is N/m², called the pascal (Pa). Newton is force, kg is mass, m² is area.
Use after: Explain phase — Problem 2: rungu vs crutch
Q4. A rungu tip (2 cm²) and a crutch base (50 cm²) each press down with 150 N. Which statement is correct?
  1. They exert the same pressure because the force is the same
  2. The crutch exerts higher pressure because it is larger
  3. The rungu exerts higher pressure because its area is smaller
  4. Neither exerts pressure because they are not moving
Correct answer: C
Rungu: 150 ÷ 0.0002 = 750 000 Pa. Crutch: 150 ÷ 0.005 = 30 000 Pa. Same force, smaller area, much higher pressure. So the crutch is safer on a soft floor.
Use after: Model Building phase — Revising the jerrycan force diagram
Q5. In which directions does atmospheric pressure push on the jerrycan?
  1. Only downward, on the top
  2. Only on the sides
  3. Inward on every surface: top, bottom and all sides
  4. Outward from the inside only
Correct answer: C
Atmospheric pressure acts on every exposed surface, from all directions. The lesson plan flags students who draw arrows only on top as holding a key misconception.
